He went up Safa, the hillock beside the Ka'bah, and began calling the clans by name: *O Banu Fihr! O Banu Adiyy!* People came, and those who could not come sent someone to find out what it was. Abu Lahab came. The leaders of Quraysh came.

Then he asked them a question instead of making a speech: if I told you there was cavalry behind this mountain about to attack you, would you believe me?

**Yes**, they said, together. *We have never received anything from you but the truth.*

Then hear me, he said: I am a warner to you before a severe punishment.

**Abu Lahab** answered first, and in public: *may you perish — is this what you gathered us for?*

Surah Al-Masad came down in reply, and it is the only place in the Qur'an where a living opponent is named and condemned by name, together with his wife.

Look at the exchange carefully, because the whole Makkan period is compressed into it. They certified his truthfulness in one breath and rejected his message in the next. Their problem was never evidence. Forty years of evidence had just been affirmed out loud by the men refusing him.

And notice what Al-Masad actually does to Abu Lahab. *Abu Lahab* means *father of flame* — a nickname he carried for his good looks. The surah keeps the nickname and lets it become the sentence: *he will burn in a flame.* His wealth, which he was relying on, *will not avail him.* His wife, who carried thorns to lay in the Prophet's path, will carry fuel, with a rope of twisted fibre around her neck.

The deed comes back in its own currency. That pattern will repeat across the whole Qur'an.
