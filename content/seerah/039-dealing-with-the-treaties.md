The covenant of Madinah bound all parties to mutual defence and non-aggression. Two tribes broke it, at different times and for different reasons, and the response in each case was a siege that ended in exile rather than blood.

**Banu Qaynuqa** were the first. After Badr they took the position that Quraysh had not known how to fight and that the Muslims should not be deceived by that victory, and matters escalated to a declaration that the treaty no longer bound them. They were besieged in their quarter for fifteen days and surrendered. They went into exile towards Syria, taking what they could carry.

**Banu an-Nadir** passed intelligence on Muslim movements to Quraysh, and then made an attempt on the Prophet's life while he sat by the wall of their settlement, intending to drop a millstone on him from the roof. He was informed, left without a word, and sent them notice.

They were besieged for around two weeks, and surrendered on terms: they would leave, with their families, carrying what their camels could take. **Surah Al-Hashr** was revealed about their expulsion, and it opens with the observation that *they thought their fortresses would protect them from Allah* (59:2).

The forts were real. They were among the strongest in the Hijaz. And the surah's point is the one the Qur'an keeps making: reliance placed in a structure is reliance placed in the wrong thing.

What these episodes were, and were not, should be stated plainly. The covenant had guaranteed freedom of religion and had not been withdrawn. What was acted on was a security clause, during wartime, in a city under threat, and in both cases the tribes left alive with their property.
