He was in the cave when the angel came, and the command was one word: **Read.**

*I cannot read*, he answered.

Jibril took hold of him and pressed him until he could bear no more, released him, and said: **Read.** The same answer. A second time, the same pressure, the same release, the same command. And a third time — and then the words:

> **Read in the name of your Lord who created — created man from a clinging clot. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, who taught by the pen; taught man what he did not know.** (96:1–5)

He came down the mountain shaking and went straight to Khadijah: *cover me, cover me.* She covered him with a cloak, and when the trembling eased he told her what had happened and said, *I feared for myself.*

Her answer was not reassurance. It was evidence: Allah will never disgrace you — you keep the ties of kinship, you carry the burden of the weak, you give to the poor, you honour the guest, you stand with people in hardship.

Then she took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, an old Christian who had read the earlier scriptures. Waraqah heard it out and said: this is the same Angel that came to Musa. Then — *if only I were young, if only I were alive when your people drive you out.*

*Will they drive me out?* he asked, astonished. He had been *al-Amin* in that city for forty years.

Yes, Waraqah said. No man has ever brought what you bring without being treated as an enemy.

Note where the Book begins. Not with a law, not with a threat, not even with the declaration of God's oneness — with **read**, and with *He taught by the pen; taught man what he did not know.* The first thing revealed about the human being is that he is ignorant and can be taught.
