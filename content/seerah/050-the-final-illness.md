He returned from Makkah and, some weeks later, the headaches began.

When it worsened he asked his wives for permission to be nursed in one house, and they gave it, and he was moved to **A'ishah's** room. He spent his last week there.

He could no longer lead the prayer. He instructed that **Abu Bakr** lead the people, and when A'ishah, knowing what the community would read into it, suggested twice that her father was too soft-hearted to hold the position, he cut it off: *you are like the women around Yusuf. Tell Abu Bakr to lead the prayer.*

He came out once more. On the Monday morning he lifted the curtain and looked at the rows praying behind Abu Bakr, and those who saw his face said it was like a page of the Qur'an, and they nearly broke the prayer out of joy at the sight of him. He gestured to them to continue, and let the curtain fall.

He was in severe pain. A'ishah said she never saw anyone suffer more in illness. He kept a vessel of water beside him and wiped his face, saying there are agonies to death.

Even in those last hours the instructions kept coming and they kept being about other people: the prayer, the prayer, and what your right hands possess. Do not take graves as places of worship. He gave away the last few dinars in the house, distressed that he should meet Allah with them still there.

He died on Monday, 12 Rabi' al-Awwal, 11 AH, in A'ishah's room, and was buried there.

Umar stood in the mosque and refused to accept it, saying that he had only gone as Musa went and would return. **Abu Bakr** came in, uncovered his face, kissed his forehead, and then went out and said the words that steadied the whole community:

*Whoever used to worship Muhammad, Muhammad has died. And whoever used to worship Allah, Allah is Living and does not die.*

Then he recited: *Muhammad is but a messenger; messengers have passed away before him. If he dies or is killed, will you turn back on your heels?* (3:144).

Umar said his legs would not carry him, and he fell to the ground, because it was as though he had never heard the verse until that moment.

He was sixty-three. He left behind no gold and no silver, no house and no land, a few personal effects, and a religion practised that morning by most of Arabia.
