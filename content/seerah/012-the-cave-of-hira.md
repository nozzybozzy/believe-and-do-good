By his late thirties something in him had turned against the life of the city. The powerful pressed the weak and called it custom. The gap between those who had and those who had not was open and unembarrassed. Labourers were treated as less than human. Immorality was ordinary. And over all of it, the idols — stones his people had shaped themselves and then asked for help.

He began to withdraw. He would take provisions and climb to the cave of Hira on the mountain outside Makkah, and stay, and think — about creation, about what had gone wrong with his people, about the Power he was certain stood behind all of it.

This is the detail that deserves attention: he did not know how to worship Him. There was no rite to perform, no form of prayer, no revealed word. He had only the conviction and the reaching.

A'ishah said the beginning of it was true dreams — he would see something in sleep and it would come like the breaking of dawn. After that, solitude became beloved to him, and he would stay at Hira for long stretches, returning to Khadijah only to resupply and go back up.

Years of this preceded the first verse. The Qur'an did not descend on an untrained man. It descended on someone who had already spent a long time refusing what everyone around him accepted and searching for what none of them could name.
