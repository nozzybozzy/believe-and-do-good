Six months after the boycott collapsed, **Abu Talib** died.

He had stood between his nephew and the whole of Quraysh for a decade and had never once wavered on that. At the end, the Prophet ﷺ asked him for one word he could argue with Allah on his behalf, and Abu Talib gave him the reason he could not say it: he feared Quraysh would say he had taken it out of fear of death. Concerning him came *You do not guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills* (28:56).

A few months later **Khadijah** died. She was sixty-five. He was fifty. Twenty-five years of marriage, and she had been the first to believe him, the one who covered him when he came down from Hira, the one who spent her wealth on the message until there was nothing left, the one he came home to after every rejection. He never stopped mentioning her. Years later, in Madinah, he would still send meat to her old friends, and A'ishah admitted she was jealous of no woman as she was of a woman she had never met.

Losing them in the same year was not two griefs but two different kinds of loss. Abu Talib was the shield. With him gone, the Prophet ﷺ had no legal protection in Makkah at all, and Quraysh knew it immediately and escalated. Khadijah was the shelter. With her gone, there was nowhere in Makkah to put the weight down.

The Arabs named the year for it: **Aam al-Huzn**, the Year of Grief.

Everything that follows in the seerah, Ta'if, the Isra, the Ansar, the Hijrah, follows from this year. The mission did not advance in spite of the worst period of his life. It advanced through it.
