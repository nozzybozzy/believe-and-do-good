Madinah was not a Muslim city. It held the Aws and the Khazraj, the newly arrived Muhajirun, and three Jewish tribes, **Banu Qaynuqa**, **Banu an-Nadir** and **Banu Qurayzah**, plus a body of people whose Islam was a public posture and nothing more.

So he wrote a document, and it is the first thing of its kind in the region.

Its terms. The parties form a single body politic for the purposes of defence. Each community keeps its own religion and its own law in its own affairs, and bears its own expenses. None may make a separate peace with an aggressor. Aggression against the city is to be met jointly. The wronged are to be supported. Any dispute is referred to Muhammad ﷺ as arbitrator.

All three Jewish tribes were parties to it.

What makes it remarkable is not tolerance in the abstract but the specific mechanism. It does not ask anyone to pretend agreement. It defines exactly who owes what to whom, and makes the obligations mutual and enforceable. Religion is left alone. Security is shared.

The Aws and the Khazraj, who had been killing each other within living memory at Bu'ath, were now inside the same covenant as each other and as the men they had once fought beside.

What happens later with the Jewish tribes is a story about this document, not about their religion, which the covenant explicitly protected, but about clauses of mutual defence that were broken one at a time, in the middle of sieges, while the city was under attack.
