Makkah filled with tribes every pilgrimage season, and every season he went among them, tribe by tribe, camp by camp, with Abu Lahab walking behind him telling them not to listen. Years of this, with nothing to show for it.

In the eleventh year of prophethood, six men of the **Khazraj** from Yathrib listened.

They had an advantage nobody in Makkah had. They lived beside Jewish tribes who had told them for years that a final prophet was coming, and who used to threaten them that when he came they would follow him and destroy the Arabs of the city. The six recognised the description in front of them, and they did not intend to be second. They accepted, and undertook to carry it home.

Yathrib was also a city exhausted by itself, the Aws and the Khazraj had bled each other for generations, most recently at Bu'ath, and there was no arbitrator either side would accept.

The following year, the twelfth of prophethood, twelve men came from Yathrib and met him at a hillock outside Makkah called **al-Aqabah**. They pledged: to associate nothing with Allah, not to steal, not to commit fornication, not to kill their children, not to slander, and not to disobey him in what is right.

That is the First Pledge of Aqabah, and there is nothing military in it. It is a pledge about conduct. Whoever keeps it, he told them, has his reward with Allah. Whoever falls short and is punished in this world, that is his expiation. Whoever falls short and is concealed by Allah, his affair is with Allah.

He sent **Mus'ab ibn Umayr** back with them, the first teacher sent out in Islam. Mus'ab had been the best-dressed young man in Makkah before he became Muslim and lost everything for it. Within a year, there was hardly a household in Yathrib where Islam had not been mentioned.
