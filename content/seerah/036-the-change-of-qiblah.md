For about sixteen or seventeen months after the Hijrah the Muslims prayed towards **Bayt al-Maqdis**. The Prophet ﷺ had wanted the Ka'bah, and the Qur'an says so plainly: *We have seen the turning of your face towards the heaven* (2:144). He did not ask. He waited.

The command came during a prayer. The congregation was midway through, facing north; the Prophet ﷺ turned, and the rows turned behind him — men moving to where the women had been and women to where the men had been, without breaking the prayer. The mosque where it happened has been called **Masjid al-Qiblatayn**, the mosque of the two qiblahs, ever since.

The reaction was immediate and hostile. *What has turned them away from the qiblah they used to face?* (2:142). If the direction was right before, it is wrong now; if it is right now, they were wrong before.

The Qur'an's answer refuses the premise entirely. **To Allah belong the east and the west** (2:142). Direction has no sanctity of its own. And then the reason, stated without decoration: *We did not appoint the qiblah which you used to face except to distinguish who follows the Messenger from who turns back on his heels* (2:143).

It was a test, and it was designed as one. There was no argument available for why Jerusalem was better, or why Makkah was better. There was only: this is the command now, and you either turn or you do not.

The prayers already prayed towards Jerusalem were not lost — *Allah would never let your faith go to waste* (2:143).
