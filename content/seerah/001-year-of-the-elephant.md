Abrahah governed Yemen for the ruler of Abyssinia, and he had a plan: a cathedral splendid enough to pull Arabia's pilgrims away from the Ka'bah. The Arabs were offended, and one of them defiled the new building. Abrahah answered with an army, and at its head the largest elephant anyone in the Hijaz had seen.

The Makkans could not fight it. They withdrew to the hills above the city and left the Sacred House to the One whose House it was. Abdul Muttalib's reply to Abrahah, when his camels were seized, has been remembered ever since: the camels have an owner who will speak for them, and the House has an Owner who will protect it.

What the Makkans watched from the heights they never forgot. Birds came over the army in flocks, carrying small stones, and dropped them. The army that had come to level the Ka'bah was scattered along the road back, and Abrahah died of his wounds.

The Arabs dated by it afterwards: *the Year of the Elephant*. And the Qur'an returns to it in Surah Al-Fil, not as a war story but as an argument. The scale is deliberately absurd. Elephants against birds, siege weight against pebbles. Every measure the world trusts is picked up here and reversed, and the reversal is the point: protection does not come from the size of what is defending you.

It was in that year, in that city, that the final Prophet ﷺ was born.
