He began with sheep. Every prophet, he said later, had herded them — and there is a reason the training is mentioned. A flock cannot be reasoned with, cannot be hurried, and scatters the moment attention lapses. It teaches patience with creatures who do not understand what you are doing for them.

Then trade. The Syrian journeys with experienced merchants had taught him the routes and the markets, and he did well — not by sharpness but by the opposite. He did not misrepresent goods. He did not exploit a buyer's ignorance. He returned what he was holding.

Makkah noticed, and Makkah named him for it: **al-Amin**, the trustworthy, and **as-Sadiq**, the truthful. These were not honorifics from his followers; he had no followers. They were the working verdict of a commercial town on a man it did business with.

Hold onto that, because of what happens later. When he stood on Safa and asked whether they would believe him if he warned them of cavalry behind the mountain, they answered without hesitation: *we have never received anything but truth from you.* The men who said that went on to reject the message anyway — and some of them went on calling him trustworthy while they did it. On the night of the Hijrah he left Ali behind specifically to return the valuables that Makkans — including men then trying to kill him — had deposited with him for safekeeping.

The Qur'an makes the point directly: *I have lived among you a lifetime before it* (10:16). Forty years of evidence were on the table before the first verse was revealed.
