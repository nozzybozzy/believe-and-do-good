Makkah came back for Badr with **three thousand** men under Abu Sufyan.

He consulted. His own view, and Abdullah ibn Ubayy's, was to hold the city and fight from inside it. But the younger men, and those who had missed Badr, pressed hard to go out and meet them. He accepted the majority view — and then, having put on his armour, refused to reverse it when they came back offering to defer to him. A decision taken by consultation is not unmade because the consulted lose their nerve.

He marched out with a thousand. **Ibn Ubayy turned back with three hundred** before the fighting, claiming he had been overruled.

Seven hundred faced three thousand. He positioned the army with Uhud at their backs and placed **fifty archers** on the pass under **Abdullah ibn Jubayr**, with an instruction of total clarity: do not leave this position — not if you see us winning, not if you see birds carrying off our corpses.

The Muslims broke the Quraysh lines and Quraysh ran. And the archers, watching the spoils being gathered below, concluded the battle was over and came down. Their commander reminded them of the order. Most left anyway.

**Khalid ibn al-Walid**, then still fighting for Quraysh, saw the empty pass, took his cavalry round it, and hit the Muslims from behind. The fleeing Makkans turned. The Muslim army was caught between two forces and disintegrated.

The Prophet ﷺ was struck in the face, his tooth broken, rings of his helmet driven into his cheek. A rumour ran through the field that he had been killed, and men stopped fighting. **Anas ibn an-Nadr** walked past a group sitting down in despair, asked what they were waiting for, was told the Messenger was dead, and said: *then what will you live for after him?* He died fighting, with over eighty wounds; only his sister could identify him, by his fingers.

**Ka'b ibn Malik** recognised him alive and shouted it, and the army re-formed around him. Of a group of nine who stood over him, eight were killed. **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas** and **Talhah** held the last ground.

**Hamzah** was killed by Wahshi, and his body was mutilated by Hind. Seventy Muslims were martyred, including **Mus'ab ibn Umayr**, the first teacher of Madinah.

The Qur'an's verdict is unsparing and entirely internal: *When a disaster struck you, though you had struck with one twice as great, you said: from where is this? Say: it is from yourselves* (3:165). And: *Allah fulfilled His promise to you when you were killing them by His permission, until you lost courage and disputed about the order and disobeyed, after He had shown you what you love* (3:152).

Then, immediately after the worst day the Muslims had had — *It is by mercy from Allah that you were gentle with them. Had you been harsh and hard-hearted they would have scattered from around you. So pardon them, ask forgiveness for them, and consult them in the matter* (3:159).

They had lost the battle by ignoring his instruction, and he was commanded to keep consulting them.
