When he was six, Aminah took him north to Yathrib. She had family there, and Abdullah's grave was there. They stayed a month. Umm Ayman, a maidservant of the household, travelled with them.

On the return journey Aminah fell ill, and at a place called Abwa she died. The child was with her.

Umm Ayman brought him the rest of the way to Makkah. She would stay near him for the rest of her life, and he would call her what remained of his family.

He was now twice bereaved, and he was six years old.

There is no attempt in the sources to soften this, and none in the Qur'an either. The Book that would come through him does not present his early life as charmed. It presents it as a sequence of losses, and then, in Ad-Duha, uses that sequence as the proof of something: *He found you an orphan and sheltered you; He found you lost and guided you; He found you in need and enriched you.* The consolation is built out of the hardship, not offered instead of it.

A man who would spend his mission telling people to guard the orphan had been one himself before he was nine.
