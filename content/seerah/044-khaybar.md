Khaybar, a hundred miles north of Madinah, was a complex of fortresses and date plantations, and it had become the organising centre of the coalition that had brought ten thousand men to the trench. Banu an-Nadir had settled there after their exile, and with Ghatafan they were preparing again.

He marched with the **fourteen hundred** who had been at Hudaybiyah, and only them — the reward, as Surah Al-Fath had promised, was for those who had pledged under the tree.

The place was divided into two sections of fortresses, and they fell one at a time over about twenty days.

On one of those evenings he said that he would give the banner in the morning to a man who loves Allah and His Messenger and whom Allah and His Messenger love, and at whose hands Allah will grant the victory. Umar said afterwards that it was the only time in his life he wanted command.

In the morning he asked for **Ali**, and was told Ali's eyes were badly inflamed and he could not see. He sent for him anyway, put his saliva on his eyes, and they were healed on the spot. He gave him the banner.

Ali asked whether he should fight them until they became like us. The instruction he was given is worth keeping: go carefully until you reach their open ground, then call them to Islam and tell them what is obliged of them — for by Allah, that Allah guides one man through you is better for you than red camels.

Ninety-three of the defenders were killed and fifteen Muslims martyred. The people of Khaybar asked to stay on their land rather than be expelled, offering half their produce. He agreed, and they remained under that arrangement.
