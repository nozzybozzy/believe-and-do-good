News came that Roman forces were massing on the Syrian frontier to end the Muslim power before it grew further.

Everything about the timing was wrong. It was the peak of summer heat, the harvest was ready, the distance was enormous and the enemy was the greatest military power of the age. Contrary to his usual practice of concealing a destination, he announced this one — the journey was too hard to be entered blind.

**Thirty thousand** were mobilised. It was called the Army of Hardship, and the name was literal: men shared a single camel in rotation, and shared dates one at a time.

He called for contributions. **Uthman** gave 10,000 dinars and 300 camels with their equipment. **Umar** brought half of everything he owned, and thought he had finally outdone Abu Bakr. **Abu Bakr** brought everything he had. Asked what he had left for his family, he said: *Allah and His Messenger.*

At the other end, this is the expedition where the hypocrites' excuses came in one after another, and the Qur'an recorded the pattern in Surah At-Tawbah: *those who were left behind rejoiced in remaining behind the Messenger of Allah... they said, do not go forth in the heat. Say: the Fire of Hell is more intense in heat* (9:81).

And between the two, the three who simply had no excuse. **Ka'b ibn Malik** kept meaning to prepare and did not, and when the army returned he refused to lie about it, though the hypocrites lied and were accepted at their word. He and two others were boycotted for fifty nights — no one in Madinah spoke to them, including their own families — until *the earth, vast as it is, closed in upon them, and their own souls closed in upon them, and they were certain that there is no refuge from Allah except in Him* (9:118). Then the forgiveness came down by name.

The Romans withdrew and there was no battle. He stayed about twenty days, made treaties with the frontier tribes, and returned.

It was his last expedition.
