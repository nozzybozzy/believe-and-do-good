His father Abdullah died before he was born, a trading journey, an illness, a grave in Yathrib. So the child arrived already an orphan, into a society that measured a man by the father standing behind him.

Aminah sent word to Abdul Muttalib, and the old man carried his grandson into the Ka'bah and thanked Allah there. Then he named him **Muhammad**, *the much-praised*, a name almost unused among the Arabs at the time.

The first to nurse him after his mother was Thuwaybah, a freed slave of Abu Lahab, the same uncle who would one day stand on a hillside and curse him in public.

Look at what the Qur'an later does with this beginning. When Allah consoles His Messenger ﷺ in the hardest stretch of his mission, He does not open with his prophethood or his miracles. He opens with his orphanhood: *Did He not find you an orphan and shelter you?* The argument runs from the smallest evidence of care to the largest. You were held when you had no one. You are not abandoned now.

Every protection the Prophet ﷺ received in his life arrived through this same pattern, rarely through the thing Arabia counted on, always through the One behind it.
