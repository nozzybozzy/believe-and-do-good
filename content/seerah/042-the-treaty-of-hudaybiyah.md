He saw in a dream that he entered the Sacred Mosque and performed Umrah. He told the companions, and they set out — about fourteen hundred men, no weapons but the sheathed sword of a traveller, driving sacrificial animals. Every element of the party announced that this was pilgrimage, not war.

Quraysh blocked the road. He turned aside and camped at **Hudaybiyah**, on the edge of the sanctuary.

Envoys went back and forth. He sent **Uthman** into Makkah, chosen because his clan was strong enough that nobody would touch him. Uthman delivered the message; the Makkans offered him a private Umrah; he refused — how could he circle the House while the Messenger of Allah was kept from it?

Then Uthman was delayed, and the rumour reached the camp that he had been killed. Fourteen hundred unarmed men, at the gates of a hostile city, gave the Prophet ﷺ their hands one by one under a tree, pledging to fight to the death. This is the **Bay'at ar-Ridwan**, the Pledge of Good Pleasure. *Allah was well pleased with the believers when they pledged to you under the tree* (48:18).

Uthman returned alive, and Quraysh, having seen what was in that camp, sent **Suhayl ibn Amr** to make terms.

The terms were these: return this year without Umrah, and come back next year for three days only. A ten-year truce. Tribes free to ally with either side. And the clause that broke the camp's heart — anyone coming from Makkah to Madinah without their guardian's permission must be returned; anyone leaving Madinah for Makkah need not be.

It got worse in the drafting. Suhayl objected to *Bismillah ar-Rahman ar-Rahim* and demanded the old formula. Granted. He objected to *Muhammad the Messenger of Allah* — if we accepted that we would not be fighting you. Granted; the Prophet ﷺ had it erased with his own instruction. And while the ink was drying, Suhayl's own son **Abu Jandal** came stumbling into the camp in chains, having escaped from Makkah as a Muslim — and had to be handed back under a clause signed minutes earlier.

**Umar** could not bear it. He went to Abu Bakr, and then to the Prophet ﷺ: are we not upon the truth and they upon falsehood? *Yes.* Then why do we give in over our religion? The answer: *I am the Messenger of Allah, I do not disobey Him, and He will help me.*

Then he ordered the camp to shave and slaughter, and nobody moved. He went to his tent and **Umm Salamah** gave him the advice that broke the deadlock: say nothing to anyone — go out, slaughter your animal, and call your barber. He did. They followed, almost pushing each other in their hurry.

On the road home came **Surah Al-Fath**: *Indeed, We have granted you a manifest victory* (48:1). Umar asked: is it a victory? He was told yes.

Within two years more people entered Islam than in the whole nineteen years before it. The truce opened Arabia. Khalid ibn al-Walid and Amr ibn al-As came over during it. And the clause that had looked most humiliating — returning Muslims to Makkah — collapsed on its own when the returned Muslims formed a band on the coast road that Quraysh themselves begged to have taken into Madinah.
