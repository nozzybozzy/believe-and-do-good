It came after Ta'if, after the Year of Grief — which is the first thing to notice about it. The honour was not given in a season of success.

There were two journeys. The **Isra** was the night journey from the Sacred Mosque in Makkah to **Bayt al-Maqdis** in Jerusalem. The **Mi'raj** was the ascent from there through the heavens.

At Jerusalem the prophets were assembled and he led them in prayer — the whole prior line of revelation standing behind one man in a single row.

Then the ascent, heaven by heaven, meeting prophets at each: Adam, Isa and Yahya, Yusuf, Idris, Harun, Musa, Ibrahim at the Frequented House. He was shown Paradise and the Fire, and specific sights he later described so that his community could take the lesson from them.

At the furthest point, Jibril — who had accompanied him the whole way — stopped, and said that if he went a finger's breadth further he would be consumed.

What he brought back was the **five daily prayers**. Fifty at first; then Musa, who knew what a community can carry, sent him back again and again to ask for less, until they were five in practice and fifty in reward. Everything else in Islam was legislated on the earth. The prayer was given above the heavens, in the one year he most needed something to hold.

In the morning he told Quraysh, and they laughed. *Look what he has come out with now.*

They went to Abu Bakr, certain this would finally break him. He asked only whether the Prophet ﷺ had really said it. Told yes, he answered: then it is true — I believe him about things further than this, about news from the heavens morning and evening. He was **as-Siddiq** from that day.

They tried to catch him out. They demanded a description of Masjid al-Aqsa — how many doors, what it looked like — and he described it. He had seen it once, at night. He said Allah raised it before his eyes as they questioned him. Then they asked about their own caravans on the Syria road, and he told them where each was and when it would arrive. The caravans arrived as he said.
