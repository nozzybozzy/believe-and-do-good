Violence had failed, bribery had failed, and Islam kept spreading. So Quraysh reached for the instrument that is always available to a majority: total social and economic exclusion.

The clans pledged that they would not trade with **Banu Hashim** and **Banu al-Muttalib**, would not marry into them, would not sit with them, would not speak to them. They wrote the pact down and hung it inside the Ka'bah to give it the weight of a sacred thing.

It applied to the whole clan — Muslim and idolater alike — because the clan as a whole was protecting him. Only Abu Lahab was spared, having publicly disowned his own family and kept his friends among the chiefs.

They were confined for three years to a narrow ravine afterwards known as **Shi'b Abi Talib**, in the seventh year of prophethood. Supplies came only when someone risked the penalty to smuggle them in. They ate leaves. People outside the valley could hear the children crying.

Nothing dramatic ended it. It ended the way such things usually end — slowly, through the consciences of individual Makkans who could not live with it any longer, and who worked quietly until the pact was broken up.

This is the stretch of the seerah with no miracle in it, and it is three years long. The Qur'an does not pretend otherwise: *Do you suppose you will enter the Garden without there coming to you the like of what came to those who passed away before you?* (2:214) — hardship and shaking, until even the Messenger and those with him said, *when is Allah's help?*

The verse answers itself in four words: *Truly, Allah's help is near.* It does not say soon.
