Abu Sufyan was bringing a large caravan down from Syria. The Prophet ﷺ moved to intercept it — Makkan wealth, much of it built on property confiscated from the Muslims who had fled. Participation was voluntary; nobody was obliged to come.

Abu Sufyan got word, changed route, and sent a rider to Makkah for help. A thousand men marched out under **Abu Jahl**. Then news came that the caravan was safe. Abu Jahl refused to turn back. He wanted the confrontation.

The Muslims were **313**, with two horses and seventy camels between them, and had come out expecting a caravan, not an army.

He consulted. The Muhajirun spoke. But he kept asking — because the Ansar's pledge at Aqabah had been to defend him *in Madinah*, and this was outside it. **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh** understood the question and answered it: we believed you and gave you our word; go where you wish, and if you crossed this sea we would cross it with you.

He spent the night before in prayer, and what he said is preserved: *O Allah, if this band perishes, You will not be worshipped on earth.* Abu Bakr eventually took hold of him and told him it was enough, that Allah would fulfil His promise.

The battle opened as Arab battles did, with single combat — Hamzah, Ali and Ubaydah against three of Quraysh's best — and then became general.

The Qur'an describes reinforcement by **angels** (8:9), and it corrects the Muslims' account of their own victory immediately afterwards: *You did not kill them, but Allah killed them; and you did not throw when you threw, but Allah threw* (8:17).

Seventy Makkans were killed, including **Abu Jahl**, Umayyah ibn Khalaf and Shaybah — nearly the entire leadership that had run the persecution. Seventy were captured. Fourteen Muslims were martyred.

The prisoners were treated well, to the point that Makkan captives later remembered that their captors gave them the bread and ate dates themselves. Those who could pay were ransomed. Those who could not, but who could read and write, bought their freedom by teaching ten children of Madinah to read.
