**Hamzah ibn Abdil Muttalib**, the Prophet's uncle, was a hunter and a fighter, and not a Muslim. He came back into Makkah one day and was told that Abu Jahl had abused his nephew in public. He went straight to the Ka'bah where Abu Jahl was sitting with the chiefs, struck him with his bow, and declared himself a Muslim on the spot — then dared anyone present to do something about it. Anyone who insulted the Prophet ﷺ again would answer to him.

Days later, **Umar ibn al-Khattab** stood up in a gathering of Makkah's leaders and undertook to end the matter by killing Muhammad ﷺ himself. He set off armed for the house of al-Arqam.

On the way a man asked him where he was going, heard the answer, and told him to put his own house in order first — his sister Fatimah and her husband had accepted Islam.

He turned and went to her house. There was recitation inside. He burst in and demanded to know what they were reading, and when they would not give it up he struck his sister hard enough to draw blood. She did not yield: *do what you like, Umar — there is no way we will leave the truth now that we have found it.*

Something in him gave way. He asked to see the pages. She made him wash first — these words are pure, and you will not handle them otherwise. He washed, and she gave him the passage.

It was **Surah Ta-Ha**. He was an Arab with an ear for language, and he read down the verses knowing, line by line, that no man had written this. By the fourteenth verse — *Indeed, I am Allah. There is no deity except Me, so worship Me and establish prayer for My remembrance* — he was finished.

He went on to the house of al-Arqam after all. They asked why he had come. He said: I set out to take his head, and I have come to give him my heart.

Makkah now had to contend with Hamzah in front of the Prophet ﷺ and Umar beside him. Persecution stopped being cost-free.
