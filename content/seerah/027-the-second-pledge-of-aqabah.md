Mus'ab returned to Makkah the following pilgrimage season with seventy-three men and two women from Yathrib.

They arranged to meet at the same hillock, at night, in secret, slipping away from their own sleeping caravan in ones and twos.

This pledge was different from the first, and everyone in the dark knew it. The first had been about how they would live. This one was about what they would risk. They pledged to protect him exactly as they would protect their own wives and children.

One of them, **al-Abbas ibn Ubadah**, stopped the proceedings to make sure they understood the contract they were signing: you are pledging war against all people, red and black. If you think you will hand him over when your wealth is destroyed and your nobles are killed, say so now and leave it, that is less shameful before Allah.

They answered that they took him on those terms. And then they asked what they would get for it, if they kept it.

**Paradise**, he said.

They stretched out their hands.

Seventy-three people from a city torn apart by its own feuds had just guaranteed the safety of a man their own allies in Makkah were planning to kill. Quraysh got word of the meeting and went out looking for them, but the caravan had already gone.

The Hijrah followed directly from this. They were now the **Ansar**, the Helpers, and they had a place to bring him.
