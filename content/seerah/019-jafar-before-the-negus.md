When the brutality did not let up, he permitted a second migration: eighty-three men and eighteen women.

Quraysh could not let it stand. They sent a delegation with gifts, asking the Negus to hand back the fugitives.

The king summoned the Muslims, and **Ja'far ibn Abi Talib** — the Prophet's cousin — spoke.

He did not begin with theology. He began with a description of what they had been: a people of ignorance, worshipping idols, eating carrion, committing outrages, cutting off relatives, the strong devouring the weak. Then: Allah sent us a Messenger from among us, whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness and integrity we already knew. He called us to worship Allah alone, to speak truthfully, to keep trusts, to honour kinship, to be good to neighbours, to leave what is forbidden and bloodshed. And for that our people turned on us.

The Negus asked him to recite something of what had been revealed.

Ja'far recited from **Surah Maryam** — the passage on Maryam and the birth of Isa. The court wept. The Negus wept into his beard.

Then he said it: truly this and what Jesus brought come from the same lamp.

He refused to surrender them. In time he and many of his people accepted Islam. When he died, the Prophet ﷺ prayed the funeral prayer for him in Madinah, for a king he had never met.

Ja'far had been asked to defend a religion, and he did it by describing conduct, and then by letting the Qur'an speak about Jesus to Christians. He argued from the common ground, and he did not shave a word off what they believed to get there.
