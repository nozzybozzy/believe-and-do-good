The community now had two halves. The **Muhajirun** had left their homes, businesses and property in Makkah and arrived with what they could carry. The **Ansar** were at home, with houses and date-palms and work.

He gathered them and paired them, one to one: each Ansari took a Muhajir as a brother, with real obligations attached — housing, maintenance, and for a period even inheritance.

The Ansar did not merely comply. They competed. Men offered to divide their property down the middle. Some offered to divorce one of two wives so that their brother could marry her after the waiting period.

The most famous exchange is the one that refused the offer. **Sa'd ibn ar-Rabi'** made exactly that proposal to **Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf** — take half my wealth, and I will divorce one of my wives for you.

Abdur-Rahman's answer: may Allah bless you in your family and your wealth — **just show me the market.**

He went to the market, traded, and within a short time was supporting himself; he later became one of the wealthiest men among the Muslims.

Both halves of that exchange are the lesson. The Ansari's offer was real and the Muhajir's refusal was not ingratitude. Generosity that great was met by a man determined not to live on it one day longer than necessary.

Of the Ansar, Allah said: *they love those who emigrated to them and find no want in their breasts for what they were given, and give preference over themselves even though poverty besets them* (59:9).
