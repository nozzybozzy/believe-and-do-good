He went to Abu Bakr, who had two camels ready and had been waiting a long time for permission to make this journey.

They went south, away from Yathrib, which was north, and hid in the cave of **Thawr** for three nights while Makkah swept the roads. **Asma**, Abu Bakr's daughter, carried food up to them, tearing her waistband in two to tie the provisions, which is why she is called *Dhat an-Nitaqayn*, she of the two belts. Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr brought them the city's news each night, and Amir ibn Fuhayrah brought sheep over their tracks to erase them.

Searchers reached the mouth of the cave. Abu Bakr, looking at their feet, whispered that if one of them looked down, he would see them.

The answer was a question: what do you think of two, whose third is Allah?

*Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us* (9:40), the only verse in the Qur'an that quotes what was said in that cave, and it is addressed to Abu Bakr.

Then the road, with a hundred camels on each of their heads. **Suraqah ibn Malik** tracked them and got close, and his horse's legs sank into the ground. He freed himself, chased again, sank again. Then he asked for safety, and was given it, and offered them provisions, which were refused. He was told only to keep their route to himself, and the Prophet ﷺ told him he would one day wear the bracelets of Khusraw. Years later, in Umar's caliphate, when the treasury of Persia was carried into Madinah, Umar put those bracelets on Suraqah's arms.

Suraqah turned back and spent the rest of that day sending every search party he met in the wrong direction.
