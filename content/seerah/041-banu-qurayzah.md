Banu Qurayzah had joined the Confederates during the siege, in direct breach of the covenant, at the moment the city was least able to absorb it. With ten thousand men outside and the tribe's quarter inside the defensive perimeter, the danger was not theoretical; the Prophet ﷺ had to detach men to guard the districts where the women and children were.

When the siege lifted, the matter could not be left.

They were besieged for about twenty-five days, and then offered to surrender — but asked that judgement be passed by **Sa'd ibn Mu'adh**, chief of the Aws, who had been their ally before Islam and whom they expected to be lenient, as Ibn Ubayy had been for Qaynuqa.

The Prophet ﷺ agreed, and both sides bound themselves to the verdict before it was given.

Sa'd, carried in badly wounded from the trench, first took confirmation from everyone present that they would accept his ruling. Then he ruled: the fighting men to be killed, the women and children taken as captives, the property divided.

That is the law of war in the Torah for a city that has broken faith and fought (Deuteronomy 20:10–14), and it is in fact the milder of the provisions there — the harsher one, for a city inside the land, leaves no one alive at all (20:16).

They were judged by an arbitrator they chose, under the law of the scripture they held.

This is a hard passage, and pretending otherwise helps nobody. It is worth being exact about what it was: a judicial sentence, passed by a named arbitrator at the request of the condemned party, on a specific tribe, for armed treachery during a siege — and not a policy toward Jews, whose covenant protections in Madinah were never revoked as a matter of religion.
