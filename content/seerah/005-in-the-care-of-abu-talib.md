Abdul Muttalib was still grieving Abdullah, and he poured that grief into the boy. A rug was spread for the chief of Quraysh in the shade of the Ka'bah, and his own sons did not sit on it; his grandson did, and the old man would not let anyone move him.

Two years later Abdul Muttalib died too. He was eight.

Guardianship passed to Abu Talib, a full brother of Abdullah, and not a rich man. He raised his nephew among his own children and, more than once, ahead of them. The bond held for the rest of Abu Talib's life, through everything Makkah would later throw at it.

Two things sit side by side here, and the seerah never resolves them, because they were never resolved.

The first is that Abu Talib's protection was decisive. While he lived, Quraysh could insult his nephew but could not kill him. Tribal obligation was the only law Makkah had, and Abu Talib used all of it.

The second is that he died outside of Islam. He told the Prophet ﷺ plainly what stopped him: not doubt, but the taunt — that Quraysh would say he had abandoned the religion of his fathers out of fear at the end of his life.

*You do not guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills* (28:56) was revealed concerning him. Loving someone, protecting them, being owed everything by them — none of it transfers guidance. That belongs to Allah alone.
