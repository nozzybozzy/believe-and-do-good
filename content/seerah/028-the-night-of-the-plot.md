The Muslims of Makkah began leaving for Yathrib, singly and in small groups, abandoning houses and property.

Quraysh understood what emigration meant: not escape only, but a base. They met in their assembly hall to settle it.

The plot they arrived at was genuinely clever. Assassination had always been impossible because a killing meant the killer's whole tribe faced Banu Hashim's vengeance. So: **one young man from every clan**, striking together as one. The blood would be spread across all of Quraysh, and Banu Hashim could not make war on the entire city. They would have to accept blood money.

*And remember when those who disbelieved plotted against you, to imprison you or kill you or expel you — they plot, and Allah plans, and Allah is the best of planners* (8:30).

He was told to leave that night. The assassins surrounded the house and waited for dawn.

He gave **Ali** two instructions. Sleep in my bed. And stay behind to return the deposits.

That second instruction is one of the most extraordinary details in the seerah. The men outside the door with drawn swords had money and valuables stored with him for safekeeping, because in all of Makkah he was still the one they trusted with their property. He was leaving a city that wanted him dead, and he would not leave with a single item of theirs unreturned.

Then he walked out of the door, reciting, and passed through them, and they did not see him.

In the morning they went in and found Ali on the bed.
