Khadijah bint Khuwaylid ran her own trade and hired men to run it for her. She heard what Makkah said about the young Hashimite and offered him a share in an expedition to Syria. She sent her servant Maysarah with him.

The caravan came back with better returns than she had seen, and Maysarah came back with an account of the man himself — how he traded, how he spoke, what he would not do for a profit.

She proposed. The offer went through a friend, and he accepted, and his uncles settled the matter with her representative. The dowry was twenty camels.

Khadijah held a position in Makkah that meant she had refused proposals from men with far more to offer than an orphaned nephew of Abu Talib. She was forty; he was twenty-five.

For the next twenty-five years, in a society where a man of means took as many wives as he could keep, he took none. That fact is usually noted and passed over. It should not be. It is the clearest early evidence of how he understood loyalty.

And it is why the first revelation lands where it does. When he came down from Hira shaking and said *cover me*, it was Khadijah who covered him, and Khadijah who answered his fear with an argument from his own record: Allah will never disgrace you — you keep family ties, you carry the weak, you give to the poor, you honour the guest, you stand by people in hardship.

She was the first believer. She believed before there was anything to join.
