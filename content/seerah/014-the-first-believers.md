The revelations that followed told him to stand in the night and to begin calling, and to warn people about what their injustice was heading toward.

The first to answer was Khadijah, who had already answered before she was asked.

Then **Zayd ibn Harithah**, a man he had freed and who had chosen him over his own father and his own tribe when given the free choice to leave. Then **Ali ibn Abi Talib**, a child in his household, who asked for a night to think and came back in the morning having decided. Then **Abu Bakr**, his friend, who did not ask for a night.

That order is not incidental. A woman, a freed slave, a boy and a merchant, before a single chief of Quraysh. The message came in through the parts of Makkan society that Makkan society did not count.

Abu Bakr then went to work on the men he knew, and through him came **Uthman ibn Affan**, **az-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam**, **Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf**, **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas** and **Talhah ibn Ubaydillah**, names that would still be running the Muslim world forty years later.

Others came in early from the other end of the social scale. **Bilal ibn Rabah**, an Abyssinian slave. **Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah**. **Uthman ibn Maz'un**.

For now it was quiet work. He taught them privately, person by person, and later in the house of al-Arqam on Safa. There were no numbers to speak of and no protection at all, which is exactly why, when persecution came, it came down hardest on the ones with no clan to answer for them.
