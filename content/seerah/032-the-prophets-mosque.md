Before any administration, any treaty, any army, he built the mosque.

It was not only for prayer. The new community needed one place where worship, teaching, justice, welfare, assembly and the settling of disputes all happened — and for the next ten years that place was this building.

The plot he wanted belonged to two orphan boys, **Sahl and Suhayl**. When they heard he wanted it, they offered it as a gift.

He refused, and paid them for it.

Read that against the society he had come out of, where an orphan's property was the easiest thing in Arabia to take, and against the fact that he could have had the land from any clan in the city for the asking. The man who had been an orphan three times over would not accept a free plot from two orphans, even when they pressed it on him and even for a mosque.

He worked on the building himself, carrying stone and mud brick with everyone else. Walls of mud and stone, palm trunks for pillars, palm branches for a roof that did not keep the rain out. The floor was sand and gravel.

Along one side was a shaded platform, **as-Suffah**, where the Muslims with no family and no home in Madinah lived — the poorest of the Muhajirun, fed from whatever came in. Abu Hurayrah was one of them, and it is largely because he had nowhere else to be that he was always present, and heard and memorised more hadith than anyone.
