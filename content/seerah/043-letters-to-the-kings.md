Hudaybiyah had bought ten years of quiet on the southern front. He used it immediately, and not on Arabia.

He had a silver seal made — *Muhammad Rasul Allah*, in three lines — because letters to kings are not read unless they are sealed, and he sent envoys out in every direction.

**Abdullah ibn Hudhafah** went to **Khusraw Parwez** of Persia, who tore the letter up in front of him. When word reached Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ said: may Allah tear his kingdom. His own son killed him not long afterwards, and within a generation the empire was gone.

**Dihyah al-Kalbi** carried the letter towards **Heraclius**, the Roman emperor. Heraclius did not tear it. He sent for any Arabs in the city and found Abu Sufyan — then still an enemy — and interrogated him closely: his lineage, whether anyone had said this before him, whether he had ever lied, whether his followers were the strong or the weak, whether they increase or decrease, whether any leaves the religion out of dissatisfaction, whether he breaks his word. Abu Sufyan later admitted he would have lied if he had not been afraid of being caught out in front of his own companions.

At the end Heraclius said that if all this was true, the man would come to possess the ground beneath his feet. His commanders nearly rioted, and he pulled back — but he had, by his own analysis, reached the right conclusion.

**Hatib ibn Abi Balta'ah** went to **al-Muqawqis** in Egypt, who answered courteously with gifts. The **Negus** of Abyssinia accepted. Letters went also to Bahrain, Oman, Yamamah and Damascus.

Note the arithmetic. This came from a city of a few thousand, whose ruler had spent the previous year unable to enter his own birthplace for three days of pilgrimage, and who had been stoned out of Ta'if within living memory.
