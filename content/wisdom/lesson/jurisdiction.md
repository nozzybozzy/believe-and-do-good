The cleanest statement of it in the whole corpus is Abdul Muttalib standing in front of Abrahah's army: I am the lord of the camels; the House has a Lord who will defend it.

That is not flippancy about the Ka'bah. It is exactness about jurisdiction. His camels were his responsibility, so he asked for them back. The House was not, so he did not pretend it was.

Surah Al-Fil then demonstrates the division by showing every human defence failing first. Dhu Nafr fought and lost. Nufayl fought and was captured. Thaqif bought safety by helping the invader. The Quraysh withdrew to the hills. And then the defence arrived, not an army but birds, not swords but pebbles.

The everyday form is in Al-Mulk, and Ibn Kathir states the rule outright: striving by using the means does not negate tawakkul. The bird leaves with an empty stomach and returns full, but the bird searches, morning and evening, while depending on Allah. It is not fed in the nest.

Al-Kahf gives the sentence for the moment of success: *why, when you entered your garden, did you not say, what Allah has willed; there is no power except with Allah* (18:39).

And Al-Hijr gives the assurance for the moment of failure: the message has a Guardian who is not the man carrying it. *Proclaim it openly, and We will handle the mockers* (15:94–95).

Most anxiety is a jurisdiction error. Either a person is trying to do God's part, or refusing to do their own.
