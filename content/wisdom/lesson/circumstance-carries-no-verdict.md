The corpus's most frequently repeated correction, and Al-Fajr states the error in the man's own words:

> As for man, when his Lord tries him and honours him and gives him ease, he says: *my Lord has honoured me.* But when He tries him and restricts his provision, he says: *my Lord has humiliated me.* **No!** (89:15–17)

Both halves are wrong, and the Qur'an interrupts before the sentence is even finished. Notice that the verse calls *both* conditions a trial — the ease as much as the restriction.

Ibn Kathir's rule is unusually blunt: **Allah gives wealth to those He loves and to those He does not love. He withholds from those He loves and from those He does not love.** Al-Mu'minun supplies the mechanism — *do they think that what We extend to them of wealth and children is because We hasten for them good things? Rather, they do not perceive* (23:55–56).

And Ibn Mas'ud's hadith is the discriminator: Allah gives the things of this world to those whom He loves and those whom He does not; **He gives religious commitment only to those whom He loves.** Which tells you exactly which gift to read as a sign, and which to read as nothing at all.

Al-Kahf's rich man makes the error twice over, assuming his garden proved he was dear to Allah. Al-Qasas sets the two failure modes side by side — Fir'awn destroyed by power, Qarun by wealth — and notes that Qarun was not an outsider but Musa's own cousin, from the community of the believers.

The practical form is two words. **If wealthy, be grateful; if poor, be patient.** The variable is never the circumstance. It is the response.
