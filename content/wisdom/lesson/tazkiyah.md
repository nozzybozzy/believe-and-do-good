In the hadith qudsi:

> I am as My servant thinks of Me, and I am with him when he remembers Me. If he remembers Me to himself, I remember him to Myself; if he mentions Me in a gathering, I mention him in a better gathering. If he draws near to Me a hand's span, I draw near to him an arm's length; if he draws near an arm's length, I draw near a fathom; and if he comes to Me walking, I come to him running. (al-Bukhari and Muslim)

Read the arithmetic in it. Every unit of approach is answered by a larger one, and the last clause abandons proportion altogether.

Dhikr has an effect out of all proportion to the effort, *light on the tongue, heavy on the scales.* And the Qur'an states the mechanism plainly: *verily, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest* (13:28).

That is what *tazkiyah al-nafs* is for. Not self-improvement in the modern sense, but the cleaning of the one organ the Day of Judgement actually inspects:

> He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who corrupts it. (91:9–10)

> Except one who comes to Allah with a sound heart. (26:89)
