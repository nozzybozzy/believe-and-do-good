> Richness is not an abundance of goods. Richness is the richness of the soul. (al-Bukhari and Muslim)

*Ghina al-nafs*, richness of the self, is an inner state of contentment and freedom from greed: genuinely satisfied with what has been decreed for you, rather than occupied with accumulating. It has three marks.

- **Qana'ah**, real satisfaction with your provision, whether it is large or small.
- **Independence from greed**, free of the constant low-grade anxiety of wanting what someone else has.
- **The dunya in your hands, not in your heart.**

It is the best armour there is against hardship, for a simple structural reason: worldly loss cannot break a person it does not own, and worldly gain cannot corrupt one either.

Three things hold it up.

*Hardship is standard issue.* We will surely test you with fear, hunger, and loss of wealth, lives and fruits, *and give good tidings to the patient* (2:155). Expecting a life without hurdles is expecting an ocean without waves.

*Ease is not queued behind hardship. It is inside it.* With hardship comes ease, said twice in consecutive verses (94:5–6).

*The trial is sized to you.* Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity (2:286). It is cut to your hidden strength, not to your visible weakness.

And the direction to look is fixed by the Prophet ﷺ: *look at those beneath you and not at those above you, for that is fitter for not belittling Allah's favour upon you.*
