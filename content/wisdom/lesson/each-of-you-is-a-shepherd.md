> **Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock.** The imam is a shepherd; a man is the shepherd of his family; a woman is the shepherd of her husband's house; a servant is the shepherd of his master's wealth. (al-Bukhari and Muslim)

The command about parents is placed in the Qur'an directly after the command to worship Allah alone, in the same sentence:

> **Your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him, and to parents, good treatment.** If one or both of them reach old age with you, *do not say to them so much as "uff"*, nor repel them, and speak to them a noble word. (17:23)

Not "do not shout at them." Not "do not strike them." The line is drawn at a syllable of irritation.

And the duties run in both directions. A child has rights over the parent before the parent has expectations of the child: a good name, an upbringing and an education, training in manners and character, to be fed and clothed and spent on — and **to be treated equally with their siblings**.

The relationship the Qur'an describes is not authority downward and obedience upward. It is a flock, and a shepherd who will be asked.
