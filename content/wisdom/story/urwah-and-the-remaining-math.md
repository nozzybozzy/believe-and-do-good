Urwah ibn az-Zubayr, the scholar, developed gangrene in his leg. It was amputated, in that age, without anaesthetic.

On the same day, his son was kicked by a horse and killed.

When people came to console him on both at once, he raised his hands and said:

> O Allah, You gave me four limbs and took one, leaving me three. You gave me four sons and took one, leaving me three. If You have afflicted me, You have also blessed me.

He is not denying the loss. He counts it accurately, one limb, one son. He simply refuses to stop counting there.

*Kanud*, the Qur'an's word for the ungrateful man, means exactly the opposite habit: counting the calamities and forgetting the favours. Urwah did the arithmetic the other way round on the worst day of his life.
