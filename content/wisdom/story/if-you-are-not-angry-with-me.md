He had lost his uncle and his wife in the same year. Makkah had closed against him. So he walked sixty kilometres to Ta'if, and Ta'if set its youths on him and stoned him out of the town until his sandals filled with blood.

He stopped in a garden. And what he said there is the closest the seerah lets us come to hearing him alone.

He complains of his weakness, and of his lowness before people, and asks whose hands He is entrusting him to. And then the sentence the whole dua exists for:

> *If You are not angry with me, I do not care.*

Not *make it stop*. Not *punish them*. Then the angel of the mountains offered to crush the town between the two hills, and he refused — perhaps Allah will bring from their descendants those who worship Him alone.

He was right. The people who threw the stones did not accept Islam. Their grandchildren did.
