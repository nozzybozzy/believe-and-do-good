Delivered on Arafah in the tenth year, to something like a hundred and twenty-four thousand people. Most of it is not theology. It is rights.

He told them their blood and property were sacred to one another, as sacred as that day, that month, that city.

He abolished the blood feuds of the age of ignorance, and the first claim he cancelled belonged to his own family.

He abolished the usury of the age of ignorance, and the first debt he cancelled was owed to his own uncle, al-Abbas.

He told them to fear Allah regarding women, that they have rights over men as men have rights over them, and that they were taken as a trust from Allah.

He said an Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab, nor white over black, nor black over white, except by taqwa.

He told them he was leaving behind what they would not go astray after, if they held to it: the Book of Allah.

Then he asked whether he had delivered the message. The crowd answered that he had. He raised his finger to the sky and said:

> O Allah, bear witness.

Every reform in it cost his own family first. That is why it held.
