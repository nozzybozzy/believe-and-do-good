*Revealed in Makkah · 165 ayat · "The Cattle"*

---

## 0. How to Read This Surah

Al-An'am is the great Makkan argument. Where Al-Ma'idah legislates for an established community, Al-An'am **reasons with idolators** — patiently, relentlessly, from every angle: creation, provision, death, sleep, storms at sea, the stars, the seed splitting in the ground, the fruit ripening on the branch. Almost every passage ends by returning to one point: *there is no god but Allah, and He alone decides what is lawful.*

Note also its **shape**. It opens with the creation of the heavens and earth and closes with mankind placed on that earth as generations succeeding one another, being tested. Between the two is the whole case.

---

## 1. Its Revelation

Ibn Kathir opens not with virtues in the usual sense but with the **manner** of its descent — because that manner is itself the point.

- **Al-Awfi, Ikrimah and Ata from Ibn Abbas:** the surah was revealed in Makkah.
- **At-Tabarani from Ibn Abbas:** *"All of Surah Al-An'am was revealed in Makkah at night, accompanied by seventy thousand angels, raising their voices in glorification of Allah."*
- **As-Suddi from Murrah from Abdullah:** revealed in the company of seventy thousand angels.

Two things follow. First, it came down **whole**, not in scattered occasions of revelation like the Madinan surahs — which is why it reads as one continuous argument. Second, the escort of angels signals its weight: this is the surah of Tawhid.

---

## 2. The Opening: Two Darknesses, One Light (6:1–3)

> **6:1** *All praise be to Allah, Who created the heavens and the earth, and originated the darkness and the light, yet those who disbelieve hold others as equal with their Lord.*

Ibn Kathir notices the grammar immediately. **Darkness is plural (*Zulumat*); light is singular (*An-Nur*)** — because light is more honoured, and because truth is one while error is many. He tells you to hold this observation, because the surah will close by using it again:

> *And verily, this is My straight path, so follow it, and follow no (other) ways, for they will separate you away from His way.* (6:153)

The path is one. The paths are many. That is the architecture of the whole surah, announced in its first verse.

Then: *yet those who disbelieve hold others as equal with their Lord.* After all this — the heavens, the earth, night and day made to serve you — **still** they set up rivals. The word *yet* carries the entire indictment.

**The two terms (6:2).** *He has decreed a stated term. And there is with Him another determined term.* Reported from Ibn Abbas through Sa'id bin Jubayr, and echoed by Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Zayd bin Aslam, As-Suddi and others: the first is **death**, the second is **the Hereafter**. On another reading from Ibn Abbas and Mujahid: the first is the term of this earthly life in general, the second the span of each man's own life.

Either way, the phrase *with Him* is decisive — **none but He knows when it comes.**

---

## 3. Signs Will Not Persuade the Unwilling (6:4–11, 6:25, 6:35, 6:109–111)

This is one of the surah's most insisted-upon themes, and Ibn Kathir returns to it four separate times.

> **6:7** *And even if We had sent down unto you a Message written on paper so that they could touch it with their hands, the disbelievers would have said: "This is nothing but obvious magic!"*

Not read about — **touched with their hands.** And still they would call it magic. Ibn Kathir stacks the parallel verses: if a gate of heaven were opened and they climbed through it, they would say their eyes were dazzled (15:14–15); if they saw a piece of the sky falling, they would call it heaped clouds (52:44).

**Why not send an angel?** (6:8–9) Because the descent of an angel is the descent of judgment — *the matter would have been judged at once, and no respite would be granted to them.* And were an angel sent as a messenger, he would have to come in human form anyway, so that people could speak with him and learn from him. Ibn Abbas: *"they will not be able to look at the angel due to light."* The confusion they created for themselves over accepting a human messenger would simply be recreated.

Ibn Kathir draws out the mercy in this: **Allah sends to every kind of creation a messenger from its own kind** — so they can be spoken to, questioned, and learned from.

**The verdict** (6:110): *And We shall turn their hearts and their eyes away, as they refused to believe therein for the first time.* Mujahid: We prevent them from faith, and even if every sign came they would not believe, just as We prevented them the first time.

The same logic returns at 6:28 — if the disbelievers in the Fire were sent back to the world, *they would certainly revert to that which they were forbidden. And indeed they are liars.* Their request to return is not repentance; it is terror at the sight of the Fire.

## 4. Mercy Written Upon Himself (6:12–16, 6:54)

> **6:12** *Say: "To whom belongs all that is in the heavens and the earth?" Say: "To Allah." He has prescribed mercy for Himself.*

The hadith Ibn Kathir cites for this — and cites again at 6:54, and again at the very last line of the surah — is from Abu Hurayrah in the Two Sahihs:

> *When Allah created the creation, He wrote in a Book that He has with Him above the Throne: "My mercy overcomes My anger."*

That this hadith appears three times, including as the closing sentence of the entire tafsir of the surah, is Ibn Kathir's editorial judgment about what Al-An'am is finally about.

**"He feeds but is not fed" (6:14).** A second reading has it *"He feeds but He does not eat."* Ibn Kathir attaches the Prophet's ﷺ long grace after a meal at Quba' — praise to the One Who feeds and is never fed, Who gave guidance and food and drink and covering; *praise that should not be neglected... all the while affirming that we will never be able to duly thank Him, nor be appreciative enough of Him, nor be free of needing Him.*

**The definition of success (6:16).** *Who is averted from (such a torment) on that Day; then He has surely been Merciful to him. And that would be the obvious success.* Read with 3:185 — *whoever is moved away from the Fire and admitted to Paradise, he indeed is successful.* Success here is defined **negatively**: escape from loss. Everything else is surplus.

---

## 5. Al-Qahir — the Irresistible (6:17–21, 6:61)

> **6:17** *And if Allah touches you with harm, none can remove it but He, and if He touches you with good, then He is able to do all things.*

Ibn Kathir attaches the Prophet's ﷺ supplication from the Sahih: *"O Allah, there is none Who can avert what You grant or give what You deprive, and no fortune ever helps the fortunate against You."*

**Al-Qahir**, he explains, means the One before Whom necks are subservient and tyrants humble; all creation is insignificant before His grace, greatness and ability. And it is paired here — as often in this surah — with *Al-Hakim, Al-Khabir*: **the All-Wise, Well-Acquainted**, Who *places everything in its rightful place.* Irresistible power without wisdom would be terror; here it is governance.

**"They recognize him as they recognize their own children" (6:20).** The People of the Book know Muhammad ﷺ by the descriptions given them: his attributes, homeland, migration, and the character of his Ummah. Yet *those who have lost themselves will not believe* — knowledge without submission is loss, not guidance.

## 6. Comforting the Messenger (6:33–36) — and the Three Men Who Listened

> **6:33** *We know indeed the grief which their words cause you; it is not you that they deny, but it is the verses of Allah that the wrongdoers deny.*

Ibn Kathir places here one of the finest narrations in the whole tafsir, from Muhammad bin Ishaq via Az-Zuhri:

Abu Jahl, Abu Sufyan and Al-Akhnas bin Shurayq each crept out at night, separately and unknown to each other, to listen to the Prophet ﷺ reciting until dawn. Meeting on the road home, each confessed, and all three swore never to do it again — *so that the young men of Quraysh would not hear and imitate them.* The next night, each came again, believing the others would keep the vow. And the third night. Each morning: the same meeting, the same recriminations, the same vow.

Afterwards Al-Akhnas went with his staff to Abu Sufyan and asked what he made of what he had heard. Abu Sufyan: *"By Allah, I have heard some things that I recognize and know their implications. I also heard some things whose meaning and implications were unknown to me."* Al-Akhnas: *"And I the same."*

Then he went to Abu Jahl, who gave the real answer:

> *"We competed with Bani Abd Manaf, and so we fed as they fed and gave away as they gave away. So when we were neck and neck with them, just as two horses in a race, they said, 'There is a Prophet from among us, to whom revelation from the heaven comes.' So how can we ever beat them at that! By Allah we will never believe in him or accept what he says."*

This is Ibn Kathir's illustration of *it is not you that they deny.* It was never about evidence. It was tribal rivalry, and Abu Jahl was honest enough to say so.

**"If their aversion is hard on you..." (6:35).** Ibn Abbas' reading: if you could tunnel into the earth or climb a ladder to the sky and fetch a better sign than the one I gave them — then do it. The gentle rebuke is that no better sign exists.

## 7. Every Creature Is a Community (6:38)

> **6:38** *There is not a moving creature on earth, nor a bird that flies with its two wings, but are Umam like you. We have neglected nothing in the Book, then unto their Lord they shall be gathered.*

- **Mujahid:** *Umam* means distinct species with names of their own.
- **Qatadah:** birds are an Ummah, humans are an Ummah, jinn are an Ummah.
- **We have neglected nothing in the Book:** every creature's name, number, movements and stillness is recorded; none is forgotten in its sustenance (11:6, 29:60).

**And they shall be gathered.** Ibn Abbas said death gathers them; others said the Day of Resurrection. Abu Hurayrah's report is the striking one: all creatures will be gathered — beasts, birds and the rest — and *Allah's justice will be so perfect that the un-horned sheep will receive retribution from the horned sheep.* Then Allah will say to them, *"Be dust!"* — and it is at that moment that the disbeliever cries, *"Woe to me! Would that I were dust!"* (78:40)

A remarkable image: the disbeliever envying an animal, because the animal's account has closed and his has not.

## 8. Prosperity as a Trap (6:42–45)

> **6:44** *So, when they forgot the warning with which they had been reminded, We opened to them the gates of every pleasant thing, until in the midst of their enjoyment in that which they were given, all of a sudden, We took them to punishment, and lo! They were plunged into destruction with deep regrets and sorrows.*

First comes hardship — *extreme poverty and loss of health* — so that they might humble themselves. When hardship fails and hearts harden, the second stage begins, and it is **worse**: the gates are opened. Ibn Kathir's own gloss is blunt: *We opened the gates of provisions for them from wherever they wished, so that We deceive them.* He adds, *"We seek refuge with Allah from such an end."*

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri:**

> *"Whomever Allah gives provision and he thinks that Allah is not testing him, has no wisdom. Whomever has little provision and thinks that Allah will not look at him, has no wisdom."*

And then, having recited the verse: *"By the Lord of the Ka'bah! Allah deceived these people, when He gave them what they wished, and then they were punished."*

The passage closes on a startling note: *So the roots of the people who did wrong were cut off. **And all praise be to Allah, Lord of all that exists.*** Praise, at the destruction of the unjust — because justice completed is itself a mercy.

## 9. Do Not Turn Away the Poor (6:50–54)

> **6:52** *And turn not away those who invoke their Lord, morning and evening seeking His Face. You are accountable for them in nothing, and they are accountable for you in nothing, that you may turn them away, and thus become of the wrongdoers.*

The chiefs of Quraysh wanted the Prophet ﷺ to dismiss the slaves and the weak before they would sit with him. The command is the opposite: *make them your companions and associates* (compare 18:28).

Ibn Kathir gathers the pattern across history:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Nuh's people** | *"Do we see any follow you but the meekest among us?"* (11:27) — and Nuh answered: *their account is only with my Lord* (26:112–113) |
| **Quraysh** | *"Is it these that Allah has favored from amongst us?"* — they mocked and tortured the weak believers |
| **Heraclius to Abu Sufyan** | *"Do the noblemen or the weak among people follow him?"* Abu Sufyan: *"Rather the weak."* Heraclius: **"Such is the case with followers of the Messengers."** |

The emperor of Rome, from outside Islam entirely, recognised the signature of prophethood in exactly the fact Quraysh used as an objection.

**The answer (6:53):** *Does not Allah know best those who are grateful?* And the hadith: *Allah does not look at your shapes or colors, but He looks at your heart and actions.*

**Then the greeting (6:54):** *When those who believe in Our Ayat come to you, say: "Salamun 'Alaykum" — your Lord has written mercy for Himself, so that if any of you does evil in ignorance, and thereafter repents and does righteous good deeds, then surely He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

Ibn Kathir notes the phrase *in ignorance* covers **every** act of disobedience — *as every person who disobeys Allah does it in ignorance.* Sin is, by definition, a failure of knowledge in the moment of acting.

**What the Prophet ﷺ does not have (6:50):** not the treasures of Allah, not knowledge of the Unseen, not angelic nature. *I but follow what is revealed to me.* Ibn Kathir: *and I never disobey the revelation in the least.*

## 10. The Keys of the Unseen (6:59) — and a Difficulty Resolved

> **6:59** *And with Him are the keys of the Ghayb, none knows them but He. And He knows whatever there is on the land and in the sea; not a leaf falls, but He knows it. There is not a grain in the darkness of the earth nor anything fresh or dry, but is written in a Clear Record.*

Al-Bukhari records from Ibn Umar that the Prophet ﷺ named **five keys of the Unseen**, taken from 31:34:

1. The knowledge of the Hour
2. The sending down of rain
3. What is in the wombs
4. What a person will earn tomorrow
5. In what land a person will die

*Not a leaf falls but He knows it* — Ibn Kathir's reasoning: if the movements of **inanimate things** are known to Him, what of living creatures, and above all of those on whom the Divine law has been imposed?

### The difficulty at 6:58

*Say: "If I had that which you are asking for impatiently, the matter would have been settled at once between you and I."*

Ibn Kathir raises the objection himself. Aishah asked the Prophet ﷺ whether he had ever known a day harder than Uhud, and he told her of the day of Ta'if — of being refused by Ibn Abd Yalil, walking away *overwhelmed with severe sorrow*, not stopping until Qarn Ath-Tha'alib, where he looked up to find a cloud shading him and Jibril within it. The Angel of the Mountains was sent, offering to crush the town between Al-Akhshabayn. The Prophet ﷺ answered:

> *No, but I hope that Allah will let them generate offspring who will worship Allah Alone, and will worship none besides Him.*

So: if the punishment **had** been in his hand, would it not have fallen — as the verse says? Ibn Kathir's resolution: the verse concerns punishment the disbelievers **demanded**; the hadith concerns punishment **offered** to him by an angel and declined out of compassion. The two do not touch.

## 11. Sleep, Death, and the Angels at the Deathbed (6:60–62)

> **6:60** *It is He Who takes your souls by night, and has knowledge of all that you have done by day, then He raises you up again, that a term appointed be fulfilled.*

**Sleep is minor death.** Ibn Kathir reads it alongside 39:42 — Allah takes the souls at death, and the souls of the living in their sleep; He keeps the ones for which death is ordained and sends the rest back for a term appointed.

*He sends guardians over you* (6:61) — the recording angels of 13:11, 82:10, and 50:17–18: *one sitting on the right and one on the left. Not a word does he utter, but there is a watcher by him, ready.*

Then Imam Ahmad's narration from Abu Hurayrah, which Ibn Kathir gives at length: the angels attend the dying. To the righteous soul: *"O pure soul from a pure body! Come out with honor and receive the good news of rest, satisfaction and a Lord Who is not angry"* — repeated until the soul leaves, then carried up, the gate of heaven opened to it with the same welcome, until it reaches the heaven above which is Allah. To the wicked soul, the mirror image: *"No welcome to the wicked soul from the wicked body. Return with disgrace, for the doors of heaven will not be opened for you"* — and it is thrown back down to the grave.

The souls are taken to **Illiyyin** or to **Sijjin**. *"We seek refuge with Allah from this end."*

## 12. The Three Supplications (6:63–65)

> **6:65** *Say: "He has the power to send torment on you from above or from under your feet, or to Yalbisakum in party strife, and make you taste the violence of one another."*

**Al-Bukhari's gloss on *Yalbisakum*:** to cover you with confusion — to divide into parties and sects.

Jabir bin Abdullah narrated the Prophet's ﷺ reaction as the verse descended, clause by clause: at *from above* — *"I seek refuge with Your Face"*; at *from under your feet* — *"I seek refuge with Your Face"*; and at *party strife and violence of one another* — **"This is less burdensome"** — or, in the other wording, *"easier."*

Then the two hadiths that give the verse its history:

**Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas:** after a long prayer at the Masjid of Bani Mu'awiyah, the Prophet ﷺ said, *"I asked my Lord for three: that He not destroy my Ummah by drowning — He gave it; that He not destroy them by famine — He gave it; and that He not make them taste the violence of one another — **but He did not give that to me.**"*

**Khabbab bin Al-Aratt**, after a night-long prayer: *"I asked my Lord not to destroy us with what He destroyed the nations before us — He gave me that. I asked my Lord not to make our enemies prevail above us — He gave me that. I asked my Lord not to cover us with confusion in party strife — **but He refused.**"*

And the hadith of the seventy-three groups, all in the Fire except one.

Ibn Kathir's placement here is deliberate: the one form of destruction this Ummah is **not** protected from is the one it inflicts on itself. Everything else was granted.

## 13. Leave the Company of Mockers (6:68–70)

> **6:68** *And when you see those who engage in false conversation about Our verses by mocking at them, stay away from them till they turn to another topic. And if Shaytan causes you to forget, then after the remembrance, sit not you in the company of those people who are the wrongdoers.*

Ibn Kathir is explicit: **this command includes every member of this Ummah**, not only the Prophet ﷺ. It is the same ruling as 4:140 — *if you stayed with them, certainly in that case you would be like them.*

Forgetfulness is excused (*"My Ummah was forgiven unintentional errors, forgetfulness and what they are coerced to do"*) — but **the moment you remember, you leave**.

And the purpose is not only your own innocence: *but their duty is to remind them, that they may attain Taqwa.* Your withdrawal is itself the reminder — it may be the thing that makes them aware of what they are doing.

**6:70 — *Tubsal*.** The Salaf gave a spread of meanings for the soul that is *Tubsal* for what it earned: *made submissive* (Ibn Abbas via Ad-Dahhak, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, As-Suddi), *exposed* (Ibn Abbas via Al-Walibi), *prevented* (Qatadah), *recompensed* (Murrah, Ibn Zayd), *reckoned* (Al-Kalbi). Ibn Kathir: they all converge — **exposure to destruction, being cut off from all good, restrained from what is desired.**

## 14. Ibrahim and the Setting Things (6:74–83)

This is the intellectual centre of the surah, and Ibn Kathir treats it as a **structured argument in three stages**, not a story of a young man discovering God.

**First, the idols of earth (6:74).** Ibrahim confronts Azar. Ibn Kathir explains what these people believed: the idols were images of **heavenly angels**, made because the people thought themselves too insignificant to approach the Creator directly — so they worshipped the angels as intercessors for provision, victory and need. The error is not crude polytheism but *misplaced humility*.

Ibn Kathir attaches Ibrahim's full plea from Surah Maryam (19:41–48) — *"O my father! Why do you worship that which hears not, sees not and cannot avail you in anything?"* — and Azar's answer: *"If you stop not, I will indeed stone you."* Ibrahim's reply: *"Peace be on you! I will ask forgiveness of my Lord for you."*

He kept asking, for the rest of his father's life, and stopped only when it became clear Azar had died an idolator (9:114). And the hadith of the meeting on the Day of Resurrection, where Ibrahim pleads *"what will be more disgraceful to me than cursing and dishonoring my father?"* — and is told to look behind him.

**Second, the heavenly bodies (6:76–78).** Ibn Kathir names the seven objects these people venerated: the Moon, Mercury, Venus, the Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn — of which the brightest and most honoured were the Sun, the Moon, and Venus.

Ibrahim takes them in ascending order of brightness — **Venus, then the Moon, then the Sun** — and demolishes each with the same word: *afala*, **it set**.

Qatadah's one-line summary is the whole argument: **"Ibrahim knew that his Lord is Eternal and never ceases."**

Ibn Kathir spells out the reasoning: Venus is *subservient to a term and course appointed that it does not defy, nor swerving right or left.* It rises in the east, sets in the west, and does it again tomorrow. **A thing under orders cannot be the one giving them.**

Note carefully: he was not confused. *"This is my lord"* is the debater's move — granting the opponent's premise in order to break it in front of him. Allah says at the outset (6:75) that He had **already** shown Ibrahim the kingdom of the heavens and earth, *that he be one of those who have faith with certainty*. The certainty precedes the argument.

**Third, the conclusion (6:79).** *Verily, I have turned my face towards Him Who has created the heavens and the earth, Hanifan, and I am not of the idolators.* Ibn Kathir: *I have purified my religion and made my worship sincere* — turning from the made things to the One Who made them, *in Whose Hand is the dominion of all things.*

### Security belongs to those who do not mix belief with Zulm (6:80–83)

> **6:81** *And how should I fear those whom you associate in worship with Allah, while you fear not that you have joined in worship with Allah things for which He has not sent down to you any Sultan? Which of the two parties has more right to be in security — if you but know?*

Ibrahim's challenge is also Hud's (11:53–56): *"So plot against me, all of you, and give me no respite."* If your gods can harm, set them on me.

> **6:82** *It is those who believe and confuse not their belief with Zulm, for them there is security and they are the guided.*

**And this verse frightened the Companions.** Al-Bukhari records from Abdullah bin Mas'ud that when it was revealed they said, *"And who among us did not commit Zulm against himself?"* The Prophet ﷺ answered:

> *It is not what you understood from it. Did you not hear what the righteous servant (Luqman) said — "O my son! Join not in worship others with Allah. Verily, Shirk is a great Zulm indeed" (31:13)? Therefore, it is about Shirk.*

A model of how a verse is interpreted: by another verse, not by fear.

## 15. The Chain of Prophets (6:84–90)

Eighteen prophets are named across four verses — Ishaq, Ya'qub, Nuh, Dawud, Sulayman, Ayyub, Yusuf, Musa, Harun, Zakariyya, Yahya, Isa, Ilyas, Isma'il, Al-Yasa', Yunus, Lut — with their fathers, offspring and brethren.

**Ibrahim's reward.** He left his people and migrated so that he could worship Allah alone; Allah *compensated Ibrahim with better than his people and tribe* — righteous children who would follow his religion. Ibn Kathir notes the tenderness in the news being of *Ishaq, and after him, of Ya'qub* (11:71): a son **and** a grandson, promised in his lifetime, since ordinarily a child born to elderly parents might be too weak to have offspring of his own. (The name *Ya'qub* itself, he notes, carries the sense of multiplying and having offspring.)

**Nuh and Ibrahim, two distinctions:** after the Flood, every human being on earth is descended from **Nuh**. And after **Ibrahim**, Allah sent no prophet except from his descendants (29:27, 57:26).

**A legal point drawn from 'Isa.** 'Isa is listed among Ibrahim's offspring — through his **mother**, having no father. Ibn Kathir uses this to settle a real question, and gives the incident:

Al-Hajjaj challenged Yahya bin Ya'mar for claiming Al-Hasan and Al-Husayn were of the Prophet's ﷺ offspring: *"I read the Qur'an from beginning to end and did not find it."* Yahya recited this passage, down to *and Yahya and 'Isa*, and asked: *"Is not 'Isa from the offspring of Ibrahim, although he did not have a father?"* Al-Hajjaj: **"You have said the truth."**

The practical rule: a bequest to one's **"offspring"** includes daughters' children; a bequest to one's **"sons"** does not.

**The warning (6:88).** *But if they had joined in worship others with Allah, all that they used to do would have been of no benefit to them.* Said of **prophets** — the highest of creation — to magnify the gravity of Shirk. The conditional does not imply possibility; compare 39:65 and 43:81.

**And the entrusting (6:89).** *If they disbelieve therein, then indeed We have entrusted it to a people who are not disbelievers therein.* Ibn Kathir: if Quraysh and the rest of the earth reject it, it passes to **the Muhajirun and the Ansar, and those who follow their lead until the Day of Resurrection.** *They will not deny any of these favors, not even one letter.* He adds: *We ask Allah to make us among them.*

**The command (6:90).** *They are those whom Allah had guided. So follow their guidance.* Al-Bukhari records that Ibn Abbas, asked by Mujahid whether there is a prostration in Surah Sad, said yes — and cited this passage. His comment: *"Your Prophet was among those whose guidance we were commanded to follow."*

## 16. The Two Books (6:91–92, 6:154–157)

> **6:91** *They did not estimate Allah with an estimation due to Him when they said: "Nothing did Allah send down to any human being."*

The reply is a single question: **then who sent down the Book which Musa brought?** A light and a guidance to mankind — *which you have made into separate paper sheets, disclosing some of it and concealing much.* Ibn Kathir: copied out from the original, altered and distorted at will, and then labelled *"this is from Allah."*

> **6:92** *And this is a blessed Book which We have sent down, confirming what came before it, so that you may warn the Mother of Towns and all those around it.*

**Umm al-Qura** is Makkah; *all those around it* is **the rest of the children of Adam, Arab and non-Arab alike**. Ibn Kathir supports this with 7:158, 6:19, 11:17 and 25:1, and with the hadith of the five things given to the Prophet ﷺ that were given to no one before him — among them: *Every Prophet was sent only to his nation, but I have been sent to all people.*

**The Tawrah praised (6:154).** *Complete for that which is best, and explaining all things in detail.* Ibn Kathir: a complete and comprehensive Book, sufficient for what Musa needed to complete his Law. *For that which is best* — as reward for his doing right (compare 2:124, 32:24: leadership is earned through patience and certainty).

**The excuse cut off (6:156–157).** Two excuses are pre-empted: *"The Book was sent down only to two sects before us"* (the Jews and Christians — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, As-Suddi, Qatadah) *"and we were unaware of what they studied"*; and *"If only the Book had been sent down to us, we would surely have been better guided than they."* Now a clear proof has come **in their own tongue**. The excuse is closed.

## 17. Claiming Revelation Falsely (6:93–94)

> **6:93** *And who can be more unjust than he who invents a lie against Allah, or says: "I have received inspiration," whereas he is not inspired in anything; and who says, "I will reveal the like of what Allah has revealed."*

Ikrimah and Qatadah: revealed concerning **Musaylimah Al-Kadhdhab**.

Then the death scene of such people: *the angels stretching forth their hands* — Ad-Dahhak and Abu Salih: *with torment*. The disbeliever near death is given the **"good news"** of chains, restraints, boiling water and the anger of the Most Merciful; the soul scatters through the body and refuses to leave; the angels beat him until it comes out.

> **6:94** *And truly you have come unto Us alone, as We created you the first time. You have left behind you all that which We had bestowed on you.*

The hadith from the Sahih: *The Son of Adam says, "My money, my money!" But what part of your money do you have, other than what you eat and is spent, what you wear and wear out, and what you gave in charity and thus remains?*

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri's picture** of the Day: a man brought forward as though he were a golden chariot. Allah asks, *"Where is what you collected?"* He answers, *"O Lord! I collected it and left it as intact as ever."* And Allah says: *"O Son of Adam! Where is what you sent forth for yourself?"* — and he realises he sent forth nothing.

---

## 18. The Signs in the Ordinary (6:95–99)

The most beautiful passage in the surah, and Ibn Kathir treats it as a single sustained proof.

> **6:95** *Verily, it is Allah Who causes the seed grain and the fruit stone to split and sprout. He brings forth the living from the dead, and it is He Who brings forth the dead from the living. Such is Allah — then how are you deluded away from the truth?*

The splitting of the seed **is** the bringing of life from the dead: the living plant out of the inert stone. Ibn Kathir extends it — the egg from the hen and the hen from the egg; and, some said, the righteous child from the corrupt parent and the reverse.

> **6:96** *Cleaver of the daybreak. He has appointed the night for resting, and the sun and the moon for reckoning. Such is the measuring of the Almighty, the All-Knowing.*

Note that this returns to the surah's opening — *He originated the darkness and the light* (6:1). And note Ibn Kathir's observation about the sun and moon: they hold **specific orbits, according to a term appointed with magnificent precision that never changes**, with distinct summer and winter positions that lengthen and shorten the days.

He then points out a habit of the Qur'an: passages on night, day, sun and moon **close on the divine attributes of power and knowledge** — *the Almighty, the All-Knowing* (compare 36:37–38, 41:12). Order in the heavens is evidence of a Knower, not of a mechanism.

**The three purposes of stars (6:97).** Some of the Salaf said: *whoever believes in other than three things about these stars has made a mistake and lied against Allah.* Allah made them (1) **adornment** for the heavens, (2) **missiles** against the devils, and (3) **navigation** through the dark recesses of land and sea. Astrology is excluded by this list.

**Mustaqar and Mustawda' (6:98).** *A place of residing and a place of storage.* Two readings from the Salaf: Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ata, An-Nakha'i, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others — the **womb** and the **father's loins**; or, also from Ibn Mas'ud — the **dwelling in this life** and **the grave**.

> **6:99** *Look at their fruits when they begin to bear, and Yan'ih (ripen). Verily, in these things there are signs for people who believe.*

*Yan'ih* — when the fruit ripens (Al-Bara bin Azib, Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak, Ata Al-Khurasani, As-Suddi, Qatadah).

Ibn Kathir's instruction is to **contemplate the ability of the Creator Who brought these fruits into existence after they were dry wood.** Dates and grapes hang *within reach and easy to pick*; olives and pomegranates are *similar, yet different* — Qatadah: the leaves alike in shape and appearance, the fruits different in shape, taste and fragrance. And 13:4: neighbouring tracts, **watered with the same water**, yet some made better than others to eat.

Same soil, same water, different fruit. That is the argument.

---

## 19. He Has No Son, and No Vision Grasps Him (6:100–103)

**How could idolators be said to worship jinn (6:100)?** They worshipped idols — but they obeyed the jinn who commanded them to do so. Obedience *is* the worship. Ibn Kathir supports it with 4:117–120, 18:50, 34:41 and above all 36:60–61: *Did I not ordain for you, O Children of Adam, that you should not worship Shaytan?*

**Badi' (6:101)** — the Originator, Who created without precedent. Ibn Kathir notes that the word for innovation, *bid'ah*, comes from this root: **that which has no precedent.**

*How can He have children when He has no wife?* A child is the offspring of two compatible spouses; Allah has no equal, and none of His creatures resembles Him.

### Seeing Allah (6:103)

> **6:103** *No vision can grasp Him, but His grasp is over all vision. He is the Most Subtle, Well-Acquainted.*

Ibn Kathir handles this carefully, because two truths must both stand.

**In this life:** Aishah said, *"Whoever claims that Muhammad has seen his Lord, will have uttered a lie against Allah"* — and recited this verse. From the previous scriptures: *"O Musa! Verily, no living thing sees Me but it dies, and no dried thing sees Me but it rolls up."* And 7:143 — the mountain collapsed to dust and Musa fell unconscious. And from Muslim, Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari: *His Veil is the Light — or Fire — and if He removes it, the Light of His Face will burn every created thing that His sight reaches.*

**In the Hereafter:** the believers **will** see Him, affirmed by numerous authentic hadiths across the Sahihs, Musnad and Sunan collections.

**The resolution:** Aishah herself affirmed the vision in the Hereafter while denying it in this life. What the verse negates is **encompassing** Him — perfectly grasping His grace and magnificence as He is — *and that is not possible for any human, angel or anything created.* Not seeing; comprehending.

---

## 20. Do Not Insult Their Gods (6:108)

> **6:108** *And insult not those whom they worship besides Allah, lest they insult Allah wrongfully without knowledge.*

Ibn Abbas gives the occasion: they said, *"O Muhammad! You will stop insulting our gods, or we will insult your Lord."* Qatadah: the Muslims used to insult the idols, and the disbelievers retaliated by insulting Allah.

Ibn Kathir states the principle plainly: **there is a clear benefit in insulting false gods, and it is forbidden anyway**, because the greater evil it provokes outweighs it.

He illustrates it with a hadith that makes the logic unforgettable:

> *Cursed is he who insults his own parents!*
>
> They said, "O Allah's Messenger! And how would a man insult his own parents?"
>
> *He insults a man's father, and that man insults his father; and insults his mother, and that man insults his mother.*

**You are responsible for the response you provoke.** The chain of consequence runs back to you.

---

## 21. Every Prophet Has Enemies (6:112–113)

> **6:112** *And so We have appointed for every Prophet enemies — Shayatin among mankind and Jinn, inspiring one another with adorned speech as a delusion.*

Ibn Kathir's definition: **Shaytan** describes one who is *dissimilar to his kind due to his or her wickedness* — hence there are devils among men as well as among jinn, and Qatadah says they inspire each other.

*Adorned speech as a delusion* — beautified language that deceives the ignorant who hear it. The instruction that follows is patience: *so leave them alone with their fabrications*, and trust that *Allah shall suffice for you.*

**Waraqah bin Nawfal**, at the very beginning, told the Prophet ﷺ: *"None came with what you came with but he was the subject of enmity."*

**A second kind of "revelation" (6:121).** Abu Zamil's report: a man told Ibn Abbas that Al-Mukhtar bin Abi Ubayd claimed to receive revelation. Ibn Abbas said, **"He has said the truth."** The man was shocked. Ibn Abbas explained: *"There are two types of revelation, one from Allah and one from the devil. Allah's revelation came to Muhammad, while the Shaytan's revelation comes to his friends"* — and recited this verse. (Ibn Umar gave the same answer.)

A brilliant piece of precision: the claim to be *receiving* something may be perfectly true. The question is who is sending.

## 22. Food, and the Sovereignty of Legislation (6:118–121, 6:145–146)

This is where the surah's abstract Tawhid becomes a concrete legal fight, because Quraysh had built an entire dietary law out of nothing.

**Mention the Name (6:118–121).** *So eat of that on which Allah's Name has been mentioned* — and *Eat not of that on which Allah's Name has not been pronounced, for surely it is disobedience.* Ibn Kathir treats this as proof that a slaughtered animal is unlawful without the Name, **even if slaughtered by a Muslim**, supported by 5:4 and hadiths on hunting with trained dogs (Adi bin Hatim, Abu Tha'labah), on slaughter (Rafi' bin Khadij), and on the 'Id sacrifice (Jundub bin Sufyan).

**The idolators' counter-argument** (As-Suddi): *"You claim that you seek Allah's pleasure. Yet you do not eat what Allah causes to die, but you eat what you slaughter."* Ibn Abbas records the devils' whisper in the same shape: *"Do you eat from what you kill but not from what Allah causes to die?"*

**And then the verse that answers it (6:121):** *and if you obey them, then you would indeed be polytheists.*

### The definition of Shirk that follows from this

Ibn Kathir: **turning away from Allah's command and legislation to the saying of anyone else, preferring other than what Allah has said, constitutes Shirk.** He proves it with 9:31 and the hadith of Adi bin Hatim:

> Adi said, "O Allah's Messenger! They did not worship them."
>
> The Prophet ﷺ said: *Yes they did. They allowed the impermissible for them and they prohibited the lawful for them, and they followed them in that. **That was their worship of them.***

**The four prohibitions (6:145).** *I find not in that which has been revealed to me anything forbidden to be eaten... unless it be:*

1. **Maytah** — a dead animal
2. **Blood poured forth** (Qatadah: poured blood; residual blood in the meat is allowed)
3. **The flesh of swine** — *for that surely is unclean*
4. **What was slaughtered in the name of other than Allah**

Ibn Kathir notes that the prohibitions in Al-Ma'idah and the hadiths **amend** this list — this is not a closed set for all time.

**Ibn Abbas' principle**, recorded by Ibn Marduwyah, Al-Hakim and Abu Dawud, is the governing rule of the whole passage:

> *"Whatever Allah allowed is lawful and whatever He prohibited is unlawful. **Whatever He did not mention, there is no sin in it.**"*

The default is permission. The burden of proof lies on prohibition — which is exactly the reverse of what Quraysh assumed.

**Sawdah's sheep.** A sheep of Sawdah bint Zam'ah died. She told the Prophet ﷺ, and he asked, *"Why did you not use its skin?"* She said, "Should we use the skin of a sheep that has died?" He recited this very verse and said: ***You will not be eating it if you tan its skin and benefit from it.*** She tanned it into a water skin and kept it until it wore out.

### What was forbidden to the Jews (6:146)

*Every animal with undivided hoof*, and *the fat of the ox and the sheep* except what adheres to their backs, or their *Hawaya* (the entrails — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak), or is mixed with bone.

*Thus We recompensed them for their rebellion* — a **penal** restriction, not an original one (compare 4:160). And Ibn Jarir notes the corrective force of *And verily, We are Truthful*: the prohibition came from Allah as punishment, **not**, as they claimed, from Israil forbidding it for himself.

**And then the loophole, and the curse.** When Umar heard that Samurah had sold liquor he said: *"May Allah fight Samurah! Did he not know that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **May Allah curse the Jews! The fats were forbidden for them, so they melted the fat and sold it.**"*

Jabir's fuller narration from the year of the conquest of Makkah: asked whether the fat of dead animals might at least be used for dyeing skins, painting ships and lamp oil, the Prophet ﷺ said, ***"No, it is still unlawful"*** — and then repeated the curse. **A prohibition on a thing is a prohibition on its price.**

## 23. The Inventions of the Idolators (6:136–144)

Ibn Kathir gathers here the full catalogue of what Quraysh had fabricated — and it is the reason the surah is called *Al-An'am*, "the Cattle."

**The divided harvest (6:136).** They assigned a share of crops and cattle to Allah and a share to their idols. Ibn Abbas describes the accounting, and it is grimly funny:

- Anything that fell accidentally from Allah's share was added to the idol's share.
- If water designated for the idol irrigated Allah's portion, the produce went to the idol.
- If Allah's produce got mixed with the idol's, they said *the idol is poor* and gave it to the idol.
- If water designated for Allah irrigated the idol's portion, they left it for the idol.

Every single ambiguity resolved in the idol's favour. *But the share of their "partners" reaches not Allah, while the share of Allah reaches their "partners"! Evil is the way they judge!* Ibn Kathir: they invented an unjust division and then **cheated even at that.**

**Killing children (6:137, 6:140).** The devils made it seem fair to them — **sons killed for fear of poverty, daughters buried alive for fear of dishonour.** Mujahid and As-Suddi both trace it to the same source. And 6:140 pronounces the verdict: *Indeed lost are they who have killed their children, foolishly, without knowledge.*

Ibn Abbas' remark, recorded by Ibn Marduwyah and by Al-Bukhari in the chapter on the virtues of Quraysh:

> *"If it pleases you to know how ignorant the Arabs used to be, then recite the Ayat beyond Ayah one hundred and thirty in Surah Al-An'am."*

**The forbidden categories (6:138–139).** *Hijr* — cattle and crops declared off-limits, edible only by whom they permitted. Cattle exempted from bearing burdens. Cattle slaughtered without Allah's Name. And the rule that the milk (or offspring) of certain animals was *for our males alone, and forbidden to our females* — but if born dead, **then everyone could share**. Ash-Sha'bi: *"if any cattle from the Bahirah died, both men and women would share in eating it."*

The four named institutions — **Bahirah, Sa'ibah, Wasilah, Ham** — are the ones Allah demolishes at 5:103. Ibn Kathir names the man responsible: **'Amr bin Luhay bin Qum'ah**, *the first person to change the religion of the Prophets*.

**The demolition (6:143–144).** Allah does not argue theology here; He counts.

> *Eight pairs: of the sheep two, and of the goats two... and of the camels two, and of oxen two. Say: "Has He forbidden the two males or the two females, or that which the wombs of the two females enclose? Inform me with knowledge if you are truthful."*

Al-Awfi's report from Ibn Abbas makes the logic explicit: *does the womb produce anything but males and females? So why do you prohibit some and allow some others?* There are only three possible categories, and Allah forbade none of them. **Then who told you?**

And the second question, sharper: *Or were you present when Allah ordered you such a thing?* Ibn Kathir calls this **mockery** — the only honest response to a law with no lawgiver.

## 24. The Ten Commandments (6:151–153)

> **Ibn Mas'ud:** *"Whoever wishes to read the will and testament of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ on which he placed his seal, let him read these Ayat."*

> **Ibn Abbas:** *"In Surah Al-An'am, there are clear Ayat, and they are the Mother of the Book."*

And Ubadah bin As-Samit reported that the Prophet ﷺ recited these verses and asked, *"Who among you will give me his pledge to do these things?"* — adding that whoever fulfils it, his reward is with Allah; whoever falls short and is punished in this life, that is his recompense; and whoever Allah defers to the Hereafter, his matter is with Allah, to punish or forgive.

| # | Command | Ibn Kathir's note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Join not anything in worship with Him** | The one sin never forgiven (4:116). Abu Dharr's hadith: whoever dies worshipping none besides Allah enters Paradise — *"Even if he stole or committed illegal sexual intercourse?"* — *"Even if..."* And: *"even if you brought the earth's fill of sins to Me, I will bring forth its fill of forgiveness"* |
| 2 | **Be kind and dutiful to your parents** | Placed beside Tawhid throughout the Qur'an (17:23, 31:14, 2:83). Ibn Mas'ud asked which deed is best: *the prayer on time*; then? *being dutiful to parents*; then? *Jihad in Allah's cause.* Kindness is owed **even to idolatrous parents** (31:15) |
| 3 | **Kill not your children because of poverty** | *We provide sustenance for you and for them.* Ibn Kathir notes the word order: here **your** provision comes first; at 17:31 (killing *for fear* of future poverty) **theirs** comes first. Each order fits its fear |
| 4 | **Come not near Al-Fawahish, open or secret** | *None is more jealous than Allah. This is why He has forbidden the immoral sins committed openly or secretly.* When Sa'd bin Ubadah said he would kill a man found with his wife, the Prophet ﷺ said: *Do you wonder at Sa'd's jealousy? By Allah, I am more jealous than Sa'd, and Allah is more jealous than I* |
| 5 | **Kill not the soul Allah has forbidden, except by right** | Already covered by (4), but restated for emphasis. The three exceptions: the married adulterer, life for life, and apostasy with abandonment of the Jama'ah. And the warning about the *Mu'ahid*: whoever kills one under treaty **shall not smell the scent of Paradise, though its scent is perceived from forty years' distance** |
| 6 | **Come not near the orphan's property except to improve it** | Ibn Abbas' account: after 4:10, guardians separated the orphans' food and drink entirely, letting leftovers spoil rather than touch them. It became a hardship, and 2:220 was revealed — *if you mix your affairs with theirs, then they are your brothers.* "Full strength" = adolescence (Ash-Sha'bi, Malik and others) |
| 7 | **Give full measure and full weight with justice** | Compare 83:1–6. Ibn Kathir: **an entire nation was destroyed** for short measure |
| 8 | *(the mercy clause)* **We burden not any person beyond his capacity** | Placed inside the commandments deliberately: one who strives honestly and errs after doing his best **incurs no sin** |
| 9 | **When you speak, say the truth even if a near relative is concerned** | Compare 5:8, 4:135. Justice in word and deed, toward near and distant alike, *at all times and in all situations* |
| 10 | **Fulfil the covenant of Allah** | Ibn Jarir: obeying what He commanded, refraining from what He prohibited, abiding by His Book and the Sunnah of His Messenger |

Each group closes with a different faculty: *that you may understand* — *that you may remember* — *that you may have Taqwa.* Understanding, then memory, then fear: the sequence in which knowledge becomes action.

### The line in the sand (6:153)

> **6:153** *And verily, this is My straight path, so follow it, and follow not other paths, for they will separate you away from His path.*

**Ibn Mas'ud** reported: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ drew a line with his hand and said, *This is Allah's path, leading straight.* Then he drew lines to its right and left and said, *These are the other paths — **on each path there is a devil who calls to it.*** Then he recited this verse.

**Jabir's version:** one line in front, two lines to its right and two to its left; *These are the paths of Shaytan.* Then he placed his hand on the middle line and recited the verse.

**Ibn Mas'ud, asked what the Straight Path is:** *"Muhammad left us at its lower end and its other end is in Paradise. To the right of this Path are other paths, and to the left of it are other paths, and there are men on these paths calling those who pass by them."*

**An-Nawwas bin Sam'an's hadith** — the same parable Ibn Kathir used in Al-Fatihah, given here in full:

> A straight path with two walls on either side, containing doorways over which curtains are lowered. A caller at the gate of the path heralds: *"O people! Come and enter the straight path all together and do not divide."* Another caller from **above** the path warns, whenever someone reaches to lift a curtain: *"Woe to you! Do not open this door, for if you open it, you will enter it."*
>
> **The path is Islam. The two walls are Allah's set limits. The open doors are Allah's prohibitions. The caller at the gate is the Book of Allah. The caller from above is Allah's admonition in the heart of every Muslim.**

**And Ibn Abbas' reading of what "other paths" means in practice:** he tied it to 42:13 — *establish religion and make no divisions in it* — and said Allah *commanded the believers to adhere to the Jama'ah and forbade them from causing divisions and disputes, and informed them that those before them were destroyed because of divisions and disputes in the religion of Allah.*

Singular path, plural paths — Ibn Kathir returns explicitly to his note on 6:1. **Truth is one; error is many.**

## 25. When Faith Comes Too Late (6:158)

> **6:158** *The day that some of the signs of your Lord do come, no good will it do to a person to believe then, if he believed not before, nor earned good with his faith.*

Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah: *The Last Hour will not commence until the sun rises from the west. When the people witness that, they will all believe* — and that is the moment this verse describes.

Ibn Jarir's narration names **three**: the sun rising from the west, the Dajjal, and the Beast of the earth. (Ahmad's version adds the Smoke.)

Abdullah bin 'Amr's account, which Muslim, Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah recorded, describes the sun each night passing beneath the Throne, prostrating, and seeking permission to return — until the night comes when permission is withheld, and withheld again, until it is told: *"Rise from your place."* And it rises upon the people from where it had set.

**And the second clause matters as much as the first.** *Nor earned good with his faith* — even for one who already believed, if he had done no good and made no repentance before that day, it will not be accepted. Faith held in reserve, unspent, is not a credit that can be drawn on at the end.

## 26. Sects, and the Tenfold Reward (6:159–160)

> **6:159** *Verily, those who divide their religion and break up into sects, you have no concern with them in the least.*

Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak and As-Suddi read it as revealed about the Jews and Christians, who had divided before Muhammad ﷺ was sent. But Ibn Kathir's own judgment is broader: **it applies to all who defy or revert from the religion of Allah** — including *those who follow the various sects, desires and misguidance.*

His reasoning: Allah's Law *is one and does not contain any contradiction or incongruity*. And the hadith: *We, the Prophets, are half brothers but have one religion.*

> **6:160** *Whoever brings a good deed shall have ten times the like thereof to his credit, and whoever brings an evil deed shall have only the recompense of the like thereof, and they will not be wronged.*

**The asymmetry is the point.** Ibn Abbas narrated the Prophet ﷺ saying of his Lord:

> *Your Lord is Most Merciful. Whoever intends to perform a good deed and does not do it, it will be written for him as a good deed. If he performs it, it will be written for him as ten deeds, to seven hundred, to multifold. Whoever intends to commit an evil deed but does not do it, **it will be written for him as a good deed.** If he commits it, it will be written for him as one sin — unless Allah erases it.*

And Abu Dharr's narration: *whoever draws closer to Me by a hand's span, I will draw closer by a forearm's length... and whoever comes to Me walking, I will come to him running.*

### Three kinds of not-doing

Ibn Kathir adds a precision here that repays attention. Not every abandoned sin is the same:

1. **Left for Allah's sake** — a good deed is written. In one narration of the Sahih, Allah says: *"He has left the sin for My sake."* Intention plus act.
2. **Left through forgetfulness or distraction** — neither sin nor reward. There was no intention either way.
3. **Left through inability or laziness, after trying and seeking the means** — *this person is just like the person who commits the sin.*

His proof for the third is the hadith of two Muslims meeting with swords: *the killer and the killed will be in the Fire.* Asked about the one killed, the Prophet ﷺ said: ***He was eager to kill his companion.***

## 27. My Prayer and My Dying (6:161–164)

> **6:162** *Say: "Verily, my Salah, my sacrifice, my living, and my dying are for Allah, the Lord of all that exists. He has no partner. And of this I have been commanded, and I am the first of the Muslims."*

Ali reported that the Prophet ﷺ opened his prayer, after the Takbir, with precisely these words — *I have directed my face towards He Who has created the heavens and earth, Hanifan...* — followed by:

> *O Allah! You are the King, there is no deity worthy of worship except You. You are my Lord and I am Your servant. I have committed wrong against myself and admitted to my error, so forgive me all my sins. Verily, You, only You forgive the sins. Direct me to the best conduct, for none except You directs to the best conduct. Divert me from the worst conduct, for only You divert from the worst conduct.*

The verse Ibrahim spoke at the end of his argument (6:79) is the verse the Prophet ﷺ opened every prayer with. The surah folds back on itself.

**"The first of the Muslims"** — Qatadah: first *of this Ummah*. Because Islam is the religion of every prophet: Nuh was commanded *to be of the Muslims* (10:72), Ibrahim submitted (2:131), Yusuf asked to die a Muslim (12:101), Musa's people were Muslims (10:84), the disciples of 'Isa bore witness they were Muslims (5:111).

**The half-brothers hadith, explained.** *We, the Prophets, are half brothers, but our religion is one.* Ibn Kathir unpacks the metaphor: half brothers share **one father, different mothers**. The father is the religion — worship of Allah alone. The mothers are the differing laws. One father, many mothers; one religion, many *shara'i'*. Muhammad's ﷺ law abrogated all before it, and nothing will ever abrogate it.

**Why the religion of Ibrahim (6:161)?** Ibn Kathir pre-empts the obvious question: does following Ibrahim's religion mean Ibrahim was more perfect in it? No — *our Prophet perfectly established the religion and it was completed for him; and none before him reached this level of perfection.* Asked which religion is best with Allah, he ﷺ answered: **Al-Hanifiyyah As-Samhah** — the easy, upright monotheism.

**And then two clauses that together define the believer's stance (6:164):**

*No person earns any sin except against himself, and no bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of another.*

Ibn Kathir notes the mercy hidden inside this strict justice. Burdens do not transfer — but **blessings can**. From 52:21: those whose offspring followed them in faith will have their offspring joined to them in rank, *and We shall not decrease the reward of their deeds in anything.* The lesser believer is raised to the parent's station by the blessing of the parent's works; the parent loses nothing. Justice sets the floor; grace raises the ceiling.

## 28. Generations, in Ranks, Being Tested (6:165)

> **6:165** *And it is He Who has made you generations coming after generations, replacing each other on the earth. And He has raised you in ranks, some above others, that He may try you in that which He has bestowed on you. Surely, your Lord is Swift in retribution, and certainly He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

**Generation after generation, century after century, offspring after forefathers** (Ibn Zayd). Compare 2:30, 7:129, 27:62.

**The ranks are the test.** Differences of provision, conduct, character, shape and colour are not accidents — *He has the perfect wisdom in all this* (43:32, 17:21). And Ibn Kathir insists the test runs **both ways**: *Allah tries the rich concerning his wealth and will ask him about how he appreciated it. He also tries the poor concerning his poverty and will ask him about his patience with it.* Neither station is exempt; neither is the safe one.

Muslim's hadith from Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri: *Verily, this life is beautiful and green, and Allah made you dwell in it generation after generation so that He sees what you will do.*

**And the final pairing.** *Swift in retribution* — *Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.* Ibn Kathir notes this as a habit of the Qur'an (13:6, 15:49–50, 40:3, 85:12–14): warning and hope held together, *so that each person is affected by it according to his or her qualities.*

His own closing prayer: *We ask Allah that He makes us among those who obey what He has commanded, avoid what He has prohibited, and believe in Him as He has informed. Certainly, He is Near, hears and answers the supplication, and He is the Most Kind, Generous and Bestowing.*

And then the last two hadiths of the surah, from Abu Hurayrah:

> *If the believer knew Allah's punishment, no one would hope in entering His Paradise. And if the disbeliever knew Allah's mercy, no one would feel hopeless of acquiring Paradise.*
>
> *Allah created a hundred kinds of mercy. He sent down one of them to His creation, and they are merciful to each other on that account. With Allah, there remain ninety-nine.*

> *When Allah created the creation, He wrote in a Book, and this Book is with Him above the Throne: **"My mercy overcomes My anger."***

Ibn Kathir ends the tafsir of Al-An'am on the same sentence he began the section on mercy with at 6:12. The surah that argues hardest against the deniers closes by saying that the anger is the smaller thing.

## 29. In One Breath

Al-An'am is the sustained case for Tawhid, delivered whole and at night with seventy thousand angels. It opens with the God Who made the heavens, the earth, the darknesses and the one light, and immediately names the absurdity: *yet they set up rivals.* It then argues that case from every direction — from the seed splitting underground and the fruit ripening on the branch, from the storm at sea when every false god is forgotten, from sleep as a rehearsal of death, from Ibrahim watching the stars set one by one and concluding that whatever sets cannot be Lord. It comforts the Messenger by showing that the denial was never about evidence, and it warns him that no evidence would suffice. It dismantles the entire invented food law of Quraysh by simply counting the animals and asking who authorised the prohibition — establishing that everything Allah did not forbid is permitted, and that preferring another's legislation to His **is** the act of worship. It closes with ten commandments the Prophet ﷺ called his sealed testament, a line drawn in the sand with devils calling from the paths on either side, a good deed multiplied tenfold against an evil deed counted once, and mankind set on the earth in ranks, generation after generation, each rank its own examination.

And the last word, above the Throne, in a Book: **My mercy overcomes My anger.**
