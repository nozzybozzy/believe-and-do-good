*Revealed in Makkah · 44 ayat · "The Ways of Ascent" — named from 70:3, where Allah is called Dhil-Ma'arij*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Ma'arij opens with a man **asking for the punishment to hurry up**, and everything that follows is the answer to that request — delivered not as an argument but as a description. *You want to see it? Here is what it looks like.*

The structure is a descent and an ascent. The surah first takes the mocker down through the Day he is demanding: the sky melting, the mountains fluffed into wool, friends who see each other and say nothing, a father offering his own children as ransom and being refused, a Fire that calls its own by name. Then, at the exact midpoint, it stops and turns: **"Verily, man was created very impatient"** — and gives the diagnosis. The mocking demand for punishment was never really about the punishment. It was impatience, the same defect that makes a man panic in hardship and hoard in ease.

And then the way out. Twelve verses describing the one type of person exempted from that verdict — and the exemption begins and ends with **the same thing: prayer**.

Notice what the surah is doing with its own title. Allah is *Lord of the ways of ascent*, and angels ascend to Him across a fifty-thousand-year day. The disbelievers, meanwhile, come out of their graves *rushing* — but horizontally, toward a flag, toward an idol, going nowhere.

---

## 1. The Demand to Hurry It Up (70:1–3)

> *A questioner asked concerning a torment about to befall.*

Ibn Kathir notes a grammatical subtlety: the letter **Ba** carries an implied meaning here. The sense is not merely "asked about" but **"asked for"** — a request to *hasten* the torment. He reads it alongside:

> *And they ask you to hasten on the torment! And Allah fails not His promise.* (22:47)

**Al-'Awfi ← Ibn 'Abbas:** this is the disbelievers questioning Allah's punishment — and it will indeed happen to them.
**Ibn Abi Najih ← Mujahid:** a man called out asking for the Hereafter's torment to come now. And Mujahid identifies the words he means — the notorious challenge of the Quraysh:

> *O Allah! If this is indeed the truth from You, then rain down stones on us from the sky or bring on us a painful torment.* (8:32)

**Waqi'** — Ibn 'Abbas glosses it simply: *coming*. It is not a possibility; it is in preparation, waiting for them. And *none can avert it* — no one can repel it if Allah wills it.

**Dhil-Ma'arij**, the Lord of the ways of ascent:
- **'Ali bin Abi Talhah ← Ibn 'Abbas:** loftiness and abundance.
- **Mujahid:** the ways of ascension into the heavens.

## 2. The Angels, the Ruh, and the Fifty Thousand Years (70:4)

*Ta'ruju* — Qatadah (via Ma'mar and 'Abdur-Razzaq) — means **to ascend**.

**Who is "the Ruh"?** Ibn Kathir gives three possibilities without forcing one:
- **Abu Salih:** creatures of Allah that resemble humans but are not humans.
- It may mean **Jibril** specifically — the particular mentioned alongside the general.
- It may mean the **souls of the children of Adam**, since at death they are carried up through the heavens, as the long hadith of Al-Bara' describes.

**The fifty thousand years.** Ibn Kathir is unusually firm here: this is the **Day of Judgement**. Ibn 'Abbas said so in a report Ibn Abi Hatim recorded with what Ibn Kathir calls an *authentic chain*; the same came from **'Ikrimah** (via Ath-Thawri ← Simak bin Harb), **Ad-Dahhak**, and **Ibn Zayd**.

And a crucial qualification from **'Ali bin Abi Talhah ← Ibn 'Abbas**: it is the Day that Allah has made *fifty thousand years long **for the disbelievers***. The length is not neutral. It is part of the punishment.

### The two hadiths of Abu Hurayrah

Ibn Kathir brings these in specifically because both end on that same phrase — *a day whose measure is fifty thousand years*.

**(i) The rich man of Bani 'Amir.** Abu Hurayrah has a wealthy man brought back to him and warns him: *Beware of the hooves of the camels and the cloven hooves of the cattle.* He repeats it until the man's colour changes. Then he narrates:

> Whoever has camels and does not give their due in their **Najdah** and their **Risl** — *(the Companions asked what these were; the Prophet ﷺ answered: "their difficulty and their ease")* — they will come on the Day of Resurrection healthier, more numerous, fatter and more unruly than they ever were. A soft, level plain will be spread out, and they will trample him. When the last has passed over him, **the first returns to begin again** — for a day whose measure is fifty thousand years, until the case of all people is decided. Then he will see his path: to Paradise, or to the Fire.

The same is then repeated for **cattle** and for **sheep** — every one with a cloven hoof trampling, every one with a horn goring, and *not one of them hornless or bent-horned*.

The man then asks what the "due" of camels actually is. Abu Hurayrah's answer is a small portrait of a generous economy: **give from your most valuable camels; lend a milking she-camel; lend your mount for riding; give the milk for people to drink; and lend the male camel for breeding.** (Also recorded by Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i.)

**(ii) The hoarder of treasure.** No owner of hoarded wealth who withholds its due escapes it being beaten into **heated metal plates** and branded onto him in the Fire — *his forehead, his side, his back* — until Allah judges between His servants on a day whose measure is fifty thousand years. Then he sees his path. In Ahmad's version the Prophet ﷺ adds the famous line:

> **The horse is for three:** for one man a reward, for another a shield, and upon another a burden.

Ibn Kathir is explicit that he cites all of this for one reason: the recurring closing phrase.

## 3. "So Be Patient, With a Good Patience" (70:5–7)

*Sabran jamila.* The address is to the Prophet ﷺ: be patient with your people's rejection and with their demand to hasten the punishment **because they think it will never come**.

> *Those who believe not therein seek to hasten it, while those who believe are fearful of it, and know that it is the very truth.* (42:18)

Then the two verses that hold the whole surah's psychology:

> *Verily, they see it afar off. But We see it near.*

Ibn Kathir's reading of **Ba'id** here is sharper than "distant" — he says it means **that which is impossible to occur**. Their "far off" is not a date; it is a denial dressed as a date.

And "near" is defined equally precisely: the believers hold its arrival to be near *even though no one knows its timing but Allah* — because **everything that is approaching is near**, and it will certainly happen.

## 4. A Day Nobody Asks After Anybody (70:8–14)

- **The sky like Al-Muhl.** Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, 'Ata, Sa'id bin Jubayr, 'Ikrimah, As-Suddi and others: *like the residue of oil* — the dregs, molten.
- **The mountains like 'Ihn.** Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi: *fluffed wool*. Compare 101:5 — *like carded wool*.

> *And no friend will ask a friend, though they shall be made to see one another.*

Ibn Kathir's comment is quietly devastating: **no close friend will ask after his close friend though he sees him in the worst of states — because he is preoccupied with himself and cannot think of anyone else.**

**Al-'Awfi ← Ibn 'Abbas** adds the sequence: at first they *will* recognise each other, and then they will flee from each other. Ibn Kathir stacks the parallel verses:

> *Every man that Day will have enough to make him careless of others.* (80:37)
> *…fear a Day when no father can avail aught for his son, nor a son for his father.* (31:33)
> *And if one heavily laden calls another to bear his load, nothing of it will be lifted — even though he be near of kin.* (35:18)
> *Then, when the Trumpet is blown, there will be no kinship among them that Day, nor will they ask of one another.* (23:101)
> *That Day a man shall flee from his brother, and from his mother and his father, and from his wife and his children.* (80:34–37)

### The ransom that is refused

The criminal would buy himself out with **his children, his wife, his brother, his Fasilah who sheltered him, and everyone on earth.**

**Fasilah:**
- **Mujahid and As-Suddi:** his tribe and his kin.
- **'Ikrimah:** the subdivision of his tribe that he belongs to.
- **Ashhab ← Malik:** *his mother.*

Ibn Kathir's expansion is worth reading slowly: no ransom would be accepted **even if he brought every person on earth, even enough gold to fill the earth — even the child who was dearer to him than the last beat of his own heart.** He would hand the child over, and the child would not be taken.

Then: **Kalla.** *By no means.*

## 5. The Fire That Knows Their Names (70:15–18)

> *Verily, it will be Laza — Nazza'ah lish-shawa!*

On **Ash-Shawa**, the commentators divide by anatomy:
- **Ibn 'Abbas and Mujahid:** the skin of the head.
- **Al-Hasan Al-Basri and Thabit Al-Bunani:** the noble parts of the face.
- **Qatadah:** the vital organs, the face, the limbs — the whole make of him.
- **Ad-Dahhak:** it scrapes the flesh and skin from the bone until nothing is left on it.
- **Ibn Zayd:** the marrow of the bones — and *Nazza'ah* is the cutting of the bones and the transformation of their skins and their form.

Then the most striking image in the surah. The Fire **calls out**. Ibn Kathir: it calls to its own children, whom Allah created for it, knowing they would do the deeds that earn it — and it will call them on the Day of Judgement **with an eloquent, articulate tongue**, and pick them out of the gathered crowds **the way birds pick out seeds.**

Who does it call? *Those who turned their backs and turned away, and collected and hoarded.*

- **Turned back and turned away** — Ibn Kathir splits this into the two halves of a person: they **denied with their hearts** and **abandoned the deeds with their limbs**.
- **Collected and hoarded** — he piled wealth up and concealed it, **refusing the right of Allah due on it**: spending, and Zakah.

And a one-line hadith that lands like a verdict on the whole passage:

> **Do not hold back, or Allah will hold back from you.**

## 6. The Diagnosis: Man Was Created Halu' (70:19–21)

> *Verily, man was created very impatient.*

Ibn Kathir points out that Allah **immediately defines His own term** in the next two verses:

- **Jazu' — apprehensive when evil touches him.** Any harm makes him frightened, anxious, completely undone by the severity of his own panic — *and despairing that any good will ever come to him after it.*
- **Manu' — suppressive when good touches him.** Given a blessing, he is stingy with it, will not let others share in it, and withholds Allah's right in it.

Note the symmetry with the opening: the man demanding the punishment early is the same man. This is *halu'* — the inability to sit inside time without either despairing of it or grabbing at it.

Ahmad records from Abu Hurayrah:

> **The worst thing that can be in a man is greedy impatience and unrestrained cowardice.** *(Shuhhun hali' wa jubnun khali')*

## 7. The Exception: Twelve Verses on Who Escapes This (70:22–35)

> *Except those who are devoted to Salah.*

Ibn Kathir's framing: man is described by these blameworthy traits **except the one whom Allah protects, helps, and guides to good, making its means easy for him** — and those are the people of prayer.

**1. "Those who with their Salah are Da'imun" (70:23).** Three readings, all accepted:
- **Guarding its times and its obligatory elements** — Ibn Mas'ud, Masruq, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i.
- **Stillness and humble concentration** — 'Uqbah bin 'Amir, matching *Those who with their Salah are Khashi'un* (23:2).
- **Constancy in the deed** — as in 'A'ishah's hadith: *The most beloved deeds to Allah are the most consistent, even if they are few.*

Ibn Kathir adds a beautiful piece of linguistic reasoning here. The same root describes **Al-Ma' Ad-Da'im — standing water**. So *Da'im* implies **stillness**, and this proves the obligation of *tuma'ninah* in prayer: whoever has no stillness in his bowing and prostration **is not Da'im in his prayer** — because he does not settle, he does not remain, **he pecks at it like a crow.** And so he has not succeeded in it.

**2. A known right in their wealth — for the asker and the deprived (70:24–25):** a *determined portion* set aside for those in need.

**3. They believe in the Day of Recompense (70:26):** certain of the return, the reckoning, and the recompense — and therefore they act as those who *hope for reward and fear punishment*.

**4. They fear their Lord's torment (70:27–28):** *none can feel secure from it.* Ibn Kathir: no one who understands what Allah has commanded is safe from it — **except by Allah's own granting of safety.**

**5. They guard their chastity (70:29–31):** keeping it from what is forbidden, within what Allah has permitted. (He notes the full discussion was already given at the start of Al-Mu'minun, and declines to repeat it.)

**6. They keep their trusts and covenants (70:32):** given a trust, they do not betray it; making a covenant, they do not break it. These are **the mirror image of the hypocrite's traits**:

> The signs of the hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when he is trusted he betrays.
> *(Other narration: when he speaks he lies, when he makes a covenant he breaks it, and when he argues he is abusive.)*

**7. They stand firm in their testimonies (70:33):** they neither add to nor subtract from what they witnessed, **nor do they conceal it** — *Whoever hides it, surely his heart is sinful* (2:283).

**8. They guard their prayers (70:34):** its times, its pillars, its obligations, its recommended acts.

**Ibn Kathir's structural observation:** Allah **opens this list with prayer and closes it with prayer** — proof of its rank. And the ending is deliberately the ending of Al-Mu'minun:

> *These are indeed the inheritors, who shall inherit Firdaws, dwelling therein forever.* (23:10–11)
> *Such shall dwell in the Gardens, honoured.* (70:35)

## 8. Back to the Mockers: Why Are You Sitting in Groups? (70:36–44)

> *So what is the matter with those disbelievers, before you Muhti'in?*

**Muhti'in:** Al-Hasan Al-Basri — *departing*, hurrying away from him. Al-'Awfi ← Ibn 'Abbas — *looking in your direction*.

**'Izin:** the singular is *'Izah*, meaning **separated**. Ibn 'Abbas: groups among the people, **on the right and the left, turning away from him ﷺ and mocking him.** Ibn Kathir sets it beside:

> *Then what is wrong with them that they run away from admonition? As if they were frightened wild donkeys fleeing from a beast of prey.* (74:49–51)

And then a hadith that gives the word a second, quieter life: **Jabir bin Samurah** narrates that the Prophet ﷺ came out to them while they sat in scattered circles and said, **"Why do I see you all 'Izin?"** *(Ahmad, Muslim, Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Jarir.)*

**Do they hope to enter the Garden of Delight?** They flee from the Messenger in aversion to the truth — and expect Paradise anyway. *Kalla.* Rather, Hell is their abode.

**The proof from origin (70:39):** *We have created them out of that which they know* — from despised fluid. Compare *Did We not create you from a despised water?* (77:20) and 86:5–10: *created from a water gushing forth, proceeding from between the backbone and the ribs… Verily He is able to bring him back.*

**The oath (70:40–41):** *I swear by the Lord of the easts and the wests that surely We are Able.* Ibn Kathir notes the oath **opens with a negation** — Allah is swearing by *a denial of their claim*, refuting the rejection of the Day of Judgement. And the evidence He swears by is the one they already accept:

> *The creation of the heavens and the earth is indeed greater than the creation of mankind; yet most of mankind know not.* (40:57)
> *Is not He Who created the heavens and the earth able to create the like of them? Yes indeed! …His command, when He intends a thing, is only that He says to it, "Be!" — and it is.* (36:81–82)

**"To replace them by better than them":** Ibn Kathir's preferred reading is that on the Day of Judgement He will bring them back **in better bodies than the ones they now have** (cf. 75:3–4; 56:60–61). Ibn Jarir preferred *a nation who will obey Us and not disobey Us*, citing 47:38. Ibn Kathir judges the first more obvious, since the surrounding verses support it.

**"So leave them to plunge in vain talk and play"** — leave them in their denial and obstinacy until they meet their Day. They will come to know the outcome and taste it.

**The Day itself (70:43–44):** they pour out of the graves *racing to a Nusub*.
- The **majority reading is *Nasb*** — a verbal noun meaning *something erected*. Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak: **as if racing to a flag**. Abu 'Aliyah, Yahya bin Abi Kathir: **as if racing to a finish line**.
- **Al-Hasan Al-Basri read *Nusub*** — meaning **an idol**. On that reading, the Ayah is bitterly exact: their rush to the reckoning looks just like the way they used to rush at an idol when they caught sight of it, **each scrambling to be the first to touch it.**

And then the last line, the reversal of everything they had done: *eyes lowered, humiliation covering them* — Ibn Kathir: **in return for the arrogance with which they refused to obey in the world.**

> *That is the Day which they were promised.*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The man who demands the punishment early and the man who hoards his wealth are the same man.** Al-Ma'arij names his condition once — *halu'*, impatient — and then shows both of its faces: panic when something is taken, grasping when something is given. Neither can bear to be inside time and wait.

The cure is not stated as a virtue. It is stated as a **posture**: *Da'im* — the one who stays still. Standing water. The list of the saved opens with prayer and closes with prayer, and the definition given in between is stillness in the bowing, stillness in the prostration, and returning to it consistently even if it is little.

Everything else in the surah is contrast. On that Day the friend does not ask after his friend; the father offers his child and is refused; the Fire calls its own out of the crowd like a bird picking seeds; and the deniers who insisted the Hour was *far off* pour out of their graves **running** — as they once ran to be first to touch a stone.

They see it as far. He sees it as near. Everything approaching is near.
