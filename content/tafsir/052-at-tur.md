*Revealed in Makkah · 49 ayat · "The Mount" — named for the oath sworn at 52:1*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

At-Tur is a **surah of sworn certainty**. It opens with Allah swearing by five things — a mountain, a book, a parchment, a house in the seventh heaven, and a sea — and the thing sworn to is stark: *the torment of your Lord will surely come to pass; there is none that can avert it.* Everything after that is the unfolding of that oath.

The surah then divides into three long movements: **what happens to the deniers** (dragged, shoved, told to enter and see whether patience helps), **what happens to those with Taqwa** (thrones, purified wine, servants like pearls, families reunited and elevated), and **a sustained defence of the Prophet ﷺ** against the four accusations Makkah threw at him — soothsayer, madman, poet, forger — answered by a chain of some of the most piercing rhetorical questions in the Qur'an.

Two men frame the whole surah, and it is worth noticing: **Jubayr bin Mut'im** appears at the start, saying he never heard a more beautiful recitation, and again at 52:35, saying his heart nearly flew out of his chest — he was still an idolater at the time. And **Umar**, who heard the oath recited at night and fell ill for a month.

---

## 1. Its Virtues

Two narrations, both in the Sahihs:

- **Jubayr bin Mut'im** (via Malik): he heard the Prophet ﷺ reciting At-Tur in **Maghrib**, and said he had never heard a more beautiful voice or recitation.
- **Umm Salamah** (Al-Bukhari): she complained of illness, and the Prophet ﷺ told her, *"Perform Tawaf behind the crowd, while you are riding."* So she performed Tawaf while he prayed beside the House — **reciting At-Tur**.

---

## 2. The Five Oaths (52:1–6)

Allah swears **by His creation**, as testimony to His great ability, that His torment will befall His enemies and they will have no escape.

| The oath | Ibn Kathir's reading |
|---|---|
| **At-Tur** | A mount that **has trees** — like the mount where Allah spoke to Musa, and the mount where the prophethood of 'Isa began. A mount without trees is called *Jabal*, not *Tur*. |
| **The Book inscribed** | Either **Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz** (the Preserved Tablet), or the revealed, inscribed Books that people recite — |
| **In parchment unrolled** | — which is why this follows immediately. |
| **Al-Bayt Al-Ma'mur** | See below. |
| **The roof raised high** | The heaven. Reported from **Ali bin Abi Talib** (via Simak ← Khalid bin Ar'arah), who then recited *"We have made the heaven a roof, safe and well-guarded"* (21:32). Also Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Zayd; preferred by Ibn Jarir. |
| **The sea that is Masjur** | Two views (see below). |

### Al-Bayt Al-Ma'mur

From the Isra' hadith in the Two Sahihs, after the ascent to the seventh heaven:

> *Then I was taken to Al-Bayt Al-Ma'mur. It is visited every day by seventy thousand angels who will not come back to visit it again.*

Ibn Kathir builds out the geography: the angels **perform Tawaf around it** as the people of earth do around the Ka'bah — it is the Ka'bah of the residents of the seventh heaven. During the Isra', the Prophet ﷺ saw **Ibrahim reclining with his back against it** — and Ibrahim was the one who built the Ka'bah on earth, *and surely the reward is compatible with the action.*

And the structure keeps going: **every heaven has its own house of worship**, which is also the direction of prayer for its residents. The one in the lowest heaven is called **Bayt Al-'Izzah**.

### Masjur

- **The majority**: the seas will be **kindled with fire** on the Day of Resurrection — *"And when the seas become as blazing fire"* (81:6) — becoming a raging fire that surrounds people in the gathering place. Reported from Ali, Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Mujahid, and others.
- **Qatadah**: Masjur means the **filled** sea. Ibn Jarir preferred this, reasoning: *the sea is not lit with fire now, so it is filled.*

## 3. What the Oath Was For (52:7–8), and Umar's Month

> *Verily, the torment of your Lord will surely come to pass. There is none that can avert it.*

This is the subject of the vow. When Allah decides to send the torment, **nothing can prevent it or stop it**.

Ibn Abi Ad-Dunya records from Ja'far bin Zayd Al-'Abdi:

> One night in Al-Madinah, **Umar** went out checking on the welfare of the Muslims and passed a house where a man was standing in voluntary prayer. Umar stopped and listened. The man was reciting At-Tur — until he reached *"Verily, the torment of your Lord will surely come to pass. There is none that can avert it."*
>
> Umar said, **"By the Lord of the Ka'bah, this is a true vow."** He dismounted his donkey, sat next to a wall for a while, then went home — **and fell ill for a month.** People visited him throughout, not knowing what had caused it.

---

## 4. The Day Itself (52:9–16)

**The heaven will Mawr** — Ibn Abbas and Qatadah: *shaking violently*; also from Ibn Abbas: *split*; Mujahid: *spin violently*. Ad-Dahhak: it will violently spin and move by Allah's command, its areas driving into each other. Ibn Jarir preferred this, because the word *Mawra* carries both spinning and shaking.

**The mountains will Sayr** — fade away and become scattered particles of dust blown by the wind.

Then the woe, and the reason for it: **they lived in falsehood, playing** — treating the religion as the subject of mockery and jest.

**The pushing** — *Yuda''un*: violently driven and shoved. Mujahid, Ash-Sha'bi, Muhammad bin Ka'b, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi and Ath-Thawri all read it the same way: *they will be violently shoved into the Fire.*

And then the angels of punishment speak, admonishing and chastising:

> *This is the Fire which you used to deny. Is this magic, or do you not see? Enter therein — and whether you are patient of it or impatient of it, it is all the same.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss on that last clause is bleak and exact: **whether you endure its torment or not, you will never avert it or be saved from it.** And the closing line removes any complaint — *you are only being requited for what you used to do* — because Allah is never unjust with anyone.

---

## 5. The Destination of the Happy Ones (52:17–20)

Placed deliberately **in contrast** to the passage before it.

- *Enjoying what their Lord has bestowed* — foods, drinks, clothes, dwellings, mounts, and more.
- *And their Lord saved them from the torment of the blazing Fire* — Ibn Kathir treats **the rescue itself as a bounty**, and then adds the second: they were entered into Paradise, which has delights **no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no heart has ever imagined**.
- *"Eat and drink with happiness because of what you used to do"* — paired with 69:24, *"Eat and drink at ease for that which you have sent on before you in days past."* This is the just reward for deeds — **and still, all of it is a favour from Allah.**
- *Thrones Masfufah* — Ibn Abbas (via Mujahid ← Husayn ← Ath-Thawri): *thrones in howdahs*. *Masfufah* means they will be **facing one another**, as in 37:44.

---

## 6. The Families Reunited (52:21) — Grace Running Both Ways

> *And those who believe and whose offspring follow them in faith — to them shall We join their offspring, and We shall not decrease the reward of their deeds in anything.*

This is the emotional centre of the surah, and Ibn Kathir treats it as an affirmation of Allah's favour, generosity and compassion. The mechanism:

When the **offspring imitate their parents in faith**, Allah **elevates the children to the parents' rank** — even though the children did not perform deeds as good as their parents' — so that **the eyes of the parents are comforted by seeing them there.** And the parents lose nothing: no reward or grade is deducted from those higher in rank for the joining.

**Ibn Abbas** (via Ath-Thawri) said exactly this, then recited the Ayah. In another report he specifies: *they are the offspring of the believers who died on the faith.*

### Khadijah's question

Abdullah, son of Imam Ahmad, records from Ali that **Khadijah** asked the Prophet ﷺ about two of her children who died during **Jahiliyyah**. He said, *"They are both in the Fire."* Seeing the sadness on her face, he said:

> *If you saw their dwelling place, you would hate them.*

She asked about her children with him. He said, *"They are in Paradise."* Then:

> *Verily, the believers and their offspring will dwell in Paradise, while the idolators and their offspring will dwell in the Hellfire.*

— and he recited this Ayah.

### And the grace runs upward too

Ibn Kathir immediately notes the reverse direction: Allah also grants favour to **parents on account of their children's invocations**. Imam Ahmad records from Abu Hurayrah:

> Allah shall elevate the grade of a righteous servant in Paradise, who will ask, *"O Lord! How did I earn this?"* Allah will reply: **"Through your son's invoking Me to forgive you."**

With its witness in Sahih Muslim:

> *When the son of Adam dies, his record of deeds ceases except in three cases: an ongoing charity, knowledge people are benefiting from, and a righteous son who invokes Allah for him.*

---

## 7. And Yet: Every Person Is a Pledge (52:21b)

Immediately after promising elevation by association, Allah closes the door on the abuse of it:

> *Every person is a pledge for that which he has earned.*

Ibn Kathir's placement is the point: **having mentioned the favour of raising children to their parents' ranks, He affirms His fairness in the same breath.** He does not punish anyone for the mistakes of others. **No sin committed by another is ever added to your load — not by parent, not by child.** Compare 74:38–41.

So the traffic between generations moves in one direction only: **mercy travels, guilt does not.**

---

## 8. The Wine That Doesn't Cost Anything (52:22–24)

*Fruit and meat such as they desire* — whatever they wish for.

*A cup, free from any Laghw and free from Ta'thim* — wine, according to Ad-Dahhak. The distinctive thing Ibn Kathir emphasises is that the Paradise cup is defined by **what it has been purified of**:

- **No idle or vain speech, no dirty or sinful words** — unlike drunken people in this life. Ibn Abbas: *Laghw* is falsehood, *Ta'thim* is lying. Mujahid: *they do not curse each other nor sin.*
- **Qatadah**: those were the consequences of drinking in this life, and Shaytan helped in that. Allah purified the wine of the Hereafter of the ills of the wine of this world.
- **No headache, no stomachache, no intoxication** — *"Neither will they have Ghawl from that, nor will they suffer intoxication"* (37:46–47); *"neither aching of the head nor any intoxication"* (56:19).

*Boy-servants like preserved pearls* — beautiful, graceful, clean and neat (cf. 56:17–18).

---

## 9. The Conversation in Paradise (52:25–28)

They draw near to one another, questioning — talking and remembering their lives, **just as people in this world talk while drinking**. And what they talk about is fear:

> *"Aforetime, we were afraid in the midst of our families."*

Meaning: in the world, surrounded by our own people, **we were afraid of our Lord** and fearful of His punishment.

> *"So Allah has been gracious to us, and has saved us from the torment of the Fire. Verily, We used to invoke Him before."*

Ibn Kathir: *we used to invoke Him with submission and humility — and He accepted our invocation and gave us what we wished.* The verse ends by naming Him **Al-Barr, Ar-Rahim** — the Most Subtle in kindness, the Most Merciful.

**Note the structure:** the fear they describe is the fear they had *while living an ordinary family life*. That is what got them there.

---

## 10. The Four Accusations, Answered (52:29–34)

Allah commands His Messenger to remind — then refutes, one by one, what Makkah said about him.

- **"Kahin"** — a soothsayer who receives information from the Jinn, who eavesdrop on news from heaven. *By the grace of Allah, you are not.*
- **"Majnun"** — possessed by Shaytan with insanity. Not that either.
- **"A poet — we await for him some calamity by time!"** They meant: *we'll wait for death to take him and rid us of his Message.* Ibn Abbas (via Mujahid ← Ibn Abi Najih ← Muhammad bin Ishaq) attaches this to **Dar An-Nadwah**, the Quraysh council: one of them proposed, *"Jail him in chains. Then wait — a calamity will strike him; he will die as the poets before him died, like Zuhayr and An-Nabighah."* This Ayah was the answer.
  - And the reply given to them: *"Wait! I am with you among those who wait!"* — **wait, and you will find out to whom the good end and the triumph belong, in this life and the next.**
- **"He has forged it"** — Nay, they simply do not believe; it is their disbelief that drives the accusation. Then the challenge: *let them produce a recitation like it.* Ibn Kathir: even if they combined their strength with all the people of the earth, **Jinn and mankind alike**, they would never produce the like of this Qur'an — nor ten Surahs, nor even one.

---

## 11. The Chain of Questions (52:35–43)

This is where the surah becomes relentless — **eleven questions in a row**, each closing off an escape route, and together an argument for Tawhid of Lordship and Divinity.

> *Were they created by nothing? Or were they themselves the creators?*

Neither is true. **Allah created them and brought them into existence after they were nothing.**

### Jubayr bin Mut'im's heart

Al-Bukhari records that Jubayr bin Mut'im said: *"I heard the Prophet ﷺ recite Surah At-Tur in Maghrib, and when he reached this Ayah —* [52:35–37] *— I felt my heart would fly away."*

Ibn Kathir supplies the context that makes it land: **Jubayr had come to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ after Badr, to ransom captured idolaters. He was still an idolater himself.** Hearing this Ayah was one of the causes of his later embracing Islam.

### The rest of the chain

- *Did they create the heavens and the earth?* — They **knew** Allah alone was the Creator without partners. They fell into idolatry anyway, **because they had no firm belief.**
- *Are the treasures of your Lord with them?* Do they hold the keys? *Or are they the tyrants* who would hold creation to account? Never — Allah alone is King and Owner, and He does what He wills.
- *Have they a stairway by which they listen?* — to the place of the angels. *Then let their listener produce some manifest proof.* They never will; **they have nothing.**
- *Has He only daughters and you have sons?* — Ibn Kathir sharpens the hypocrisy: the pagans **chose male offspring for themselves**, and the face of one of them **would darken with suppressed anger** at news of a daughter — yet they assigned the angels to Allah as daughters and worshipped them besides Him.
- *Do you ask them a wage, so they are burdened with debt?* — No. And note the psychology: a person burdened this way **complains of the least bothersome thing.**
- *Is the Unseen with them, and they write it down?* — **None in the heavens or the earth knows the Unseen except Allah.**
- *Do they intend a plot?* — **Then they are the ones plotted against.** Their plots will only harm themselves.
- *Have they a god other than Allah?* — a harsh refutation of worshipping idols and calling on rivals, closed by Allah glorifying His own Most Honourable Self above what they ascribe to Him.

---

## 12. Even a Falling Sky (52:44–47)

> *And if they were to see a piece of the heaven falling down, they would say: "Clouds gathered in heaps!"*

Ibn Kathir: even with the punishment visibly descending on them, **they would not believe it was coming their way** — they would call it layered cloud. The parallel is 15:14–15: *even if We opened a gate of heaven and they kept ascending through it, they would say, "Our eyes have been dazzled; rather, we are a people bewitched."*

So: *leave them alone until they meet their Day* — the Day of Resurrection, in which they will sink into a fainting, and in which **their plotting will not avail them at all.**

And before that Day, another punishment: **the torment of this world.** Compare 32:21 — *We will make them taste of the near torment prior to the supreme torment, that they may return.* Ibn Kathir's reading of why *most of them know not*: they are tormented and tested with hardships **so they might repent** — but they do not understand what is happening or why, because **when the torment is lifted, they revert to the worst of what they were doing before.**

---

## 13. The Closing Command: Patience, and Tasbih (52:48–49)

> *So wait patiently for the decision of your Lord, for verily, you are under Our Eyes.*

Ibn Kathir: **be patient with their annoyance and do not be concerned about it — you are under Our Eyes and Our Protection, and We will surely protect you from the people.**

Then: *and glorify the praises of your Lord when you get up* (*hina taqum*) — and here Ibn Kathir preserves **four readings, all of which he supports with practice**, so the Ayah ends up covering the whole day:

1. **When you stand for Salah** — Ad-Dahhak. The opening supplication: *"Glorious are You, O Allah, and with Your praise; blessed is Your Name, exalted is Your majesty, and there is no god except You."* Muslim records that **Umar** recited this when beginning Salah; Ahmad and the Sunan compilers record it from Abu Sa'id and others as the Prophet's ﷺ own practice.
2. **When you rise from sleep** — Abu Al-Jawza': *from your sleep, from your bed.* **Chosen by Ibn Jarir**, supported by the hadith of Ubadah bin As-Samit: whoever wakes at night and says *La ilaha illallah, alone without partner; His is the dominion and His is the praise, and He has power over all things; Subhan Allah, wal-hamdu lillah, wa la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar, wa la hawla wa la quwwata illa billah* — then says *"O Lord, forgive me,"* or supplicates — **he will be answered; and if he then performs wudu and prays, his prayer will be accepted.**
3. **From every gathering you sit in** — Mujahid (via Ibn Abi Najih).
4. **When you rise to leave a gathering** — Abu Al-Ahwas. With the *kaffarat al-majlis* hadith of Abu Hurayrah (At-Tirmidhi: *Hasan Sahih*; also An-Nasa'i and Al-Hakim): whoever talks excessively and idly in a gathering but says before standing — *"Glory be to You, O Allah, and with Your praise; I testify there is no god except You; I seek Your forgiveness and I repent to You"* — **Allah will forgive him what was said in that gathering.**

**And in the night, glorify His praises** — remembrance, Qur'an and **night prayer**; compare 17:79, *offer Tahajjud as an additional prayer for you; it may be that your Lord will raise you to Maqam Mahmud.*

**And at the setting of the stars** — the **two rak'ahs before Fajr**, per a hadith from Ibn Abbas; a established Sunnah performed before the stars are about to set. A'ishah, in the Two Sahihs: *the Prophet ﷺ was never more regular and particular about any voluntary prayer than the two rak'ahs of Fajr.* And in Muslim:

> *Two Rak'ahs before Fajr are better than this life and all that is in it.*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**At-Tur is a sworn oath and then its evidence.** Allah swears by a mountain, a book, a parchment, a house circled by seventy thousand angels who never return, and a sea that will one day burn — and what He swears to is that the torment is coming and nobody can turn it aside. Umar heard that line at night and it put him in bed for a month.

**The surah's cruellest verse is the one about patience.** *Enter it — whether you are patient of it or impatient of it, it is all the same.* In this world, patience is a strategy. There, it buys nothing. The place to be patient is here, which is exactly what the Prophet ﷺ is told to do at the end: *wait patiently for the decision of your Lord — you are under Our Eyes.*

**Three things worth carrying:**

- **Mercy travels between generations; guilt does not.** Children are raised to their parents' rank without the parents losing a thing — and parents are raised by their children's *istighfar* after death. But *every person is a pledge for what he has earned*: no one carries anyone else's sin.
- **The people of Paradise describe themselves in one sentence** — *"Aforetime, we were afraid in the midst of our families."* Not monks, not warriors. People living ordinary family lives who were quietly afraid of their Lord.
- **Deniers are not short of evidence.** They would watch the sky fall on them and call it weather. Ibn Kathir's diagnosis at 52:36 is the whole surah in miniature: **they knew Allah alone was the Creator — they had no firm belief.** The gap was never information.

And the small, strange grace-note at both ends of the surah: **Jubayr bin Mut'im**, an idolater standing there to ransom prisoners, hearing *"Or were they created by nothing?"* and feeling his heart nearly leave his body. He came for the captives and left with something else.
