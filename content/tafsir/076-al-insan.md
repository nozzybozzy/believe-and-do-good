*Revealed in Makkah · 31 ayat · "Man" / "Time" — recited by the Prophet ﷺ, together with As-Sajdah, in the Friday morning prayer*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Insan begins with a question that empties a person out — *was there not a period when he was not a thing worth mentioning?* — and ends by emptying him out again: *you cannot will, unless Allah wills.* Everything between those two poles is the story of what Allah does with someone who was nothing.

The movement is precise. **He creates him** from a mixed drop. **He equips him** with hearing and sight. **He shows him the road** and leaves the choosing to him — grateful or ungrateful. Then the surah splits: two verses of chains and fire for those who refuse, and eighteen verses of extraordinary, patient, physical detail for the **Abrar** — the righteous — describing silver cups thin enough to see through, fruit that lowers itself when you sit down, and a drink that cleans the envy out of a person's chest.

What earns all of it is deliberately unglamorous. Ibn Kathir's Abrar are not distinguished by miracles. They keep their vows, they fear a bad Day, and **they hand over food they themselves are hungry for** — to a poor man, an orphan, and a prisoner — while saying nothing to anyone about why. That is the whole résumé. Abu Sulayman Ad-Darani, hearing *because they were patient*, said it meant patient *in leaving off their desires in the world* — which is exactly what feeding someone else with food you want looks like.

The surah's name, *Ad-Dahr*, is Time; its opening is about the stretch of time before you existed. Its closing is about the Day you have put behind you. You are, on this surah's reckoning, a short interval between two absences — and what you do with the interval is the only thing at issue.

---

## 1. Its Place in the Sunnah

Recorded in Sahih Muslim from Ibn ʿAbbas: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to recite, in the **Morning (Fajr) prayer on Friday**, Surah As-Sajdah (*Alif Lam Mim. The revelation…*) and Surah Al-Insan (*Has there not been over man…*).

Ibn Kathir does not elaborate here, but the pairing is worth noticing: one surah on the revelation coming down, one on man being made, guided, and judged.

---

## 2. Man Before He Was Anything (76:1–3)

> *Has there not been over man a period of time, when he was not a thing worth mentioning?*

Ibn Kathir: Allah is informing us that He **brought man into existence after he was not even worth mentioning** — a statement about lowliness and weakness, not modesty.

**The drop, mixed.** *We created man from Nutfah Amshaj* — *Amshaj* meaning **mixed**; the words *Mashaj* and *Mashij* mean something combined together. Ibn ʿAbbas: it is *the fluid of the man and the fluid of the woman when they meet and mix.* Then man changes from stage to stage, condition to condition, colour to colour. ʿIkrimah, Mujahid, Al-Hasan and Ar-Rabiʿ bin Anas all said the same.

**The purpose.** *In order to try him* — meaning, *We test him*; the same purpose stated at 67:2, *that He may test you which of you is best in deed.*

**The equipment.** *So We made him hearer and seer* — Ibn Kathir reads this as instrumentation for the test: the faculties of hearing and sight were given **so that he would be able to use them for obedience and disobedience.** The tools are neutral. That is the point of giving them.

**The road, shown.** *Verily, We guided him to the way* — meaning, *We explained it to him, made it clear to him, and showed it to him.* Guidance here is disclosure, not compulsion; Ibn Kathir proves it with 41:17 — *as for Thamud, We guided them but they preferred blindness to guidance* — and 90:10, *We guided him to the two ways*, i.e. **the path of good and the path of evil, both explained.** This is the position of ʿIkrimah, ʿAtiyah, Ibn Zayd and (in the well-known report) Mujahid, and of the majority.

**And then the fork.** *Whether he be grateful or ungrateful* — with this, Ibn Kathir says, he is either wretched or happy. He closes the point with a hadith from Abu Malik Al-Ashʿari (Muslim):

> **"All of mankind wakes up in the morning the merchant of his own soul. So he either imprisons it or sets it free."**

---

## 3. What Waits for Those Who Refuse (76:4)

> *Verily, We have prepared for the disbelievers iron chains, iron collars, and Saʿir.*

**Saʿir** is the flame and the fire of Hell. Ibn Kathir sets the verse beside 40:71–72 — *when iron collars will be rounded over their necks, and the chains, they shall be dragged along, in the boiling water, then they will be burned in the Fire.*

Two lines, and the surah moves on. The imbalance is deliberate: this surah is not built to frighten. It is built to describe what is on offer.

---

## 4. The Cup of Kafur, and the Spring They Command (76:5–6)

> *Verily, the Abrar shall drink of a cup mixed with Kafur.*

**Kafur** is camphor, and Ibn Kathir treats it as a known thing: **cooling, finely fragrant** — and in Paradise, delicious as well. Al-Hasan: *the coolness of the camphor will be in the pleasantness of ginger.*

Then the verse escalates. What the righteous get *mixed* into their cup, a nearer group drinks **neat**:

> *A spring wherefrom the servants of Allah will drink, causing it to gush forth abundantly.*

Kafur turns out to be a spring, drunk **directly and undiluted** by Allah's close servants, and drunk to the full — Ibn Kathir notes that *Yashrabu* here carries the sense of *Yarwa*, to have one's thirst completely quenched.

**And they control it.** *Tafjir* means to cause to gush forth or flow out (as at 17:90 and 18:33). Mujahid: *they will divert it to wherever they wish* — into their castles, homes, sitting rooms and residences. ʿIkrimah, Qatadah and Ath-Thawri say the same. The reward is not merely served to them; **it obeys them.**

---

## 5. The Résumé of the Righteous (76:7–10)

This is the heart of the surah, and Ibn Kathir gives it more space than all the descriptions of Paradise combined.

### They fulfil vows, and they fear the Day

*They fulfil vows* — meaning they discharge what Allah **made obligatory** on them by the Law, *and* they honour the additional obligations they took on themselves. The governing hadith, from ʿA'ishah (Malik, and Al-Bukhari):

> **"Whoever makes a vow to obey Allah, then he should obey Him. And whoever makes a vow to disobey Allah, then he should not disobey Him."**

*And they fear a Day whose evil will be wide-spreading* — Ibn ʿAbbas: *spreading.* Qatadah: *by Allah, the evil of that Day will spread until it fills the heavens and the earth.* And it spreads over everyone **except those upon whom Allah has had mercy.** The fear is what makes them abandon what He forbade.

### They give away food they want

> *And they give food, in spite of their love for it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive.*

A grammatical question with a moral payoff: does *their love for it* mean love of **Allah** or love of **the food**? Ibn Kathir reports the first view, then rejects it — the apparent meaning is the food: **they give it while they themselves love it and desire it.** So Mujahid and Muqatil, and this was Ibn Jarir's preference.

He stacks the parallels: *and gives his wealth, in spite of love for it* (2:177); *you shall not attain Al-Birr unless you spend of that which you love* (3:92); and from the Sahih:

> **"The best charity is that which you give while you are healthy, covetous, hoping for wealth and fearing poverty."**

That is, *in the condition of your love for wealth, your eagerness for it, and your need for it.* Charity that costs nothing is not what is being praised here.

**Who is the captive?** Ibn Kathir preserves the disagreement:
- **Saʿid bin Jubayr, Al-Hasan, Ad-Dahhak**: the captive from among the people of the Qiblah — i.e. Muslims.
- **Ibn ʿAbbas**: at the time the Ayah was revealed, the Muslims' captives were **idolaters** — and the proof is Badr, where the Messenger ﷺ commanded his Companions to treat the captives respectfully, and *they would give them preference over themselves when eating their meals.*
- **ʿIkrimah**: they are the slaves. Mujahid: the prisoner.

**Ibn Jarir preferred the general reading** — the Ayah covers Muslim and idolater alike. And Ibn Kathir adds the weight of the Prophet's ﷺ repeated instruction on servants, noting that his final counsel before death was:

> **"The prayer, and what your right hands possess."**

### They say nothing about it

> *"We feed you seeking Allah's Face only. We wish for no reward, nor thanks from you."*

Meaning: hoping for Allah's reward and pleasure — *we do not seek any reward from you in return, nor are we seeking for you to thank us in front of the people.* Mujahid and Saʿid bin Jubayr:

> **"By Allah! They do not say this with their tongues — but Allah knows it in their hearts and He commends them for it. Every seeker should seek after this."**

The speech in the verse is a speech never actually made aloud. That is why it counts.

### What they are afraid of

> *Verily, We fear from our Lord a Day that is ʿAbus and Qamtarir.*

- **Ibn ʿAbbas** (via ʿAli bin Abi Talhah): *ʿAbus* means difficult, *Qamtarir* means long.
- **Ibn ʿAbbas** (via ʿIkrimah): a Day that will make faces look horrible from extreme dislike of it — *the disbeliever will frown on that day until sweat flows between his eyes like tar.*
- **Mujahid**: *ʿAbus* is the frowning of the two lips; *Qamtarir* is drawing the face up into a scowl.
- **Saʿid bin Jubayr and Qatadah**: faces made to frown from dismay; *Qamtarir* is the contraction of the forehead and the space between the eyes.
- **Ibn Zayd**: *ʿAbus* is the evil, *Qamtarir* is the severity.

## 6. The Face and the Heart (76:11–12)

> *So Allah saved them from the evil of that Day, and gave them Nadrah and joy.*

**Saved** — He protects them from the very thing they feared. **Nadrah** — a light of beauty, **in their faces**. **Joy** — **in their hearts**. So Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Qatadah, Abu ʿAliyah and Ar-Rabiʿ bin Anas.

Ibn Kathir's explanation of the pairing is one of the loveliest lines in the surah: **if the heart is happy, then the face is enlightened.** He supports it not with a proof-text but with two memories of the Prophet ﷺ — Kaʿb bin Malik's report that when he was happy *his face would be radiant until it was as if it were a piece of the moon*, and ʿA'ishah's: *"The Messenger of Allah entered into my home happy, and his facial expression was glowing."*

> *And their recompense, because they were patient, shall be Paradise and silken garments.*

A spacious home, a delightful life, fine clothing. And the gloss Ibn Kathir chose to record, from Ibn ʿAsakir — Surah Ad-Dahr was recited to **Abu Sulayman Ad-Darani**, and at this verse he said:

> **"Because they were patient in leaving off their desires in the world."**

---

## 7. The Garden, in Detail (76:13–22)

Ibn Kathir slows right down here. The details are not decorative — each one answers a discomfort of the body.

**Reclining, in one climate.** They recline on **Al-Ara'ik**, which he has already defined as couches beneath curtained canopies. *Neither the excessive heat nor the excessive cold* — no disturbing heat, no painful cold, **one climate, always, that they will never want changed.**

**Shade and fruit that come to you.** *The shade thereof is close upon them* — the branches are near. *And the bunches of fruit will hang low within their reach* — whenever he reaches for fruit, **it comes nearer and lowers itself from its highest branch as if it hears and obeys** (cf. 55:54, 69:23). Mujahid: *if he stands it rises with him; if he sits it lowers itself; if he lies down it lowers further still.* Qatadah: **no thorn and no distance will keep their hands from it.**

**Vessels of silver and Qawarir.** Servants circulate with silver containers of food and **cups without handles or spouts**. Then the paradox: *Qawarir — crystal-clear, made of silver.* Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan Al-Basri and others: **the whiteness of silver with the transparency of glass.** Ibn Kathir notes that *Qawarir* normally means glass — so these are silver vessels so finely made that what is inside shows through. **There is nothing like it in this world.**

**Measured exactly.** *They will determine the measure thereof* — filled to precisely the amount that quenches the drinker's thirst, **not more and not less**, prepared to the individual. So Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Saʿid bin Jubayr, Abu Salih, Qatadah, Ibn Abza, ʿAbdullah bin ʿUbayd bin ʿUmayr, Ash-Shaʿbi and Ibn Zayd. Ibn Kathir calls this *the most excellent provision, distinction and honour* — the honour is in the precision.

**Ginger, and Salsabil.** A cup mixed with **Zanjabil**. Ibn Kathir sets the two drinks against each other: **camphor is cool, ginger is hot** — so on one occasion they are given the cold drink and on another the warm one, *so that their affair will be balanced*. But **those nearest to Allah drink from all of it however they wish** (Qatadah). The spring is called **Salsabil** — ʿIkrimah: the name of a spring in Paradise; Mujahid: named for **its continuous flowing and the force of its current.**

**Boys like scattered pearls.** *Boys of everlasting youth* — meaning **in one state forever, never changing, never ageing**. *If you see them, you would think them scattered pearls*: when you see them dispersing to serve, their number, their beautiful faces, their fine clothing and ornaments — **there is nothing nicer to look at than scattered pearls in a beautiful place.**

**A delight, and a great dominion.** *And when you look there* — addressed to Muhammad ﷺ — *you will see a delight, and a great dominion*: a vast kingdom belonging to Allah, dazzling and splendid. Ibn Kathir measures it by the bottom rung: it is confirmed in the Sahih that Allah will say to **the last man taken out of the Fire and the last to enter Paradise**, *"you will have similar to the world, and ten worlds like it."* If that is the least of it, consider the rest.

**Green silk, silver bracelets, and a drink that cleans you out.** Garments of **Sundus** (fine silk, worn as shirts and undergarments) and **Istabraq** (the glittering, shimmering silk worn as outer clothes). Bracelets of **silver** — and Ibn Kathir marks the distinction: this is the description of the righteous; those *near* to Allah are described at 22:23 with **bracelets of gold and pearls**.

Then, after all the outward adornment, the inward one:

> *And their Lord will give them a purifying drink.*

Ibn Kathir: **it will purify their insides** of envy, spite, hatred, harm and every other reprehensible trait. He records from ʿAli bin Abi Talib:

> "When the people of Paradise come to the Gate of Paradise, they will find two springs there. It will be as if they were inspired with what to do: they will drink from one, and Allah will remove whatever harmfulness there may be within them. Then they will bathe in the other, and a glow of delight will run all over them."

**Outward condition and inner beauty, both.** That is the sequence the surah has been building.

**And the verdict.** *Verily, this is a reward for you, and your endeavour has been accepted* — said to them **in honour of them** (cf. 69:24, *eat and drink at ease for what you sent forth in days past*; 7:43, *this is the Paradise you have inherited for what you used to do*). Ibn Kathir's gloss on the last clause: **Allah will reward you for a small amount with a large amount.**

## 8. To the Prophet ﷺ: Be Patient, and Do Not Obey Them (76:23–26)

> *Verily, it is We Who have sent down the Qur'an to you by stages.*

A reminder of the blessing before the instruction. Then:

*Therefore be patient with constancy to the command of your Lord* — **just as you have been honoured by what was revealed to you, be patient with His decree and decision, and know that He will handle your affairs well.**

*And obey neither a sinner nor a disbeliever among them* — do not obey the disbelievers and hypocrites if they wish to deter you from what has been revealed; **convey it, and put your trust in Allah, for Allah will protect you from the people.** Ibn Kathir separates the two words carefully:

- **Athim** — the sinner **in his deeds**.
- **Kafur** — the disbeliever **in his heart**.

Then the positive command, which is the antidote to both:

*Remember the Name of your Lord every morning and afternoon* — at the beginning of the day and at its end. *And during the night, prostrate yourself to Him, and glorify Him a long night through* — the same instruction as 17:79 (*offer the Salah with it as an additional prayer for you; it may be that your Lord will raise you to Maqam Mahmud*) and 73:1–4 (*stand all night, except a little… and recite the Qur'an with Tartil*).

---

## 9. The People Who Love the Hasty Life (76:27–28)

> *Verily, these love the present life of this world, and put behind them a heavy Day.*

Ibn Kathir: this is a reprimand to the disbelievers **and those similar to them** who love the world, are devoted and dedicated to it, and **put the abode of the Hereafter behind them in disregard.** The heavy Day is the Day of Judgement.

> *It is We Who created them, and We have made them of strong build.*

Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid and others: this means **their creation** — the very strength they are relying on is on loan.

> *And when We will, We can replace them with others like them with a complete replacement.*

Two readings, both given: **resurrection** — when We wish, We will raise them on the Day of Judgement and repeat their creation in a new form (the first creation being the proof of the second) — and, per Ibn Zayd and Ibn Jarir, **substitution**: *if We wish We can bring another group of people in their place.* Compare 4:133, *if He wills, He can take you away, O people, and bring others*; and 14:19–20, *He can remove you and bring a new creation, and that is not hard for Allah.*

---

## 10. The Reminder, and the Will Behind the Will (76:29–31)

> *Verily, this is an admonition, so whosoever wills, let him take a path to his Lord.*

A **reminder** — and a **path, a way** — for whoever wishes to be guided by the Qur'an. Ibn Kathir attaches 4:39: *what loss would they have if they had believed in Allah and the Last Day?*

Then the verse that closes the door the previous one opened:

> *But you cannot will, unless Allah wills.*

**No one is able to guide himself, enter into faith, or bring about any benefit for himself** — unless Allah wills. And the divine names at the end are the explanation: *Verily, Allah is Ever All-Knowing, All-Wise.* He is **most knowledgeable of who deserves to be guided**, so He makes guidance easy for him and predestines the causes of it; and whoever deserves misguidance, He averts guidance from him. **Unto Him belongs the most excellent wisdom and the most irrefutable argument.**

> *He will admit to His mercy whom He wills, and as for the wrongdoers — He has prepared a painful torment.*

Ibn Kathir's final sentence on the surah:

> **Whoever He guides, there is no one who can lead him astray; and whoever He leads astray, there is no one who can guide him.**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**You were not a thing worth mentioning.** The surah opens by removing any basis for self-importance and closes by removing any basis for self-sufficiency — *you cannot will unless Allah wills.* Between the two, everything you have (hearing, sight, the knowledge of the road) is described as issued equipment, given so that it can be used one way or the other.

**Guidance is disclosure; the choosing is yours.** *We guided him to the two ways* means the path of good and the path of evil were both explained. Which is why the daily verdict in Abu Malik's hadith is so blunt: **every morning a person trades in his own soul, and either imprisons it or sets it free.**

**The entrance requirement is smaller and harder than it looks.** The Abrar are not described by anything spectacular. They keep their vows, they fear a bad Day, and they give away food *while wanting it* — to a poor man, an orphan, and a captive who may not even share their religion — and they say the reason to no one but Allah. Ibn Kathir's supporting hadith is the sharpest test of it: **the best charity is the one you give while healthy, covetous, hoping for wealth and fearing poverty.**

**Patience means leaving off desires now.** Abu Sulayman Ad-Darani's reading of *because they were patient* is the hinge between the two halves of the surah: the fruit that lowers itself, the silver you can see through, the drink measured exactly to your thirst — all of it is the return on desires **declined** in a world where declining them cost something.

**And the last gift is internal.** After the silk, the bracelets and the crystal, the final thing the Lord gives them is *a purifying drink* — which Ibn Kathir says removes envy, spite and hatred from inside them. Even in Paradise, the crowning favour is a clean chest.
