*Both revealed in Makkah · 5 and 6 ayat · known together as **Al-Mu'awwidhatayn**, "the two surahs of seeking refuge" — the closing words of the Qur'an*

---

## 0. What These Two Surahs Are Doing

The Qur'an opens with a servant praising his Lord and asking to be guided. It closes with the same servant taking shelter. **Al-Fatihah is the plea; Al-Mu'awwidhatayn is the flight to safety.** Ibn Kathir treats the two final surahs as a single unit — the Prophet ﷺ almost never mentions one without the other — and their design is symmetrical and deliberate.

**Al-Falaq** names **one** attribute of Allah (*Lord of the daybreak*) and takes refuge from **four** evils, all of them external and mostly unseen: creation at large, the night, the witch, the envier.

**An-Nas** names **three** attributes of Allah (*Lord, King, God*) and takes refuge from **one** evil — the whisperer. The proportions are inverted on purpose. The whisper is one enemy, but he is the one already inside the walls, assigned to you personally, running in you like blood. He requires the whole weight of Allah's lordship, sovereignty and divinity to face down.

Notice too what the surahs do *not* do. They do not teach you to fight, argue, or fortify. They teach you to **say two words**: *I seek refuge*. The last instruction the Qur'an gives its reader is to admit he cannot protect himself.

---

## 1. Ibn Mas'ud's Position — and Why Ibn Kathir Opens With It

Imam Ahmad recorded, through Zirr bin Hubaysh, that Ubayy bin Ka'b said **Ibn Mas'ud did not record the Mu'awwidhatayn in his Mushaf**. Ubayy's answer was not an argument but a testimony:

> I testify that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ informed me that Jibril said to him, *"Qul a'udhu bi Rabbil-Falaq"* — so he said it. And Jibril said to him, *"Qul a'udhu bi Rabbin-Nas"* — so he said it. **Therefore, we say what the Prophet said.**

The reasoning is worth pausing on. The very first word of both surahs is **"Say."** Jibril's instruction was an instruction to *recite these words back*. That they are Qur'an is proved by the fact that the Prophet ﷺ obeyed the command to say them — and the community's position is simply to keep saying them.

---

## 2. Their Virtues

Ibn Kathir stacks six narrations, and their cumulative force is unusual:

- **Muslim, from Uqbah bin Amir** — the Prophet ﷺ: *"Do you not see that there have been Ayat revealed to me tonight the like of which has not been seen before?"* — and then recited these two. (Also Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i; At-Tirmidhi: *Hasan Sahih*.)

- **On the road** — Uqbah was leading the Prophet's ﷺ mount when he was told: *"Shall I not teach you two Surahs that are of the best two Surahs that the people recite?"* He taught him these, then recited them in the prayer he led immediately afterwards, and told Uqbah: **"Recite these two Surahs whenever you go to sleep and whenever you get up."** (An-Nasa'i, Abu Dawud.)

- *"Verily, the people do not seek protection with anything like these two."*

- **The "Qul!" narration (An-Nasa'i)** — walking together, the Prophet ﷺ said, *"Say!"* Uqbah asked what he should say. Silence. *"Say!"* Again he asked. Then came *Qul a'udhu bi Rabbil-Falaq*, recited to the end; then *Say!* again, and *Qul a'udhu bi Rabbin-Nas*, recited to the end. And then: **"No person beseeches with anything like these, and no person seeks refuge with anything like these."** The teaching method is itself the lesson — the answer to *what should I say?* is the surah.

- **Ibn Abis Al-Juhani** — *"Shall I guide you to the best thing that those who seek protection use for protection?"* … *"These two Surahs."*

- **A'ishah (Malik; also Al-Bukhari, Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah)** — when the Prophet ﷺ was ill he would **recite the Mu'awwidhatayn over himself and blow**. When the pain grew severe, **she** would recite them over him, take his hand, and wipe it over himself, seeking the blessing of those surahs.

- **Abu Sa'id** — he used to seek protection from the evil eyes of jinn and mankind by other means; **when the Mu'awwidhatayn were revealed, he used them and abandoned everything else besides them.** (At-Tirmidhi — *Hasan Sahih* — An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah.)

That last one is the summary of the whole section: these two surahs did not join the arsenal. They replaced it.

# Al-Falaq

> *Say: "I seek refuge with the Lord of Al-Falaq, from the evil of what He has created, and from the evil of the Ghasiq when Waqab, and from the evil of the blowers in knots, and from the evil of the envier when he envies."*

## 3. "Lord of Al-Falaq" (113:1)

**Al-Falaq is the morning.** So said Jabir, Ibn Abbas (via Al-'Awfi), Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Abdullah bin Muhammad bin Aqil, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi, Ibn Zayd, and Zayd bin Aslam.

Al-Qurazi, Ibn Zayd and Ibn Jarir tie it to Allah's saying: ***"He is the Cleaver of the daybreak"*** (6:96).

The image is precise and consoling: you are seeking refuge with the One who **splits the darkness open every single morning without fail**. Everything the surah goes on to name is a thing that hides in the dark. You are appealing to the Lord of the thing that ends it.

## 4. "From the evil of what He has created" (113:2)

**The evil of all created things** — a comprehensive first request that covers everything before the surah narrows.

Thabit Al-Bunani and Al-Hasan Al-Basri: *"Hell, Iblis and his progeny, from among that which He created."*

Note the theology embedded in the phrase: evil belongs to *what He has created*, not to Him. You flee **to** the Creator **from** the creation.

## 5. "The Ghasiq when Waqab" (113:3)

The most contested phrase in the surah, and Ibn Kathir preserves the whole disagreement:

| Authority | Reading |
|---|---|
| **Mujahid** (cited by Al-Bukhari), Ibn Abbas, Al-Qurazi, Ad-Dahhak, Khusayf, Al-Hasan, Qatadah | **Ghasiq is the night**; *when it Waqab* = the setting of the sun — *"the night when it advances with its darkness"* |
| **Az-Zuhri** | The sun when it sets |
| **Abu Hurayrah** (via Abu Al-Muhazzim) | The star |
| **Ibn Zayd** | The Arabs said it was the declining of the **Pleiades** — the sick and the plague-stricken grew more numerous as it declined, fewer as it rose |
| **Ibn Jarir** reports | Others: **the moon** |

The moon view rests on a hadith Imam Ahmad recorded from Al-Harith bin Abi Salamah, from **A'ishah**:

> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ took me by my hand and showed me the moon when it rose, and said: **"Seek refuge with Allah from the evil of this Ghasiq when it becomes dark."** (At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i, in their Books of Tafsir.)

Whichever reading you take, the sense converges: **the hours when harm moves and you cannot see it coming.**

## 6. "The blowers in knots" (113:4)

**Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak: this means the witches** — Mujahid adding, *"when they perform their spells and blow into the knots."*

Ibn Kathir places here the hadith of **Jibril's ruqyah** over the Prophet ﷺ:

> *"Are you suffering from any ailment, O Muhammad?"* — *"Yes."*
> **"In the Name of Allah, I recite prayer (Ruqyah) over you, from every illness that harms you, from the evil of every envious person and evil eye. May Allah cure you."**

That single formula gathers both of the surah's last two evils — the sorcery and the envy — which is why it sits between them.

## 7. The Bewitchment of the Prophet ﷺ

From **Al-Bukhari's Book of Medicine**, from A'ishah: the Prophet ﷺ was bewitched until he thought he had been with his wives when he had not. Sufyan: *"This is the worst form of magic when it reaches this stage."*

Then the Prophet ﷺ told her what he had been shown. Two men came; one sat by his head, one by his feet.

> *"What is wrong with this man?"* — *"He is bewitched."*
> *"Who bewitched him?"* — *"**Labid bin A'sam**, a man of Banu Zurayq, an ally of the Jews, and a hypocrite."*
> *"With what?"* — *"With a comb and hair from the comb."*
> *"Where?"* — *"In the dried bark of a male date palm, under a rock, in a well called **Dharwan**."*

He went to the well. *"This is the well that I saw. It was as if its water had henna soaked in it, and its palm trees were like the heads of devils."* And he removed it.

A'ishah asked: **"Will you not make this public?"**

> **"Allah has cured me, and I hate to spread wickedness to any of the people."**

Two things Ibn Kathir wants you to take from this. First, **sorcery is real and its target can be the best of creation** — which is why Allah taught him this surah. Second, the response of the man who was harmed: he was cured, he identified the culprit by name in private, and then he **refused to broadcast it**. Vindication was available to him and he declined it.

## 8. "The envier when he envies" (113:5)

The surah closes with the evil that needs no instrument at all — no night, no knot, no blowing. Just a look and a wish. Ibn Kathir's treatment here is carried by the Jibril narration above: *from the evil of every envious person and evil eye.* Note the conditional — ***when* he envies**. The danger begins at the moment the feeling is acted on.

# An-Nas

> *Say: "I seek refuge with the Lord of An-Nas, the King of An-Nas, the God of An-Nas, from the evil of the whisperer who withdraws, who whispers in the breasts of An-Nas, of jinn and An-Nas."*

## 9. Three Attributes for One Enemy (114:1–3)

Ibn Kathir is exact:

> These are three attributes from the attributes of the Lord, the Mighty and Majestic: **lordship, sovereignty and divinity.**

- He is the **Lord** of everything → all things are **created** by Him
- the **King** of everything → all things are **owned** by Him
- the **God** of everything → all things are **subservient** to Him

*Therefore*, He commands whoever seeks protection to seek it with the One who holds these three — **from the evil of the whisperer who withdraws.**

The escalation matters. Against sorcery and envy, one name sufficed. Against the whisper, the surah invokes creation, ownership and worship together — because the whisperer's whole project is to get you to hand one of those three to something else.

## 10. The Companion Assigned to You (114:4)

> This is **the devil that is assigned to man**. For verily, there is not any of the Children of Adam except that he has a companion who beautifies wicked deeds for him. This devil will go to any lengths to confuse and confound him. **The only person who is safe is he whom Allah protects.**

Confirmed in the Sahih:

> *"There is not a single one of you except that his companion has been assigned to him."*
> They said: *"What about you, O Messenger of Allah?"*
> **"Yes. However, Allah has helped me against him and he has accepted Islam. Thus, he only commands me to do good."**

And from the Two Sahihs, from Anas — the night the Prophet ﷺ walked his wife **Safiyyah** home from I'tikaf, and two men of the Ansar, seeing him, hurried away:

> **"Slow down! This is Safiyyah bint Huyay!"**
> They said: *"Glory be to Allah, O Messenger of Allah!"*
> **"Verily, Shaytan runs in the Son of Adam like the running of the blood. And verily, I feared that he might cast something into your hearts."**

Two men above suspicion, and he still stopped and named his wife aloud. The surah's enemy does not need you to be a bad person. He needs a gap.

## 11. How the Whisperer Works — and the One Thing That Removes Him

**Al-Khannas** means *the one who withdraws, retreats, slinks back*. The commentators describe a rhythm:

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Sa'id bin Jubayr; also Mujahid and Qatadah): *"The devil is squatting upon the heart of the Son of Adam. So when he becomes absentminded and heedless, he whispers. Then, **when he remembers Allah, he withdraws**."*
- **Sulayman** (via his son Al-Mu'tamir): *"He blows into the heart of the Son of Adam **when he is sad and when he is happy**. But when he remembers Allah, Shaytan withdraws."*
- **Ibn Abbas** (via Al-'Awfi): *"He whispers, and then **when he is obeyed**, he withdraws."*

Three observations sit inside these. The whisperer is **posted at the heart**, not visiting it. He works **at both emotional extremes** — grief and joy are equally good cover. And he retreats in exactly two situations: when you **remember Allah**, or when you **obey him** and he no longer needs to be there.

## 12. "Of jinn and An-Nas" (114:5–6)

Ibn Kathir raises the question honestly: does *"whispers in the breasts of An-Nas"* mean only mankind, or mankind and jinn both? **Two views.** The word *An-Nas* is often used inclusively — Ibn Jarir notes the Qur'an itself says ***Rijalun min al-jinn***, "men from the jinn," so applying *An-Nas* to them is not strange.

The final verse then resolves it: the whisperers themselves are **of jinn and of men**. There are human whisperers. This matches:

> *And so We have appointed for every Prophet enemies — **Shayatin among mankind and jinn** — inspiring one another with adorned speech as a delusion.* (6:112)

## 13. The Last Hadith in the Tafsir

Imam Ahmad, from Ibn Abbas — a man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said:

> *"O Messenger of Allah! Sometimes I say things to myself that I would rather fall from the sky than say aloud."*

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> **"Allahu Akbar! Allahu Akbar! All praise is due to Allah, Who sent his plot back as only a whisper."**

(Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i also recorded it.)

This is where Ibn Kathir ends the entire tafsir of the Qur'an, and the placement is not accidental. A man is tormented by intrusive thoughts and assumes they condemn him. The answer is **takbir** — and a reframing: the fact that the enemy is reduced to *whispering* at you is evidence of his **defeat**, not of your corruption. He wanted your deeds. He got a noise you hated hearing. That is a plot sent back.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The Qur'an ends by teaching you to say two words.** Not an argument, not a weapon, not a technique — *a'udhu*, I seek refuge. The last thing the Book asks of its reader is the admission that he cannot secure himself.

**Al-Falaq guards the perimeter; An-Nas guards the heart.** Outside there is the dark, the knot, the envious eye. Inside there is one voice, permanently stationed, working through your heedlessness and through your moods. The surah spends three divine names on that one voice, because it is the more serious of the two threats.

**And the counter-measure is embarrassingly small.** *When he remembers Allah, he withdraws.* No exorcism, no confrontation — dhikr, and he slinks off. He returns the moment you forget. Which is why the Prophet ﷺ prescribed these two surahs for the two hinges of every day: **"Recite them whenever you go to sleep and whenever you get up."**

**Abu Sa'id's testimony is the practical verdict:** when these were revealed, everything else was set aside. Not supplemented — abandoned.
