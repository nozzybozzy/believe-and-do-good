*Revealed in Makkah · 75 ayat · "The Throngs" — named for the groups in which humanity is led to its final destination (39:71, 73)*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Az-Zumar has one governing word: **ikhlas** — sincerity. Not *whether* you worship, but *whether it is for Him alone and uncontaminated*. The demand is stated four times in the opening fifteen ayat (39:2, 3, 11, 14), and everything that follows is either an argument for it, an illustration of it, or a picture of where its absence leads.

Ibn Kathir opens with A'ishah's report that **the Prophet ﷺ used to recite Al-Isra' and Az-Zumar every night**. The surah earns that place: it contains the verse Ibn Mas'ud called *the Ayah in the Qur'an which brings the most relief* (39:53), the most vivid description in the Qur'an of what the revelation does to a believing body (39:23), and the closing sequence in which all of humanity is finally sorted into two processions moving toward two sets of gates.

---

## 1. The Argument the Idolaters Always Make (39:3)

> *We worship them only that they may bring us near to Allah.*

Ibn Kathir treats this as the perennial defence of shirk, ancient and modern. They made their idols in the image of angels — or so they claimed — so that worshipping the image was, in their minds, honouring the angel, who would then intercede for provision and worldly need. (As for resurrection, they simply denied it.)

Their Hajj talbiyah in the Jahiliyyah preserved the logic exactly: *"At Your service, You have no partner — except the partner You have; he and all he owns belong to You."*

Ibn Kathir's refutation is that **this is the very thing every messenger was sent to abolish**: *"We sent among every nation a Messenger: worship Allah and avoid Taghut"* (16:36). And the analogy fails at its root — the angels themselves do not intercede except by His leave, for one with whom He is pleased. They are not courtiers who lobby a king behind his back for people he dislikes. *"So put not forward similitudes for Allah"* (16:74).

**39:4** disposes of the "son" claim with a conditional that is *impossible*, not merely unrealised: had He willed offspring, He could have chosen from His creation — but glory be to Him, the One, the Irresistible. Ibn Kathir notes it is legitimate in Arabic to construct a conditional around an impossibility when it exposes the ignorance of the claim.

---

## 2. The Signs, and the Three Veils of Darkness (39:5–6)

The night pushed into the day and the day into the night, each pursuing the other rapidly; the sun and moon running to an appointed term that ends on the Day of Resurrection.

Then, more intimately: **you were made from a single soul** — with all your races, tongues and colours — *"He creates you in the wombs of your mothers, creation after creation, in three veils of darkness."*

Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatadah and others: the three darknesses are **the darkness of the womb, the darkness of the placenta that blankets and protects the child, and the darkness of the belly**. The verse pairs the astronomical with the embryonic — the same Hand at both scales.

Note the seam in 39:5: *"Verily, He is the Almighty, the Oft-Forgiving."* Ibn Kathir: alongside all His might and greatness, He forgives those who disobey Him and then turn back.

---

## 3. What He Needs From You, and What He Loves (39:7)

> *If you disbelieve, then verily, Allah is not in need of you; He likes not disbelief for His servants. And if you are grateful, He is pleased therewith for you.*

Two things sit together here and should not be collapsed:

- **He gains nothing from your faith.** From the hadith Qudsi in Sahih Muslim: if the first and last of you, men and jinn alike, were as evil as the most evil among you, it would not diminish His sovereignty in the least.
- **He still does not like disbelief for you, and He is pleased by your gratitude, and increases you for it.**

That is the whole psychology of the surah: an independent Lord who nevertheless *wants* good for you. And each carries his own file — *"No bearer of burdens shall bear the burden of another."*

**39:8** names the specific failure: in distress, man cries out to Him alone, sincerely — then when relief comes, he forgets the very thing he was begging for and sets up rivals. This exact loop returns at **39:49**, where the man who is rescued says: *"Only because of knowledge I obtained it"* — meaning, *Allah knows I deserve this; He regards me as special.* Ibn Kathir links it straight to Qarun (28:76–78). The verdict: **"Nay, it is only a trial."**

---

## 4. The One Who Stands in the Depths of the Night (39:9)

> *Is one who is obedient to Allah, in the hours of the night, prostrating and standing, fearing the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord…?*

*Ana'a al-layl* — Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, as-Suddi and Ibn Zayd: **in the depths of the night**.

Ibn Kathir extracts a rule of the spiritual life from *"fearing… and hoping"*: **both are essential, and the balance shifts.** During your lifetime, fear should be the stronger. **At death, hope should be uppermost.**

The proof is a deathbed scene: the Prophet ﷺ entered upon a dying man and asked how he felt. He said, *"I am both afraid and hopeful."* The Prophet ﷺ replied that these two do not co-exist in a heart at such a moment except that **Allah gives him what he hopes for and protects him from what he fears.**

Attached here: *whoever recites one hundred ayat in one night, it is recorded as if he prayed the whole night* (Ahmad, from Tamim ad-Dari).

And the closing question — *"Are those who know equal to those who know not?"* — is, in Ibn Kathir's reading, not a general praise of learning but a comparison with the man who sets up rivals to Allah. Only people of understanding can see the difference.

---

## 5. The Earth Is Spacious (39:10)

> *Good is for those who do good in this world, and Allah's earth is spacious! Only those who are patient shall receive their reward in full, without reckoning.*

Mujahid on *spacious*: **"So emigrate through it, strive hard, and keep away from idols."** The verse is a licence to leave — geography is never the excuse.

Al-Awza'i on *without reckoning*: their reward will not be weighed or measured; it is simply immense. Ibn Kathir adds: **in Paradise.**

---

## 6. What the Qur'an Does to a Body (39:23)

> *Allah has sent down the Best Statement — a Book, its parts resembling each other, oft-repeated. The skins of those who fear their Lord shiver from it. Then their skin and their heart soften to the remembrance of Allah.*

On *mathani* (oft-repeated), Ibn Kathir gathers the views: Mujahid — the whole Qur'an's parts resemble each other; Ikrimah and al-Hasan — an ayah in one surah resembles an ayah in another; and from Sufyan bin Uyaynah, the most useful reading: **it pairs a thing with its opposite** — believers then disbelievers, Paradise then Hell, the righteous in 'Illiyyin then the wicked in Sijjin. He is explicit that this is *not* the *mutashabihat* of 3:7, which is a different matter entirely.

Then the physiology of listening: the skin shivers at the warnings, then skin and heart soften at the hope of mercy. Ibn Kathir lists three ways such people differ from the heedless — what they choose to listen to, that they fall in prostration when Ar-Rahman's ayat are recited, and their **etiquette while listening**.

That last point gets a hard edge. **Qatadah:** this is the description of the friends of Allah — their skin shivers, their eyes weep, their hearts find rest. *He did not say that they lose their minds and swoon; that is the characteristic of the people of innovation, and it comes from Shaytan.* The Companions sat quietly, with fear, and did not shout or perform.

## 7. Two Parables (39:21, 39:29)

**Water and crops (39:21).** Water originates in the sky, settles into the earth, is released as springs, brings out crops of every colour — then they wither, yellow, and crumble to brittle pieces. Ibn Kathir: *this world is exactly that.* Green and fresh and beautiful, then old and ugly; the young man becomes a frail old man; then death. **"The blessed one is the one whose state after death is good."**

**The slave with many masters (39:29).** A man owned by several quarrelling partners, each pulling him a different way, against a man owned wholly by one master. *Are the two equal?* Ibn Abbas and Mujahid: this is the parable of the idolater and the sincere believer. The point is not merely that shirk is false — it is that shirk is **exhausting**. Divided ownership means being torn.

And then: *"All praise be to Allah"* — Ibn Kathir: praise for having established the proof so plainly.

---

## 8. "Verily, You Will Die, and They Too Will Die" (39:30–31)

This is the ayah **Abu Bakr recited when the Prophet ﷺ died**, so that people would accept that he had truly gone (alongside 3:144).

The sequel — *"Then, on the Day of Resurrection, you will be disputing before your Lord"* — troubled the Companions deeply. Az-Zubayr asked: *"O Messenger of Allah, will our disputes be repeated for us?"* He said, **Yes — they will be repeated until everyone entitled has his rights restored to him.** Az-Zubayr said, *"By Allah, it is a very serious matter."*

Ibn Umar recalled that when the verse came down they could not imagine what Muslims would dispute about — *"there is no dispute between us and the People of the Book"* — **until the Fitnah occurred.** Then he said: *this is what our Lord promised us we would dispute about.*

Ibn Abbas expands the scope: the truthful will dispute with the liar, the oppressed with the oppressor, the guided with the misguided, the weak with the arrogant. **Even the soul will dispute with its own body.** The soul says, *"You did such and such."* The body answers, *"You told me to, and you tempted me."* An angel judges between them with a parable: a man who could see but not walk, and a blind man who could walk, entering a garden and stealing fruit together — *which is the wrongdoer?* **"Both of them."** *"Then you have passed judgement against yourselves."*

## 9. Is Not Allah Sufficient for His Servant? (39:36–38)

At this verse Ibn Kathir places the great hadith of Ibn Abbas, one of the summits of the whole tafsir:

> Be mindful of Allah and He will protect you. Be mindful of Allah and you will find Him ever with you. Turn to Allah in times of ease and He will turn to you in times of difficulty. If you ask, ask Allah; if you seek help, seek it from Allah. Know that if the entire nation gathered to harm you with something Allah has not decreed, they could never do it; and if they gathered to benefit you with something He has not decreed, they could never do it. **The pages have dried and the pens have been lifted.** Know that in patiently bearing what you dislike there is much good; that victory comes with patience, a way out comes from difficulty, and with hardship comes ease.

The context is that the idolaters tried to frighten the Prophet ﷺ with their gods. **39:38** dismantles the fear: ask them who created the heavens and the earth and *they themselves will say "Allah"* — so ask further whether the things they call upon could remove a harm He intended, or withhold a mercy He intended. They cannot do any of it at all.

## 10. Sleep as the Lesser Death (39:42)

> *It is Allah Who takes away the souls at the time of their death, and those that die not during their sleep. He keeps those for which He has ordained death and sends the rest for a term appointed.*

Two deaths: the **greater** (the soul taken from the body) and the **lesser** (sleep). Ibn Abbas: **He keeps the souls of the dead and returns the souls of the living, and He does not make any mistakes.**

The practical consequence is the bedtime sunnah (Bukhari and Muslim, from Abu Hurayrah): brush down the bed with your garment, then say —

> *"In Your Name, O Lord, I lay down my side and in Your Name I raise it up. If You take my soul, have mercy on it; and if You send it back, protect it with that which You protect Your righteous servants."*

Related, from 39:46 — A'ishah's report of how the Prophet ﷺ **opened his night prayer**: *"O Allah, Lord of Jibril, Mika'il and Israfil, Creator of the heavens and the earth, Knower of the unseen and the seen, You will judge between Your servants concerning that wherein they differ. Guide me by Your leave regarding that wherein there is dispute concerning the truth, for You guide whom You will to the straight path."* (Sahih Muslim)

---

## 11. The Verse of Greatest Relief (39:53)

> *Say: "O My servants who have transgressed against themselves! Despair not of the mercy of Allah. Verily, Allah forgives all sins. Truly, He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful."*

Ibn Mas'ud's ranking of the Qur'an's summits: the greatest ayah is Ayat al-Kursi; the most comprehensive on good and evil is 16:90; **the ayah that brings the most relief is this one**; and the clearest on reliance upon Allah is 65:2–3.

**The occasion** (al-Bukhari, from Ibn Abbas): people of shirk who had killed extensively and committed zina extensively came to the Prophet ﷺ and said, *"What you say and call to is good — if only you could tell us there is an expiation for what we have done."*

**The one condition.** Ibn Kathir is careful and repeats it: *this cannot be interpreted as meaning sins are forgiven without repentance*, since shirk is forgiven only for the one who repents of it. But within repentance, the door is **without limit** — Ibn Abbas lists those invited by this verse: those who said the Messiah is God, that Allah has a son, that Allah is poor, that His Hand is tied, that He is the third of three — *and even the one who said, "I am your lord most high."*

**Al-Hasan al-Basri:** *"Look at this generosity and kindness! They killed His close friends, and He still called them to repentance and forgiveness."*

And Ibn Abbas' warning to the other extreme: **anyone who makes the servants of Allah despair of His mercy after this has rejected the Book of Allah.**

Two hadiths sealed here (Ahmad, from Anas and Abu Ayyub): *if you were to sin until your sins filled the space between heaven and earth, then asked Allah for forgiveness, He would forgive you* — and *if you did not sin, Allah would bring people who sin and seek forgiveness, so that He could forgive them.*

The story of **the man who killed ninety-nine and then a hundredth** is placed here too: he died on the road toward the town of repentance, and the angels of mercy took him, because he was found nearer to the town he was travelling toward than the one he had left. Ibn Kathir preserves the detail that as he was dying **he dragged himself forward**, and Allah commanded the good town to move nearer and the other to move away.

## 12. Three Regrets, Spoken Before They Are Spoken (39:54–59)

Repent and submit **before** the torment arrives; follow the best of what was revealed **before** it comes suddenly, *while you perceive not.* Then Allah quotes, in advance, what the unrepentant will say:

1. *"Alas, my grief that I was undutiful to Allah — and I was indeed among those who mocked."* (Ibn Kathir: *my actions were those of one who ridicules, not one who is sincere.*)
2. *"If only Allah had guided me, I should have been among those who have Taqwa."*
3. *"If only I had another chance, I should be among the doers of good."*

Ibn Abbas: **Allah tells us what His servants will say before they say it, and what they will do before they do it.**

And a hadith of Abu Hurayrah that makes the first two regrets symmetrical: **every person of Hell will be shown his place in Paradise** and will say *"If only Allah had guided me"* — so it becomes his grief; and **every person of Paradise will be shown his place in Hell** and will say *"Were it not that Allah guided me"* — so it becomes his gratitude.

The reply to all three is one line: *"Yes! Verily, there came to you My Ayat, and you denied them, and were proud, and were among the disbelievers."*

## 13. They Did Not Estimate Him As He Deserves (39:67)

> *And on the Day of Resurrection the whole of the earth will be grasped by His Hand, and the heavens will be rolled up in His Right Hand.*

Ibn Abbas' definition is a useful measure: **whoever believes Allah is able to do all things has made a just estimate of Him; whoever does not, has not.** Muhammad bin Ka'b: *had they estimated Him rightly, they would not have lied.*

Two scenes Ibn Kathir preserves without interpreting away:

- A rabbi came to the Prophet ﷺ describing Allah placing the heavens on one finger, the earths on one finger, the trees on one, water and dust on one, and the rest of creation on one, then saying *"I am the King."* **The Prophet ﷺ smiled so broadly that his molars showed**, in confirmation, and recited this verse. (al-Bukhari, from Ibn Mas'ud)
- Ibn Umar: the Prophet ﷺ recited it on the minbar, moving his hand forward and back, relating that the Lord glorifies Himself — *I am the Compeller, I am the Proud, I am the King, I am the Mighty, I am the Most Generous* — **and the minbar shook so much that we feared he would fall.**

Ibn Kathir's method here is worth noting: these reports are to be accepted and transmitted **without twisting or altering the meaning**.

*Related, 39:63:* **Maqalid** — Mujahid, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd, Sufyan bin Uyaynah: *"keys," a Persian word*; others: *the treasures*. Both amount to the same thing — control of everything is in His Hand.

## 14. The Trumpet (39:68–70)

The blast that kills: everyone in the heavens and the earth swoons away **except whom Allah wills**; then the remaining souls are taken, until **the last to die is the Angel of Death** — and there remains only the Ever Living. Then Israfil is the first restored to life, and sounds the trumpet of Resurrection.

Between the two blasts: *"forty"* — and Abu Hurayrah, asked whether forty days, months or years, said each time, **"I do not know."** Every part of a man disintegrates **except the root of the backbone**, and from that he is created anew.

> *And the earth will shine with the light of its Lord, and the Book will be presented, and the Prophets and the witnesses will be brought forward.*

The Prophets bear witness that they conveyed the Message (Ibn Abbas); the *witnesses* are the recording angels. Judgement is *with truth* — and *"each person will be paid in full of what he did."*

## 15. The Two Processions and the Two Sets of Gates (39:71–75)

The surah's name lives here. Everyone arrives **in groups**, and the two arrivals are described in deliberately parallel language — which makes the differences the entire point.

| | The disbelievers (39:71) | Those with Taqwa (39:73) |
|---|---|---|
| **Motion** | *driven* — pushed, forced, thirsty, some on their faces | *led* — group after group, the nearest to Allah first, each with their own kind: prophets with prophets, martyrs with martyrs, scholars with scholars |
| **The gates** | opened **on arrival** — Ibn Kathir: *quickly, to hasten the punishment* | opened **after** the Sirat and the bridge where all remaining injustices between them are settled and they are purified |
| **The greeting** | rebuke: *"Did not Messengers come to you from yourselves…?"* — and they answer **"Yes"** | *"Salam Alaykum! You have done well, so enter here to abide forever"* — Ibn Kathir: *your deeds were good, your words were good, your efforts were good, and your reward is good* |

Then Ibn Kathir makes the observation that gives the surah its final beauty. **The sentence about the believers is grammatically incomplete** — a conditional whose answer is never stated. Where the disbelievers' passage closes with *"Enter you the gates of Hell,"* the believers' passage simply opens the gates and stops. *What happens after this is not mentioned; it is left for the imagination to think of its dearest wishes and be filled with hope.*

Paradise has **eight gates** — and those who spend a pair of anything for Allah's sake are called from the gate matching their deed: the gate of Prayer, of Charity, of Jihad, **Ar-Rayyan** for those who fasted. Abu Bakr asked whether anyone would be called from all of them; the Prophet ﷺ said *"Yes — and I hope you will be one of them."* Between two gateposts is the distance from Makkah to Hajar; in another report, a forty-year journey — *yet a day will come when they are packed with crowds.*

The believers' first words on seeing it: *"All praise be to Allah, Who has fulfilled His promise to us."*

And the last verse of the surah is not attributed to any named speaker — Ibn Kathir reads this as deliberate: **the whole of creation, animate and inanimate, testifies.** Qatadah's closing note:

> *Allah began His creation with praise — "All praise be to Allah, Who created the heavens and the earth" (6:1) — and He ended it with praise: "And it will be said, All praise be to Allah, the Lord of all that exists."*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Sincerity is the whole subject.** Not whether you worship, but whether anything is standing between you and Him — including your own reasons.
- **The intercession argument is the oldest self-deception there is.** Nothing you call upon can remove a harm He intended or withhold a mercy He intended. *"Is not Allah sufficient for His servant?"*
- **Fear and hope are both required, and the ratio changes:** fear the stronger while you live, hope uppermost at the end.
- **39:53 has no ceiling and one condition.** No sin is too large — but the door is repentance, and to make anyone despair of that mercy is itself a rejection of the Book.
- **Do it before.** Three regrets are recorded in advance, and all three are versions of *too late*.
- **Everyone arrives in groups; only one procession is walked toward gates that stay open.** And the sentence describing that arrival is left unfinished — because what waits behind it is more than the verse would say.
