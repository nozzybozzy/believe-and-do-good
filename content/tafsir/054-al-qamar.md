*Revealed in Makkah · 55 ayat · "The Moon" — named for the miracle in its first verse · recited by the Prophet ﷺ, together with Qaf, at the ʿId prayers of Al-Adha and Al-Fitr*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ibn Kathir opens with the reason the Prophet ﷺ chose this surah for the great gatherings: it contains **Allah's promises and warnings, the origin of creation, the Resurrection, Tawhid, and the affirmation of Prophethood** — the major objectives, compressed.

Al-Qamar is built like a hammer. A miracle is shown; it is called magic. Then four nations are marched past in quick succession — **Nuh, ʿAd, Thamud, Lut, then Firʿawn** — each one a variation on the same failure, each one closed with the same two refrains:

> *Then how was My torment and My warnings!*
> *And We have indeed made the Qur'an easy to remember; then is there any that will remember!*

The repetition is the argument. Nothing new is being said each time, because nothing new needs to be said: the pattern has already been demonstrated, and the Quraysh are simply the next name on a list. The surah then turns to face them directly — *are your disbelievers better than these?* — and ends by naming the principle beneath everything that has come before: **Qadar**. Every destruction, every rescue, every locust-swarm of the risen dead was measured out before it happened.

There is an irony in the name. The surah called "The Moon" is the surah about people who watched a mountain appear between two halves of the moon and concluded it would wear off.

---

## 1. The Hour Is Close, and the Moon Has Split (54:1)

Two announcements in one verse, and Ibn Kathir treats them as one point: **time is short and the proof has already been given.**

On the nearness of the Hour he gathers a cluster of hadiths that all work by gesture rather than calculation:

- **Anas**: the Prophet ﷺ addressed his Companions as the sun was setting — what remains of this world compared to what has passed is like what remains of this day of yours. Anas adds: *only a small sliver of the sun was still visible.*
- **Ibn ʿUmar**: the same image, with the sun sitting above Mount Quʿayqaʿan after ʿAsr.
- **Sahl bin Saʿd**: *"I was sent like this with the Hour"* — and he pointed with his middle and index fingers together. (In the Two Sahihs.)
- **Wahb As-Suwaʾi**: *"like the distance between this and this; the latter almost overtook the former."*
- **Anas**, asked by Al-Walid bin ʿAbdul-Malik what he had heard on the subject: *"You and the Hour are as close as these two."*

Supporting this is one of the Prophet's ﷺ own names — **Al-Hashir**, the Gatherer: he is the first to be gathered, and all people are gathered after him.

### The splitting of the moon

Ibn Kathir states the position flatly: it happened in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, it is transmitted by **Mutawatir** hadiths, and the scholars agree on it. Five narrations are stacked:

| Narrator | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Anas** | The Makkans demanded a miracle; the moon split into two parts — *until they saw Mount Hira' between them* (Bukhari) |
| **Jubayr bin Mutʿim** | One part over one mountain, one over another. They said: *"Muhammad has taken us by his magic"* — then: *"if he can bewitch us, he cannot bewitch all people"* |
| **Ibn ʿAbbas** | It occurred before the Hijrah; they saw it in two parts |
| **Ibn ʿUmar** | The Prophet ﷺ said: **"O Allah, be witness"** |
| **Ibn Masʿud** | *"I saw the mountain between the two parts of the moon"* |

Note what the Quraysh's own objection concedes. Their argument was not *it did not happen*; it was *it will not scale*.

---

## 2. "This Is Passing Magic" (54:2–5)

**Mustamir** — Mujahid, Qatadah and others: *it will soon go away.* The Quraysh's verdict on the greatest sign given to their Prophet was that it was a trick with a short shelf life.

Ibn Kathir's gloss on *they turn away*: it is not failure to see. They saw. They **abandoned it behind their backs**.

- *They denied and followed their own lusts* — they rejected the truth when it arrived, following the ignorance their desires called them to.
- *Every matter will be settled* — **Qatadah**: good deeds carry their doers to what is good, evil deeds carry theirs to what is evil. **Ibn Jurayj**: each matter settles according to its people. **Mujahid**: on the Day of Resurrection.
- *News wherein there is **Muzdajar*** — the accounts of the earlier nations, containing enough to deter anyone from persisting.
- *Perfect wisdom — but warners benefit them not.* Warning does not benefit those upon whom misery has been written and whose hearts are sealed. Ibn Kathir asks the question the verse implies: **who can guide such people after Allah?** (cf. 6:149; 10:101)

---

## 3. The Caller and the Locusts (54:6–8)

*So withdraw from them* — turn away, Muhammad, from people who watch a miracle and call it a trick. And wait.

The scene when the caller calls is one of the most physical in the Qur'an, and Ibn Kathir keeps it physical: eyes **covered with disgrace**, bodies pouring out of the graves *as if they were locusts spread abroad* — hurrying toward the place of Reckoning in crowds, unable to hesitate or slow down. The disbelievers' single line of dialogue: **"This is a hard Day."** (cf. 74:9–10)

## 4. Four Nations, One Pattern

### Nuh (54:9–17)

They called him a madman. **Wazdujir** — Mujahid: he was *driven out, accused of madness*. Ibn Zayd (which Ibn Kathir calls sound): he was rebuked and threatened, as in *"If you do not stop, O Nuh, you will be among those stoned"* (26:116).

His entire prayer is two words: ***"I am overcome, so help."*** Ibn Kathir reads it as: *I am weak and cannot resist my people — so aid Your religion.*

The response is a co-ordinated assault from above and below. **Munhamir** — As-Suddi: abundant. **Ibn ʿAbbas**: torrential rain that came *from the opened gates of heaven, not from clouds* — the only water in history to fall that way, before or since. And from the earth, springs gushing from every part of it, even from ovens where fire had been burning. *So the waters met for a matter predestined.*

- ***Dusur*** = **nails** (Ibn ʿAbbas, Saʿid bin Jubayr, Al-Qurazi, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd; Ibn Jarir preferred this).
- *Floating under Our Eyes* — **by Our command, under Our protection and observation.**
- *A reward for him who had been rejected* — recompense for their disbelief, and reward for Nuh.
- *We left this as a sign* — Qatadah: the ark itself survived for the first generation of this Ummah to see. **Ibn Kathir prefers**: Allah kept *ships* as the sign (36:41–42; 69:11–12).

A small, lovely detail on the refrain: **Ibn Masʿud** said the Prophet ﷺ recited *fahal min muddakir* to him; in Bukhari's version, Ibn Masʿud recites it and the Prophet ﷺ recites it straight back.

### ʿAd (54:18–22)

A **Sarsar** wind — bitterly cold and furious — on *a day of calamity, continuous*: Ibn Kathir says continuous because the destruction of that day carried straight on into the Hereafter.

*Plucking out men as if they were uprooted stems of date palms.* The wind lifted a man so high he could no longer be seen, then drove him down head-first into the ground: the head smashed, the body left standing headless. Hence the image of the palm stump.

### Thamud (54:23–32)

Their objection is one every prophet hears: ***"A man, alone among us — shall we follow him?"*** They were offended that the Reminder went to *him* out of all of them, so they called him an insolent liar. The answer is a dated threat: *tomorrow they will know.*

The she-camel was a test built to their own specification — a superb, pregnant camel **emerging from solid rock at their request**, so that it would stand as a proof against them. The water was shared by turns (26:155), and **Mujahid** adds the arrangement: on her day they drank her milk; on their day they drank the water.

*But they called their comrade* — **Qudar bin Salif**, the evilest among them, *"the most wicked man among them"* (91:12). One **Sayhah**, and they were gone entirely.

***Al-Muhtazir*** — As-Suddi: dry desert grass, burned and scattered by the wind. **Ibn Zayd**: the Arabs built fences (*hizar*) of dried bushes around their cattle — they ended up like that trampled, broken fencing.

### Lut (54:33–40)

A sin *no people in the history of mankind had committed before* — met with a punishment never inflicted on any nation before: Jibril was commanded to raise their cities to the sky and overturn them, followed by stones of marked **Sijjil**.

The rescue happens *bi-sahar*, in the last part of the night. **Lut's wife stayed and shared her people's end.** Ibn Kathir's telling of the night itself is vivid: the three angels — Jibril, Mikaʾil and Israfil — arriving as handsome young men, *as a test*; Lut's wife, "the evil old one," sending word to her people; the crowd converging from every direction; Lut with his back to the door, offering *"these are my daughters"* (15:71) — meaning their own women — and receiving the reply that damns them: *"you know we have neither desire nor need of your daughters"* (11:79).

Then Jibril steps out and strikes their eyes **with the tip of his wing**. They went home feeling along the walls, still shouting threats about what they would do in the morning. The morning brought a torment they had no way of escaping.

### Firʿawn (54:41–42)

Compressed to two verses, and Ibn Kathir keeps it compressed: Musa supported by Harun, tremendous miracles, total rejection — and then Allah *seized them the way the All-Mighty, the All-Capable does*: He destroyed them all, **leaving none surviving to tell the story of what happened to them.**

## 5. The Turn to the Quraysh (54:43–46)

Now the surah stops narrating and points.

> *Are your disbelievers better than these? Or have you immunity in the Divine Scriptures?*

Meaning: do you hold some exemption that means the punishment will not touch you? Their reply is the confidence of numbers — *"We are a great multitude, victorious"* — the belief that a large enough gathering will protect them from anyone who intends them harm.

**The answer was given at Badr.** Bukhari records, from Ibn ʿAbbas, that the Prophet ﷺ was in a domed tent praying:

> *"O Allah! I ask You for the fulfilment of Your covenant and promise. O Allah! If You wish, You will never be worshipped on the earth after today."*

Abu Bakr took him by the hand: *this is sufficient, O Messenger of Allah.* The Prophet ﷺ came out in his armour reciting **"Their multitude will be put to flight, and they will show their backs."**

And **ʿA'ishah** — in Bukhari — supplies the timing that makes it extraordinary: *"When I was still a young playful girl in Makkah, this verse was revealed."* Years before Badr, in a city where the Muslims were a persecuted handful, the rout was announced in the past tense.

Then the correction that outranks even Badr: *Nay, but the Hour is their appointed time — and the Hour will be more grievous and more bitter.*

## 6. "We Created All Things with Qadar" (54:47–50)

This is the surah's theological centre, and Ibn Kathir gives it more space than anything else in the chapter.

The criminals are *in error and will burn* — Ibn Kathir: misguided from the truth and **engulfed in confusion**, from the doubt and uncertainty they live in. A description, he notes, that fits every disbeliever and every innovator of every sect. And the punishment matches the crime exactly: *just as they were consumed in doubt and hesitation, they are dragged on their faces — unaware of where they are being taken.*

> **إِنَّا كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقْنَاهُ بِقَدَرٍ**
> *Verily, We have created all things with Qadar.* (54:49)

Read alongside *"He created everything and measured it exactly"* (25:2) and *"Who created and proportioned, Who measured and then guided"* (87:1–3).

**The Imams of the Sunnah relied on this verse** as evidence that Allah created creation with destined limits before it existed — knowing and recording everything before it occurred — and used it to refute the **Qadariyyah**, the sect that arose in the later time of the Companions.

The hadith material Ibn Kathir assembles here:

- **Abu Hurayrah**: the Quraysh idolaters came arguing with the Prophet ﷺ, *discounting Qadar* — and this very verse was revealed. (Muslim, Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah)
- The Prophet ﷺ, after reciting it: *these verses concern some members of my Ummah who will come before the end of time and deny Al-Qadar.*
- **Ibn ʿAbbas**, drawing water at Zamzam, told that people had begun disputing Qadar: *"By Allah, this verse was revealed only about them. They are the worst of this Ummah. Do not visit their sick or pray over their dead."*
- **Ibn ʿUmar** wrote to a friend in Ash-Sham who had taken up the subject: *do not write to me again.*
- *Everything is predetermined — **even laziness and intelligence.*** (Muslim)
- ***"Seek the help of Allah and do not succumb to feebleness."*** When affliction strikes, say *Allah decreed this, and He does as He wills* — and do **not** say *"had I done this or that…"*, because **"if" opens the door wide for the work of Shaytan.**
- To **Ibn ʿAbbas**: if the whole Ummah gathered to benefit you with something Allah has not decreed, they could not; if they gathered to harm you with something He has not written, they could not. ***The pens have gone dry and the Books of Record have been closed.***
- **ʿUbadah bin As-Samit**, dying, propped up to give his son a final instruction: *you will not taste the delight of faith until you believe in Al-Qadar, its good and its not-so-good* — knowing that **what missed you was never going to reach you, and what reached you was never going to miss you**. The first thing Allah created was the Pen; He said *"Record!"*, and it recorded everything until the Day of Resurrection.
- **ʿAbdullah bin ʿAmr** (Muslim): Allah recorded the measurements of creation **fifty thousand years before** He created the heavens and the earth — *and His Throne was over the water.*

And immediately after all this, the counterweight against imagining Allah's decree as slow machinery: *Our commandment is but one, as the twinkling of an eye* — one command, never repeated, executed **faster than a blink, without delay for an instant.**

## 7. The Ledger, and the Two Endings (54:51–55)

*We have destroyed your likes* — the earlier nations, offered as memory-material: *then is there any that will remember?* (cf. 34:54)

*Everything they have done is noted in **Az-Zubur*** — the Books of Record entrusted to the angels — *and everything, small and large, is written down.* Nothing escapes the count. Ibn Kathir attaches ʿA'ishah's hadith, which is where this doctrine actually lands in a life:

> **"O ʿA'ishah! Beware of small sins, because there is someone assigned by Allah who records them."**

Against the dragging on faces, the final image:

*Those who have Taqwa will be in the midst of **Gardens and Rivers** — in a **seat of truth**, near the **Muqtadir King**.* Ibn Kathir: the Dwelling of Allah's honour, encompassed by His pleasure, favours, bounties, generosity and compassion — near the Magnificent King **Who created everything and measured its destiny**, and is therefore able to grant them whatever they wish and ask for.

Note how the surah closes its own circle: the God who *measured* everything (54:49) is the God whose *measuring* is the guarantee that the request of the God-fearing can be met (54:55). Qadar is the threat in one half of the surah and the security in the other.

The last hadith belongs to those who deal justly: *the just will be with Allah on podiums of light, to the right of Ar-Rahman — and both of His Hands are right* — those who are just in their judgement, with their families, and with all whom they are responsible for.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**A miracle is not an argument to someone who has already decided.** The Quraysh saw the moon split over two mountains and said it would wear off. Ibn Kathir's whole point in this surah is that the evidence was never the problem — *warners benefit them not*, because *they followed their own lusts*.

**Four nations, one sentence.** Nuh's people, ʿAd, Thamud, Lut's people — different sins, identical structure: a messenger arrives, is called mad or a liar, and the town is erased. The refrain *then how was My torment and My warnings* is not decoration; it is the surah insisting there is no fifth pattern.

**Everything is measured, and the measuring was done fifty thousand years before the heavens.** This is the verse the Sunnah rests on for Qadar — and the practical shape it takes is not fatalism but the opposite: *seek Allah's help and do not succumb to feebleness*, and when something goes wrong, refuse the word *"if."*

**And the small things are written too.** Between a cosmology of fifty thousand years and a sky with its gates opened, the surah's most usable line is a warning to ʿA'ishah about minor sins — because *everything, small and large, is written down.*
