*Revealed in Makkah · 77 ayat · "The Criterion" — that which separates truth from falsehood*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Furqan is built as a long argument with the mockers of Makkah, and it moves in a very deliberate way. It opens by **naming the Qur'an as the Criterion** and blessing the One who sent it down. It then lets the disbelievers speak — their objections are quoted at length, in their own words, four times over — and answers each one. It shows them what their objections will cost them on the Day of Resurrection. And then, having spent seventy verses describing people who chose badly, it closes with a portrait of the people who chose well: **the 'Ibad ar-Rahman**, the Servants of the Most Gracious, one of the most beloved passages in the Qur'an.

The surah's own logic is: *this Book is the criterion — so be the kind of person the criterion produces.*

---

## 1. Tabaraka, and Nazzala (25:1–2)

> *Blessed be He Who sent down the Criterion to His servant, that he may be a warner to all nations.*

Ibn Kathir stops on two words:

- **Tabaraka** — the verbal form implies an **ongoing, permanent, eternal** blessing. Not a blessing given, but a blessing that never stops issuing.
- **Nazzala** — a form implying something done **repeatedly, over time**. This is deliberate. Allah contrasts it with *Anzala* in 4:136. Each previous Book came down at one time; the Qur'an came in stages — ayah after ayah, ruling after ruling, surah after surah. Ibn Kathir says this is *more eloquent, and indicative of greater care for the one to whom it is revealed*.

**Why "Al-Furqan"?** Because it is the criterion that decides between truth and falsehood, guidance and misguidance, right and wrong, lawful and unlawful.

**"To His servant."** Ibn Kathir notes something beautiful here. *'Abd* — servant — is a title of praise, and Allah reaches for it at the three highest moments:

| Moment | Ayah |
|---|---|
| The Night Journey | *Glorified be He Who took **His servant** by night* (17:1) |
| The Prophet ﷺ standing in prayer, the Jinn crowding to listen | *When **the servant of Allah** stood up invoking Him* (72:19) |
| The revelation of this Book | *sent down the Criterion to **His servant*** (25:1) |

At the summit of honour, the title is servanthood.

**"A warner to all nations."** This is the surah's first universal claim, and Ibn Kathir gathers the hadiths for it: *"I have been sent to the red and the black."* And: *"A Prophet was sent only to his own people, but I have been sent to all of mankind."*

Then 25:2 closes the theology: no son, no partner, and — **"He has created everything, and has measured it exactly according to its due measurement."** Everything apart from Him is created and subject to Him.

---

## 2. The Gods Who Can Do Nothing (25:3)

The idols are described by a stacking of negations: they created nothing, **they are themselves created**, they possess no harm or benefit *for themselves*, and no power over death, life, or resurrection.

Ibn Kathir's line: they could not create the wing of a gnat. They could not help themselves — so how could they do anything for their worshippers?

Resurrection, meanwhile, costs Allah nothing:

> *The creation of you all and the resurrection of you all are only as a single person.* (31:28)

He lines up the verses of the single instant — *one as the twinkling of an eye* (54:50), *a single Zajrah* (79:13), *a single Sayhah* (36:53) — to make the point that the thing they find impossible takes less than a moment.

---

## 3. The Four Accusations (25:4–9)

The disbelievers' objections are quoted verbatim, and they fall into two clusters.

**About the Book:**
- *"This is nothing but a lie he has invented, and others have helped him in it."*
- *"Tales of the ancients, which he has written down, and they are dictated to him morning and afternoon."*

Ibn Kathir dismantles this on grounds of plain fact. Muhammad ﷺ **never learned to read or write**, at the beginning or the end of his life. He grew up among these very people for some forty years. They knew his character intimately — they had called him **Al-Amin**, the Trustworthy, since he was young, because they had watched him. Only when Allah honoured him did they suddenly discover accusations, and they could not settle on one: sorcerer, poet, madman, liar, all in turn.

> *See what examples they have put forward for you. So they have gone astray, and never can they find a way.* (17:48)

**About the Messenger:**
- *"Why does this Messenger eat food and walk about in the markets?"* — i.e. why does he need what we need?
- *"Why is no angel sent down with him? Why no treasure, no garden?"*

Ibn Kathir points out that this is Fir'awn's argument, word for word in spirit: *"Why then are not golden bracelets bestowed on him?"* (43:53). All of it was easy for Allah — He simply had a reason for not doing it, *and with Him is the perfect proof*.

**The answer to all of it** comes in 25:6, and it is not what you expect:

> *Say: "It has been sent down by Him Who knows the secret of the heavens and the earth. Truly, He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful."*

Ibn Kathir reads that second sentence as **an invitation, mid-argument**. After all their lies, mockery and slander of the Messenger and the Book — He still opens the door.

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri:** *"Look at this kindness and generosity! They killed His friends and He is calling them to repentance and mercy."*

---

## 4. What They Are Actually Denying (25:10–16)

Allah answers the "why no treasure, no garden" complaint by pointing far past it: *Blessed be He Who, if He wills, will assign you better than all that* — gardens and palaces. (Mujahid notes the Quraysh called any stone house a "palace", large or small.)

Then the diagnosis: **"Nay, they deny the Hour."** The objections were never sincere inquiry. They come from below, from an unwillingness to face the Day.

What follows is one of the most vivid Hell passages in the Qur'an, and Ibn Kathir lets it breathe:

- Hell **sees them** from a far place, and they hear its raging and roaring — out of hatred towards them. Compare 67:7–8: *it almost bursts up with fury*.
- They are thrown into a **narrow place, chained together**. Abdullah bin 'Amr described the narrowness as *"like the point of a spear."* Abu Salih on "chained together": tied from the shoulders.
- They call out *thubur* — destruction — and are told: not one destruction. Many.

Two narrations Ibn Kathir preserves here are worth reading twice.

**Ibn Abbas** (chain graded Sahih): a man is dragged towards Hell, which expands and contracts, and **Ar-Rahman says to it, "What is the matter with you?"** It says, "He is seeking refuge from me." Allah says: *"Let My servant go."* Another is dragged and says, "O Lord, I never expected this from You." — "What did you expect?" — "I expected that Your mercy would be great enough to include me." And Allah says: *"Let My servant go."*

**Ubayd bin Umayr:** Hell will utter a moan such that no angel near to Allah and no Prophet sent to mankind will remain standing — even Ibrahim ﷺ will fall to his knees.

Against that, the contrast (25:15–16): *Is that better, or the Paradise of Eternity?* — theirs as **reward and final destination**, with everything they desire, forever. And the phrase Ibn Kathir dwells on: **Wa'dan Mas'ula** — the linguists say *a binding pledge*, one that must inevitably come to pass.

---

## 5. The Gods Disown Their Worshippers (25:17–19)

On that Day, those who were worshipped are gathered with those who worshipped them. Mujahid says this includes **'Isa, 'Uzayr and the angels**. Allah asks them directly: *Was it you who misled these My servants?*

Their answer is a disavowal: *Glorified be You! It was not for us to take any protectors besides You.* Ibn Kathir gives both readings of the phrase — *it was never right for anyone to worship other than You*, or *it was never right for us to be taken as protectors, for we are Your servants and in need of You* — and notes the meanings converge. Compare 'Isa's answer in 5:116–117.

Then the reason it happened:

> *But You gave them and their fathers comfort, till they forgot the warning, and became a lost people.*

Comfort and long life, uninterrupted, is what made them forget. Ibn Abbas glosses *lost* as **destroyed**; Az-Zuhri: *"There was no good in them."*

And the verdict on those who thought their idols were intercessors: *then you can neither avert the punishment nor find help.*

---

## 6. "Will You Have Patience?" (25:20)

> *And We never sent before you any of the Messengers but verily, they ate food and walked in the markets. And We have made some of you as a trial for others; will you have patience? And your Lord is Ever All-Seer.*

The objection of 25:7 is answered with history: **every** messenger ate, traded, earned a living. That never diminished them; Allah compensated with fine character, true speech, noble deeds, and clear miracles.

Then the pivot — and this is the surah's most quietly demanding line. **We have made some of you a trial for others.** Ibn Kathir cites Muhammad bin Ishaq: *"If I had willed that the world be such that no one would oppose My Messengers, I could have made it so — but I wanted to test My servants by means of them."*

And the hadith in Sahih Muslim from Iyad bin Himar: **"I will test you, and test others by means of you."**

The section closes with the choice the Prophet ﷺ was offered — Prophet *and king*, or servant *and messenger* — and which he chose.

---

## 7. The Day They Do See the Angels (25:21–24)

They demanded angels and demanded to see their Lord. Ibn Kathir's comment: *Indeed they think too highly of themselves.*

They will get the angels. It will not be what they hoped:

> *On the Day they will see the angels — no good news will there be for the criminals that day. And they will say: "Hijran Mahjura."*

Ibn Kathir gives both timings, and refuses to choose: this is true **at death** and true **at the Resurrection**, and there is no contradiction, because the angels appear at both.

The two deathbeds, set side by side:

- To the disbeliever's soul: *"Come out, O evil soul from an evil body — come out to fierce hot wind and boiling water and the shadow of black smoke."* It refuses, and scatters through the body, and they strike him (8:50; 6:93).
- To the believer's soul (hadith of Al-Bara' bin 'Azib): *"Come out, O good soul in a good body, as you were dwelling in it. Come out to rest, and pleasant fragrances, and a Lord Who is not angry."*

**On "Hijran Mahjura":** the root of *hijr* is prevention. Hence *hajara al-qadi* — the judge restrained a bankrupt man from his wealth. Hence **Al-Hijr** beside the Ka'bah, which prevents people from performing tawaf inside it. Hence the mind is called *hijr*, because it prevents a person from what does not befit him. The majority (Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ibn Jarir) read it as the angels' words: *success is forbidden to you this day.*

Then the most sobering image in the surah:

> *And We shall turn to whatever deeds they did, and We shall make such deeds as scattered floating particles of dust.*

**Why** their deeds are voided: because a deed must be both **sincere** and **in accordance with the Shari'ah**. Fail either, and it is futile — and the disbelievers' deeds failed one or both.

The Salaf competed to describe *haba'*:
- **Ali:** the rays of the sun passing through a small aperture. **Al-Hasan** adds: if anyone tries to grasp them, he cannot.
- **Ali** (another chain): the dust raised by animals.
- **Qatadah:** dry leaves blown off a tree by the wind.
- **Ya'la bin Ubayd:** ashes stirred up by the wind.

Compare 14:18 (works as ashes in a fierce wind), 2:264 (dust on a smooth rock, washed bare by rain), 24:39 (a mirage the thirsty man runs to and finds nothing).

Against that: **the dwellers of Paradise have the best abode and the fairest place of repose.** Sa'id bin Jubayr said Allah will complete the Judgement by midday, and the people of Paradise will take their **midday rest** in Paradise. Ikrimah adds the detail — that hour when in this world people return home for a siesta — and says they will be fed the liver of the whale, and all eat their fill.

---

## 8. The Sky Splits (25:25–26)

> *And the Day when the heaven shall be rent asunder with clouds, and the angels will be sent down, with a grand descending.*

The angels of heaven come down and surround all creatures at the place of gathering, and then the Lord comes to pass judgement (cf. 2:210).

**"The sovereignty that Day will be the true sovereignty of the Most Gracious"** — and Ibn Kathir brings the hadith of the folding of the heavens: Allah will fold the heavens in His Right Hand and take the earths in His other Hand, and say:

> *"I am the Sovereign. I am the Judge. Where are the kings of the earth? Where are the tyrants? Where are the arrogant?"*

*And it will be a hard Day for the disbelievers* — hard because it is the Day of exact justice. For the believers: *the greatest terror will not grieve them.*

---

## 9. Biting the Hands (25:27–29)

> *"Oh! Would that I had taken a path with the Messenger. Ah! Woe to me! Would that I had never taken so-and-so as an intimate friend."*

Ibn Kathir notes the occasion — 'Uqbah bin Abi Mu'it, and the friend variously identified as Umayyah or Ubayy bin Khalaf — and then insists on the general ruling: **whether it was revealed about him or someone else, it applies to every wrongdoer.**

The regret is specific: not merely that he disbelieved, but that he had a *friend* who cost him everything. And the friend, when needed, is gone:

> *And Shaytan is to man ever a deserter.*

He leads him to falsehood, uses him for it, calls him to it — and abandons him at the hour of need.

---

## 10. "My People Have Deserted This Qur'an" (25:30)

> *And the Messenger will say: "O my Lord! Verily, my people took this Qur'an as something abandoned."*

This is the most-quoted passage of Ibn Kathir's Furqan, and it deserves to be. He begins with the Makkans' literal behaviour — *"Listen not to this Qur'an, and make noise in the midst of it"* (41:26); when he recited, they would talk over him.

Then he expands *hajr* — desertion — into a ladder, and every rung is a form of it:

1. **Not listening** to it, or drowning it out.
2. **Not believing** in it.
3. **Not pondering** its meanings, or trying to understand it.
4. **Not acting** on it — not following its commands, not heeding its prohibitions.
5. **Turning away from it** in favour of poetry, other speech, songs, idle talk, or any other way.

The complaint is not confined to disbelievers. Any of the five is desertion, and the Prophet's ﷺ words are a complaint *about his own people*.

Ibn Kathir then breaks his own register and prays — one of the rare moments he speaks in the first person:

> *We ask Allah, the Most Generous, the Bestower of bounty, the One Who is able to do what He wills, to keep us safe from doing that which earns His wrath, and to use us to do that which will earn His pleasure — of preserving and understanding His Book, and following its commandments night and day, in the manner which He loves and which pleases Him. For He is Generous and Kind.*

And the consolation immediately after (25:31): **every Prophet was given an enemy among the criminals** (cf. 6:112) — *but sufficient is your Lord as a Guide and a Helper.* Ibn Kathir notes the pairing is exact: they tried to *prevent* guidance and to make their way *prevail*, so Allah answers as **Guide** and as **Helper**.

---

## 11. Why It Came in Pieces (25:32–34)

> *"Why is not the Qur'an revealed to him all at once?" Thus, that We may strengthen your heart thereby. And We have revealed it to you gradually, in stages.*

The objection assumes the Tawrah/Injil/Zabur model. The answer is that gradual revelation was **the point**: over twenty-three years, responsive to events, circumstances, and whatever rulings were needed — *to strengthen the heart of the believers*.

Ibn Abbas' famous statement is cited here: the Qur'an was sent down **all at once to the lowest heaven on Laylat al-Qadr**, and then revealed over twenty years — so both descriptions are true at different levels (cf. 17:106).

And the promise attached: *no example or argument do they bring, but We reveal to you the truth, and the better explanation thereof.* Whatever objection they raise, the answer arrives — clearer and more eloquent than the objection.

**25:34** — those gathered to Hell **on their faces**. A man asked how that could be, and the Prophet ﷺ said: *"The One Who caused him to walk on his two feet is able to make him walk on his face on the Day of Resurrection."*

---

## 12. The Roll-Call of Ruined Towns (25:35–40)

A rapid sequence of warnings, aimed squarely at Makkah:

- **Musa and Harun** — a brother given as a helper, and Fir'awn's people denied them both.
- **Nuh's people** — note the plural: *when they denied the Messengers*, though only Nuh was sent to them. Ibn Kathir's principle: **whoever denies one Messenger denies all of them**, since there is no difference between one and another; had all the Messengers been sent to them, they would have denied every one. Nuh stayed among them **950 years**; *none believed with him except a few*; and they were made *a sign for mankind* (cf. 69:11–12 — the ships you ride are the reminder).
- **'Ad, Thamud, and the Dwellers of Ar-Rass.** On Ar-Rass: Ibn Abbas says they were people of a village of Thamud; Ikrimah says **Ar-Rass was a well in which they buried their Prophet**.
- **"And many generations in between."** Ibn Kathir discusses *qarn*: some said 120 years, some 100, 80, 40 — but the soundest view is that a generation is **a set of contemporaries**; when they pass and others succeed them, that is another generation. Hence: *"The best of generations is my generation, then the one that follows it, then the one that follows that."*

**And for each, We put forward examples** — they were given proof and clear evidence. Qatadah: *"They had no excuse."*

Then the closing image (25:40): the Quraysh **physically walked past Sodom** on their trade routes — *you pass by them in the morning, and at night* (37:137–138) — the town rained with stones of baked clay, and looked at it, and learned nothing.

> *Nay! But they used not to expect any resurrection.*

The ruins were in front of their eyes. Only the absence of belief in the Day made them invisible.

---

## 13. Taking Your Desire as Your God (25:41–44)

Their mockery: *"Is this the one whom Allah has sent as a Messenger?"* — belittling, looking for faults (cf. 21:36, 6:10).

And then a confession they did not notice they were making:

> *"He would have nearly misled us from our gods, had it not been that we were patient and constant in their worship!"*

They boast of *sabr* — in the service of idols. Ibn Kathir's answer is Allah's: *they will know, when they see the torment, who is most astray from the path.*

**25:43** is the surah's sharpest diagnosis:

> *Have you seen him who has taken as his god his own vain desire?*

Ibn Kathir: whatever he admires and sees as good **in his own desires becomes his religion and his way** (cf. 35:8). And Ibn Abbas gives the perfect illustration — *"During the Jahiliyyah, a man would worship a white rock for a while; then if he saw another that looked better, he would worship that and leave the first."*

The judgement (25:44): they are **like cattle — no, further astray**. Why worse? Because cattle do exactly what they were created to do. These were created to worship Allah alone, had evidence established against them and Messengers sent to them — and did otherwise.

---

## 14. The Signs: Shadow, Night, Rain (25:45–50)

Having exposed the false gods, the surah turns to the evidence — and it is all ordinary.

**The shadow (25:45–46).** The Salaf (Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar, Mujahid, Qatadah, and others) read *the shadow* as the stretch from first dawn until sunrise. *If He willed, He could have made it still* — fixed, unchanging. **"But We have made the sun its guide"** — Ibn Kathir's note is quietly philosophical: without the sun rising there would be no shadow at all, *for a thing can only be known in contrast to its opposite*. Then it is withdrawn *gradually* — As-Suddi: a gentle, concealed withdrawal until nothing is left but shade under a roof or a tree. Ayyub bin Musa: *"Little by little."*

**Night, sleep, day (25:47).** Night is a **covering** — it conceals all things (92:1). Sleep is a **halt to movement** so that bodies may rest; the limbs tire from a day of earning, and night's stillness lets them stop. And the day is **Nushur** — the same root as resurrection: people rise and disperse to seek provision. A daily rehearsal of dying and being raised.

**The winds and the water (25:48–50).** The winds are of many kinds by function: some form the clouds, some carry them, some drive them, some run ahead as heralds, some stir up the earth, some seed the clouds to make them rain.

*And We send down pure water* — **tahur**, meaning water that is itself purifying. Hence the hadith about the well of Buda'ah, into which rubbish was thrown: *"Water is pure, and nothing makes it impure."*

Rain revives a land that has waited a long time, until *its hills are covered with all kinds of colourful flowers* (41:39).

And the distribution is deliberate — rain falls on this land and not that, clouds pass over one place to reach another. **Ibn Abbas and Ibn Mas'ud:** *"One year does not have more rain than another, but Allah distributes the rain as He wills."*

Then: *but most men refuse, out of ingratitude.* Ikrimah says this means those who say the rain came *because of such-and-such a star* — which matches the hadith in Sahih Muslim, after a night's rain:

> *"This morning some of My servants became believers in Me and some became disbelievers. Whoever said, 'We have been given rain by the mercy and grace of Allah' — he is a believer in Me and a disbeliever in the stars. And whoever said, 'We have been given rain by such-and-such a star' — he is a disbeliever in Me and a believer in the stars."*

The whole difference between faith and disbelief, located in a single sentence about the weather.

## 15. Jihad with the Qur'an, and the Two Seas (25:51–54)

> *And had We willed, We would have raised a warner in every town. So obey not the disbelievers, but strive against them with it, with the utmost endeavour.*

**"With it"** — Ibn Abbas: **with the Qur'an**. The greatest striving named in this surah is argument with the Book, not the sword. And the "warner in every town" is withheld precisely because **this** Messenger is for all of them (6:19; 11:17; 42:7; 7:158).

**The two seas (25:53).** Sweet water and salt water, kept apart by a *barzakh*, a barrier. Ibn Kathir (with Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Jarir) is emphatic that the sweet water means **rivers, springs and wells** — nowhere in creation is there a sea that is fresh — and that Allah has portioned it out among His creatures *according to their needs, in every land, according to what they need for themselves and their lands*.

Then a remarkable passage of medieval physical geography: the Atlantic and its straits, the Red Sea, the Arabian Sea, the Persian Gulf, the China Sea, the Indian Ocean, the Mediterranean, the Black Sea — their swelling in winter, their tides ebbing and flooding with the lunar month. And a reason for the salt: **so that the air does not turn putrid** from them, and the earth does not spoil from the animals dying in them. *Because its water is salty, its air is healthy and its dead are good to eat* — hence, asked whether seawater may be used for wudu':

> *"Its water is pure and its dead are lawful."*

**Man from water (25:54).** Created from a weak drop, then given kinship **by blood** and kinship **by marriage** — first someone's child, then a husband, then himself a father-in-law with a whole web of relatives. *All of this comes from a despised liquid.*

---

## 16. Trust, and the Name They Refused (25:55–60)

The disbeliever is *ever a helper against his Lord* — Mujahid: **he supports Shaytan in disobedience to Allah**. Ibn Kathir illustrates with 36:74–75: the idols cannot help their worshippers, but the worshippers are marshalled as *troops* for the idols, ready to fight and defend their sanctuaries — and the victory will still belong to Allah, His Messenger, and the believers.

The Messenger's role is bounded: *only a bearer of good news and a warner*, and **"No reward do I ask of you for this"** — nothing from your wealth. Except one thing: *save that whosoever wills may take a path to his Lord.* His only fee is that you take the road.

**25:58** — *And put your trust in the Ever Living One Who dies not.* Everything you rely on will die; He will not. And *glorify His praises* — Ibn Kathir notes the pairing of tasbih with hamd, which is why the Prophet ﷺ said *Subhanaka Allahumma wa bihamdik*.

**25:59** — *The Most Gracious! Ask Him, as He is the All-Knower.* Ibn Kathir reads: **find out about Him from the one who knows Him best.** And no one knows Allah better than His servant and Messenger ﷺ, who does not speak of his own desire. Which means: what accords with his words and deeds is right, and what contradicts them is rejected — *no matter who says or does it.*

**25:60** — *And when it is said to them: "Prostrate to Ar-Rahman!" they say: "And what is Ar-Rahman?"*

They objected to the **Name itself**. Ibn Kathir ties it to Hudaybiyyah: when the Prophet ﷺ dictated *"In the Name of Allah, Ar-Rahman, Ar-Rahim,"* they said, *"We do not know Ar-Rahman or Ar-Rahim — write what you used to write: Bismika Allahumma."* Hence 17:110: *Invoke Allah or invoke Ar-Rahman, by whatever name you invoke Him — to Him belong the Best Names.*

They refused the Mercy in His Name, and *it increased them only in aversion*.

> **Note:** this is a verse of prostration. The scholars agree it is recommended for the reciter and the listener to prostrate here.

---

## 17. Al-Buruj, and the Second Chance Built Into Time (25:61–62)

*Blessed be He Who has placed in the heaven Al-Buruj* — the **giant stars**, per Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Al-Hasan and Qatadah (cf. 67:5). *And a great lamp* — the sun, which burns. *And a moon giving light* — which does not burn but **shines with light borrowed from elsewhere**. Ibn Kathir marks the distinction carefully, citing 10:5 and 71:15–16, where the two words are never interchanged.

Then **khilfatan** — night and day in succession, each following the other in a never-ending alternation, *for such as desires to remember, or desires to show gratitude.*

Ibn Kathir's reading of that phrase is generous and practical: **whoever misses an act of worship in the night can make it up in the day, and whoever misses it in the day can make it up in the night.** Time itself is built as a second chance.

And the hadith that matches it exactly:

> *Allah spreads forth His Hand at night for the one who has done evil during the day to repent, and He spreads forth His Hand during the day for the one who has done evil during the night to repent.*

---

## 18. 'Ibad ar-Rahman — The Servants of the Most Gracious (25:63–74)

The surah has spent sixty verses on people defined by what they refused. It ends with people defined by what they are. Ten qualities:

**1. They walk on the earth *hawna*.** With dignity and humility, not arrogance or pride (cf. 17:37). But Ibn Kathir immediately closes off the misreading: this does **not** mean walking like a sick man, making a show of humility — the leader of the sons of Adam ﷺ *walked as if he were coming downhill, and as if the earth were folded up beneath him*. What is meant is **serenity and dignity**. He proves it with the prayer: *"When you come to the prayer, do not come rushing. Come calmly and with tranquillity; whatever you catch, pray, and whatever you miss, make up."*

**2. When the ignorant address them, they say *salama*.** Insulted, they do not answer in kind. They forgive, overlook, and say nothing but good. Ibn Kathir: *the more ignorant the people, the more patient he ﷺ would be* (cf. 28:55).

**3. They spend the night prostrate and standing.** Their nights are the best of nights (cf. 51:17–18; 32:16; 39:9).

**4. They ask to be spared Hell** — *its torment is ever an inseparable punishment.* **Al-Hasan:** anything that strikes the son of Adam and then departs is not *gharam*; the inseparable punishment is the one that **lasts as long as the heaven and earth**. And note the etiquette here: after nights of standing in prayer, they still ask for rescue rather than assume it.

**5. They spend with balance** — neither extravagant nor stingy. Not spending beyond need, and not withholding from their families what they need. *The best of matters are those which are moderate, neither one extreme nor the other* (cf. 17:29).

**6. No shirk, no unlawful killing, no zina.** Ibn Kathir attaches the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud, who asked which sin is most serious:

> *"That you appoint a rival to Allah, when He has created you."* — Then what? — *"That you kill your child, for fear that he may eat with you."* — Then what? — *"That you commit adultery with your neighbour's wife."*

And Ibn Mas'ud said Allah revealed this very ayah confirming it. On **Athama**: Abdullah bin 'Amr and Ikrimah say it is a **valley in Hell**; As-Suddi says it means the punishment itself, which Ibn Kathir prefers as closer to the apparent meaning, since the next verse explains it as doubled torment and lasting disgrace.

**7. Except those who repent (25:70) — and their evil deeds are *exchanged for good ones*.**

This is the surah's mercy passage, and Ibn Kathir loads it. He affirms that **the repentance of a murderer is accepted**, reconciling this with 4:93 (which he reads as concerning one who does not repent), and citing the man who killed a hundred and was forgiven.

Then two narrations:

- **Abu Dharr**, in Muslim: *"I know the last person to be brought out of Hell and the last to enter Paradise."* His major sins are set aside, and he is questioned on the minor ones — *on such a day you did such and such* — and he confesses, unable to deny any of it. Then he is told: **"For every evil deed, you now have one good merit."** And he says, *"O Lord, I did things that I do not see here."* Abu Dharr said the Messenger of Allah ﷺ **smiled so broadly that his molars could be seen.**
- **Makhul's report:** a very old man with sunken eyes came and said there was no evil deed he had not done; *if his sins were distributed among all mankind, they would all be doomed.* Is there repentance for him? The Prophet ﷺ asked, *"Have you become Muslim?"* He gave the shahadah. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Allah will forgive you all of that, and will replace your evil deeds with good merits."* — *"Even my betrayals and immoral actions?"* — **"Even your betrayals and immoral actions."** The man walked away saying *La ilaha illallah* and *Allahu Akbar*.

**8. They do not bear witness to falsehood (*az-zur*), and pass by idle evil with dignity.** *Zur* covers lies, immorality, disbelief, foul speech and false words; 'Amr bin Qays applies it to gatherings of sexual immorality; others to giving false testimony. Ibn Kathir's reading from the context is that they **do not attend where falsehood occurs at all** — and if they happen to pass it, they do not let it contaminate them in the slightest.

The hadith of Abu Bakrah sits here: *"Shall I not tell you of the greatest of major sins?" — "Associating others with Allah, and disobeying one's parents."* Then he sat up from lying down and added: **"Beware false speech, and bearing witness to falsehood"** — and kept repeating it until the Companions wished he would stop.

**9. When reminded of the Ayat of their Lord, they do not fall deaf and blind.** They are moved by what they hear (cf. 8:2) — unlike those whose response to revelation is to continue exactly as they were, *as if they had not heard* (cf. 9:124–125).

**10. They pray for their families — and for something beyond them:**

> *"Our Lord! Bestow on us from our wives and our offspring the comfort of our eyes, and make us leaders of those who have Taqwa."*

Ibn Abbas: offspring who strive in obedience to Allah, bringing joy in this world *and* the Hereafter. On *"make us leaders"* — Ibn Abbas, Al-Hasan, As-Suddi, Qatadah: **leaders taken as examples in good**; others: guides who call others to goodness. Ibn Kathir's summary of the du'a is beautiful: they wanted their worship to be **connected to the worship of their children**, and their guidance to go beyond themselves and benefit others.

Which is why the hadith of the three continuing deeds is placed here: *When a son of Adam dies, his deeds cease apart from three — a righteous child who prays for him, knowledge others benefit from after him, or ongoing charity.*

> **The Miqdad interruption.** Ibn Kathir preserves a story attached to this ayah that is worth the whole page. A man passing Al-Miqdad bin Al-Aswad said, *"How blessed are these two eyes that saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ! Would that we had seen what you saw."* Al-Miqdad got angry — surprisingly, since the man had said nothing but good — and answered: *What makes a man wish to be present where Allah caused him to be absent, when he does not know how he would have behaved had he been there?* There were people who **saw** the Messenger ﷺ and will be thrown on their faces into Hell, because they did not accept him. *Are you not grateful that Allah brought you out of your mother's womb already believing in your Lord and in what your Prophet brought — and that the test went to others and not to you?* He then described that time: a long ignorance, no religion seen as better than idol worship — until the **Furqan** came and separated truth from falsehood, and separated a father from his son, so that a man knew his own father or brother would go to Hell and could not rest for it. *That,* said Al-Miqdad, *is what this ayah is about.*

## 19. The Reward, and the Last Word (25:75–77)

> *Those will be rewarded with the **Ghurfah** — the highest place — because of their patience. Therein they shall be met with greetings and the word of peace.*

Note the causal word: **because of their patience** — *bima sabaru*. The whole ten-part portrait is classified, in the end, as *sabr*. Abu Ja'far Al-Baqir, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak and As-Suddi say *Ghurfah* is so named for its **elevation**. And *abiding therein* means they will never leave, never move, never die, and **never wish to be anywhere else**.

Then the final verse, addressed to the Makkans, and to everyone:

> *Say: "My Lord pays attention to you only because of your invocation to Him. But now you have indeed denied. So the torment will be yours forever."*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is stark: **He would not care to pay attention to you at all, were it not for your worship** — for mankind was created to worship Him alone and glorify Him morning and evening. Your entire standing with Allah rests on your calling upon Him.

On *lizama* — "forever" — two readings, and Ibn Kathir keeps both: Ibn Mas'ud, Ubayy bin Ka'b, Mujahid, Qatadah and others say it refers to **the day of Badr**; Al-Hasan Al-Basri says **the Day of Resurrection**. *And there is no conflict between the two interpretations.*

## The Thread

Al-Furqan sets two lists against each other and asks you which one you are on.

The first list is made of objections: *why does he eat food, why no angel, why no treasure, why not all at once, what is Ar-Rahman?* Every one is answered, and none of them was ever the real problem — **"Nay, they deny the Hour."** Their deeds, at the end, are dust in a sunbeam: visible, weightless, ungraspable.

The second list is made of habits: walk gently, answer insult with peace, pray at night, ask for rescue, spend in the middle, keep away from falsehood, let the Book actually move you, and pray for your children to outlive you in worship. Nothing on it is spectacular. All of it is called *sabr*, and all of it is rewarded with the highest room.

And running between the two lists is the Prophet's ﷺ complaint that the surah makes impossible to read comfortably: **"My people took this Qur'an as something abandoned."** Ibn Kathir will not let it stay a Makkan grievance. Not listening is desertion. Not reflecting is desertion. Not acting on it is desertion. The Criterion only separates truth from falsehood for someone who has not set it down.

*Blessed be He who sent it down — gradually, in stages, for hearts that needed strengthening slowly.*
