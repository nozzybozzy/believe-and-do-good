*Revealed in Makkah · 60 ayat · "The Scattering Winds" — named for the oath it opens with*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Adh-Dhariyat is an argument with one conclusion, and it states that conclusion in its sixth line: **"And verily, Ad-Din is sure to occur."** Everything else in the surah is either evidence for that claim or a consequence of it.

The structure is unusually clean. Allah swears by four created things and declares the promise true. Then: *this* is what the deniers are like; *this* is what the God-fearing are like; *this* is what happened to four earlier nations who denied; *this* is what the sky and earth and your own body testify to. And then, at the end, the question underneath the whole surah is answered outright — **why any of us are here at all**: *I created not the Jinn and mankind except that they should worship Me.*

---

## 1. Four Oaths, and Ali on the Minbar (51:1–6)

The surah opens on four unnamed things, and Ibn Kathir explains them through one of the great scenes of the early community.

Ali bin Abi Talib ascended the minbar in Kufah and announced that he would explain any ayah of the Book or any Sunnah he was asked about that day. Ibn Al-Kawwa stood and asked him, one after another, about these four oaths:

| Oath | Ali's answer |
|---|---|
| **Adh-Dhariyat** — the scattering | The wind |
| **Al-Hamilat** — the laden | The clouds |
| **Al-Jariyat** — the steady/running | The ships |
| **Al-Muqassimat** — the distributors of command | The angels |

Ibn Kathir then records a second reading of the third: some scholars held **Al-Jariyat Yusra** to be *the stars*, floating in their orbits with ease. He notes what this reading does to the sequence — it makes the four an **ascent**. Winds bring the clouds; the stars are above them; and above all of these are the angels who descend with Allah's legislative orders and the decrees He determines.

Four rungs of creation are sworn by, and the oath lands on one thing: **the Resurrection will come to pass.** The promise is truthful, and the Recompense will surely occur.

## 2. The Sky Above, the Confusion Below (51:7–14)

> *By the heaven full of Hubuk — certainly, you have different ideas.*

**Hubuk** draws two explanations that Ibn Kathir says return to the same meaning. Ibn Abbas (followed by Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah and others): full of beauty, grace, magnificence and perfection. Ad-Dahhak and Al-Minhal bin 'Amr: the *meandering* — the rippled tracks the wind carves in water, sand and plants. Beauty and intricate structure, in other words. The sky is high, clear yet thick, firmly built, spacious, adorned with sun, moon and orbiting planets.

Against that single, coherent, ordered sky, Ibn Kathir sets the incoherence of those who deny it. **"You have different ideas"** — the deniers' opinions are confused and do not conform to each other. Qatadah applies it directly to the Qur'an: some accept it as true, some deny it, and they cannot agree.

Then the diagnosis, which is the sharpest thing in the passage:

- **"Turned aside therefrom is he who is turned aside"** — this confusion only fools those already inwardly misguided. Falsehood becomes convincing only to someone predisposed to it. Ibn Kathir cites 37:161–163: *you cannot lead astray except those who are predetermined to burn.*
- **"Cursed be Al-Kharrasun"** — Mujahid: *the liars*, those who claim they will never be brought back to life. Ibn Abbas: *cursed be the doubters*. Qatadah: the people of doubt and suspicion. Mu'adh, in a sermon: *destroyed be the doubters*.
- **"Under a cover of Sahun"** — Ibn Abbas: sunk in disbelief and doubt, heedless and playful.

They ask, mockingly, *when is the Day of Ad-Din?* The reply names the day by what will happen on it: **Yuftanun** — punished, said Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Al-Hasan; *burnt*, said Ikrimah, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Zayd bin Aslam and Ath-Thawri. Mujahid's image: **just as gold is forged in the fire.** And then: *taste your trial — this is what you used to ask to be hastened.*

## 3. The People of Taqwa, in Four Strokes (51:15–19)

Gardens and springs, receiving with joy what their Lord gives them — *they were, before this, doers of good.* Ibn Kathir then unpacks exactly what that good consisted of, and it is strikingly concrete.

**Stroke one: "They used to sleep but little by night."** Two readings, both preserved:

- *They stayed awake a little of every night* — Ibn Abbas: they worshipped Allah every night, even for a small part of it. Mutarrif bin Abdullah: hardly a night passed without prayer, at its beginning or its middle. Anas bin Malik and Abu Al-Aliyah: they prayed between Maghrib and 'Isha'.
- *They slept only a little* — preferred by Ibn Jarir. Al-Hasan Al-Basri: they prayed voluntarily through the night, active, continuing until they were seeking forgiveness right before dawn.

Two hadiths are placed here. **Abdullah bin Salam**, describing the first words he heard from the Prophet ﷺ on his arrival in Madinah — *"When I saw his face, I knew it was not the face of a liar"*: **feed food, keep ties with kin, spread the Salam, pray at night while people are asleep — and you will enter Paradise in peace.** And from Abdullah bin Umar: there are lofty rooms in Paradise whose outside is visible from within and inside from without — for those who use soft speech, feed food, and spend the night in prayer while people sleep.

**Stroke two: "In the hours before dawn, they were asking for forgiveness."** Mujahid and others: they were praying. Others: they prayed through the night and *deferred* their istighfar to the last hours before Fajr, because forgiveness sought inside the prayer is better (cf. 3:17). Ibn Kathir attaches the hadith of the descent: **each night, when the last third remains, Allah descends to the lowest heaven** — *Is there anyone repenting, that I may accept his repentance? Anyone seeking forgiveness, that I may forgive him? Anyone asking of Me, that I may grant him?* — until Fajr begins. Many mufassirun add that when Ya'qub told his sons *I will ask my Lord for forgiveness for you* (12:98), he waited for these same hours.

**Stroke three: "In their wealth there was a right."** Not charity as overflow — **a designated portion**, set aside as an obligation. It belongs to two people:

- **The Sa'il** — the poor person who asks, and who has a due right.
- **The Mahrum** — Ibn Abbas and Mujahid: the poor person who receives no stipend from the treasury, has no income and no profession. A'ishah: the displaced person, with no trade to earn from. Qatadah and Az-Zuhri: **the one who does not ask people for anything.**

Az-Zuhri's addition is the hadith that reframes the whole category: *the poor person is not the one who goes round asking for a mouthful or a date or two. The poor is the one who has not enough for his needs and whose condition is not known to others, so that others might give to him.* The Mahrum is invisible by definition — which is why the obligation has to be pre-assigned rather than triggered by a request.

## 4. Signs Outside You, Signs Inside You (51:20–23)

> *And on the earth are signs for those who have faith with certainty. And also in yourselves. Will you not then see?*

Ibn Kathir reads the earth as evidence: plants, animals, valleys, mountains, deserts, rivers, oceans. Then humanity — different languages, colours, intentions, abilities, powers of comprehension, deeds, and ultimately happiness or misery. And more intimately still: **every organ placed exactly where it is most needed.** Qatadah's comment is the memorable one: *whoever thinks about his own creation will realise he was made with flexible joints, so that acts of worship would be easy for him.*

**"In the heaven is your provision"** — rain. **"And that which you are promised"** — Paradise (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and others).

Then the surah's most human oath. Allah swears **by His own honourable Self** that all of it — Judgement, Resurrection, Recompense — is as true **"just as you can speak."** Do not doubt its coming any more than you doubt that you are speaking right now. Mu'adh used the same measure with friends: *"What I am saying is as true as your being here."*

## 5. Ibrahim's Guests: A Masterclass in Hospitality (51:24–30)

Three angels — **Jibril, Mika'il and Israfil** — come to Ibrahim in the form of handsome young men, which is why he says *"a people unknown to me."*

The first lesson is in a single letter. They greet with **Salaman**; he answers with **Salamun**. Ibn Kathir points out that the second form is the stronger, more permanent construction — so the Friend of Allah returned the greeting *with better than it*, exactly as 4:86 commands.

Then the second lesson, which Ibn Kathir treats as a complete etiquette of hosting, drawn from three short phrases. Ibrahim:

- turned aside **discreetly and in haste** — the guests did not know food was being made for them;
- did not announce the favour first (*"we will make food for you"*);
- brought out **the best he had** — a young, fat calf, roasted on hot coals;
- **placed it near them** rather than setting it down and inviting them to come to it;
- and did not command them to eat, but invited them with the gentlest possible phrasing: **"Will you not eat?"**

When their hands did not reach for it, he conceived fear — explained by the parallel passage at 11:70–71. They reassure him and give glad tidings of a son having knowledge. His wife comes forward with a cry, **strikes her own face** — Ibn Abbas: as women do when confronted with something astonishing — and says *"A barren old woman!"* Her objection is doubled: not only am I old, I was barren even when young. The reply closes it: *Even so says your Lord. He is the All-Wise, the All-Knower* — All-Knowing of the honour you are worthy of, All-Wise in what He decides.

## 6. Lut, and the Marked Stones (51:31–37)

The same delegation is on its way to a second errand. Ibn Kathir first recalls 11:74–76 — Ibrahim, once the fear left him and the good news reached him, **began to plead for the people of Lut**, being *forbearing, given to invoking Allah with humility, and repentant* — and was told to forsake it; the command had gone forth.

The stones sent down are **marked** — and Ibn Kathir records the explanation that they were recorded with Allah by name: **each stone carried the name of its companion.** The believers are brought out first: Lut and his family, except his wife (29:32). *But We found not there any household of the Muslims except one* — a single house in an entire city.

And the site itself is left as evidence: the dwelling place made a putrid, dead sea — *a sign for those who fear the painful torment.*

## 7. Four Ruins in Nine Verses (51:38–46)

The surah now moves at speed, and the pace is the point.

- **Fir'awn** — Musa came with manifest authority; Fir'awn *turned away along with his hosts*, in rebellion and arrogance (Ibn Kathir cites 22:9, "bending his neck in pride"), and called him **"a sorcerer, or a madman."** He and his armies were dumped into the sea, *for he was blameworthy* — a denying sinner, a stubborn disbeliever worthy of blame.
- **'Ad** — *the barren wind*: it destroys everything and produces nothing (Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah). It spared nothing it reached, leaving only broken, rotted ruin. Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib called it a southerly wind, but Ibn Kathir cites the Sahih hadith from Ibn Abbas: **"I have been made victorious with the Saba (easterly wind), and 'Ad were destroyed with the Dabur (westerly wind)."**
- **Thamud** — *"Enjoy yourselves for a while"*: Ibn Kathir explains this as **the three days of respite** during which they waited for the punishment. In the early morning of the fourth day the Sa'iqah took them while they watched. They could neither rise and escape it nor save themselves. Compare 41:17: *We guided them, but they preferred blindness to guidance.*
- **The people of Nuh**, before all of them — *a people who were rebellious.*

## 8. The Built Sky, the Spread Earth, the Paired Everything (51:47–51)

> *With Hands We constructed the heaven... And We have made the earth a Firash.*

**"With Hands"** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah and Ath-Thawri all read this as **with strength/power**. The heaven is a high roof, protected from falling, raised without pillars, hanging independently — and *We are able to extend its vastness*.

The earth is a **Firash**, a bedding or resting place, spread out for its inhabitants — *how excellent a spreader are We*.

**"And of everything We have created pairs."** Ibn Kathir lists them, and the list is worth reading slowly: heaven and earth, night and day, sun and moon, land and sea, light and darkness, faith and disbelief, death and life, misery and happiness, Paradise and Fire — and then the animals and plants. Everything created comes in twos. Only the Creator is One.

Which produces the surah's imperative: **"So, flee to Allah."** Seek shelter with Him, says Ibn Kathir, and trust in Him in all of your affairs. And *set not up any other god along with Him* — the two commands framed on both sides by the same sentence, repeated verbatim: *Verily, I am a plain warner to you from Him.*

## 9. The Purpose Verse (51:52–60)

The consolation first. **No Messenger came to any earlier people but they said the same two words: "a sorcerer, or a madman."** Have they passed this script down to each other? No — *they are a people transgressing beyond bounds*. Tyrannical hearts are alike, so the words come out alike, without needing to be taught.

Hence: **"Turn away from them, you are not blameworthy"** — turn from the Quraysh idolaters; you are not blamed for it. **"And remind"** — because only believing hearts profit from being reminded.

Then the verse the whole surah has been walking toward:

> *And I created not the Jinn and mankind except that they should worship Me.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss guards it from a misreading: *I created them only so that I would order them to worship Me* — **not because I need them.** Ibn Abbas, via Ali bin Abi Talhah: so that they worship Me, willingly or unwillingly.

The next ayat make the independence explicit: *I seek not any provision from them, nor do I ask that they should feed Me. Verily, Allah is the All-Provider, Owner of power, the Most Strong.* Ibn Kathir notes that the Prophet ﷺ taught this line as a phrase to be recited (via Ibn Mas'ud; collected by Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i, graded *Hasan Sahih*).

The relationship runs entirely one way — He is alone their Creator and Provider, and they need Him in every condition. Which is why the divine saying Ibn Kathir places here lands so hard (from Abu Hurayrah, collected by At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah):

> **"O Son of Adam! Busy yourself in worshipping Me, and I will fill your chest with riches and dissipate your meekness. Otherwise, I will fill your chest with distracting affairs and will not do away with your meekness."**

The surah closes where it began: wrongdoers will get their due share of torment, like the share their predecessors received — *so let them not ask Me to hasten it*, for it will surely come. **Then woe to those who disbelieve from their Day which they have been promised** — the Day of Resurrection.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

Adh-Dhariyat swears by winds, clouds, ships and angels that **the Recompense is coming**, then spends fifty verses proving the point with a sky you can look at, an earth you can walk on, a body whose joints bend so that you can bow, and four nations whose ruins you can visit.

Between the deniers and the delivered, the surah draws the line by **habit, not belief**: the saved are the ones who sleep little, seek forgiveness in the dark before dawn, and have already assigned a portion of their wealth to a poor man who will never ask for it.

And when the reason for all of it is finally given, it is a single sentence — *I created not the Jinn and mankind except that they should worship Me* — immediately protected from being misunderstood as divine need. He gains nothing. **The worship was always the gift, not the payment.**
