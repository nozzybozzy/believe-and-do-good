*Revealed in Makkah · 54 ayat · named for the Yemeni kingdom whose two gardens and broken dam are described at 34:15–19*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Saba' is a Makkan surah built around a single question the Quraysh kept throwing at the Prophet ﷺ: *when will the Hour come, and who told you it would?* The surah answers by moving between two registers. In the first, it makes claims about **knowledge** — Allah knows what enters the earth and what leaves it, and nothing the weight of a dust-speck escapes Him, so scattered bones are no obstacle to resurrection. In the second, it makes claims about **gratitude** — and to do that it sets two histories side by side.

One is the house of Dawud and Suleiman, given mountains that glorified with them, iron soft as thread, wind that carried a month's journey before noon — and told: *Work, O family of Dawud, with thanks.*

The other is Saba' — given a dam, two gardens, towns so close together you could travel from Yemen to Syria without carrying provisions — who said instead: *Our Lord, make the stages between our journeys longer.*

Between the two verdicts sits the line the whole surah turns on: **"But few of My servants are grateful."**

---

## 1. Praise in Both Houses (34:1–2)

The surah opens by claiming praise for Allah in *this* world and in the Hereafter — because, Ibn Kathir explains, He is the Giver and Bestower in both, the Sovereign and Controller of all (cf. 28:70, 92:13). Praise in the Hereafter is not a repetition: there He will be worshipped forever and praised for eternity.

Then the knowledge claim, in four directions:

- **What goes into the earth** — raindrops sinking to its depths, seeds sown, things hidden in it
- **What comes forth from it** — how many, how they grow, what they look like
- **What descends from the sky** — rain and provision
- **What ascends to it** — righteous deeds, and other things

**Az-Zuhri** compresses the two closing names into a sentence: *He is All-Aware of His creation, All-Wise in His commands.*

And the ayah ends not with a threat but with mercy: **the Most Merciful, the Oft-Forgiving** — meaning He does not hasten to punish, and He forgives those who repent and rely on Him.

---

## 2. One of Three Oaths in the Qur'an (34:3)

Ibn Kathir flags this verse as belonging to a set of exactly three — *there is no fourth* — where Allah commands His Messenger to **swear by his Lord that the resurrection is real**, precisely because the deniers were so stubborn:

| Surah | The oath |
|---|---|
| **Yunus 10:53** | *"Yes! By my Lord! It is the very truth, and you cannot escape it."* |
| **Saba' 34:3** | *"Yes, by my Lord, it will come to you."* |
| **At-Taghabun 64:7** | *"Yes! By my Lord, you will certainly be resurrected, then informed of what you did."* |

The oath is immediately backed by the reason it can be sworn: *not even the weight of a speck of dust escapes His knowledge.*

**Mujahid and Qatadah:** nothing is hidden or concealed from Him. Ibn Kathir draws the conclusion the deniers were resisting — bones may scatter and disintegrate, but **He knows where each has gone and where it dispersed**, and will bring them back as He created them the first time.

---

## 3. Why the Hour Exists at All (34:4–6)

Not as spectacle, but as **recompense**. Two outcomes, stated flatly:

- Those who believe and do righteous deeds → *forgiveness and generous provision*
- Those who strive against His signs to frustrate them (i.e. turn others away from the path) → *a severe painful torment*

Ibn Kathir supports the principle with 59:20 (*not equal are the dwellers of the Fire and the dwellers of Paradise*) and 38:28 (*shall We treat those who believe like those who cause mischief?*).

Then a third kind of wisdom in 34:6: **those given knowledge will see** that what was revealed is the truth — what they knew from the Books of Allah in this world, they will now watch with their own eyes, and they will say, *Indeed the Messengers of our Lord did come with the truth* (7:43); *This is what the Most Gracious promised, and the Messengers spoke truth* (36:52).

---

## 4. "Either a Liar or a Madman" (34:7–9)

The Quraysh's argument, reconstructed by Ibn Kathir as they meant it: *when your bodies have crumbled into the earth and dispersed without a trace, this man says you will be made new again.* So he is doing one of two things — **deliberately fabricating** and attributing it to Allah, or **not deliberate but deluded**, as the insane are.

Allah's reply reverses the accusation: *Nay, but those who disbelieve in the Hereafter are themselves in a torment, and in far error.*

- **in a torment** — their disbelief, which is what will lead them to the torment of Allah
- **in far error** — far from the truth, in this world

And then a warning built out of geography: *See they not what is before them and what is behind them, of the heaven and the earth?* Ibn Kathir: **wherever they turn, the sky is above them and the earth beneath them** (cf. 51:47–48). If He willed, He could sink the earth with them or drop a fragment of heaven on them — *but We delay it because We are Patient and Forgiving.*

The verse closes with a sign *for every Munib servant*:

- **Ma'mar ← Qatadah:** al-Munib is everyone who repents
- **Sufyan ← Qatadah:** al-Munib is the one who turns to Allah

The sign is an argument from the greater to the lesser: the One able to create these heavens with their vast reaches, and this earth as deep and wide as it is, **is able to revive decayed bones** (36:81, 40:57).

---

## 5. Dawud: The Voice, and the Iron (34:10–11)

Two miracles, and both are about **hardness yielding**.

**The voice.** When Dawud glorified Allah, the firm high mountains joined him, and the free-roaming birds — out in the morning, back in the evening — stopped for him. Ibn Kathir notes he was given Prophethood, kingship, huge numbers of troops, and the ability to speak all languages.

*Awwibi* (glorify) — per Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and others — comes from a root (*ta'wib*) meaning **to repeat or respond**: the mountains and birds were commanded to answer him back.

The hadith Ibn Kathir attaches here is the loveliest thing in the passage. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ heard Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari reciting at night, stopped, listened, and said:

> *This man has been given one of the sweet melodious voices of the family of Dawud.*

**Abu Uthman An-Nahdi:** *I never heard any cymbal, stringed instrument or chord more beautiful than the voice of Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari.*

**The iron.** **Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Qatadah, Al-A'mash** and others: he needed no fire to heat it and no hammer to beat it — **he simply twisted it in his hands, like a thread**.

And the craft that came out of it:

- **Qatadah:** Dawud was the **first person ever to make chain mail**; before him, they wore plated armour.
- **Mujahid** on *balance well the rings*: don't set the rivets so loose that the rings shake, nor so tight they can't move — **make it just right**.
- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah): *Sard* means a ring of iron.

The instruction that closes it is the surah's real subject: *and work righteousness* — meaning, **with regard to what Allah has given you of blessings** — *truly I am All-Seer of what you do.*

---

## 6. Suleiman: Wind, Copper, and Jinn (34:12–13)

The inheritance continues and enlarges. The wind carried his carpet a month's journey each way.

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri** gives it as an itinerary: *he set out from Damascus in the morning, landed at Istakhar where he ate a meal, then flew on and spent the night in Kabul* — each leg a full month's travel for a swift rider.

- **The fount of Qitr** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ata' Al-Khurasani, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Malik ← Zayd bin Aslam and others: **copper**. Qatadah adds: it was in Yemen.
- **The jinn** worked before him *by the leave of his Lord* — by Allah's decree and subjugation, not Suleiman's own power — and any who tried to rebel would taste the torment of the blazing Fire.

What they built:

| Term | Meaning per the Salaf |
|---|---|
| **Maharib** | Beautiful structures — the best and innermost part of a dwelling. *Ibn Zayd:* dwellings. |
| **Tamathil** | Images. *Atiyah Al-Awfi, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi.* |
| **Jawab** | Plural of *Jabiyah* — cisterns or tanks holding water |
| **Qudur Rasiyat** | Cauldrons so large they stay in one place and are never moved. *Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak.* |

---

## 7. "Work, O Family of Dawud, With Thanks" (34:13)

Ibn Kathir treats this as the hinge of the surah, and reads it as a command: *Work with thanks for the blessings We have bestowed on you in this world and the Hereafter.* Which establishes the principle that **thanks is expressed by actions as much as by words and intentions**.

**Abu Abdur-Rahman Al-Hubuli:** *Prayer is thanks, fasting is thanks, every good deed you do for the sake of Allah is thanks — and the best of thanks is praise.*

The hadith of the two Sahihs, on how that family actually worked:

> The most beloved prayer to Allah is the prayer of Dawud: he slept half the night, stood in prayer a third of it, and slept a sixth.
> The most beloved fasting to Allah is the fasting of Dawud: he fasted a day and not the next — **and he never fled the battlefield.**

Then the exchange narrated by **Fudayl** (via Ibn Abi Hatim), which resolves the whole problem of gratitude:

> Dawud said: *"O Lord! How can I thank You, when thanks itself is a blessing from You?"*
> He said: *"Now you have truly given thanks to Me — for you have realised that it is a blessing from Me."*

And the verdict that follows immediately: **"But few of My servants are grateful."** Ibn Kathir's comment is one line: *This is a reflection of reality.*

---

## 8. The Death of Suleiman, and What It Exposed (34:14)

Allah concealed his death from the jinn who were subjugated to hard labour under him. He remained **leaning on his staff** — so **Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah** and others — for a long time, **nearly a year**, while they kept working.

Then a creature of the earth, a worm, ate through the stick; it weakened and he fell.

The point is not the worm. The point is what fell with him: it became clear to jinn and men alike that **the jinn do not know the unseen**, as they had imagined and as they had tried to make people believe. *It became clear to the people that they were lying.*

---

## 9. Who Saba' Were (34:15)

Saba' refers to the kings and people of the Yemen. The **Tababa'ah (Tubba')** were of them, and **Bilqis**, the queen who met Suleiman, was one of them.

Ibn Kathir records the hadith of Farwah bin Musayk Al-Ghutayfi (via Ibn Jarir; At-Tirmidhi has it in fuller form and calls it *Hasan Gharib*). A man asked: *O Messenger of Allah, tell me about Saba' — was it a land or a woman?*

> *It was neither a land nor a woman. It was a man who had ten children, six of whom went to the Yemen and four to Ash-Sham.*

| Went to Ash-Sham | Went south (Yemen) |
|---|---|
| Lakhm, Judham, Amilah, **Ghassan** | Kindah, Al-Ash'ariyyun, **Al-Azd**, Madhhij, Himyar, Anmar |

Asked who Anmar were, he said: *those among whom are Khath'am and Bajilah.*

The genealogists, Muhammad bin Ishaq among them: Saba's name was **Abd Shams bin Yashjub bin Yarub bin Qahtan**. He was called *Saba'* because he was the first Arab tribe to **disperse**, and *Ar-Ra'ish* because he was the first to take booty in war and give it to his people (the Arabs call wealth *rish* or *riyash*). The three views on Qahtan's own descent — from Iram bin Sam bin Nuh, from 'Abir (another name for Hud), or from Isma'il — are noted and referred to Ibn Abdul-Barr's book for detail.

Ibn Kathir then draws the line that would have mattered most to the first audience: **the Ansar — both Aws and Khazraj — were from Ghassan, from the Arabs of Yemen, from Saba'**, who settled in Yathrib when Saba' was scattered by the flood. Hence the Prophet ﷺ passing a group of Aslam practising archery and saying, *Shoot, O sons of Isma'il, for your father was an archer.*

And Hassan bin Thabit's line, on why they were called Ghassan — the name of the water beside which they camped: *If you ask, then we are the community of the noble descendants; our lineage is Al-Azd and our water is Ghassan.*

*Ten sons among the Arabs* means these ten were **of his lineage**, not born of his loins — there may have been two or three generations between him and some of them, or more.

---

## 10. The Dam of Ma'arib, and the Life It Made (34:15)

The water came down between two mountains, gathered from rainfall and the valleys. Their ancient kings built a huge, strong dam, the water rose high between the two mountains, and they planted trees — *the best fruits that could ever be harvested, plentiful and beautiful.*

**Qatadah** and a number of the Salaf preserve the detail that makes the loss intelligible: a woman could walk beneath the trees with a basket on her head, and **the fruit would fall and fill it without her needing to pick anything**, because it was so plentiful and ripe.

Others said there were **no flies, mosquitoes, fleas or vermin** in their land — the weather was good, the people healthy, and Allah took care of them *so that they would single out and worship Him alone*.

This was Ma'arib, three days' journey from San'a'. The two gardens *on the right and on the left* were the two mountain flanks, with their land between them. And the terms were stated plainly: **"Eat of the provision of your Lord, and be grateful to Him. A fair land and an Oft-Forgiving Lord"** — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, *He would forgive you if you continue to worship Him alone.*

---

## 11. The Rats, the Flood, and the Bitter Fruit (34:16–17)

*But they turned away* — from worshipping Allah alone and from thanking Him — **and began worshipping the sun instead**, exactly as the hoopoe reported to Suleiman: *I found her and her people worshipping the sun instead of Allah, and Shaytan has made their deeds fair-seeming to them* (27:22–24).

How the dam went, per **Ibn Abbas, Wahb bin Munabbih, Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak**: Allah sent beasts to it — **large rats** — which bored a hole through it.

**Wahb bin Munabbih** adds the detail that turns it into a parable about the futility of foreknowledge without obedience:

> They found it written in their Scriptures that the dam would be destroyed by these rats. So they brought cats, for a while. But **when the decree came to pass, the rats overran the cats**, went into the dam, made a hole in it, and it collapsed.

**Qatadah:** the rats gnawed at the base until it weakened; then the flood season came, the water struck the structure and it gave way, tearing down the valley and destroying everything in its path — buildings, trees, all of it.

The water drained away from the trees on the mountain flanks, and they dried up. The replacement is itemised:

- ***Ukul khamt*** — **Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ata' Al-Khurasani, Qatadah, As-Suddi:** Arak and bitter, bad fruit
- ***Athl*** — **Al-Awfi ← Ibn Abbas:** tamarisk; others, a tree resembling it, or gum acacia
- ***A few lote trees*** — and Ibn Kathir catches the sting in the phrasing: because the lote was **the best of the replacement trees, there were only a few of them**

Two gardens of shade, flowing water and beautiful views, exchanged for thorn trees with huge thorns and little fruit.

*Never do We requite in such a way except those who are ungrateful.* **Mujahid:** He does not punish anyone except the disbelievers. **Al-Hasan Al-Basri:** *Allah has spoken the truth — no one will be punished in a manner that befits the sin except the ungrateful disbelievers.*

---

## 12. "Make the Stages Longer" (34:18–19)

Before the flood, their trade route was the second blessing. Between Saba' and the blessed towns — **the towns of Syria**, per Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Malik ← Zayd bin Aslam, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd; **Al-Awfi ← Ibn Abbas** adds that they were blessed *by putting Jerusalem among them* — lay a chain of towns visible one from the next.

So they carried **no provisions and no water**. Wherever they stopped there was water and fruit; they could take their noon rest in one town and pass the night in another. *Travel in them safely, both by night and by day.*

Then the request that names their sickness: ***"Our Lord! Make the stages between our journeys longer"*** — *and they wronged themselves.*

**Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan** and others: they wanted to travel long distances through empty wilderness, where they would have to carry provisions and cross intense heat in a state of fear. They asked, in short, **for the blessing to be removed** because they had stopped feeling it.

They got it. *So We made them as tales, and dispersed them all totally* — something for people to talk about in the evening: how Allah plotted against them and scattered them after a life of luxury. Hence the Arab proverb, said of any people broken apart: **"They have been scattered like Saba'."**

---

## 13. Signs for the Steadfast and Grateful (34:19)

The closing clause of that verse — *signs for every steadfast, grateful person* — pulls Ibn Kathir into the surah's best hadith cluster, on the two-sided temperament of the believer.

Imam Ahmad ← Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas:

> *I am amazed at what Allah has decreed for the believer: if something good befalls him he praises his Lord and gives thanks, and if something bad befalls him he praises his Lord and has patience. **The believer will be rewarded for everything — even the morsel of food he lifts to his wife's mouth.***

And the corroborating hadith of Abu Hurayrah in the two Sahihs:

> *How amazing is the affair of the believer! Allah does not decree anything for him but it is good for him. If something good happens, he gives thanks, and that is good for him; if something bad happens, he bears it with patience, and that is good for him. **This is not for anyone except the believer.***

**Mutarrif** (via Qatadah) states the same thing as a beatitude: *How blessed is the grateful, patient servant. If he is given something he gives thanks; if he is tested he bears it with patience.*

Note the placement. This comes **directly after** the story of a people who could not do either.

---

## 14. Iblis Was Right About Them (34:20–21)

*And indeed Iblis did prove true his thought about them.* **Ibn Abbas** links it to what Iblis said outright after refusing to prostrate:

> *If You give me respite to the Day of Resurrection, I will surely seize and mislead his offspring, all but a few* (17:62)
> *Then I will come to them from before them and behind them, from their right and from their left, and You will not find most of them as thankful ones* (7:17)

He was making a **prediction**, and it came true — *and they followed him, all except a group of true believers.*

But: *he had no authority over them.* **Ibn Abbas:** this means **he had no proof**. No compulsion, no evidence, only a suggestion that most people chose to accept.

So why permit it at all? *Except that We might test him who believes in the Hereafter from him who is in doubt about it.* The permission is diagnostic — it separates those who worship their Lord properly in this world from those who are unsure the reckoning is coming.

*And your Lord is Watchful over everything* — Ibn Kathir's gloss cuts both ways: **despite His watching, the followers of Iblis go astray; and by His watching and care, the believers who follow the Messengers are saved.**

---

## 15. Gods Who Own Nothing (34:22–23)

A demolition in four moves. Call on whomever you assert besides Allah — they:

1. **possess not even a dust-speck's weight** in the heavens or the earth (cf. 35:13, *they own not even a Qitmir*, the thin membrane on a date stone)
2. have **no share** in either — not independently, not as partners
3. offer Him **no support** — on the contrary, all of creation is in need of Him
4. cannot even **intercede** without permission (2:255, 53:26, 21:28)

On the fourth, Ibn Kathir brings in the hadith of **Al-Maqam Al-Mahmud**: the Messenger ﷺ, the leader of the sons of Adam and the greatest intercessor, will not begin by interceding. He will prostrate, and be left prostrate as long as Allah wills, and be inspired with words of praise — and only then:

> *O Muhammad, raise your head. **Speak, you will be heard; ask, you will be given; intercede, your intercession will be accepted.***

---

## 16. When Fear Is Banished From Their Hearts (34:23)

The image of what happens in heaven when Allah speaks. The inhabitants hear it and tremble so violently that they swoon (**Ibn Mas'ud, Masruq**). When the fear lifts, they ask one another: *What is it that your Lord has said?* And the answer is passed down, exactly, without addition or subtraction: **"The truth."**

Al-Bukhari's hadith from Abu Hurayrah, which Ibn Kathir gives in full:

> When Allah decrees a matter in heaven, the angels **beat their wings in submission to His words, making a sound like a chain striking a smooth rock**. When the fear is banished from their hearts they say: *What is it that your Lord has said?* They say: *the truth — and He is the Most High, the Most Great.*
> Then the eavesdropper hears it. The eavesdroppers stand **one above the other** — Sufyan demonstrated by holding his hand vertically with the fingers outspread — and each passes it to the one below, until it reaches the lips of the soothsayer.
> Maybe a meteor strikes him first, maybe he passes it on first. **He tells a hundred lies alongside it** — but when one thing comes true, people say *did he not tell us that on such a day such a thing would happen?* — and they believe him, **because of the one thing that was heard from heaven.**

This is the surah's account of how false claims to the unseen actually work: one stolen fragment, ninety-nine fabrications, and a reputation built on the arithmetic.

---

## 17. "One of Us Is Wrong" (34:24–27)

*Who gives you provision from the heavens and the earth? Say: Allah.* They already admitted it — no one but Allah sent down water and made crops grow — so the conclusion should follow: **no god is worthy of worship besides Him**.

Then the fairest sentence in the surah's polemic: ***And verily, either we or you are rightly guided, or in plain error.***

Ibn Kathir refuses to soften it into pluralism: *one of the two sides must be speaking falsehood and one must be telling the truth. There is no way you and we could both be following true guidance, or both be misguided.*

- **Qatadah:** the Companions said this to the idolaters — *by Allah, we and you cannot be following the same thing; only one of us can be truly guided.*
- **Ikrimah and Ziyad bin Abi Maryam:** it means *we are rightly guided and you are in plain error.*

*You will not be asked about our sins, nor shall we be asked of what you do* — a formal disowning (cf. 10:41, and Surah Al-Kafirun entire): **respond and you belong to us; reject and we have nothing to do with you.**

The dispute is then adjourned rather than settled: *Our Lord will assemble us all together, then judge between us with truth* — He is **Al-Fattah, Al-'Alim**, the Just Judge, the All-Knower of the true state of affairs (cf. 30:14–16).

---

## 18. Sent to All Mankind (34:28–30)

*We have not sent you except as a giver of glad tidings and a warner to all mankind* — to all of creation among those accountable for their deeds (7:158, 25:1). **Muhammad bin Ka'b:** to all the people. **Qatadah:** to Arabs and non-Arabs alike, *so the most honoured of them with Allah is the one most obedient to Allah.*

The hadith of Jabir, in the two Sahihs — five things given to no Prophet before him:

- Aided by fear cast **a month's journey** ahead
- **The whole earth made a Masjid and a means of purification**, so any of his Ummah may pray wherever the prayer time finds him
- The **spoils of war** made lawful
- **Intercession**
- The Prophets before were sent to their own people — **he was sent to all mankind**

And: *I have been sent to the black and the red* — **Mujahid:** to jinn and mankind; others: to Arabs and non-Arabs. Ibn Kathir: **both meanings are correct.**

To *"when is this promise?"* the answer is not a date but a fixed appointment (cf. 42:18): **an appointed Day you cannot put back by an hour nor bring forward** (71:4, 11:104–105).

---

## 19. The Argument in the Fire (34:31–33)

The surah stages the future quarrel between followers and leaders — Allah *threatens them with the humiliating position they will be in before Him, arguing and disputing with one another.*

| The weak (the followers) | The arrogant (leaders and masters) |
|---|---|
| *Had it not been for you, we would certainly have been believers* — i.e. if you hadn't stopped us, we would have followed the Messengers | *Did we keep you back from guidance after it had come to you? Nay, but you were criminals* — i.e. we only called; you followed **without evidence**, against the proof the Messengers brought, because of your own desires. **It was your own choice.** |
| *Nay, but it was your **plotting by night and day**, when you ordered us to disbelieve in Allah and set up rivals to Him* — tempting us with promises and false hopes, fabricating far-fetched ideas to lead us astray | |

**Qatadah and Ibn Zayd** (and Malik ← Zayd bin Aslam similarly) read *plotting by night and day* simply: *you plotted by night and day.*

Then: *each of them will conceal their own regrets when they behold the torment* — **both leaders and followers**. Iron collars — a chain tying their hands to their necks. And no injustice in the distribution: **the leaders punished for what they did, the followers for what they did**, as in *For each one there is double torment, but you know not* (7:38).

Ibn Abi Hatim ← Abu Hurayrah: *When the people of Hell are driven towards it, it will meet them with its flames.*

---

## 20. Wealth Is Not a Verdict (34:34–39)

Ibn Kathir extracts a historical law from 34:34 and consoles the Prophet ﷺ with it: **no Prophet was ever sent to a township but its affluent denied him, and the weaker people followed him.**

- Nuh's people: *shall we believe in you, when the weakest follow you?* (26:110); *we see none follow you but the meanest among us* (11:27)
- Salih's people: the arrogant said to the weak who believed, *verily we disbelieve in that which you believe in* (7:75–76)
- *Is it these that Allah has favoured from among us?* (6:53); *We have set up in every town great ones of its wicked people to plot therein* (6:123); *when We decide to destroy a town, We send a definite order to those who lead a life of luxury* (17:16)

**Qatadah** on *those given the worldly wealth and luxuries*: **they are their tyrants, chiefs and leaders in evil.**

Their theology was simple: *We are more in wealth and in children, and we are not going to be punished* — treating prosperity as evidence of Allah's approval, and therefore as insurance against the Hereafter. Ibn Kathir calls this **too far-fetched**, and answers with four passages: 23:55–56 (*do they think that in wealth and children We hasten unto them with good things? Nay, they perceive not*), 9:55, 74:11–17 (the man given resources, sons and a smooth life who still wants more), and the owner of the two gardens, whose wealth and children could not help him when it was all taken **before** he even reached the Hereafter.

The correction (34:37): *it is not your wealth nor your children that bring you nearer to Us, but only he who believes and does righteous deeds.* Imam Ahmad ← Abu Hurayrah:

> *Allah does not look at your outward appearance or your wealth; rather He looks at your hearts and your deeds.*

For those, **multiplied reward** — ten to seven hundred times — and *the high dwellings, in peace and security*: the lofty rooms of Paradise, safe from misery, fear and harm. Ibn Abi Hatim ← Ali: rooms *whose outside can be seen from the inside and whose inside from the outside.* A bedouin asked who they were for:

> *For those who **speak well, feed the hungry, persist in fasting, and pray at night while people are asleep**.*

On why provision differs at all (34:39): He expands and restricts by wisdom no one but Him can comprehend — and the inequality of this world previews the inequality of the next (*the Hereafter will be greater in degrees*, 17:21): one in the highest apartments of Paradise, another in the lowest reaches of Hell.

The Prophet ﷺ, describing the best-off person in this world:

> *He truly succeeds who becomes Muslim, is given just enough provision, and Allah makes him content with what He has given.* (Muslim)

And on spending — *whatsoever you spend of anything, He will replace it*:

- *Allah says: **Spend, I will spend on you.***
- Every morning two angels descend; one says *O Allah, bring destruction on the one who withholds*, the other *O Allah, give compensation to the one who spends.*
- *Spend, O Bilal, and **do not fear that the One Who is on the Throne will withhold from you**.*

## 21. The Angels Disown Their Worshippers (34:40–42)

On the Day of Resurrection Allah will rebuke the idolaters before all creation by questioning **the angels** they claimed to be worshipping — the idols having been made in the angels' supposed likeness, on the theory that they would bring them nearer to Allah.

*Was it you that these people used to worship?* — i.e. **did you command them to worship you?** (Compare 25:17, and the same question put to 'Isa in 5:116.)

Their answer: *Glorified be You! **You are our Protector instead of them***— we are Your servants, and we disown these people before You.

Then the correction of the record: *Nay, but they used to worship the jinn* — meaning **the Shayatin**, who made idol-worship attractive to them and misguided them (cf. 4:117–118).

*So today, none of you can profit or harm one another* — those you worshipped hoping for help in stress and calamity have **no power to benefit or harm you now**.

---

## 22. Their Three Charges, and Their Empty Hands (34:43–45)

When the clear verses were recited to them, fresh from the lips of the Messenger ﷺ, they said three things:

1. *This is naught but a man who wishes to hinder you from what your fathers worshipped* — i.e. the religion of their fathers was the truth and the revelation was false
2. *This is nothing but an invented lie* — referring to the Qur'an
3. *This is nothing but evident magic*

Against which: *We had not given them Scriptures that they could study, nor sent to them before you any warner.* Ibn Kathir sharpens the irony — **they used to wish for exactly this**, saying *if only a warner came to us, or a Book were revealed to us, we would be more guided than others.* When Allah gave it, they rejected it.

And the appeal to numbers and strength doesn't work either: earlier nations *did not receive even a tenth of what We had granted to those of old* — **Ibn Abbas** (with Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd): of the power of this world. They had hearing, sight and hearts more firmly established than these (46:26), were *more in number and mightier in strength* (40:82) — **and none of it protected them.** *Then how terrible was My denial!* — meaning, *how severe was My wrath, and how great My avenging of My Messengers.*

---

## 23. The One Thing He Asks (34:46)

Against the charge of madness, the Prophet ﷺ is told to ask for one thing only — and it is a request for **honest thinking**:

> *I exhort you to one thing only: that you stand up for Allah's sake, in pairs and singly, and reflect. There is no madness in your companion.*

Ibn Kathir's reading, supported by **Mujahid, Muhammad bin Ka'b, As-Suddi and Qatadah**: stand sincerely before Allah, **not moved by your own desires or tribal feeling**, and ask one another — is Muhammad crazy? Advise one another. *And reflect* means let each person look into himself about this man, and if he is still confused, ask others, and then **think it through**.

Al-Bukhari ← Ibn Abbas, on the incident this recalls. The Prophet ﷺ climbed As-Safa and shouted *Ya sabahah!* The Quraysh gathered. He said:

> *What do you think — if I told you the enemy were approaching and would reach you in the morning or the evening, **would you believe me**?*

They said: *of course.* He said: *then I am a warner to you in the face of a severe punishment.* Abu Lahab said, *may you perish — you called us only for this?* And Allah revealed *Perish the two hands of Abu Lahab* (111:1).

The argument is entirely about **track record**. They conceded he had never lied to them about anything they could check.

---

## 24. No Fee, No Claim (34:47–50)

*Whatever wage I might have asked of you is yours* — I want nothing from you for conveying the Message, advising you, and calling you to worship Allah. *My wage is from Allah only, and He is a Witness over all things* — He knows exactly how I am conveying it to you, **and He knows all about you**.

*My Lord sends down the truth, the All-Knower of the Unseen* — He sends the angel with revelation to whichever of His servants He wills (40:15), and **nothing whatsoever is hidden from Him** in the heavens or the earth.

*The truth has come, and falsehood can neither create anything nor resurrect.* Ibn Kathir attaches the moment this verse was recited in the world: when the Messenger of Allah ﷺ entered Al-Masjid Al-Haram on the **day of the conquest of Makkah** and found the idols standing around the Ka'bah, **he struck them with his bow**, reciting *the truth has come and falsehood has vanished* (17:81) and this verse. *(Al-Bukhari, Muslim, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i.)*

Then the humility that closes the passage: *If I go astray, I stray only to my own loss; if I remain guided, it is because of the Revelation of my Lord to me.* **All good comes from Allah; error comes from the self.** Ibn Kathir cites Abdullah bin Mas'ud, asked about a legal question:

> *I will say what I think. If it is correct, it is from Allah; if it is wrong, it is from me and from the Shaytan — and Allah and His Messenger have nothing to do with it.*

*Truly, He is All-Hearer, Ever Near* — He hears every word of His servants and is always near to answer. An-Nasa'i, and the two Sahihs, from Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari:

> *You are not calling upon one who is deaf or absent; you are calling upon One Who is **All-Hearer, Ever Near and Responsive**.*

---

## 25. Faith From Too Far Away (34:51–54)

The surah ends where it began — with the Hour — but now from inside it. *If you could but see, when they will be terrified with no escape, and seized from a near place.* No chance of flight; **seized at the first instant**. **Al-Hasan Al-Basri:** when they come forth from their graves.

*And they will say: We do believe now.* They will affirm Allah, His angels, Books and Messengers — the same reversal as *Our Lord! We have now seen and heard, so send us back* (32:12).

*But how could they receive it (at-tanawush) from a place so far off?* Ibn Kathir's answer is about **realm, not distance**: they have crossed out of the realm of trial and testing into the realm of reward and punishment. Faith offered there is faith offered from somewhere it cannot be reached — *just as a person cannot get a thing if he is far away from it.*

- **Mujahid:** *at-tanawush* means, how could they attain that?
- **Az-Zuhri:** they will wish for faith once they have reached the Hereafter and been cut off from this world
- **Al-Hasan Al-Basri:** they will seek something when they have no way of attaining it — **they will seek faith from a distant place**

And why they cannot: *they used to conjecture about the unseen from a far place.* **Zayd bin Aslam** (via Malik): **by guessing** — as in *guessing at the unseen* (18:22). Their guesses were: a poet, a soothsayer, a sorcerer, a madman. And on resurrection: *we do not think it but as a conjecture, and we have no firm convincing belief* (45:32). **Qatadah and Mujahid:** their belief that there would be no resurrection, no Paradise and no Hell **rested on nothing but conjecture.**

*And a barrier will be set between them and that which they desire.* Two readings, which Ibn Kathir declines to arbitrate:

| Reading | Authorities |
|---|---|
| It means **faith** | Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Ad-Dahhak and others |
| It means **repentance** | As-Suddi; also Ibn Jarir |
| It means **this world** and its wealth, luxuries and people | Mujahid; similarly Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas; and the view of Al-Bukhari and the Group |

**The correct view is that there is no contradiction:** a barrier is set between them and what they desired *in this world*, and what they seek *in the Hereafter* is denied them. Both at once.

*As was done in the past with the people of their kind* — as happened to the nations who, when the punishment arrived, wished they had believed and were refused (40:84–85: *then their faith could not avail them when they saw Our punishment; this has been the way of Allah in dealing with His servants*).

And the last words of the surah name the disease: ***Verily, they have been in grave doubt*** — in this world they doubted, so their faith will not be accepted when they see the punishment with their own eyes.

**Qatadah** has the final line, and it is a warning about the continuity of a state across death:

> *Beware of doubt. For whoever dies doubting will be raised doubting; and whoever dies believing with certainty will be raised believing with certainty.*

---

## The Thread

Saba' is a surah about **who knows, and who thanks** — and it turns out these are the same question.

The knowledge half runs the length of the surah. It opens with what enters the earth and what leaves it, and swears by the Lord *of the unseen* that the Hour is coming. Then it dismantles every rival claim to that knowledge, one at a time: the **jinn**, exposed by a worm eating a staff while they worked on for a year under a dead man; the **soothsayer**, working from one stolen fragment and a hundred lies; the **idolaters**, whose entire case against resurrection was *conjecture from a far place*. Even the **angels**, at the end, disown the people who thought they were worth asking.

The gratitude half is the surah's argument about what to do with a blessing while you still have it. Two houses were given more than they asked for. One was told *Work, O family of Dawud, with thanks* — and Dawud, unable to find a thanks that wasn't itself a gift, was told **that recognition is the thanks**. The other was told *Eat of the provision of your Lord and be grateful* — and asked, instead, for the distances to be made longer, for the shade to be thinned, for the ease to be taken back. It was.

Between those two houses stands the verdict: **"But few of My servants are grateful."** And beside it stands the temperament the surah recommends in its place — *if something good befalls him he gives thanks, and if something bad befalls him he has patience; and this is not for anyone except the believer.*

The rest is timing. The dam holds until the rats have finished. The staff stands until the worm is done. Faith offered at the graveside is offered from too far away to reach.

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Saba'. Allah, may He be glorified and exalted, is the Guide to the right way.*
