*12 ayat · "The Prohibition" — named for the opening question, "Why do you forbid what Allah has made lawful to you?"*

*(A note on the heading: this PDF prints "Revealed in Makkah." The surah is Madinan by consensus, and the tafsir itself confirms it — every event in it happens in Madinah, among the Prophet's ﷺ households, with Umar recalling "when we came to Al-Madinah.")*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

At-Tahrim is the most **domestic** surah in the Qur'an, and it uses that domesticity to make a universal point.

It opens inside the Prophet's ﷺ own home — a jar of honey, a whispered plot between two wives, a secret told, a month of withdrawal, a rumour of divorce sweeping Madinah. Then, without warning, at 66:6 it turns outward and commands **every** believer: *protect yourselves and your families from a Fire.* And it closes with four women — two who lived beside prophets and were lost, two who had no believing husband at all (one was married to Fir'awn) and were saved.

That closing pair of parables is the key to the whole surah. **Nearness to a righteous person saves nobody.** Nuh's wife shared his bed and his table for centuries and entered the Fire. Asiyah shared Fir'awn's palace and was shown her house in Paradise before her body hit the ground. What passes between spouses does not transfer faith — and this is precisely why the command in the middle of the surah is *teach your family*, not *trust that they are near you*.

The surah is also, incidentally, the great documentary record of **Umar bin Al-Khattab** — his longest personal narration in the whole tafsir sits inside it.

---

## 1. The Honey, the Plot, and the Oath (66:1–2)

> *O Prophet! Why do you forbid that which Allah has allowed to you, seeking to please your wives?*

Ibn Kathir gives two versions of the incident, both authentic, and then addresses the discrepancy honestly.

**Version A — Al-Bukhari, from Ubayd bin Umayr ← A'ishah** (Book of Vows). The Prophet ﷺ would linger in Zaynab bint Jahsh's house drinking honey. A'ishah and Hafsah agreed that whichever of them he visited would say: *I smell Maghafir on you — have you eaten Maghafir?* He replied: **"No, but I drank honey in the house of Zaynab bint Jahsh, and I will never drink it again."** In Hisham's addition: *I have taken an oath to that, so do not inform anybody about it.*

**Version B — Al-Bukhari, from Urwah ← A'ishah** (Book of Divorce). The honey was Hafsah's, a gift from a woman of her family. A'ishah, noticing the Prophet ﷺ staying longer than usual with Hafsah, became jealous and enlisted **Sawdah bint Zam'ah** and **Safiyyah** in the same script. Sawdah's line is unforgettable: *"It was under compulsion that I had decided to say what you told me — and by Allah, he was already standing at my door."* Each wife in turn said the honeybees must have fed on the 'Urfut tree. When Hafsah later offered him the honey again, he said: **"I have no need of it."** Sawdah: *"By Allah, we have stopped him from drinking honey!"* A'ishah: *"Keep quiet!"*

**What Maghafir is, and why it worked.** Al-Bukhari: a type of sap; Al-Jawhari: the *'Urfut* is a shrub that secretes *Maghfur*. Muslim's narration supplies the motive — **the Messenger of Allah ﷺ hated to have a bad odour coming from him.** The wives chose the one accusation guaranteed to make him stop.

**On the two versions:** Ibn Kathir says some claim these were two separate incidents, but he considers it unlikely that the same Ayat were revealed about both. *Allah knows best.* Ahmad's narration in the Musnad, and Umar's own testimony, both name **A'ishah and Hafsah** as the two.

**The legal outcome (66:2).** *Allah has already ordained for you the absolution from your oaths.* Making something lawful forbidden to oneself does not make it forbidden — it is an oath, and an oath has a **kaffarah** (expiation). The gentleness of the sequence is the point: the censure comes first, and immediately after it comes the way out.

## 2. The Secret, and Who Told Him (66:3)

> *And when the Prophet disclosed a matter in confidence to one of his wives, then she told it... he informed part thereof and left a part.*

Allah informed him of the disclosure. When he confronted her, he **told her some of what she had said and passed over the rest** — a detail Ibn Kathir preserves without editorial, and one that speaks for itself about the Prophet's ﷺ manner in confrontation.

*"Who told you this?"*
**"The All-Knower, the All-Aware has told me."**

---

## 3. Umar's Story: The Longest Narration in the Surah (66:4)

> *If you two turn in repentance to Allah, your hearts are indeed so inclined.*

Ibn Abbas said he waited **a full year** to ask Umar who the two women were, out of sheer awe of him. He finally asked on the road back from Hajj, while pouring water over Umar's hands for wudu. Umar: *"I am astonished at your question, O Ibn Abbas"* — Az-Zuhri notes he **disliked the question but answered it anyway**: `A'ishah and Hafsah.

Then the story unspools, and it is worth reading in full sequence:

- **"We, the people of Quraysh, used to have authority over our women."** In Madinah, the Ansari women had the upper hand over their men — and Quraysh women began learning the habit. Umar's own wife talked back to him. When he objected, she said: *the wives of the Prophet ﷺ talk back to him, and some of them will not speak to him for a whole day.*
- Umar went to **Hafsah** — his daughter — and confirmed it. His warning to her: *don't ask too much of him, don't retort, demand from me whatever you like, and don't be tempted to imitate your neighbour, for she is more beautiful than you and more beloved to the Messenger than you.* He meant `A'ishah.
- Umar and an Ansari neighbour took turns attending the Prophet ﷺ, each bringing the other the day's news of revelation. One night the neighbour knocked: *a grave thing has happened.* Umar assumed the **Ghassan** tribe had invaded. It was worse: **the Messenger of Allah ﷺ has divorced his wives.**
- Umar found Hafsah weeping — she did not know. He went to the upper room, was refused entry three times through a servant boy, sat by the Minbar among a crowd of weeping people, could not bear it, tried again, and was finally admitted.
- **He found the Prophet ﷺ lying on a bare mat, the weave of it printed into his side.** The room contained nothing of value but three hides.
- *"Have you divorced your wives?"* He raised his eyes: **No.** Umar said *Allahu Akbar*, then retold the whole story of his wife and of his warning to Hafsah — and the Prophet ﷺ **smiled**, twice.
- Umar, seeing the room, said: *invoke Allah to make your followers prosperous, for the Persians and Byzantines have been given worldly luxuries and they do not even worship Allah.* The Prophet ﷺ sat upright:

> **"O Ibn Al-Khattab! Do you have any doubt? These people have been given the rewards of their good deeds in this world only."**

He had sworn not to go near his wives **for one month**, out of severe anger, until Allah censured him.

## 4. Who Is "the Righteous among the Believers"?

> *...then verily, Allah is his Protector, and Jibril, and the righteous among the believers; and after that the angels are his helpers.*

Umar's own words to the Prophet ﷺ in Muslim's narration anticipate the verse almost exactly: *if you have divorced them, verily Allah is with you, His angels, Jibril, Mika'il, I, Abu Bakr and the rest of the believers.* He adds: *often when I spoke, I hoped Allah would confirm the words I uttered* — and the Ayah came down.

- **Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ikrimah, Muqatil bin Hayyan, Ad-Dahhak:** Abu Bakr and Umar.
- **Al-Hasan Al-Basri:** adds Uthman.
- **Mujahid** (via Layth bin Abi Sulaym): includes Ali bin Abi Talib.

**Umar's *muwafaqat* — where his words preceded the revelation.** Ibn Kathir lists them here: the command of Hijab (33:53), the ruling on the Badr captives (8:67), taking the Station of Ibrahim as a place of prayer (2:125), and this. Add a fourth from this episode: after Umar shouted from the door of the Masjid that the Prophet ﷺ had **not** divorced his wives, **4:83** was revealed — *when a matter of peace or alarm comes to them they broadcast it; had they referred it to the Messenger and those in authority, those who investigate would have known it.* Umar's comment: *"It was I who understood and properly investigated this matter."*

## 5. The Eight Qualities of the Better Wives (66:5)

> *Maybe his Lord, if he divorces you, will give him instead of you wives better than you —*

**Muslimat** (submitting) · **Mu'minat** (believing) · **Qanitat** (obedient) · **Ta'ibat** (turning in repentance) · **'Abidat** (worshipping sincerely) · **Sa'ihat** · **thayyibat** (previously married) · **abkar** (virgins).

Anas, via Al-Bukhari, has Umar saying the wives were all jealous for his affection, and that he said this verse **to them** — and then it was revealed.

Ibn Abi Hatim, from Anas: Umar told them plainly, *"Either stop bothering Allah's Messenger, or Allah might give him better wives than you."*

## 6. The Pivot: Protect Yourselves and Your Families (66:6)

> *O you who believe! Protect yourselves and your families against a Fire whose fuel is men and stones.*

This is where the surah turns from one household to every household. Ibn Kathir stacks the salaf on a single meaning — **the command is to teach**:

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah): work in obedience to Allah, avoid disobedience, and **order your family to remember Allah** — then Allah will save you from the Fire.
- **Mujahid:** have Taqwa of Allah, and order your family to have Taqwa of Him.
- **Qatadah:** he commands them to obey, helps them act on it, and when he sees disobedience he stops it and forbids it.
- **Ad-Dahhak and Muqatil:** it is an **obligation** on the Muslim to teach his near family, and his male and female slaves, what Allah has made obligatory and forbidden for them.

The confirming hadith (Ahmad, Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi — *Hasan* — from Ar-Rabi' bin Sabrah ← his father):

> **"Order the children to pray when they reach seven, and when they reach ten, discipline them for not performing it."**

**The fuel.** Men — the Children of Adam feed the Fire. And *stones*: the idols that were worshipped, per 21:98 — *you and what you worship besides Allah are fuel for Hell.* Ibn Mas'ud, Mujahid, Abu Ja'far Al-Baqir and As-Suddi: these are **sulphur stones**, which Mujahid says are more putrid than rotting corpses.

**The angels over it.** *Ghilaz* — stern: **the mercy has been removed from their hearts** toward those who disbelieve in Allah. *Shidad* — severe: their very structure is powerful, strong and frightening. They obey whatever they are commanded **without the delay of a twinkling of an eye**. These are **Az-Zabaniyah**, the keepers and guards of Hell. *May Allah give us shelter from them.*

## 7. No Excuses (66:7)

> *O you who disbelieve! Make no excuses this Day!*

On the Day of Resurrection the disbeliever is told: **do not offer an excuse, because it will not be accepted.** You are only being repaid for what you did. Today you receive the wage of your own actions.

---

## 8. Tawbatan Nasuha — Sincere Repentance (66:8)

> *Turn to Allah with sincere repentance!*

Ibn Kathir's definition is threefold: a **true, firm** repentance that (i) **erases** the evil that preceded it, (ii) **mends the shortcoming** in the repenting person, and (iii) **directs him to quit** the evil he used to do. It is not only regret for the past; it is a change in the trajectory.

> *It may be that your Lord will expiate from you your sins and admit you into Gardens...*

**"And when Allah says *'it may be,'* it means He shall."**

**The light.** *Their light will run forward before them and in their right hands.* Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Al-Hasan Al-Basri: the believers say *"Our Lord, keep perfect our light for us"* at the precise moment they **witness the light of the hypocrites being extinguished** — which is why the prayer is for the light to be *completed*, not merely given.

And the du'a Imam Ahmad records from a man of Banu Kinanah, who prayed behind the Prophet ﷺ in the year of the Conquest of Makkah and heard him say:

> **"O Allah! Please, do not disgrace me on the Day of Resurrection."**

---

## 9. The Command to Strive (66:9)

> *Strive hard against the disbelievers and the hypocrites, and be severe against them.*

Ibn Kathir distinguishes the two methods precisely: **the disbelievers with weapons and armaments; the hypocrites by establishing Allah's legislated penal code.** *Be severe against them* — in this life. *Their abode will be Hell* — in the Hereafter.

---

## 10. The Two Parables: Proximity Does Not Save (66:10)

> *Allah sets forth an example for those who disbelieve: the wife of Nuh and the wife of Lut. They were under two of Our righteous servants, but they both betrayed them.*

The lesson stated plainly: the disbelievers' **mixing and mingling with Muslims** in this life will not help them, nor avail them with Allah, unless faith reaches their hearts.

These two women were married to **Messengers of Allah** — companions by day and night, eating with them, sleeping with them, sharing everything a marriage contains. And all that intimate knowledge did not help them or prevent the punishment. *Enter the Fire along with those who enter.*

**A crucial clarification Ibn Kathir insists on.** *They betrayed them* does **not** mean illegal sexual intercourse. **No wife of any Prophet ever committed adultery** — they were immune from it, by the honour Allah granted His Prophets (as explained in Surah An-Nur). The betrayal was **in religion**: refusing to accept the message their husbands brought.

The concrete form it took, per **Al-'Awfi ← Ibn Abbas**:
- **Nuh's wife** exposed his secrets — informing his oppressive people whenever anyone accepted the faith.
- **Lut's wife** informed the men of the city whenever her husband received a guest.

**Ad-Dahhak ← Ibn Abbas**, and likewise Ikrimah and Sa'id bin Jubayr: *rather, they betrayed them by refusing to follow their religion.*

---

## 11. Asiyah: Faith Inside the House of a Tyrant (66:11)

> *My Lord! Build for me a home with You in Paradise, and save me from Fir'awn and his work, and save me from the wrongdoing people.*

The mirror image of the previous parable. The governing principle is 3:28 — believers should not take disbelievers as protecting friends — but here Allah shows that **association with disbelievers, where unavoidable, does not harm the one whose heart is sound.**

**Qatadah:** Fir'awn was the most tyrannical and most disbelieving man on earth. *By Allah, his wife was not affected by her husband's disbelief, because she obeyed her Lord.* Let it be known that **Allah is the Just Judge Who punishes no one except for their own sins.**

Notice the order of her du'a: **the neighbour before the house.** She asks for a home *with You* first, and only then for the Garden.

**Ibn Jarir ← Sulayman:** she was tortured under the sun, and when Fir'awn's session ended, **the angels shaded her with their wings**, and she was shown her house in Paradise.

**Ibn Jarir ← Al-Qasim bin Abi Bazzah:** she used to ask, *"Who prevailed?"* When told *"Musa and Harun prevailed,"* she said, **"I believe in the Lord of Musa and Harun."** Fir'awn sent his men with orders: find the biggest stone; if she persists, drop it on her — otherwise she is my wife. When they came, she looked up at the sky and **saw her house in Paradise**. She held to her faith, her soul was taken — and the stone landed on a body that was already gone.

Her name: **Asiyah bint Muzahim**, may Allah be pleased with her.

---

## 12. Maryam (66:12)

> *And Maryam, the daughter of `Imran, who guarded her chastity.*

She **protected and purified her honour**, chaste and free of immorality. *And We breathed into it through Our Ruh* — meaning **the angel Jibril**, sent to her in the form of a man in every respect, commanded to blow into the opening of her garment; the breath reached her womb, and this is how `Isa was conceived.

*She testified to the truth of her Lord's **Kalimat** and His **Kutub*** — **His decree and His legislation**. *And she was of the Qanitin* — the devoutly obedient.

---

## 13. The Four Women, and Where This Ends

Imam Ahmad ← Ibn Abbas: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ **drew four lines on the ground** and asked, *"Do you know what these represent?"* They said Allah and His Messenger know best. He said:

> **"The best among the women of Paradise are Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, Fatimah bint Muhammad, Maryam bint `Imran, and Asiyah bint Muzahim, the wife of Fir'awn."**

And in the Two Sahihs, from Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari:

> **"Many men have reached the level of perfection, but none among women except Asiyah — Fir'awn's wife, Maryam — the daughter of `Imran, and Khadijah — the daughter of Khuwaylid. And the superiority of `A'ishah over other women is like the superiority of Tharid over other meals."**

Ibn Kathir notes he has gathered the chains for these in *Al-Bidayah wan-Nihayah*, in the story of `Isa and his mother.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Making the lawful unlawful for yourself is not piety — it is an oath, and it has an expiation.** The surah opens by correcting even the Prophet ﷺ on this, gently, and providing the way out in the very next verse.

**The Prophet's ﷺ house was a bare mat, three hides, and a jar of honey he gave up to keep the peace.** Umar wept at the mark the mat had left on his side; the answer he received was that Persia and Byzantium had already been paid in full, here.

**"Protect yourselves *and your families*"** — and every commentator reads it the same way: this is a command to **teach**. Prayer at seven. Not neutrality, not hoping they absorb it.

**And then the surah's final argument, delivered entirely through four women:** the wives of Nuh and Lut, who had everything a person could have by way of access to guidance and did not take it; Asiyah, who had a tyrant for a husband and asked for a house next to her Lord before she asked for the Garden; and Maryam, who guarded what was hers and testified to the truth.

**Nobody is saved by proximity. Everybody is judged by their own sins.** That is the whole surah, from the honey to the stone.
