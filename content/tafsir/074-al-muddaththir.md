*Revealed in Makkah · 56 ayat · "The One Enveloped in Garments" — the surah that turned a frightened man wrapped in a blanket into a Messenger*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Muddaththir begins at one of the most consequential moments in human history: the end of the **pause in revelation**. After *Iqra'* in the cave of Hira there was silence — a *fatrah* — and then the angel returned. The Prophet ﷺ fled home in terror and asked to be wrapped up. The surah's first word is Allah addressing him **in that blanket**, and its second command is the one that ends his private life forever: ***Arise and warn.***

Everything that follows is the content of that warning, delivered in three movements:

- **The mandate** (1–7): stand up, magnify, purify, abandon idols, give without calculating a return, and be patient — six instructions that between them cover the entire prophetic career.
- **The case file** (8–30): the Day when the Trumpet sounds, and then the portrait of a single named man — **Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah** — who received every blessing Allah could give a person and spent them deciding what to call the Qur'an. The Qur'an follows him through his own deliberation, frown by frown, until he lands on the word *magic*.
- **The reckoning** (31–56): the nineteen guardians of Hell, the Quraysh joking about overpowering them, the oaths by moon and night and dawn, and finally the four-line confession of the damned — the shortest and clearest account in the Qur'an of what actually sends a person to the Fire.

The surah's spine is the contrast between the man told to **arise** and the man who **sat, thought, frowned, and turned back**.

---

## 1. The First Ayat Revealed After *Iqra'* (74:1)

> *O you enveloped in garments!*

Ibn Kathir builds this section on the hadith of **Jabir bin Abdullah** in the Two Sahihs, describing the period of paused revelation. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *While I was walking I heard a voice from the sky. So I lifted my gaze and saw the same angel who had come to me at the cave of Hira', sitting on a chair between the sky and the earth. So I fled from him until I fell to the ground. Then I went to my family and said, "Wrap me up, wrap me up."*

And Allah revealed the opening five ayat. Abu Salamah adds the gloss that **Ar-Rujz means idols**. After this, revelation came "strongly and frequently in succession."

Ibn Kathir draws a careful conclusion from the wording. Because the Prophet ﷺ says *"the same angel who had come to me at the cave of Hira'"*, this proves **revelation had already begun** — the first was Jibril with *Read! In the Name of your Lord Who created* (96:1–5). Al-Muddaththir is therefore not the first revelation, but the first **after the pause**.

**A second occasion is also preserved.** At-Tabarani records from Ibn Abbas that Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah laid on a feast for the Quraysh and, once they had eaten, asked them what verdict to give on Muhammad ﷺ. They cycled through *magician* — no; *soothsayer* — no; *poet* — no; and finally settled on **"magic from that of old."** When word reached the Prophet ﷺ he was grieved, covered his head, and wrapped himself up — and the surah was revealed.

## 2. The Six Commands (74:2–7)

This is the shortest complete job description ever given to a prophet.

**"Arise and warn!"** — prepare to go forth with zeal and warn the people. Ibn Kathir marks the constitutional point here: **with this ayah he attained Messengership, as he had attained Prophethood with the first revelation.** *Iqra'* made him a prophet; *Qum fa-andhir* made him a messenger.

**"And magnify your Lord!"** — declare His greatness.

**"And purify your garments!"** — and here Ibn Kathir gathers a remarkable spread of readings, none of which he discards:

| Authority | Reading |
|---|---|
| Ibn Abbas (via Al-Awfi) | Do not let the clothes you wear come from **unlawful earnings** |
| (also said) | Do not wear your clothes **in disobedience** |
| Muhammad bin Sirin | **Clean them with water** — literally |
| Ibn Zayd (preferred by Ibn Jarir) | The idolaters did not clean themselves; he was commanded to purify **himself and his garments** |
| Sa'id bin Jubayr | Purify your **heart and your intentions** |
| Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi & Al-Hasan Al-Basri | **Beautify your character** |

Outer cleanliness, lawful income, inward intention and moral character — all read out of one short command.

**"And keep away from Ar-Rujz!"** — the idols. Ibn Abbas (via Ali bin Abi Talhah), Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatadah, Az-Zuhri and Ibn Zayd all agree. Compare *O Prophet! Have Taqwa of Allah, and obey not the disbelievers and the hypocrites* (33:1), and Musa's instruction to Harun: *act well and follow not the way of the corrupters* (7:142).

**"And give not a thing in order to have more."** Ibn Abbas: **do not give a gift hoping to get back more than it.** Mujahid (via Khusayf) reads it differently — *do not slacken in seeking more good*, since *tamnun* in Arabic can mean to become weak.

**"And be patient for the sake of your Lord!"** Mujahid: make your patience with their harm be **for the Face of your Lord**. Ibrahim An-Nakha'i: be patient in your giving, for Allah's sake.

## 3. When the Naqur Is Sounded (74:8–10)

> *Then, when the Naqur is sounded. Truly, that Day will be a Hard Day — far from easy for the disbelievers.*

**An-Naqur is the Trumpet** — the agreed reading of Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ash-Sha'bi, Zayd bin Aslam, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas, As-Suddi and Ibn Zayd. Mujahid adds that it is **shaped like a horn**.

Ibn Abi Hatim narrates from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *How can I be comfortable when the one with the horn has placed it in his mouth, leaned his forehead forward, and is waiting to be commanded so that he can blow?*

The Companions asked what they should do. He replied: **say *Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal-Wakil, 'alallahi tawakkalna*** — Allah is sufficient for us, and what an excellent Trustee He is; upon Allah we rely.

*"Far from easy for the disbelievers"* — it will not be light for them; compare *The disbelievers will say: "This is a Hard Day"* (54:8).

**And a story Ibn Kathir places here deliberately.** Zurarah bin Awfa, the judge of Al-Basrah, was leading the morning prayer and reciting this surah. When he reached *Truly, that Day will be a Hard Day — far from easy for the disbelievers*, **he made a moaning sound and fell down dead.** May Allah have mercy on him.

---

## 4. The Man Created Alone (74:11–17)

> *Leave Me alone (to deal) with whom I created lonely.*

The subject is **Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah Al-Makhzumi**, one of the chiefs of Quraysh. The ayat itemise the gifts:

- **Created lonely** — he came out of his mother's womb alone, with no wealth and no children.
- **Resources in abundance** — vast and extensive wealth.
- **Children attending.** Mujahid: *"they are not absent."* They never had to travel for trade; servants and hired workers handled all of it while the sons sat with their father, and he enjoyed their company.
- **Life made smooth and comfortable** — he was enabled to amass wealth and luxuries.

And then the pivot: ***After all that he desires that I should give more. Nay! Verily, he has been opposing Our Ayat*** — obstinately. This is **ingratitude after full knowledge** of the blessings.

**"I shall force him to Sa'ud!"** Four readings:

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Qatadah): a rock in Hell that the disbeliever is dragged across **on his face**.
- **As-Suddi**: a slippery rock in Hell he is forced to climb.
- **Mujahid**: a harsh portion of the torment.
- **Qatadah**: a torment containing **no break for relief**.

---

## 5. The Deliberation (74:18–25)

This is the passage that makes Al-Muddaththir unlike anything else in the Qur'an: Allah narrates a man's internal process, step by step, in real time.

> *Verily, he thought and plotted. So let him be cursed, how he plotted! And once more let him be cursed, how he plotted! Then he thought. Then he frowned and he looked in a bad tempered way. Then he turned back, and was proud.*

Ibn Kathir's reading: he was brought close to the harsh torment **because he was far from faith** — and specifically because *he thought and plotted*, meaning he **deliberated over what he should say about the Qur'an** when asked, and set about inventing a statement against it. The double curse is a supplication against him.

- *Then he frowned* — he contracted his eyebrows together.
- *And he looked in a bad tempered way* — he scowled, disgusted.
- *Then he turned back, and was proud* — he turned from the truth and **arrogantly refused to submit** to the Qur'an.

The verdict he manufactures: ***"This is nothing but magic from that of old"*** — meaning Muhammad ﷺ got it from those before him and is repeating it — ***"This is nothing but the word of a human being!"*** — meaning: **not the Words of Allah.**

---

## 6. How Abu Jahl Talked Him Out of It (the narration of Al-Awfi ← Ibn Abbas)

The surrounding story is one of the most psychologically exact episodes in the whole tafsir.

Al-Walid went to **Abu Bakr bin Abi Quhafah's** house and asked him about the Qur'an. Abu Bakr explained it. Al-Walid left and told the Quraysh:

> *What a great thing this is that Ibn Abi Kabshah is saying. I swear by Allah that it is not poetry, nor magic, nor the prattling of insanity. Verily, his speech is from the Words of Allah!*

The Quraysh panicked: **"By Allah, if Al-Walid converts, all of Quraysh will convert."** Abu Jahl volunteered to handle it — and note that he did not argue theology at all. He went to Al-Walid and said: *don't you see your people are collecting charity for you?* Then: *they are saying you only went to Ibn Abi Quhafah's house to get some of his food.*

Al-Walid, stung: *Is this what my tribe is saying? Nay, by Allah, I am not seeking to be close to Ibn Abi Quhafah, nor Umar, nor Ibn Abi Kabshah. **And his speech is only inherited magic of old.***

He was not reasoned out of belief. He was **shamed** out of it.

Qatadah preserves his own account of the same conclusion: *"By Allah, I thought about what the man says, and it is not poetry. Verily, it has a sweetness and it is truly elegant. Verily, it is exalted and it is not overcome. And I have no doubt that it is magic."* He conceded every quality of the Qur'an and then filed it under magic anyway.

## 7. Saqar (74:26–30)

> *I will cast him into Saqar. And what will make you know what Saqar is? It spares not, nor does it leave (anything)! Scorching for the humans! Over it are nineteen.*

**"I will cast him into Saqar"** — `I will engulf him in it from all his sides.`

**"And what will make you know what Saqar is?"** — asked to frighten and to emphasise its gravity.

**"It spares not, nor does it leave"** — it consumes flesh, veins, nerves and skins; then their organs are changed into something else, and they remain in that state, **neither living nor dying**. (Ibn Buraydah, Abu Sinan and others.)

**"Scorching for the humans"** — Mujahid: for the skin. Qatadah: burning the skin. Ibn Abbas: burning the skin of man.

**"Over it are nineteen"** — the foremost guardians of Hell, magnificent in appearance and severe in character.

---

## 8. The Nineteen, and the Joke That Backfired (74:31)

The single longest ayah of the surah, and it exists because the Quraysh treated the number as an opening.

**Abu Jahl said:** *"O people of Quraysh! Are not every ten among you able to defeat one of them?"* And **Abu Al-Ashaddayn** — Kaladah bin Usayd bin Khalaf — said: *"O people of Quraysh! You defend me against two of them and I will defend you against seventeen."* He said it out of vanity about his own strength: they claimed he could stand on a cowhide while ten men pulled at it, and the hide would tear to pieces before it moved from under his feet.

Allah's answer is that the guardians are **angels** — *stern and severe*, extremely strong in their very creation, who **cannot be stood against nor defeated**. The strength being boasted about was the wrong category entirely.

Then the ayah lists the **five purposes** of naming the number:

1. **A trial for the disbelievers** — the number was mentioned precisely as a test.
2. **So the People of the Scripture arrive at certainty** — they find it matching the revealed Scriptures already with them, and know this Messenger is true.
3. **So the believers increase in faith** — witnessing the truthfulness of their Prophet's information.
4. **So no doubt is left** for either group.
5. **So those with disease in their hearts** (the hypocrites) and the disbelievers say: *"What does Allah intend by this example?"* — i.e., what is the wisdom in mentioning this here?

*Thus Allah leads astray whom He wills and guides whom He wills* — by examples of exactly this kind, faith becomes firm in some hearts and is shaken in others. It carries profound wisdom and irrefutable proof.

**"And none can know the hosts of your Lord but He"** — so that no one makes the mistake of thinking the angels number only nineteen in total. Ibn Kathir cites the hadith of Al-Isra' on **Al-Bayt Al-Ma'mur** in the seventh heaven:

> *Seventy thousand angels enter it every day, and they do not return to it* — that one visit is all that is due upon them in their existence.

**"And this is nothing but a reminder to mankind"** — Mujahid and others: *this* refers to **the Hellfire just described**.

## 9. The Oaths (74:32–37)

> *Nay! And by the moon. And by the night when it withdraws. And by the dawn when it brightens. Verily, it is but one of the greatest (signs). A warning to mankind — to any of you that chooses to go forward, or to remain behind.*

*One of the greatest* — of the **great things**; and it refers to **the Hellfire**. So said Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak and others of the Salaf.

*To go forward or to remain behind* — for whoever wishes to **accept the warning and be guided**, or to hold back, turn away and reject it. Allah swears by the receding night and the brightening dawn, and what He swears to is that every person is choosing a direction.

---

## 10. The Conversation Across the Two Realms (74:38–47)

> *Every person is a pledge for what he has earned, except those on the Right.*

Every soul is **bound to its deed** on the Day of Judgement — held as security against it. Ibn Abbas and others said this. The people of the Right are the exception.

And then, from their **lofty rooms in the Gardens**, they call down to the criminals in the lowest levels of Hell and ask a question:

> *"What has caused you to enter Hell?"*

The answer is four sentences, and Ibn Kathir treats them as the most economical diagnosis in the Qur'an:

1. ***"We were not of those who used to offer the Salah"***
2. ***"Nor did we feed the poor"*** — together: `we did not worship Allah, nor did we do good to His creatures.` **The rights of Allah and the rights of people, both abandoned.**
3. ***"And we used to speak falsehood with vain speakers"*** — we spoke about what we had no knowledge of. Qatadah: *every time someone went astray, we would go astray with them.*
4. ***"And we used to deny the Day of Recompense — until Al-Yaqin came to us."*** *Al-Yaqin*, the certainty, means **death**. Compare *And worship your Lord until there comes to you the certainty* (15:99).

**"So no intercession of intercessors will be of any use to them."** Intercession is only effective when its conditions are met; whoever comes before Allah on that Day as a disbeliever gets the Fire and there is no avoiding it.

---

## 11. Fleeing the Lion (74:49–53)

> *Then what is wrong with them that they turn away from admonition? As if they were wild donkeys, fleeing from a Qaswarah.*

They flee the truth the way a **wild donkey bolts from a lion**. Abu Hurayrah said this. And Ibn Abbas (via Yusuf bin Mihran ← Ali bin Zayd ← Hammad bin Salamah) gives the philology: *Qaswarah* is the lion — **Qaswarah** in Arabic, **Sher** in Persian, **Awba** in Nabatean.

**"Nay, everyone of them desires that he should be given pages spread out."** Each idolater wanted his **own personal scripture** revealed to him, as it was revealed to the Prophet ﷺ. Compare *"We shall not believe until we receive the like of that which the Messengers of Allah received" — Allah knows best with whom to place His Message* (6:124). Qatadah's version: **they want to be declared innocent without doing any deeds.**

**"Nay! But they fear not the Hereafter."** That, finally, is the whole corruption: a lack of faith in it and a rejection of its occurrence.

---

## 12. The Reminder, and the Two Titles (74:54–56)

> *Nay, verily, this is an admonition. So, whosoever wills receives admonition from it! And they will not receive admonition unless Allah wills.*

The Qur'an is a reminder — and the will to be reminded is itself given. Compare *And you cannot will unless Allah wills* (81:29).

The surah ends on two of Allah's descriptions:

> ***He is Ahl at-Taqwa and Ahl al-Maghfirah*** — the One deserving to be feared, and the One deserving to forgive.

Qatadah: **He deserves to be feared, and He is eligible to forgive the sin of whoever turns to Him and repents.** A surah that spent thirty ayat on Saqar closes by naming forgiveness as something Allah is *entitled* to give.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Muddaththir is the moment the private ends.** A man wrapped in a blanket, shaking, is told *Arise and warn* — and Ibn Kathir marks it as the exact point where Prophethood became Messengership. Whatever comfort the blanket offered, the instruction was to get up out of it.

**The surah's villain is not a brute — he is a man who thought carefully and chose wrongly.** Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah conceded that the Qur'an was not poetry, not soothsaying, not madness; that it had sweetness and elegance; that it was *exalted and not overcome*. He had the analysis right. What broke him was **a taunt about charity and food** from Abu Jahl. Al-Muddaththir preserves his frown and his scowl in scripture forever, and the lesson is that unbelief is very often a matter of **pride rather than evidence**.

**Three things worth carrying:**

- **Give without calculating the return.** *Give not a thing in order to have more* — Ibn Abbas reads it as: no gift given in expectation of a bigger one back. It sits in the founding charter of the mission, alongside prayer and patience.
- **The damned name their own crime, and it is ordinary.** No salah, no feeding the poor, idle talk with idle people, and denial of the Day — the rights of God and the rights of people, dropped quietly over a lifetime. Nothing exotic sent them there.
- **Blessings prove nothing.** Al-Walid was given wealth, sons who never had to leave his side, and a life made smooth — and the ayah that follows is *he desires that I should give more.* Ibn Kathir's word for it is **obstinate**: ingratitude that arrives *after* full knowledge of the gift.

And the small human detail that outlives all the argument: **Zurarah bin Awfa**, a judge leading Fajr, reaching *far from easy for the disbelievers* — and never finishing the surah.
