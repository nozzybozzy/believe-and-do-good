*Said to be revealed in Al-Madinah, and also said in Makkah · 3 ayat · "The Abundance" — the shortest surah in the Qur'an*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Three verses. And the whole surah is a **reply to an insult**.

The enemies of the Prophet ﷺ had a word for a man whose sons had died and whose name would therefore die with him: ***abtar*** — cut off, docked, severed at the root. When the Prophet's ﷺ sons died, they used it about him. Abu Lahab went out to the idolaters and announced that Muhammad had been cut off that night.

The surah answers the word with its opposite. Not *you are not cut off*, but something far larger: ***We have given you Al-Kawthar*** — from *kathrah*, abundance. Where they said *severed*, Allah said *overflowing*. And then, in the last verse, He returns their word to them: **it is the one who hates you who is the one cut off.**

Between the two — the gift at the start and the verdict at the end — sits the single instruction of the surah: **pray, and sacrifice.** Given everything, respond with everything, and give it to nobody but Him.

---

## 1. How It Was Revealed: A Smile in the Masjid

Recorded by **Muslim, Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i** from **Anas**:

They were with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in the Masjid when he dozed off, then lifted his head **smiling**. They asked what had made him laugh. He said a surah had just been revealed to him — and recited Al-Kawthar.

Then he asked them: **"Do you all know what Al-Kawthar is?"** They said Allah and His Messenger know best. His answer is the tafsir's centrepiece, and it comes in two parts.

---

## 2. Al-Kawthar as a River (108:1)

> *Verily, We have granted you Al-Kawthar.*

From the same hadith:

> **"Verily, it is a river that my Lord, the Mighty and Majestic, has promised me, and it has abundant goodness."**

Ibn Kathir stacks the descriptions from several narrations:

- **From Anas (Ahmad, and in Al-Bukhari and Muslim, set during the Isra):** the Prophet ﷺ came upon a river whose banks were **tents — or domes — of hollowed pearl**. He thrust his hand into the flowing water and found it was **the strongest musk**. He asked, *"O Jibril, what is this?"* Jibril replied: **"This is Al-Kawthar, which Allah has given you."**
- **From Anas (Ahmad),** when a man asked outright what Al-Kawthar was: a river in Paradise, **whiter than milk and sweeter than honey**, with **birds in it whose necks are long like carrots**. `Umar said, "O Messenger of Allah, they will be beautiful!" The Prophet ﷺ replied: **"The one who eats them will be more beautiful than them, O `Umar."**
- **From Ibn `Umar (Ahmad; also At-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Jarir — At-Tirmidhi: *Hasan Sahih*):** its **banks are of gold**, its water **runs over pearls**, whiter than milk and sweeter than honey.

---

## 3. And the Hawd — with a Warning Attached

The Prophet's ﷺ answer did not stop at the river. It continued into the Pond:

> **"It is a pond where my Ummah will be brought on the Day of Judgement. Its containers are as numerous as the stars in the sky."**

And then, inside this most generous of surahs, the hadith turns sharply:

> A servant from among them will be **prevented** from reaching it. The Prophet ﷺ will say: *"O Lord! Verily, he is from my Ummah."*
>
> And Allah will say: **"Verily, you do not know what he introduced after you."**

This is Muslim's wording. It is worth sitting with the placement: the abundance is real, and the invitation is to the Ummah — but arriving at it depends on having kept to what he ﷺ left, not what was invented after him.

---

## 4. The Wider Meaning: Abundant Goodness

**Al-Bukhari, from Sa`id bin Jubayr ← Ibn `Abbas** — Al-Kawthar is *"the good which Allah gave to him."*

Abu Bishr pressed Sa`id on it: *people are saying it is a river in Paradise.* Sa`id answered:

> **"The river which is in Paradise is part of the goodness which Allah gave him."**

And in another narration Ibn `Abbas said simply: **"Al-Kawthar is the abundant goodness."**

Ibn Kathir's own verdict settles the two readings into one. The word comes from **kathrah**, and linguistically means an abundance of good — so the explanation **includes the river and other things as well**. The river is not a rival meaning; it is a specimen of the meaning.

---

## 5. Pray, and Sacrifice — For Him Alone (108:2)

> *Therefore turn in prayer to your Lord and sacrifice.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as the direct consequence of verse 1: *just as We have given you abundant goodness in this life and the Hereafter — and the river is part of it — then*

- make your **obligatory and optional prayer** solely and sincerely for your Lord;
- make your **sacrifice** for Him alone, pronouncing **His Name alone**, ascribing no partner to Him.

The cross-reference he gives is the one that says it most completely:

> *Say: "Verily, my Salah, my sacrifice, my living, and my dying are for Allah, the Lord of all that exists. He has no partner. And of this I have been commanded, and I am the first of the Muslims."* **(6:162–163)**

**Ibn `Abbas, `Ata', Mujahid, `Ikrimah and Al-Hasan** took the sacrifice here to mean the **Budn** (the sacrificial camels) — and **Qatadah, Muhammad bin Ka`b Al-Qurazi, Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi`, `Ata' Al-Khurasani, Al-Hakam, Isma`il bin Abi Khalid** and others of the Salaf said the same.

The point of the verse, Ibn Kathir stresses, is **contrast**: this is the exact opposite of the way of the idolaters, who **prostrate to other than Allah and sacrifice in other than His Name** — hence *"And do not eat from what Allah's Name has not been pronounced over; indeed that is Fisq."* (6:121)

---

## 6. The One Cut Off Is Not You (108:3)

> *For he who hates you, he will be cut off.*

Meaning: whoever hates you, O Muhammad, and hates **what you came with — of guidance, truth, clear proof and manifest light** — *he* is the one who is **most cut off, meanest, lowliest, and who will not be remembered**.

**Who it was revealed about** — Ibn Kathir preserves the whole disagreement rather than choosing:

| Named | Reported by |
|---|---|
| **Al-`As bin Wa'il** — who used to say of the Prophet ﷺ, *"Leave him, he is a man cut off with no descendants; when he dies he will not be remembered"* | Ibn `Abbas, Mujahid, Sa`id bin Jubayr, Qatadah |
| **`Uqbah bin Abi Mu`ayt** | Shamir bin `Atiyah |
| **Ka`b bin Al-Ashraf and a group of the Quraysh disbelievers** | Ibn `Abbas, `Ikrimah |
| **Abu Lahab**, who announced when a son of the Prophet ﷺ died: *"Muhammad has been cut off tonight"* | `Ata' |

The fullest of these is **Al-Bazzar's report from Ibn `Abbas — a chain Ibn Kathir calls authentic.** Ka`b bin Al-Ashraf came to Makkah, and the Quraysh appealed to him as an arbiter: *You are the leader of the people. What do you think of this worthless man, cut off from his own people, who claims he is better than us — and we are the people of the place of pilgrimage, the custodians of the Ka`bah, the ones who give the pilgrims water?* Ka`b told them: **"You are better than him."** And Allah revealed *inna shani'aka huwa-l-abtar*.

**As-Suddi** supplies the linguistic background that makes the whole exchange land: when a man's male children died, the Arabs said **"he has been cut off."** So when the Prophet's ﷺ sons died, they said it of him.

---

## 7. The Verdict That History Has Already Delivered

Ibn Kathir closes with something close to indignation:

> They thought, in their ignorance, that if his sons died his remembrance would be cut off.
>
> **Allah forbid.** To the contrary — Allah **preserved his remembrance for all the world to see**, and obligated all the servants to follow his Law. And this will continue for all time, until the Day of Gathering and the coming of the Hereafter.

The men who called him *abtar* are remembered now only as footnotes in the commentary on the verse that answered them. That is the argument of the surah, settled by fourteen centuries of evidence.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **The surah is a reply.** They said *cut off*; Allah answered with *abundance*, and then handed their own word back to them in the final verse.
- **Al-Kawthar is a river — and more than a river.** A river in Paradise with banks of gold and pearl, water whiter than milk and sweeter than honey, smelling of the strongest musk. But Ibn `Abbas's reading is broader and Ibn Kathir prefers it: **abundant goodness**, of which the river is one part.
- **It flows into the Hawd**, where the Ummah is brought on the Day of Judgement, with vessels as numerous as the stars — **and some will be turned away for what they introduced after him ﷺ.** The gift is vast; access is not unconditional.
- **The one instruction of the surah is two acts: prayer and sacrifice** — both directed to Allah alone, both performed in His Name alone. Given abundantly, respond with sincerity, and give it to no one else.
- **The verse was revealed while he was smiling.** Whatever the insult had cost him, the answer to it arrived as good news.
- **Remembrance is not carried by lineage.** They bet that his name would die with his sons. His name is now said in every call to prayer on earth.
