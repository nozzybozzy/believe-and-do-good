*Revealed in Makkah · 25 ayat · "The Splitting Asunder" — named for its opening verse · contains a prostration of recitation*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Inshiqaq is built on a contrast it never states outright, but which runs through it from the first verse to the last.

**The sky obeys. The earth obeys.** When the command comes, the heaven tears open and the earth is stretched flat and empties out its dead — and twice the surah pauses to say the same thing about them: *it listens to and obeys its Lord, and it must do so.* Not reluctantly, not as an exception, but because obedience is simply what is *right* for a creature standing before that Lord.

Then the surah turns to man — and the accusation, when it lands, is precisely that he does *not*:

> *What is the matter with them, that they believe not? And when the Qur'an is recited to them, they fall not prostrate.*

The heavens submit without being asked twice. The human being hears the words of his Lord and stays standing.

Between those two poles the surah does three things. It tells you **where you are going** — *you are labouring toward your Lord, and you will meet it*. It shows you **the two ways the meeting goes**: a record in the right hand and an easy reckoning, or a record handed round behind the back and an invocation for destruction. And it swears an oath on **the twilight, the gathering night, and the completed moon** to establish one fact about the road: *you shall certainly travel from stage to stage.*

Nothing in your life stays as it is. That is the argument.

---

## 1. The Prostration in This Surah

Ibn Kathir opens not with the text but with a practice.

**Abu Salamah** reports that Abu Hurayrah, leading them in prayer, recited *When the heaven is split asunder* and prostrated at it. Afterwards he told them the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had prostrated during its recitation. *(Muslim and An-Nasa'i, on the authority of Malik.)*

**Al-Bukhari, from Abu Rafi':** he prayed the night prayer behind Abu Hurayrah, who recited it and prostrated. Abu Rafi' questioned him about it. Abu Hurayrah answered:

> *"I prostrated behind Abul-Qasim ﷺ, and I will never cease prostrating during its recitation until I meet him."*

A small detail worth holding onto: the surah that ends by condemning those who hear the Qur'an and do **not** prostrate is itself a surah at which you prostrate.

---

## 2. The Sky and the Earth Do As They Are Told (84:1–5)

> *When the heaven is split asunder, and listens to and obeys its Lord — and it must do so.*

This is the Day of Judgement. The heaven *listens* means it hears its Lord's command to split, and complies.

On **"and it must do so"** (*wa ḥuqqat*) Ibn Kathir gives the reasoning: it is right and fitting for it to obey the command of its Lord, **because He is great, and cannot be rejected nor overcome.** Rather, He overpowers everything, and everything is submissive to Him.

> *And when the earth is stretched forth* — expanded, spread out, extended.

> *And has cast out all that was in it and became empty* — it throws out the dead inside it and empties itself of them. *(Mujahid, Sa'id, and Qatadah.)*

Then the same sentence again, word for word, about the earth: **it listens to and obeys its Lord, and it must do so.**

The repetition is not filler. It is the standard the rest of the surah measures man against.

---

## 3. You Are Already Travelling Toward Him (84:6)

> *O man! Verily, you are labouring toward your Lord — a sure labouring — and you will meet.*

*Kadiḥ* — Ibn Kathir glosses it as **hastening toward your Lord and working deeds**. It is not a description of the pious. It is a description of everyone. The motion is not optional; only its direction of effort is yours.

**"And you will meet"** — the pronoun is left open, and Ibn Kathir preserves both readings:

- **You will meet your deeds** — whatever you did of good or evil.
- **You will meet your Lord** — who will reward you for your work and pay you for your efforts.

He then says the two views are **connected**, not rival: meeting Him *is* meeting what you sent ahead.

**Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas:** *"Whatever deed you do, you will meet Allah with it, whether it is good or bad."*

And the hadith Ibn Kathir cites as proof — Abu Dawud At-Tayalisi, from Jabir — is the sharpest three lines in the surah. **Jibril said to the Prophet ﷺ:**

> *O Muhammad —*
> *Live how you wish, for verily you will die;*
> *love what you wish, for verily you will part with it;*
> *and do what you wish, for verily you will meet it.*

Two of the three end in loss. Only the deed follows you out.

---

## 4. The Right Hand, and the Difference Between Presentation and Interrogation (84:7–9)

> *Then as for him who will be given his Record in his right hand, he surely will receive an easy reckoning.*

**Easy** means without difficulty — **he will not be investigated for the minute details of his deeds.** And Ibn Kathir explains why that mercy is the whole difference: *whoever is reckoned like that will certainly be destroyed.*

The hadith behind it, recorded by **Imam Ahmad from A'ishah**, is one of the most consequential exchanges in the tafsir:

The Prophet ﷺ said, **"Whoever is interrogated during the reckoning will be punished."**

A'ishah objected — but Allah says *he will receive an easy reckoning*.

He replied:

> *"That is not the Reckoning; that is the presentation. Whoever is interrogated during the Reckoning on the Day of Judgement will be punished."*

Two distinct things, then. **The presentation (*'arḍ*)** — your deeds are shown to you, acknowledged, and passed over. **The interrogation (*munāqashah*)** — every item pursued to its end, and no one survives it. The believer's hope is not that his file is clean; it is that it is not opened line by line.

*(Also in Al-Bukhari, Muslim, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i and Ibn Jarir.)*

> *And will return to his family Masrur!*

He returns to **his family in Paradise** (Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak), *masrur* meaning happy and delighted by what Allah has given him.

---

## 5. The Record Behind the Back (84:10–15)

> *But whosoever is given his Record behind his back, he will invoke destruction, and he shall enter a blazing Fire.*

Ibn Kathir is exact about the posture: **the book is given in his left hand, from behind his back, his hand bent round behind him.** He will call for *thubūr* — loss, ruin, destruction.

Then the verse that pairs him with the man of the right hand, using the same word:

> *Verily, he was among his people **Masrur**!*

He was happy too. That is the point. Both men were delighted; the difference is what they were delighted about. Ibn Kathir's reading of this joy is severe and precise:

> He did not think about the consequences, nor fear what was in front of him. **His light happiness will be followed by long grief.**

> *Verily, he thought that he would never return!*

He believed there was no return to Allah, that he would not be brought back after death. *(Ibn Abbas, Qatadah and others.)*

> *Yes! Verily, his Lord has been ever beholding him!*

Two things at once. **Allah will certainly repeat his creation just as He began it**, and reward him according to his deeds. And throughout the life in which the man assumed no one was watching, his Lord was **ever watchful of him** — All-Knowing, All-Aware.

---

## 6. The Oaths: Twilight, Night, and the Full Moon (84:16–18)

> *But no! I swear by Ash-Shafaq, and by the night and what it gathers, and by the moon when it becomes full.*

**Ash-Shafaq.** Ibn Kathir gathers a long line of authorities — Ali, Ibn Abbas, Ubadah bin As-Samit, Abu Hurayrah, Shaddad bin Aws, Ibn Umar, Makhul, Malik, and others — all saying it is **the redness in the sky**. One report from Abu Hurayrah (via Abdur-Razzaq) says the whiteness.

- **Al-Khalil bin Ahmad:** the redness appearing after sunset until the time of the last 'Isha'; when it goes, *the Shafaq has disappeared.*
- **Al-Jawhari:** the remaining light of the sun and its redness at the start of the night, until true darkness.
- **Ikrimah:** what is between Maghrib and 'Isha'.

And the hadith that settles it — **Muslim, from Abdullah bin Amr**: *the time of Al-Maghrib lasts as long as the Shafaq has not disappeared.* Ibn Kathir notes this is proof for the definition of Al-Khalil and Al-Jawhari.

**And what the night *wasaqa*** — what it **gathers**. Qatadah: *the stars and animals it gathers.* Ikrimah: what it drives home by its darkness, **because when night comes, everything goes to its home.**

**And the moon when it *ittasaqa*** — when it comes together and becomes complete and full (Ibn Abbas, Al-Hasan); when it completes its cycle (Qatadah).

Three oaths, and each one is a thing in **transition**: light draining out of the sky, darkness drawing the scattered world in, a crescent filling to a disc. The oath fits what it is sworn upon.

---

## 7. Stage After Stage (84:19)

> *You shall certainly travel from stage to stage.*

**Al-Bukhari records from Mujahid, from Ibn Abbas:** *"Stage after stage. Your Prophet ﷺ has said this."*

**Ikrimah:** stage after stage — *weaned after being breast-fed, and an old man after being a young man.*

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri** gives the fullest reading, and it is the one to keep:

> Ease after difficulty,
> difficulty after ease,
> wealth after poverty,
> poverty after wealth,
> health after sickness,
> and sickness after health.

Notice the symmetry. He does not list a ladder going one way. Every pair runs in both directions. The verse is not a promise that things improve; it is a statement that **nothing holds still** — which is a consolation when you are in the hard half and a warning when you are in the easy one.

---

## 8. Why Don't They Prostrate? (84:20–24)

> *What is the matter with them, that they believe not? And when the Qur'an is recited to them, they fall not prostrate.*

Ibn Kathir turns it into a genuine question: **what prevents them** from believing in Allah, His Messenger and the Last Day — and what is wrong with them, that when Allah's Ayat and His Words are recited they do not prostrate **out of awe, respect and reverence**?

> *Nay, those who disbelieve deny.*

Their character is **rejection, obstinacy, and opposition to the truth**. It is not a failure of information.

> *And Allah knows best what they gather.*

Mujahid and Qatadah: **what they conceal in their chests.** The public denial has a private interior, and it is not hidden.

> *So announce to them a painful torment.*

Inform them, O Muhammad, that Allah has prepared for them a painful torment. The Arabic uses the verb of glad tidings for a message of punishment — the surah's last piece of irony.

---

## 9. The Exception (84:25)

> *Save those who believe and do righteous good deeds — for them is a reward that will never come to an end.*

Ibn Kathir marks this as **a clear exception**, and splits it in the way he always does: *believe* refers to what is **in their hearts**; *do righteous good deeds* to what they do **with their limbs**. Neither stands alone.

*Ghayr mamnūn* — the reward that will never come to an end:

- **Ibn Abbas:** without being decreased.
- **Mujahid and Ad-Dahhak:** without measure.
- **As-Suddi:** without end and without decrease.

Ibn Kathir's summation: the result of all their statements is that **it is without end**. Cross-referenced to *A gift without an end* (11:108).

Set against §7, this is the surah's final move. Everything in this life is stage after stage, reversal after reversal, nothing holding still. The one thing that does not turn over is the reward at the end of the road.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The sky and the earth obey because it is only right that they should. The surah's charge against man is that he heard the same Lord and did not go down.**

Four things to carry:

1. **You are already moving.** *Kadiḥ* — labouring toward Him — is said of everyone, believer and denier alike. The only question is what you are carrying when you arrive.
2. **Jibril's three lines.** Live how you wish, you will die. Love what you wish, you will lose it. Do what you wish — *you will meet it.*
3. **Pray for the presentation, not the audit.** *Whoever is interrogated during the Reckoning will be punished.* The easy reckoning is not a clean record; it is a record not pursued line by line.
4. **Both men were happy.** The one in Paradise and the one entering the Fire are described with the same word — *masrur*. One was happy about the right thing; the other was happy because he never thought about what was ahead. **His light happiness will be followed by long grief.**
