*Revealed in Makkah · 206 ayat · "The Heights" — the longest of the Makkan surahs*

---

## 0. The Shape of the Surah

Ibn Kathir opens this surah without a chapter on its virtues — he goes straight to the text. But the architecture is unmistakable once you see it, and it explains the surah's method:

1. **The Book, and the warning** (1–10)
2. **Adam and Iblis** — the first refusal, the first envy (11–25)
3. **O Children of Adam** — four addresses to Adam's descendants about clothing, Shaytan, adornment and messengers (26–36)
4. **The Hereafter, argued aloud** — the disputes between Paradise, Hell, and the wall between them (37–53)
5. **The Lord of creation, and how to ask Him** (54–58)
6. **Five prophets, one sermon** — Nuh, Hud, Salih, Lut, Shu'ayb (59–93)
7. **The law of the towns** — how Allah deals with a community that rejects (94–102)
8. **Musa and Fir'awn** — the longest single narrative (103–171)
9. **The covenant in the loins, and the man who threw it away** (172–177)
10. **Names, hearts, the Hour, and the closing commands** (178–206)

The logic: *this is what happened to your father Adam; this is what happened to every nation that heard a messenger; you are hearing one now.*

---

## 1. The Opening: Don't Let Your Chest Be Narrow (7:1–3)

The surah begins with the disconnected letters **Alif-Lam-Mim-Sad**, on which Ibn Kathir refers back to his discussion at the head of Al-Baqarah.

Then a striking instruction to the Prophet ﷺ: *let not your breast be narrow therefrom.*

- **Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi:** meaning, do not have doubt about it.
- **Another view:** do not hesitate to convey it and warn with it — paired with *be patient as did the Messengers of strong will* (46:35).

And then the address widens from the Prophet ﷺ to the whole world: **follow what was sent down to you from your Lord, and follow not any Awliya' besides Him.** Ibn Kathir's gloss is sharp — to disregard what the Messenger brought and follow something else is *to deviate from Allah's judgment to the decision of someone else.*

---

## 2. Destroyed at Night or at Noon (7:4–7)

*Our torment came upon them by night or while they were taking their midday nap.*

Both are times of **rest, leisure, heedlessness and amusement** — that is the point. Their only cry when it came was an admission: *"Verily, we were wrongdoers."*

Then the double questioning of the Day of Resurrection: **the nations will be asked how they answered the Messengers, and the Messengers will be asked whether they conveyed.** Ibn Abbas: they will be asked "about what they conveyed."

And the guarantee behind it: *indeed We have not been absent.* Nothing escapes His observation — not what the eyes are deluded by, not what the hearts conceal.

---

## 3. The Balance: What Exactly Gets Weighed? (7:8–9)

A genuinely interesting piece of Ibn Kathir's method. Three positions existed among the scholars, each with evidence:

| View | Evidence cited |
|---|---|
| **The deeds themselves** are weighed, given physical weight by Allah | Al-Baghawi from Ibn Abbas; Al-Baqarah and Al 'Imran coming as two clouds; the Qur'an appearing as a pale-faced young man to its companion |
| **The scrolls of records** are weighed | The hadith of the man with ninety-nine scrolls of sins, each as long as the eye can see, outweighed by a single card bearing *La ilaha illallah* — "the scrolls will go up, as the card becomes heavier" (At-Tirmidhi: authentic) |
| **The person** is weighed | A fat man brought forth who "will not weigh with Allah equal to the wing of a mosquito"; and of Ibn Mas'ud's thin legs: *"By He in Whose Hand is my soul! They are heavier on the Balance than Uhud."* |

**Ibn Kathir's resolution:** it is possible to combine them. All of it will truly occur — sometimes deeds, sometimes scrolls, sometimes the doer. *And Allah knows best.*

---

## 4. Adam and Iblis: The First Analogy (7:11–18)

Allah reminds the Children of Adam of **the honor of their father and the enmity of Shaytan**, so they will beware of him and not walk in his footsteps.

Iblis's refusal, and his excuse — *I am better than him; You created me from fire, and him from clay* — draws Ibn Kathir's most quotable line: **"this excuse is worse than the crime itself."**

Why it fails:

- **He looked at the origin of creation, not the honor bestowed** — that Allah created Adam with His Hand and blew life into him.
- **The claim itself was false.** Clay has the qualities of *wisdom, forbearance, patience and assurance*; it is where plants grow and increase and give. Fire has the qualities of *burning, recklessness and hastiness*.
- The two origins ran true to type: fire drove Iblis to ruin; clay led Adam back to Allah in repentance and humility.

**Iblis was the first to use Qiyas.** Ibn Jarir records this from Al-Hasan and from Ibn Sirin, both with authentic chains. Ibn Sirin's version has a bite to it: *"The first to use Qiyas was Iblis — and would the sun and moon be worshipped if it were not for Qiyas?"*

**The four directions of attack** (7:17). Ibn Abbas, via Ali bin Abi Talhah:

| Direction | Attack |
|---|---|
| From before them | Raising doubts about the Hereafter |
| From behind them | Making them eager for this life |
| From their right | Confusing them in the religion |
| From their left | Luring them to sins |

Ikrimah from Ibn Abbas notes what is *missing* from the list: **"He did not say that he will come from above them, because the mercy descends from above."**

Shaytan's assumption turned out true (34:20), which is why the Prophet ﷺ used to say morning and evening: *O Allah! Protect me from before me, from behind me, from my right, from my left and from above me. I seek refuge with Your greatness from being killed from below me.* (Waki': "This refers to earthquakes.")

There is also the hadith of **Shaytan sitting in the paths**: he sat in the path of Islam ("would you abandon the religion of your forefathers?"), in the path of Hijrah ("would you leave your land and sky?"), in the path of Jihad ("you will be killed, your wife married, your wealth divided") — and the son of Adam disobeyed him each time. Whoever does this and then dies, is killed, drowns, or has his neck broken by his mount, *it is a promise from Allah that He admits him into Paradise.*

---

## 5. The Fall, and What It Uncovered (7:19–25)

Shaytan's method was **an oath**. He swore by Allah that he was a sincere well-wisher — and Ibn Kathir pauses on this: *it is a fact that the believer in Allah might sometimes become the victim of deceit.* Qatadah's reading: "I was created before you, and I have better knowledge than you. Therefore, follow me."

The consequence was exposure. Wahb bin Munabbih (authentic chain via Ibn Jarir): *the private parts of Adam and Hawwa' had a light covering them* which fell away when they ate. Ibn Abbas: they covered themselves with **fig leaves**.

Ubayy bin Ka'b's account: Adam, tall as a palm tree, ran through Paradise in fright until a tree caught him by the head. Allah called, *"O Adam! Do you run away from Me?"* He said, **"O Lord! I felt ashamed before You."**

**The decisive contrast** — Qatadah, via Abdur-Razzaq: Adam asked, *"O Lord! What if I repented and sought forgiveness?"* and was told, *"Then I will admit you into Paradise."* **As for Shaytan, he did not ask for forgiveness — he asked for respite. Each one of them was given what he asked for.**

On where they landed on earth, Ibn Kathir refuses to speculate: those accounts are Israelite tales, and *if knowing them were useful in religion or life, Allah would have mentioned them in His Book.*

---

## 6. O Children of Adam: Clothing, and the Naked Tawaf (7:26–33)

Two kinds of garment, then a third:

- **Libas** — the clothing that covers the private parts. Essential.
- **Rish** — outer adornment for beautification (Ibn Jarir includes furniture and outer clothes). Complementary.
- **The garment of Taqwa — "that is better."** Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam: *"When one fears Allah, Allah covers his errors."*

**The occasion.** These verses answer a real practice. Ibn Abbas (recorded by Muslim, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Jarir): the idolators performed Tawaf around the House **naked** — men by day, women by night — claiming *"we perform Tawaf as our mothers gave birth to us,"* and claiming further that **Allah had commanded it.** Allah's reply: *Nay, Allah never commands Fahishah. Do you say about Allah what you know not?*

Ibn Kathir adds the detail: the Quraysh (**Al-Hums**) circled in their regular clothes; the rest of the Arabs either borrowed a garment from Al-Hums or went naked.

Against that background comes **7:31 — "Take your adornment to every Masjid."**

- Ibn Abbas: wear clean, proper clothes that cover; people were commanded to wear their best for every prayer.
- Hence the encouragement to dress well for prayer, especially Jumu'ah and 'Id; to wear perfume (men); to use the Siwak, which "completes adornment."
- **White is best.** *"Wear white clothes, for it is among your best clothes, and also wrap your dead with it. And Ithmid is among the best of your Kuhl, for it clears the sight and helps the hair grow."*

**And on the other side: "waste not by extravagance."**

Al-Bukhari records Ibn Abbas: *"Eat what you wish and wear what you wish, as long as you avoid two things: extravagance and arrogance."*

And the famous measure: *The Son of Adam will not fill a pot worse for himself than his stomach. It is enough for him to eat a few bites that strengthen his spine. If he must, then a third for food, a third for drink, and a third for his breathing.*

Ibn Jarir defines *israf* (extravagance) with unusual precision: **it runs in both directions** — going to excess in what Allah allowed, *allowing what He prohibited, or prohibiting what He allowed.* Allah wants the permitted treated as permitted and the forbidden as forbidden. "This is the justice that He has commanded."

**7:33 — the list of what is actually forbidden:** the Fawahish (open and secret), Ithm, transgression without right, Shirk, and saying about Allah what you do not know. As-Suddi and Mujahid distinguish them: *Ithm* is the sin one commits against oneself; *oppression* is transgression against others.

## 7. Two Groups, and the Question of Fitrah (7:29–30)

*"A group He has guided, and a group deserved to be in error."*

Ibn Kathir gathers the readings of *As He brought you in the beginning, so shall you be brought into being again*: Mujahid and Al-Hasan take it as resurrection; Ibn Zayd (preferred by Ibn Jarir) takes it as *He will return them as He started them — some believers, some disbelievers.*

He then holds this together with the Fitrah texts rather than letting either cancel the other:

- *Every child is born upon the Fitrah; it is only his parents who turn him into a Jew, a Christian or a Zoroastrian.*
- *Allah said: "I created My servants Hunafa, but the devils came to them and deviated them from their religion."*
- And the hadith of the man who works the deeds of Paradise until an arm's length separates him from it, *but that which was written in the Book takes precedence.*

**His synthesis:** Allah originally created all His servants able to recognize Him and took their covenant to that effect — and He has decided that some will be miserable and some happy. *"All people go out in the morning and sell themselves; some free themselves and some destroy themselves."*

Ibn Jarir draws a legal-theological consequence: the misguided group *believed they were guided* (7:30), and Allah still distinguished them from the guided — "doing so in both name and judgement."

## 8. The Hereafter, Argued Aloud (7:37–53)

This passage is almost entirely **dialogue**, and Ibn Kathir treats it as such.

**At death** (7:37): the angels ask the idolator, *"Where are those whom you used to invoke besides Allah?"* — and the reply is a confession: *"They have vanished and deserted us."*

**Entering the Fire** (7:38–39): each nation curses the one before it. The followers demand double torment for those who misled them; the leaders answer, *"You were not better than us."* Ibn Kathir gathers the parallel scenes from 2:166–167 and 34:32–33, where each party conceals its regret when it sees the torment.

**The gates that do not open** (7:40): two explanations from Ibn Abbas, and Ibn Kathir keeps both —

- their **deeds and supplications** will not ascend, or
- their **souls** will not be admitted.

The second is supported by Al-Bara's long hadith on the wicked soul: the angels carry it up, and at every gathering of angels they are asked *"whose wicked soul is this?"* — until they ask for the lowest heaven's door to be opened and **it is not opened.** Ibn Abbas's fuller version combines the two: *"not for their deeds or their souls."*

On *until the Jamal goes through the eye of the needle* — the male camel; Ibn Abbas is reported as reciting it **Jummal**, meaning a thick rope.

**The removal of Ghill** (7:43): envy and hatred taken out of the breasts of the people of Paradise. Al-Bukhari's hadith of the bridge between Paradise and Hell, where the wrongs done between believers in this world are settled *until they are purified and cleansed*. As-Suddi describes two springs at the door of Paradise: they drink from one and hatred leaves the heart; they bathe in the other and the brightness of delight radiates from their faces.

Their words on entering — *"never could we have found guidance, were it not that Allah had guided us"* — are matched with the hadith: **the good deeds of one of you will not admit him into Paradise.** *"Not even you, O Messenger of Allah?" "Not even I, unless Allah grants it to me out of His mercy."*

**Badr as a rehearsal.** When Paradise calls to the Fire, *"have you found true what your Lord promised?"*, Ibn Kathir recalls the Prophet ﷺ at the well of Badr addressing the corpses by name — O Abu Jahl, O Utbah, O Shaybah. Umar objected that he was speaking to carrion. He said: *"By He in Whose Hand is my soul! You do not hear what I am saying better than they do, but they cannot reply."*

**"We shall forget them as they forgot"** (7:51) — not literal forgetting; Allah *"neither errs nor forgets"* (20:52). Ibn Abbas: *"Allah will forget the good about them, but not their evil."* Mujahid: "We shall leave them in the Fire."

## 9. Al-A'raf — the Surah's Name (7:46–49)

Between Paradise and the Fire there is a **barrier** — the wall of 57:13, with a gate: mercy inside, torment outside. Ibn Jarir: *A'raf* is the plural of *'Urf*; the Arabs call any elevated ground *'Urf*. Mujahid: a barrier that has a gate.

**Who stands on it?** As-Suddi explains the name itself: its residents are so called because they *recognize* (**ya'rifun**) the people. And who they are — from Hudhayfah, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and many of the Salaf:

> **A people whose good and bad deeds are equal. Their evil deeds prevented them from qualifying for Paradise, and their good deeds qualified them to avoid the Fire. So they are stopped there on the wall until Allah judges them.**

They greet the people of Paradise with *Salam* while not yet having entered, **but they hope to.** Al-Hasan, reciting this, said: *"By Allah! Allah did not put this hope in their hearts, except for an honor that He intends to bestow on them."*

Turning the other way toward the Fire, they pray: *"Our Lord! Place us not with the people who are wrongdoers."*

And then they call out to the arrogant chiefs they recognize below: **"Of what benefit to you was your gathering, and your arrogance?"** — your numbers, your wealth. *"Are they those of whom you swore that Allah would never show them mercy?"* — and it is said to the very people who were sworn against: **Enter Paradise, no fear shall be on you, nor shall you grieve.**

## 10. The Lord of All Creation, and How to Ask Him (7:54–58)

**Six days.** Sunday through Friday; on Friday the whole creation was assembled and Adam was created. Saturday saw no creation — *As-Sabt* means stoppage. There is disagreement whether these were standard days or each a thousand years (reported from Mujahid, Ahmad, and Ibn Abbas via Ad-Dahhak). Ibn Kathir cites Ahmad's hadith from Abu Hurayrah listing the sequence: dust, mountains, trees, unpleasant things, light, the spread of creatures, and Adam last, after 'Asr on Friday.

**Istawa.** This is where Ibn Kathir plants his creed, briefly and firmly:

> We follow the way of the righteous predecessors — Malik, Al-Awza'i, Ath-Thawri, Al-Layth, Ash-Shafi'i, Ahmad, Ishaq bin Rahwayh. We accept the apparent meaning **without discussing its essence, without equating it with the attributes of creation, and without altering or denying it.** *There is nothing like Him* (42:11).

He quotes Nu'aym bin Hammad, Al-Bukhari's teacher: whoever **likens** Allah to His creation has committed Kufr; whoever **denies** what Allah described Himself with has committed Kufr; and whoever affirms what the plain Ayat and authentic Hadiths say, in the manner that suits Allah's majesty while rejecting all shortcomings from Him, **has taken the path of guidance.**

**Then: how to ask Him** (7:55–56).

- *Tadarru'an wa Khufyah* — in humbleness and humility, in the heart, not loudly to show off (Ibn Jarir).
- The Prophet ﷺ to Companions raising their voices: *"O people! Take it easy on yourselves. You are not calling one who is deaf or absent; the One you are calling is All-Hearer, Near."*
- **"He likes not the aggressors"** — including aggression *in du'a itself*. Abu Mijlaz: such as asking to reach the rank of the Prophets. Abdullah bin Mughaffal heard his son ask for "the white castle on the right side of Paradise" and corrected him: *ask Allah for Paradise and seek refuge from the Fire* — for the Prophet ﷺ said, **"There will come some people who transgress in supplication and purification."**
- *Do not do mischief on the earth after it has been set in order* — because when affairs are in order and then corruption enters, the harm to people is at its maximum.
- *Invoke Him with fear and hope* — fearing His severe torment, hoping in His tremendous reward. Matar Al-Warraq: **"Earn Allah's promise by obeying Him, for He ordained that His mercy is near to the good-doers."**

**The rain parable** (7:57–58): the winds carry heavy clouds to dead land, and fruit comes out — *"similarly, We shall raise up the dead."* And the parable of the two soils, which Ibn Kathir pairs with Abu Musa's hadith on the three kinds of ground receiving rain: fertile soil that produces, hard ground that holds water for others to use, and barren land that neither holds nor produces — the three responses to knowledge.

## 11. Five Prophets, One Sermon (7:59–93)

Ibn Kathir notes the design: after Adam's story, Allah begins the prophets, "the first then the latter of them." Read together, the five cycles are almost identical in shape — **call to Tawhid → accusation → denial → destruction → the prophet's parting speech.** The variations are where the meaning sits.

### Nuh (59–64)
Nuh bin Lamak bin Matushalakh bin Khanukh — and Khanukh, they say, was **Idris**, the first to write with a pen.

Ibn Abbas on **how idolatry began**: righteous men died, their people built places of worship over their graves and made images of them to remember and imitate their devotion. Time passed; the images became statues; the statues were worshipped and named after the righteous men — **Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq, Nasr.** Only then was Nuh sent.

His people's charge: *"we see you in plain error."* Ibn Kathir's comment: **this is the attitude of evil people — they consider the righteous to be the misguided ones.**

Nuh's answer sets the template repeated by every prophet after him: *I convey the Messages of my Lord, and give sincere advice to you, and I know from Allah what you know not.* Ibn Kathir: these three — **conveying, sincerity, and knowledge of Allah** — are the marks of a Messenger. He pairs it with 'Arafah: *"O people! You will be asked about me, what will you say?"* — "We testify that you have conveyed and delivered and offered sincere advice." — *"O Allah! Bear witness."*

### Hud and 'Ad (65–72)
'Ad of Iram, the people of lofty pillars (89:6–8), living in the **Ahqaf** (sand mounds) of Yemen between Oman and Hadramawt. Ali bin Abi Talib once described to a man from Hadramawt a red sand mound with Arak and Lote trees — *"there is the grave of Hud in its vicinity."*

Their signature was **strength misread as security**: *"Who is mightier than us in strength?"* (41:15). Hud's rebuke — *"Do you dispute with me over names which you have named, you and your fathers, with no authority from Allah?"* — is the argument that idols are nothing but labels people invented.

They were destroyed by a barren wind, seven nights and eight days, leaving them *as if they were hollow trunks of date palms* (69:7).

**The emissary of 'Ad.** Ibn Kathir gives the full story from Al-Harith Al-Bakri's hadith. In a famine, 'Ad sent Qayl for relief. He stopped with Mu'awiyah bin Bakr, was supplied with wine and two singing girls, and stayed a month. Then he went to the mountains and prayed for rain. Black clouds appeared and he was told to choose one. He pointed — and a voice came from it: *"Take it, as ashes that will leave none in 'Ad."* The wind sent against them, it was said, was no more than would pass through a ring. Hence the Arab proverb Abu Wa'il quotes: when sending an emissary, *"Do not be like the emissary of 'Ad."*

### Salih and Thamud (73–79)
Thamud lived between the Hijaz and Ash-Sham. **The Prophet ﷺ passed their ruins at Al-Hijr on the way to Tabuk** and ordered the army to spill the water they had drawn from Thamud's wells and give the dough to the camels — and said: *"Do not enter upon these who were tormented, unless you do so while crying. If you are not crying, then do not enter, so that what befell them does not befall you."*

**The she-camel** came out of a rock they themselves chose (Al-Katibah), after Salih took their covenant that they would believe. It emerged pregnant, its fetus visibly moving. Their chief Jundu' bin 'Amr believed; the rest were held back by their nobles.

**Who killed it.** Ibn Kathir gives the detail: two wealthy women, **Unayzah bint Ghanm** and **Saduf bint Al-Muhayya**, offered themselves and their daughters as the prize. **Qudar bin Salif** and **Musaddi' bin Mihraj** did the deed with seven others — the nine of 27:48. But Qatadah reports that the killer polled everyone, *including women in their rooms and children*, and found the whole tribe agreed — which is why 91:14 says *they killed it* and *made them equal in destruction*.

**The three days of respite**, colour by colour: Thursday their faces turned **yellow**, Friday **red**, Saturday **black**. On Sunday they put on the perfume used for shrouding the dead and waited. When the sun rose, the Sayhah came from above and the tremor from below — *all in an hour.*

The nine plotters had already been killed by falling stones the night they conspired to murder Salih. And **Abu Righal**, who was inside the Sacred Area, was spared until he stepped outside — then a stone fell and killed him. The Prophet ﷺ identified his grave and the golden branch buried with it.

### Lut (80–84)
Lut, son of Haran, nephew of Ibrahim. Ibn Kathir is explicit that the act was **an invention without precedent**: *"Never before the people of Lut did a male have sex with another male"* — not among the Children of Adam, nor any other creature, nor had it crossed their minds.

Their answer to the call was to try to expel him — *"these are indeed men who want to be pure!"* Qatadah's remark cuts: **"They shamed them with what is not a shame at all."**

His wife stayed. Ibn Kathir's reading: she did not leave the town, and Lut was not told to bring her — *she was of the Ghabirin*, those who remained. She had been the one signalling to her people who came to visit him.

### Shu'ayb and Madyan (85–93)
Madyan: descendants of Madyan son of Ibrahim; the city near Ma'an on the route to the Hijaz. Shu'ayb's Syriac name, per Ibn Ishaq, was **Yathrun (Jethro)**. He is called **"Speaker of the Prophets"** — *Khatib al-Anbiya'* — for the eloquence of his advice.

His people's sin was **commercial**: short measure and short weight. His prohibitions were three:

1. *Give full measure and weight, and wrong not men in their things.*
2. *Sit not on every road, threatening* — As-Suddi: they were bandits demanding money on pain of death (Ibn Kathir prefers this reading over "waylaying believers", because Shu'ayb says *every* road first and then adds the hindering).
3. *Do not do mischief on the earth after it has been set in order.*

And a reminder: *remember when you were but few, and He multiplied you.*

The chiefs answered with a threat of **expulsion or forced reversion** — and the reply of the believers is one of the great statements of the surah: *"Even though we hate it? … And it is not for us to return to it unless Allah, our Lord, should will. Our Lord comprehends all things in His knowledge. In Allah we put our trust."*

Ibn Kathir notes the punishments differ by surah **and match the taunt in each**: here and in Hud, the earthquake and the Sayhah (they had mocked his prayer); in Ash-Shu'ara', the **Day of the Shadow** — a gloomy cloud containing fire and tremendous light — because they had demanded *a piece of heaven falling on us*.

Both Salih and Shu'ayb address their people **after** the destruction — *"I have indeed conveyed my Lord's Messages, and I have given you good advice."* Ibn Kathir explains this with Badr again: they hear, but they cannot reply.

## 12. The Law of the Towns (7:94–102)

This is the passage where the five stories are turned into a **general principle**, and it is the heart of the surah's argument.

**Step one — hardship.** *We seized its people with Ba'sa' and Darra'* — Ba'sa' being physical sickness and ailment, Darra' being poverty and humiliation — **so that they might humble themselves.** They did not.

**Step two — relief.** *Then We changed the evil for the good, until they 'Afaw* — increased in numbers, wealth and offspring. And they misread it completely: *"Our fathers were touched with evil and with good"* — i.e., this is just a cycle, hardship and ease, nothing more.

> They failed both tests. Neither this nor that compelled them to change their ways.

Against them, the believer's posture: *The matter of the believer is amazing — nothing Allah decrees for him is not better for him. If harm strikes him he is patient, and that is better; if he is given prosperity he is grateful, and that is better.* And: *The believer will continue to be tested by afflictions until he ends up pure from sin. And the parable of the hypocrite is that of a donkey — it does not know why its owners tied it or released it.*

**Step three — the sudden seizure.** *So We seized them all of a sudden while they were unaware.* And the hadith: *sudden death is a mercy for the believer, but a sorrowful punishment for the disbeliever.*

**The counterfactual (7:96):** *had the people of the towns believed and had Taqwa, We would have opened for them blessings from the heaven and the earth* — rain from above, vegetation below. Ibn Kathir notes that **no city ever believed in its entirety except the people of Yunus** (10:98), and they believed only after the punishment appeared.

**The three securities no one should feel** (7:97–99): safe from punishment *by night while asleep*; safe from it *in the forenoon while playing*; safe from **Allah's plan** altogether. Al-Hasan Al-Basri's summary of the difference between two kinds of people:

> **"The believer performs the acts of worship, all the while feeling fear, in fright and anxiety. The Fajir commits the acts of disobedience while feeling safe."**

**And the closing indictment** (7:102): *most of them We found not true to their covenant.* Which covenant? Ibn Kathir identifies it as **the Fitrah** — the covenant taken in the loins of their fathers, that He is their Lord and there is no deity but Him. They affirmed it, then threw it behind their backs.

## 13. Musa and Fir'awn (7:103–137)

The longest narrative in the surah, and Ibn Kathir gives it room.

### The confrontation
Musa's opening is a statement of professional obligation: *"Proper it is for me that I say nothing concerning Allah but the truth"* — because of what I know of His might and power. And his demand: *"So let the Children of Israel depart along with me"* — release them from slavery; they are the offspring of an honourable Prophet, Israel, son of Ishaq, son of Ibrahim, the Khalil of Allah.

**The staff.** Ibn Abbas: it became a huge snake that opened its mouth and rushed at Fir'awn, its lower jaw on the ground and its upper jaw reaching the top of the palace wall. Fir'awn jumped from his throne, **wet himself for the first time in his life**, and begged Musa to take it away, promising belief and release. Musa took it and it became a staff again — and Fir'awn promptly reverted.

### The magicians
The chiefs' analysis — *"he wants to get you out of your land"* — and Ibn Kathir's observation that **what they feared came upon them exactly** (28:6).

Musa let them throw first. Ibn Kathir suggests the wisdom: *the people would see the sorcery first, and when the truth came at the moment they were all anticipating it, it would be more impressive to their hearts.*

Then the reversal. Al-Qasim bin Abi Bazzah: when they fell in prostration, **"they did not raise their heads before seeing Paradise, the Fire, and the recompense of their inhabitants."** Ibn Ishaq records their reasoning: *"Had Musa been a magician, he would not have prevailed over us."*

Fir'awn's response was the accusation of conspiracy — *"this is a plot which you have plotted in the city"* — which Ibn Kathir dismantles: Musa had just arrived from Madyan and had never met these men, whom Fir'awn himself had summoned from across Egypt. *"Thus he fooled his people, and they obeyed him"* (43:54).

Their reply to the threat of amputation and crucifixion is the surah's high point of courage: *"Verily, we are returning to our Lord… Our Lord! Pour out on us patience, and cause us to die as Muslims."* And the line preserved from Ibn Abbas, Ubayd bin Umayr, Qatadah and Ibn Jurayj:

> **"They started the day as sorcerers and ended it as martyrs."**

### The nine signs
When the persecution of the Children of Israel resumed, Musa's instruction was: *"Seek help in Allah and be patient. Verily, the earth is Allah's; He gives it as a heritage to whom He wills."*

Then the escalating signs, recounted at length by Sa'id bin Jubayr and Ibn Ishaq. The pattern is identical each time — **affliction, promise, relief, betrayal**:

| Sign | What it did |
|---|---|
| **Drought and lack of fruits** | "The date tree used to produce only one date" (Raja' bin Haywah) |
| **Tuwfan** (flood/heavy rain) | Ruined the produce; the water lay on the land so nothing could be tilled |
| **Locusts** | Ate the crops, and even the nails on their doors, leaving the wood — until the doors fell |
| **Qummal** (grain bugs/weevils) | Ten bags of grain milled down to three; overwhelmed their homes and reserves |
| **Frogs** | A man would sit in frogs up to his chin and could not open his mouth without one jumping in |
| **Blood** | Every river, well and container turned to blood; Fir'awn's answer was "Musa has bewitched you" |

Five times they said *"Invoke your Lord… we shall believe in you and let the Children of Israel go."* Five times Musa invoked, Allah removed it, and **they broke their word.**

The end: drowning in the sea — public drowning, Ibn Kathir notes, *added disgrace to their punishment, while adding comfort to the hearts of Allah's party.* And the inheritance of the blessed land, east and west (Al-Hasan and Qatadah: Ash-Sham), *because of their endurance.*

### Immediately after the miracle: "Make for us a god"
They crossed the sea, saw the power, and passed a people devoted to idols — Ibn Jarir says **idols in the shape of cows**, which is what surfaced later in the calf. And they said: *"O Musa! Make for us a god as they have gods."*

Musa: *"Verily, you are an ignorant people."*

The reason Ibn Kathir spends time here is Abu Waqid Al-Laythi's hadith. On the way to Hunayn, the Companions passed a great green lote tree and — seeing that the pagans had a tree called **Dhat Anwat** on which they hung their weapons — said, *"O Messenger of Allah! Appoint for us a Dhat Anwat as they have."* He said:

> *"By He in Whose Hand is my soul! You have said just what the people of Musa said to him: 'Make for us a god as they have gods.'"*

The lesson is aimed squarely at this Ummah.

## 14. The Mountain, the Calf, and the Tablets (7:142–156)

**Forty nights.** Thirty appointed, then ten added. Musa fasted them; when the thirty ended he cleaned his teeth with a twig, and was commanded to complete the term.

**"Show me, that I may look upon You."** Ibn Kathir handles the theology directly:

- *Lan tarani* — "you cannot see Me" — **does not mean seeing Allah will never occur**, as the Mu'tazilah claimed. The hadiths of **Mutawatir** grade affirm that the believers will see Allah in the Hereafter (75:22–23).
- From earlier Scripture: *"O Musa! No living soul sees Me but will perish, and no solid but will be demolished."*
- On *when his Lord appeared to the mountain*: the Prophet ﷺ said **"Like this"** — and held out the tip of his little finger (At-Tirmidhi: Hasan Sahih Gharib; Al-Hakim: Sahih by Muslim's criteria). Ibn Abbas: only the extent of the little finger appeared.

Musa's response on waking — *"Glory be to You, I turn to You in repentance"* — Mujahid: **repenting from having asked to look at Him.**

Related, and worth keeping: the Jew who swore *"By He Who has chosen Musa over mankind"* and was struck by an Ansari. The Prophet ﷺ rebuked the striker: *"Do not prefer me above the Prophets. On the Day of Resurrection, people will be struck unconscious, and I will be the first to wake. Then I will find Musa holding onto a pillar of the Throne. I do not know whether he woke before me, or he was compensated for his unconsciousness at At-Tur."*

**The calf.** As-Samiri made it from the borrowed Coptic ornaments and threw in a handful of dust from the trace of Jibril's horse; it made a sound. Ibn Kathir records the two views — that it actually came alive and lowed, or that it stayed gold and the air passing through it produced the sound. *Allah knows best.* Either way the Qur'an's refutation is the same: *it could neither speak to them nor guide them to the way.*

**The Tablets thrown.** Musa returned *angry and grieved* — Abu Ad-Darda': **Asif (regret) is the severest type of anger.** He threw the Tablets and seized his brother by the head, fearing Harun had not tried hard enough to stop them. Harun's plea, *"O son of my mother"* — deliberately invoking their mother, Ibn Kathir notes, to draw more mercy, though Harun was his full brother.

And the hadith that explains the whole scene:

> *May Allah grant His mercy to Musa! Surely, he who observes something is nothing like he who is informed about it. His Lord told him that his people were tested after him, and he did not throw the Tablets. When he saw them with his eyes, then he threw them.*

**Wrath and humiliation** (7:152) — and Ibn Kathir generalises the closing phrase *thus do We recompense those who invent lies* to **every innovator in religion**. Abu Qilabah: *"By Allah! This Ayah is for all those who invent a lie, until the Day of Resurrection."* Al-Hasan Al-Basri: *"The disgrace of innovation will weigh on their shoulders even if they gallop on their mules."*

And immediately after, the door left open (7:153): *those who committed evil deeds and then repented and believed — your Lord after all that is indeed Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.* Ibn Mas'ud, asked about a man who fornicated with a woman and then married her, **recited this verse ten times** and neither permitted nor forbade it.

**The seventy men** (7:155). Three accounts:

- **Ibn Abbas:** their du'a asked Allah for *"what You have never given anyone before us and will never give anyone after us"* — Allah disliked it, and the earthquake took them.
- **As-Suddi and Ibn Ishaq:** they demanded *"we will not believe until we see Allah plainly"* after having heard Him speak to Musa from within the cloud, and were seized.
- **Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Mujahid, Ibn Jarir:** they were seized because **they neither shunned nor forbade** the calf-worshippers — supported by Musa's own words, *"would You destroy us for the deeds of the foolish among us?"*

Musa's prayer that follows is one of the great supplications, and Ibn Kathir reads it structurally: **first the warding off of evil, then the request for good.** *"It is only Your trial… You are our protector, so forgive us and have mercy on us… And ordain for us good in this world and in the Hereafter. Certainly we have Hudna unto You"* — *Hudna*, on the agreement of Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi, Qatadah and others, means **"we repent, we return to You."**

### 7:156 — The Mercy Verse

> *My punishment I afflict therewith whom I will, and **My mercy embraces all things.** That mercy I shall ordain for those who have Taqwa, and give Zakah, and believe in Our Ayat.*

Ibn Kathir attaches the hundred-mercies hadiths here. The Bedouin who prayed *"O Allah! Grant Your mercy to me and to Muhammad, and do not give a share in it to anyone else"* was told: **"You have restricted a vast mercy!"** Allah created a hundred mercies and sent down one, by which all creation — men, Jinn and animals — shows mercy to one another; ninety-nine He kept with Him for the Day of Resurrection.

Note the movement of the verse: mercy that *embraces all things* is then **ordained** specifically for those with Taqwa. And note that Ibn Kathir observes this was revealed in Makkah, before Zakah was fixed in shares — so *"give Zakah"* may mean purification of the self, or wealth, **or both.**

## 15. The Unlettered Prophet, Described in Their Own Books (7:157–158)

The verse that the surah has been building toward: *those who follow the Messenger, the unlettered Prophet, **whom they find written of with them in the Tawrah and the Injil***.

**Abdullah bin 'Amr's description**, given to 'Ata' bin Yasar and recorded by Al-Bukhari — the Prophet ﷺ as the Tawrah described him:

- *a witness, a bearer of glad tidings, and a warner* (33:45), and **a safe refuge for the unlettered ones**
- *"You are My servant and Messenger. I have called you **Al-Mutawakkil** — not hard, not harsh."*
- Neither uttering foul speech in the markets nor returning evil with evil; **rather he forgives and forgoes.**
- Allah will not end his life until He straightens through him the crooked religion, so they proclaim *La ilaha illallah*; and He will open through him **sealed hearts, deaf ears and blind eyes.**

'Ata' then asked Ka'b the same question — *"and his answer did not differ from Abdullah's, even concerning one letter."*

There is also the Bedouin's account: the Prophet ﷺ walking between Abu Bakr and Umar, coming upon a Jewish man reading the Tawrah over his dying son, and asking, *"I ask you by He Who sent down the Tawrah, do you not find the description of me and my advent in your Book?"* The father shook his head — and **the dying son said, "Rather, yes, by He Who sent down the Tawrah,"** and testified. The Prophet ﷺ told the Companions to take charge of their brother, then personally saw to his funeral and led the prayer over him.

**What the Prophet ﷺ does, in the verse's own words:**

| The Prophet's function | Ibn Kathir's gloss |
|---|---|
| Commands good, forbids evil | The greatest instance being: worship Allah alone, abandon all else |
| Makes lawful the good things | Lifting invented prohibitions — Bahirah, Sa'ibah, Wasilah, Ham |
| Forbids the evil things | Pork, Riba, and what had been wrongly treated as lawful |
| **Releases them from their burdens and the fetters upon them** | He came with leniency and an easy religion |

On that last point Ibn Kathir piles up the evidence: *"I was sent with the easy way of Hanifiyyah."* To Mu'adh and Abu Musa going to Yemen: *"Bring glad tidings and do not drive people away; make things easy and do not make them difficult."* Abu Barzah: *"The nations before us had things made difficult for them in their laws."* And the forgiveness of *what occurs within themselves as long as they do not utter or act on it*, and of **unintentional error, forgetfulness, and what one is forced to do** — which is exactly the du'a of 2:286, after each phrase of which Allah said, *"I shall accept."*

**And then the universality** (7:158): *"O mankind! I am sent to you all"* — red and black, Arab and non-Arab. Ibn Kathir cites the five things given to no Prophet before him: sent to all mankind; made victorious by fright a month's journey ahead; spoils made lawful; the earth made a place of prayer and purification; and **the Shafa'ah — which he saved for his Ummah, and which will reach those who associate none with Allah.**

There is also the moving Abu Bakr–Umar incident: when the Prophet ﷺ reminded Umar, *"I said, 'O people! I am the Messenger of Allah to you all,' and you said, 'You lie,' but Abu Bakr declared, 'You said the truth.' Will you leave my Companion alone!"*

## 16. The Children of Israel: Sabbath, and the Three Groups (7:159–171)

Ibn Kathir first notes the fairness of 7:159: *of the people of Musa there is a community who lead with truth* — paired with 3:113, 3:199, 28:52–54 and 17:107–109. **Not all of them.**

Then the Sabbath village — **Aylah**, on the shore of the Red Sea, between Madyan and At-Tur (Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi). The trial was precisely calibrated: the fish came **openly, visible on the surface**, on the Sabbath, and not on other days.

Ibn Kathir's verdict on the method they used to get around it:

> These were a people who used **a trick** to violate Allah's prohibitions, taking an action that seemed legal on the surface, while in reality it was meant to transgress. *"Do not repeat what the Jews committed, and violate Allah's prohibitions using deceitful tricks."*

**The three groups** (7:164–165) — and this is the passage's real subject:

1. Those who **transgressed**.
2. Those who **forbade** them: *"Why do you preach to a people whom Allah is about to destroy?"* — *"In order to be free from guilt before your Lord, and perhaps they may fear Allah."*
3. Those who **neither transgressed nor forbade**, and asked the second group what the point was.

The verse names only two outcomes: *We rescued those who forbade evil, and We seized those who did wrong.* **The third group's fate is not stated.** Ibn Kathir's comment — *the compensation is comparable to the deed; this type neither did what warrants praise nor committed wrong warranting admonishment.*

Ikrimah's report is worth keeping for how scholarship actually worked: *"Ibn Abbas said: I do not know whether or not the people were saved who said 'why do you preach…'. So I continued discussing it with him until I convinced him that they were. Then he gave me a garment."*

**"Be you monkeys, despised"** — *khasi'in*: humiliated, disgraced, rejected.

**The long humiliation** (7:167): the declaration that He would keep sending against them, till the Day of Resurrection, those who afflict them with humiliating torment — which Ibn Kathir traces through the production tax under Musa, then the Chaldeans and Greeks, then the Christians, then the Jizyah under Islam. He adds the eschatological note that in the future they will support the Dajjal, and 'Isa and the Muslims will fight them near the end.

But the verse ends *He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful* — and Ibn Kathir points out the deliberate pairing: **Allah so often mentions warning and encouragement together "so that hearts always have a sense of hope and fear."**

**The evil generation** (7:169): they inherited the Book and studied it, and chose the goods of this low life, saying *"it will be forgiven us"* — **and if the same offer came again, they would seize it again.** Mujahid: *"They will consume anything they can consume, legally or illegally. Yet they wish for forgiveness."* As-Suddi's illustration: every judge they appointed took bribes; the honest ones took covenants against it; each new judge did the same and said *"I will be forgiven"* — and the very people who admonished him would loot the world if given the chance.

Ibn Abbas identifies what the covenant of the Book forbade them from saying about Allah: **"their claim that Allah will forgive the sins they keep committing without repenting from them."**

## 17. The Covenant in the Loins (7:172–174)

*And when your Lord brought forth from the Children of Adam, from their loins, their seed, and made them testify: "Am I not your Lord?" They said: "Yes! We testify."*

The purpose is stated in the verse: so that no one can plead **ignorance** on the Day of Resurrection, and no one can plead **inheritance** — *"it was only our fathers who took partners, and we were merely their descendants."*

Ibn Kathir grounds this in the Fitrah (30:30), the hadith *every child is born upon the Fitrah*, and *I created My servants Hunafa.*

He then gives At-Tirmidhi's hadith (graded Hasan Sahih; Al-Hakim: Sahih by Muslim's criteria): Allah wiped Adam's back and every person to be created fell out of it, a glimmering light between the eyes of each. Adam saw one whose light he liked — **Dawud**, given sixty years — and gave him forty years from his own life. When the angel of death came, Adam objected that forty years remained. *"Have you not given them to your son Dawud?"* He denied it —

> **Adam denied, and his offspring followed suit. Adam forgot, and his offspring forgot. Adam made a mistake, and his offspring made mistakes.**

A note on how "testimony" works here: Ibn Kathir observes it is sometimes given in **words** and sometimes by **circumstance** — as in 9:17, where the idolators "testify against their own selves of disbelief" not by speech but by their state.

## 18. Bal'am bin Ba'ura: The Man Who Threw It Away (7:175–177)

*Recite to them the story of him to whom We gave Our Ayat, but he threw them away; so Shaytan followed him up, and he became of those who went astray.*

Identifications vary — Bal'am bin Ba'ura' from Yemen (Ibn Abbas via Al-'Awfi), from Al-Balqa' in Jordan (Ka'b), Sayfi son of Ar-Rahib (another report from Ibn Abbas). What they agree on is the essential fact: **he knew Allah's Greatest Name**, and his supplication was answered. Malik bin Dinar: people sought his lead in du'a in times of difficulty.

The story: when Musa entered the land of the Canaanites, Bal'am's people came begging him to supplicate against Musa. He knew exactly what it would cost — *"If I supplicate that He turns back Musa and those with him, I will lose in this life and the Hereafter"* — and, in Ibn Ishaq's version, *"Here is Allah's Prophet, with whom the angels and believers are! How can I supplicate against them when I know from Allah what I know?"*

**They kept luring him until he went.** He rode toward Mount Husban; the donkey sat down and refused, twice, and he beat it up each time. When he finally tried to curse, **Allah turned his tongue** — he cursed his own people and blessed the Israelites. And with his tongue lolling from his mouth, he said: *"Now I have lost this life and the Hereafter."*

**The dog.** *Had We willed, We would surely have elevated him therewith, but he clung to the earth and followed his own vain desires. So his parable is the parable of a dog: if you drive him away, he pants; or if you leave him alone, he still pants.*

Three readings, all preserved:

1. Literally the tongue — his lolled out like a dog's, whether driven or left.
2. **The uselessness of advice** to such a person: he pants either way, warned or unwarned — like *it is the same to them whether you warn them or not* (2:6) and *even if you ask seventy times for their forgiveness* (9:80).
3. **The restlessness of a heart devoid of guidance**, which keeps faltering (Al-Hasan Al-Basri).

Ibn Kathir's application is direct and aimed at the People of the Book of the Prophet's ﷺ own day: Allah gave them knowledge, gave them the description of Muhammad ﷺ *"which would allow them to recognize him as they recognize their own children"* — and **they, among people, have the most right to follow and support him.** Whoever hides it will get disgrace in this life and humiliation in the next.

And the general principle: *whoever goes out of the area of knowledge and guidance and seeks satisfaction for his lusts and vain desires is just like a dog.*

## 19. Created for Hell? Hearts, Eyes, Ears (7:178–179)

*And surely, We have created many of the Jinn and mankind for Hell.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is careful: **"We prepared them for it by their performance of the deeds of its people."** When Allah intended to create the creation, He knew what their work would be before they existed, and wrote it fifty thousand years before the heavens and earth (Muslim, from Abdullah bin 'Amr) — *and certainly, the matter of Al-Qadar is of utmost importance, yet this is not where we should discuss it.*

*They have hearts wherewith they understand not, eyes wherewith they see not, ears wherewith they hear not.* Not that the faculties are absent — **they simply do not benefit from them for guidance.** *It is not the eyes that grow blind, but it is the hearts which are in the breasts that grow blind* (22:46).

*They are like cattle — nay, even more astray.* Why *more*? Ibn Kathir's answer is precise and rather devastating:

> Cattle at least respond to their shepherd's voice, though they do not understand the words; and cattle fulfil the purpose for which they were created. **The disbeliever was created to worship Allah alone — and did the opposite.** Therefore obedient people are more honourable than some angels, while cattle are better than those who disbelieve.

## 20. The Most Beautiful Names (7:180)

*And to Allah belong the Most Beautiful Names, so call on Him by them.*

- The famous hadith of the **ninety-nine Names**, "a hundred less one"; whoever counts and preserves them enters Paradise. *Allah is Witr, and loves Al-Witr.*
- **But the Names are not limited to ninety-nine.** Ibn Kathir proves it with the hadith of grief, in which one invokes Allah *"by every Name that You have and that You called Yourself by, sent down in Your Book, taught to any of Your creatures, **or kept with You in the knowledge of the Unseen** — make the Glorious Qur'an the spring of my heart, the light of my chest, the remover of my grief and the dissipater of my concern."* Asked whether they should learn these words, he said: **"Yes. It is an obligation on all those who hear this supplication to learn it."**

**Ilhad in the Names** — deviating with them. Ibn Abbas and Mujahid give the concrete case: the idolators derived **Al-Lat** from *Allah* and **Al-'Uzza** from *Al-'Aziz*. The word itself means deviation, wickedness, straying — and Ibn Kathir notes the niche in the grave is called *lahd* for the same root reason: it is a hole turned aside, toward the Qiblah.

---

## 21. Istidraj: Being Given Enough Rope (7:182–186)

*Those who reject Our Ayat, We shall gradually seize them with punishment in ways they perceive not. And I respite them; certainly My plan is strong.*

Ibn Kathir: **the doors of provision and livelihood will be opened for them, and they will be deceived by it and think they are on the correct path** — the state described in 6:44–45, *until, in the midst of their enjoyment, all of a sudden We took them.*

Then the defence of the Prophet ﷺ against the charge of madness (7:184). Qatadah's account of the occasion: he stood on As-Safa calling the Quraysh sub-tribe by sub-tribe, warning them of Allah's might, and some said, *"This companion of yours is mad — he kept shouting until the morning."* The Qur'an's answer is a challenge to reason, given fully at 34:46: **stand for Allah's sake, in pairs and singly, and reflect.** Sincerely, without stubbornness or bias, alone and in groups — and it will become clear.

And the invitation to look at *the dominion of the heavens and the earth* — with the sting in the tail: *and that it may be that the end of their lives is near.* **In what message after this will they then believe?**

---

## 22. The Hour (7:187–188)

*They ask you about the Hour. Say: "The knowledge thereof is with my Lord alone."*

**"Heavy is its burden through the heavens and the earth."** The commentators split it:

- **Qatadah:** its *knowledge* is heavy on the residents of the heavens and earth; they have no knowledge of it.
- **Al-Hasan:** when it comes, it will be heavy on them.
- **Ibn Abbas:** all creatures will suffer its heaviness.
- **Ibn Jurayj:** the heavens torn, the stars scattered, the sun wound round, the mountains passing away — *that* is its weight.

**"They ask you as if you have Hafi of it."** Ibn Abbas: as if you were their close friend. Mujahid, whom Ibn Kathir prefers: **as if you had asked about it, so its knowledge is with you.**

The evidence that even the greatest Messengers had no access:

- **Jibril**, in the shape of a Bedouin, asked the Prophet ﷺ when the Hour would be, and was told: *"He who is asked about it has no more knowledge of it than the questioner."*
- On the night of Isra', **Ibrahim, Musa and 'Isa** discussed it; Ibrahim said "I have no knowledge of it," Musa said "I have no knowledge of it," and 'Isa said *"as for when it will occur, only Allah knows"* — and then described the signs he would live to see: the Dajjal dissolving before him like lead, Gog and Magog swarming from every mound, and finally: **the Hour will be like a pregnant woman at full term, whose family does not know when she will surprise them, by night or by day.**
- Hudhayfah's narration: *"I will tell you about its portents. Before it commences there will be **Fitnah** and **Harj**."* — "What is Harj?" — **"It means killing, in the language of the Ethiopians."** And: *isolation and loneliness will be common between people, so that almost no one will recognize any other.*

**How suddenly?** Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah: two men will have spread a garment between them and will have time neither to conclude the sale nor to fold it; a man will have milked his animal and not have time to drink; a man will be making his watering hole and not use it; a man will raise a bite to his mouth and not eat it.

And when Bedouins asked, the Prophet ﷺ would point at the youngest person present: *"If this one lives, he will not become old before your Hour starts"* — meaning, Ibn Kathir explains, **the end of their own lives, which introduces them to the life of Barzakh.**

**7:188 — the corollary about the Prophet ﷺ himself:** *Say: "I possess no power over benefit or harm to myself except as Allah wills. If I had knowledge of the Unseen, I should have secured for myself an abundance of wealth, and no evil should have touched me."* Ibn Abbas's illustration is beautifully mundane: *"I would know how much profit I would make with what I buy, and I would always sell what I would profit from — and poverty would never touch me."*

**"I am but a warner, and a bringer of glad tidings."**

---

## 23. Idols Have No Feet (7:189–198)

After a passage on the origin of mankind from a single soul — and the honest note on 7:190, where Ibn Kathir follows **Al-Hasan Al-Basri** (with authentic chains, and he calls it "one of the best interpretations") that the shirk mentioned belongs **not to Adam and Hawwa' but to the idolators among their offspring**; Allah mentions the individual first, then continues about the species —

comes the most withering argument in the surah against idols. It runs in a straight line:

- They **created nothing**, and were themselves created.
- They cannot help their worshippers, **nor help themselves.**
- Calling them or staying silent is exactly the same.
- *Have they feet? Hands? Eyes? Ears?* — **their worshippers have all four and are therefore better than the objects they worship.**
- Their eyes seem to look at you but do not see; the verse even uses the grammar of rational beings for them, since they were carved in human shape with eyes drawn on.

Ibn Kathir adds 22:73–74: if every false god gathered, they could not create a fly — and if a fly snatched something from them, they could not get it back. *So weak are both the seeker and the sought.*

And then a real story, from Madinah. **Mu'adh bin 'Amr ibn Al-Jamuh and Mu'adh bin Jabal**, young new Muslims, spent their nights raiding the idols of the idolators — breaking them, disfiguring them, and using them as **firewood for needy widows**. 'Amr bin Al-Jamuh, a chief of his people, had an idol he perfumed; the two would turn it on its head and smear it with waste. He would clean it, perfume it again, and finally set a sword beside it saying, *"Defend yourself."* One night they tied it to a dead dog and threw it down a well on a rope. When 'Amr saw it, he said:

> **"By Allah! Had you been a god who has might, you would not end up tied to a dog on a rope!"**

He became Muslim, and was strong in his Islam, and was martyred at Uhud.

The believer's counter-position: *Call your partners and plot against me, and give me no respite. **Verily, my protector is Allah Who has revealed the Book, and He protects the righteous.*** — the same stand Hud took (11:54–56) and Ibrahim took (26:75–78).

---

## 24. The Closing Commands (7:199–206)

The surah ends with four instructions, and they are unusually practical after 200 verses of history.

### (1) *Khudh al-'afw* — Show forgiveness, enjoin Al-'Urf, turn away from the ignorant (7:199)

Al-Bukhari records from Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr: **this verse "was only revealed about the people's character"** — i.e., about how to handle people's behaviour. Al-Bukhari glosses *Al-'Urf* as **righteousness**.

Sufyan bin Uyaynah's report of the Prophet ﷺ asking Jibril what it meant:

> *"Allah commands you to **forgive those who wronged you, give to those who deprived you, and keep relations with those who cut theirs with you.**"*

And the case that proves it works. **Uyaynah bin Hisn** got in to see Umar and said to his face, *"O Ibn Al-Khattab! You neither give to us sufficiently nor rule with justice between us."* Umar was angry enough to strike him. His nephew Al-Hurr bin Qays said: *"O Chief of the Faithful! Allah said to His Prophet, 'Show forgiveness, enjoin Al-'Urf, and turn away from the foolish' — and this man is one of the fools."*

> **By Allah, Umar did not do anything after he heard that Ayah recited — for he was one who adhered to the Book of Allah.**

Ibn Kathir's practical division: people are of two kinds. The good-doer — accept his good and do not ask of him more than he can bear. The one who falls short — enjoin righteousness on him; and if he persists in ignorance and difficulty, **turn away, so that your ignoring him might avert his evil.**

### (2) *And if a whisper comes from Shaytan, seek refuge with Allah* (7:200)

Ibn Kathir marks the pattern: in **three** places (Al-A'raf, Al-Mu'minun 23:96–98, and Fussilat 41:34–36) Allah pairs *repel evil with what is better* with *seek refuge from the Shaytan.* The logic behind the pairing:

> **Leniency may deter a human enemy** — *he between whom and you there was enmity will become as though he were a close friend.* **Leniency will never deter the devil**, because he seeks your total destruction. He is an open enemy to you, just as he was to your father before you.

Ibn Jarir applies it exactly: if the devil lures you to anger, pushing you away from forgiving the ignorant and toward punishing him — **that** is the moment to seek refuge.

### (3) The difference between two kinds of people under attack (7:201–202)

- **Those with Taqwa:** when a thought from Shaytan touches them, *they remember* — Allah's punishment and His reward, His promises and His threats — *and then they see aright.*
- **The devils' brothers:** the devils *plunge them deeper into error, and they never stop short.* Ibn Abbas: **"Neither do mankind stop short of the evil they are doing, nor do the devils stop short of luring them."**

### (4) Listening, and remembering (7:204–206)

*So, when the Qur'an is recited, listen to it, and be silent, that you may receive mercy* — an order to honour the Qur'an, deliberately the opposite of the Quraysh's tactic: *"Listen not to this Qur'an, and make noise in the midst of its recitation"* (41:26).

*And remember your Lord within yourself, humbly and with fear, and without loudness in words, in the mornings and in the afternoons.* Ibn Kathir notes this was revealed in Makkah **before the five prayers were ordained** on the night of Isra'. Dhikr is recommended **not** in a loud voice — and when the Companions asked whether their Lord was near, to be called secretly, or far, to be called loudly, the answer came down as 2:186: *I am indeed near.* And on the road: *"Take it easy on yourselves; He Whom you are calling is not deaf or absent — He is closer to one of you than the neck of his animal."*

The surah ends with the angels **who are never too proud to worship Him, who glorify His praise and prostrate.** Ibn Kathir marks the point of law:

> **This is the first place in the Qur'an where prostration is legitimized — by the agreement of the scholars — for the reciter and for those listening.**

The reason is given in a hadith he cites: *Why do you not stand in line for the prayer as the angels stand in line before their Lord?* You are being shown the model, and then invited to join it.

## 25. In One Breath

Al-A'raf takes the longest possible route to the shortest possible point. It begins with a Book and a chest that must not be narrow; it shows the Balance and asks what could possibly be heavy on it; it shows the first refusal — an argument from a superior origin, made by a creature of fire against a creature of clay — and shows that the clay repented and the fire did not. Then it walks the reader through five prophets who said the same sentence to five nations who gave the same answer, and through the plagues of Egypt where a people promised belief six times and broke it six times, and through a nation that received the Tablets and made a calf while their prophet was still on the mountain. Between Paradise and Hell it pauses on a wall, where men whose scales came out exactly even stand and hope. It reminds every human being that they already said *Yes* once, in their father's loins, so that no one can plead ignorance or inheritance. It shows what becomes of a man who was given the Greatest Name and used it for a king's favour — a dog, panting either way. And having established that the Hour's timing is known to no angel and no messenger, and that the Prophet ﷺ himself could not secure a dirham of profit by it, it closes not with terror but with three small instructions you can carry out this afternoon: **forgive people their character, take refuge when the whisper comes, and when the Qur'an is recited, be quiet and listen.**
