*Revealed in Makkah or Madinah (disputed) · 18 ayat · "Mutual Loss and Gain" — named for the Day described at 64:9 · the **last** of the Musabbihat*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

At-Taghabun is the final surah in the group that opens by declaring that everything in creation glorifies Allah. Ibn Kathir notes this placement explicitly — it is the last of the *Musabbihat*, and it closes that sequence by moving from the widest possible frame down to the most intimate.

It begins with the heavens and the earth, and ends with a man's wife, his children, his money, and what he does with his fear.

The movement is deliberate. First: **Allah owns everything, made everything, sees everything, and knows what is inside your chest.** Then: **the nations who refused this were destroyed, and you will be raised whether you accept it or not.** Then the pivot at 64:9 — the **Day of At-Taghabun**, a commercial word, the day the final ledger is read and it becomes clear who gained and who lost. And then, having established all that, the surah turns and speaks to a man in his own house: *the calamity that hit you came by permission; your family can become your enemy; forgive them anyway; fear Allah as much as you are able; and spend.*

That last instruction — "have Taqwa of Allah **as much as you can**" — is one of the most merciful phrasings in the Qur'an, and Ibn Kathir reads it exactly as it sounds: as much as you are able and can bear.

---

## 1. Everything Glorifies Him, and He Made You Both Ways (64:1–2)

> *Whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth glorifies Allah. His is the dominion, and to Him belongs the praise, and He is Able to do all things.*

Ibn Kathir has covered this opening in the earlier Musabbihat and keeps it brief: all creatures declare the glory of their Creator and Owner.

- **His is the dominion** — He has control over all creation.
- **To Him belongs the praise** — He is the One praised for everything He created and everything He decreed.
- **Able to do all things** — whatever He wills happens without resistance; whatever He does not will never occurs.

Then comes the verse that does the real work:

> *He it is Who created you, then some of you are disbelievers and some of you are believers.*

Ibn Kathir does not soften this. Allah created you with these characteristics and **willed that for you**; therefore there will be believers and there will be disbelievers. He is the One Who sees who deserves guidance and who deserves misguidance, and He is the Witness over His servants' deeds who will recompense them completely.

Which is why the verse ends not with a discussion of destiny but with a warning about behaviour: **And Allah is All-Seer of what you do.** The decree is His; the deeds are yours; the watching is constant.

---

## 2. He Shaped You, and Made Your Shape Good (64:3–4)

> *He has created the heavens and the earth with truth* — with equity and wisdom.

> *and He shaped you and made good your shapes* — He made you in the best shapes and forms.

Ibn Kathir places two other ayat beside this, and together they read as a single argument about ingratitude:

> *O man! What has made you careless about your Lord, the Most Generous, Who created you, fashioned you perfectly and gave you due proportion; in whatever form He willed, He put you together.* (82:6–8)

> *Allah, it is He Who has made for you the earth as a dwelling place and the sky as a canopy, and has given you shape and made your shapes good, and has provided you with good things.* (40:64)

*And to Him is the return* — the return and the final destination.

Then the scope of His knowledge, narrowed in three steps: what is in **the heavens and the earth**, then what you **conceal** and what you **reveal**, then — the last and closest — **what is in the breasts**.

---

## 3. The Nations That Said "Shall Mere Men Guide Us?" (64:5–6)

> *Has not the news reached you of those who disbelieved aforetime? And so they tasted the evil result of their disbelief, and theirs will be a painful torment.*

Ibn Kathir separates the two punishments carefully:

- **"They tasted the evil result"** — the punishment and humiliation they received **in this life**, the consequence of their denial and their sinful actions.
- **"A painful torment"** — in the **Hereafter**, added on top of what they already received.

And the reason is stated:

> *That was because there came to them their Messengers with Bayyinat* — supporting arguments, evidence, and clear proofs — *but they said: "Shall mere men guide us?"*

This is the objection at the heart of it. They **discounted and dismissed the possibility** that a message would be sent to a human being, and that their guidance would come at the hands of a man like themselves. So they denied the truth and turned away from abiding by it.

The answer given is not an argument but an indifference: **But Allah was not in need.** He is Free of all need, and Worthy of all praise — their refusal costs Him nothing.

---

## 4. The Third Oath (64:7)

> *The disbelievers pretend that they will never be resurrected. Say: "Yes! By my Lord, you will certainly be resurrected, then you will be informed of what you did; and that is easy for Allah."*

Ibn Kathir draws attention to something structural here. This is **the third and last ayah in the Qur'an** in which Allah orders His Messenger ﷺ to swear by his Lord that the Resurrection will occur:

| | Surah | Verse |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | Yunus (10:53) | *"Is it true?" Say: "Yes! By my Lord! It is the very truth, and you cannot escape it!"* |
| **Second** | Saba' (34:3) | *"The Hour will not come to us." Say: "Yes, by my Lord, it will come to you..."* |
| **Third** | At-Taghabun (64:7) | *Say: "Yes! By my Lord, you will certainly be resurrected..."* |

Three times the denial is met not with a proof but with an oath — and each time the oath is sworn on the Lord Himself.

*Then you will be informed of what you did* — of **all** your actions, whether major or minor, big or small. *And that is easy for Allah* — the resurrecting and the recompensing are both easy for Him.

---

## 5. Believe in the Light (64:8)

> *Therefore, believe in Allah and His Messenger and in the Light which We have sent down.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is one word: **the Qur'an**.

*And Allah is All-Aware of what you do* — none of your deeds ever escapes His knowledge.

---

## 6. The Day of At-Taghabun (64:9–10)

> *The Day when He will gather you on the Day of Gathering, that will be the Day of At-Taghabun.*

**The Day of Gathering** is the Day of Resurrection — the day the earlier and later generations are all gathered in **one area**, where a single caller would be heard by every one of them and a single glance would take them all in. Ibn Kathir supports it with:

> *That is the Day whereon mankind will be gathered together, that is a Day when all will be present.* (11:103)

> *Say: "Verily, those of old and those of later times — all will surely be gathered together for the appointed meeting of a known Day."* (56:49–50)

### The word itself

*Taghabun* is a word from the marketplace: mutual gain and loss, one party coming out ahead of another in a transaction. Ibn Kathir gathers three readings, and they agree:

- **Ibn Abbas**: it is one of the names of the Day of Judgement, so called **because the people of Paradise will have gained over the people of the Fire.** (Qatadah and Mujahid said the same.)
- **Muqatil bin Hayyan**: **there is no mutual loss and gain greater than these entering Paradise and those being taken to the Fire.**

Every bargain ever struck is a small version of this one. On that Day the true price of everything traded — time, belief, wealth, family — is finally posted.

### The two ledgers

> *And whosoever believes in Allah and performs righteous deeds, He will expiate from him his sins, and will admit him to Gardens beneath which rivers flow, to dwell therein forever; that will be the great success.*

> *But those who disbelieved and denied Our Ayat, they will be the dwellers of the Fire, to dwell therein forever. And worst indeed is that destination.*

---

## 7. Nothing Reaches You Except by Permission (64:11)

> *No calamity occurs, but by the permission of Allah, and whosoever believes in Allah, He guides his heart.*

Ibn Kathir first sets it beside its parallel in Al-Hadid:

> *No calamity occurs on the earth or in yourselves but it is inscribed in a record before We bring it into existence. Verily, that is easy for Allah.* (57:22)

**Ibn Abbas** on "by the permission of Allah": *by the command of Allah* — meaning from His decree and His will.

Then the second half, which is the most quoted line of the surah, and Ibn Kathir gives it in full:

> Whoever suffers an affliction and **knows that it occurred by Allah's judgement and decree**, and patiently abides, awaiting Allah's reward — then Allah guides his heart, and **compensates him for his loss in this life by granting guidance to his heart and certainty in his faith.** Allah will replace whatever he lost for Him with the same or with something better.

The compensation, note, is not the return of the thing. It is *certainty* — and it is described as the better trade.

**Ali bin Abi Talhah ← Ibn Abbas**, on the same phrase:

> Allah will guide his heart to certainty. Therefore he will know that **what reached him would not have missed him, and what missed him would not have reached him.**

### The hadith on the believer's position

Agreed upon by Al-Bukhari and Muslim:

> **Amazing is the believer** — there is no decree that Allah writes for him but it is better for him.
> - If an affliction strikes him, he is **patient**, and that is better for him.
> - If a bounty is granted to him, he is **thankful**, and that is better for him.
>
> **This trait is only for the faithful believer.**

Two doors, both of them exits. Nobody else has this.

---

## 8. Obey, and If You Turn Away (64:12–13)

> *Obey Allah, and obey the Messenger; but if you turn away, then the duty of Our Messenger is only to convey clearly.*

Allah commands obedience to Him and to His Messenger ﷺ in everything He legislates and in implementing His orders, and forbids everything His Messenger ﷺ forbids.

If you refrain from abiding by the faith — the Messenger's ﷺ mission is to convey; **your** mission is to hear and obey.

**Az-Zuhri** compresses the entire relationship into one sentence:

> *From Allah comes the Message, its deliverance is up to the Messenger, and the adherence is up to us.*

### Tawhid

> *Allah! La ilaha illa Huwa. And in Allah therefore let the believers put their trust.*

He is the One Whom all creation needs and seeks, other than Whom there is no true God. Ibn Kathir points out that the ayah first states the Tawhid and then draws the consequence: **to single Him out for worship, be purely devoted to Him, and rely upon Him.** The same pairing appears at:

> *The Lord of the east and the west; La ilaha illa Huwa. So take Him alone as Trustee.* (73:9)

Knowing that He is One is not the point of arrival. **Depending on Him** is.

---

## 9. Your Family Can Be Your Enemy — Forgive Them Anyway (64:14)

> *O you who believe! Verily, among your wives and your children there are enemies for you; therefore beware of them! But if you pardon (them) and overlook, and forgive, then verily, Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

Some wives and children are enemies to their husbands and fathers in a specific sense: **the man becomes busied with them rather than with good deeds.** The parallel is the ayah just before this surah:

> *O you who believe! Let not your properties or your children divert you from the remembrance of Allah. And whosoever does that, then they are the losers.* (63:9)

**"Beware of them"** — for your religion, said Ibn Zayd.

**Mujahid** explains the mechanism: they might push a man to sever his family ties or to disobey his Lord — and **the man, because he loves his wife and children, might obey them in it.** The danger is not malice. It is love pointed the wrong way.

### The occasion of revelation

**Ibn Abi Hatim ← Ibn Abbas**, answering a man who asked him about this very ayah:

> There were men who embraced Islam in Makkah and wanted to migrate to Allah's Messenger ﷺ — but their wives and children **refused to let them go**. Later, when they finally joined him ﷺ, they found that the Companions who had been with him had gained knowledge in the religion — and they were **about to punish their wives and children** for what they had cost them.
>
> So Allah sent down: *But if you pardon and overlook, and forgive, then verily, Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

At-Tirmidhi collected this and graded it **Hasan Sahih**.

This is the turn that makes the ayah what it is. The warning is real — they *were* an obstruction. And the instruction that follows the warning is still: **pardon, overlook, forgive.** The verse names the grievance and then removes the right to act on it.

---

## 10. Wealth and Children Are a Trial (64:15)

> *Your wealth and your children are only a Fitnah, whereas Allah! With Him is a great reward.*

They are a **test and a trial** from Allah for His creatures, so that He knows who obeys Him and who disobeys Him. *With Him* means on the Day of Resurrection; *a great reward* is what waits there.

Ibn Kathir brings the catalogue of what men want:

> *Beautified for men is the love of things they covet: women, children, heaped-up hoards of gold and silver, branded beautiful horses, cattle and well-tilled land. This is the pleasure of the present world's life; but Allah has the excellent return with Him.* (3:14)

### The Prophet ﷺ interrupted by two boys

**Imam Ahmad ← Buraydah**:

> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was delivering a speech when Al-Hasan and Al-Husayn came in wearing **red shirts**, walking and tripping over themselves. He ﷺ **came down from the Minbar**, picked them up, placed them in front of him, and said:
>
> *Allah and His Messenger have spoken the truth — "Verily, your wealth and your children are a Fitnah." I saw these two boys walking and tripping, and I could not be patient until I stopped my speech and picked them up.*

Recorded by the Sunan compilers; At-Tirmidhi said *Hasan Gharib*.

It is a remarkable way to teach a verse: he ﷺ demonstrated the pull of the very thing he was warning about, on himself, from the pulpit. *Fitnah* here is not condemnation. It is the honest name for something you love enough to be moved by.

## 11. As Much As You Can (64:16)

> *So have Taqwa of Allah as much as you can; listen and obey, and spend in charity; that is better for yourselves. And whosoever is saved from his own greed, then they are the successful ones.*

**"As much as you can"** — as much as you are able and can bear or endure.

The Two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayrah:

> *When I command you to do something, **do as much as you can of it**; and whatever I forbid for you, then avoid it.*

Note the asymmetry, which Ibn Kathir preserves without comment: commands are scaled to capacity; **prohibitions are not.** Leaving something alone requires no strength.

**"Listen and obey"** — obey what Allah and His Messenger ﷺ command and do not stray to the right or the left. Do not speak or decide before Allah and His Messenger ﷺ have spoken or decided. Do not neglect what you were ordered to do, nor commit what you were forbidden.

**"And spend"** — give from what Allah has granted you: to your relatives, the poor, the needy, and the weak. **Be kind to Allah's creatures, just as Allah was and still is kind to you.** This is better for you in this life and the next; and if you do not, it is worse for you in this life and the next.

**"Whosoever is saved from his own greed"** — Ibn Kathir refers back to the identical phrase in Al-Hashr (59:9), where he gave the relevant hadiths, and declines to repeat them.

---

## 12. The Loan (64:17–18)

> *If you lend to Allah a handsome loan, He will double it for you, and will forgive you.*

Whatever you spend, **Allah will replace it**, and the reward of whatever you give in charity is on Him.

Ibn Kathir lingers on the astonishing framing: Allah calls charity a **loan to Himself**. He cites the Qudsi hadith:

> *Who will give a loan to He Who is neither unjust nor poor?*

The One asking to borrow is neither in need of the money nor capable of failing to repay it. The "loan" is entirely for the lender's benefit. Hence:

> *So that He may multiply it to him many times.* (2:245)

**"And will forgive you"** — He will erase your mistakes.

Then the two Names that close the transaction, and Ibn Kathir's glosses on them are the heart of the ending:

- **Ash-Shakur** — *the Appreciative*: **He gives abundantly in return for what was little.**
- **Al-Halim** — *the Forbearing*: **He forgives, pardons, covers and absolves the sins, mistakes, errors and shortcomings.**

> *All-Knower of the unseen and the seen, the Almighty, the All-Wise.*

Its explanation, he says, has already preceded several times.

---

## 13. In One Breath

At-Taghabun opens with everything in existence praising its Owner and ends with a man being told to give away part of his salary. In between, it establishes that Allah made you, shaped you well, sees your deeds, knows what is in your chest, will raise you — He swears to it — and will gather everyone in one place on the day the true profit and loss of every life is finally posted.

Then it comes home. **The disaster that struck you came by His permission**, and the believer who accepts that is paid back in certainty rather than in property. **Your own family can pull you away from Him** — and you are still ordered to pardon them. **Fear Him as much as you are able**, because that is the actual standard. And **spend**, because what you give away is not lost; it is lent to One who returns much for little and forgives on top of it.

The surah is named for the day of loss and gain. Its entire practical instruction is a set of trades made in advance — patience for certainty, forgiveness for forgiveness, wealth for a multiplied wealth — so that when the ledger is read, you are on the side that gained.
