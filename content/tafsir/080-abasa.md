## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

`Abasa begins with something no other scripture would begin with: **a public correction of the Prophet ﷺ himself**, preserved permanently in the Book he was sent to deliver, over a matter that would not register as a sin in most human courts — a frown, a turned shoulder, a moment of attention given to the wrong man.

The incident is small. Its logic is not. A leader of the Quraysh was being invited to Islam; a blind man who was *already* a believer came asking a question; the Prophet ﷺ, hoping for the greater conversion, brushed the interruption aside. And the surah's correction is not "you were rude" — it is **"how can you know?"** Neither the value of a soul nor the outcome of a conversation is available to human estimation. The rich man's guidance was never the Prophet's responsibility, and the poor man's purification was never his to postpone.

From there the surah widens in a straight line. If human status cannot be read from the outside, then look at what a human being actually *is*: a drop of fluid, proportioned, eased into the world, eased out of it, buried, and raised. Look at what he eats: water poured, earth split open, seeds pushed up through the dirt — the same resurrection he says is impossible, performed in front of him every season, on his plate.

And then look at where all of it ends. The surah closes on **As-Sakhkhah**, the deafening shout, on which a man flees his brother, his mother, his father, his wife and his children — every social tie that generated the original moment of preference dissolving at once. Two categories survive: faces that are bright and laughing, and faces covered in dust and darkness. Not noble and weak. Not rich and poor.

The surah opens with a man being ranked above another man, and ends with the only ranking that was ever real.

## 1. The Incident (80:1–10)

> *He frowned and turned away, because there came to him the blind man.*

Ibn Kathir records what more than one scholar of tafsir mentions: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was in direct conversation with **one of the great leaders of the Quraysh**, hoping he would accept Islam. **Ibn Umm Maktum** — blind, and among those who had accepted Islam in its earliest days — came and began urgently pressing him with a question. The Prophet ﷺ, still hoping for the other man's guidance, asked him to wait, **frowned in his face and turned away** to continue. Then the verses came down.

The reprimand runs through three precise cuts:

**"And how can you know that he might become pure?"** — that he might attain purification and cleanliness in his soul. *You could not have known what you were turning from.*

**"Or he might receive admonition, and the admonition might profit him"** — that he might take the reminder and abstain from what is forbidden.

**"As for him who thinks himself self-sufficient — to him you attend"** — you face the rich man, hoping he may be guided — **"what does it matter to you if he will not become pure?"** *You are not responsible for him if he does not purify himself.* Meanwhile the one who came to you **running**, and in **fear** — seeking you, wanting to be guided by whatever you tell him — **him you neglect**, because you are too busy.

Ibn Kathir draws the ruling out plainly. Here **Allah commands His Messenger not to single anyone out with the warning**: he is to warn the noble and the weak, the poor and the rich, the master and the slave, the men and the women, the young and the old — **equally**. Guidance to the straight path is then Allah's to give to whom He chooses; He has the profound wisdom and the decisive proof.

*Chain note:* Abu Ya`la and Ibn Jarir both record from `Aishah that these verses were revealed concerning Ibn Umm Maktum. At-Tirmidhi records the hadith without mentioning `Aishah as its narrator; Ibn Kathir adds that it is reported this way in **Al-Muwatta'** as well.

## 2. What the Qur'an Is, and Who Carries It (80:11–16)

> *Nay; indeed it is an admonition. So, whoever wills, let him pay attention to it.*

**"Nay"** — the correction closes and the subject lifts. *It* is an admonition: this surah, says Ibn Kathir, or this very advice about **conveying knowledge equally among people, whether they are of noble or low class**. Qatadah and As-Suddi both read it simply as **the Qur'an**.

*Whoever wills, let him pay attention* — whoever wills, let him remember Allah in all his affairs. (The pronoun may also refer to the revelation itself, since that is what the passage is alluding to.)

Then four descriptions, each one a step up:

- **In Records held in honour** — respected and revered. All of the Qur'an, Ibn Kathir notes, is in honoured pages.
- **Exalted** — elevated in status.
- **Purified** — from impurity, from additions, from deficiency.
- **In the hands of Safarah** — *ambassadors*. Ibn `Abbas, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak and Ibn Zayd all say: **these are the angels**. Al-Bukhari explains the word: ambassadors travel to rectify matters between parties, and the angels, when they descend with Allah's revelation, bring it like an ambassador who sets matters right between people.
- **Honourable and obedient** — noble and honourable in their very creation, and their character and deeds righteous, pure and perfect.

Ibn Kathir then draws the point he wants: **it is necessary that whoever carries the Qur'an be one who follows righteousness and guidance** — and supports it with the hadith of `Aishah, recorded by Imam Ahmad and reported by the group:

> *He who recites the Qur'an proficiently will be with the noble, righteous, ambassador angels; and he who recites it with difficulty will receive two rewards.*

The same word — *Safarah* — used for the angels in the ayah, and for the company the struggling human reciter is promised.

## 3. Man, Cursed and Ungrateful (80:17–23)

> *Qutila mankind! How ungrateful he is!*

A rebuke of those who deny the Resurrection. Ad-Dahhak reports from Ibn `Abbas that *Qutila* means **"may man be cursed."** Abu Malik says the same, and specifies the target: **the rejecting kind of man, whose denial is abundant and unsupported by any argument** — he denies simply because he finds it far-fetched and because he lacks knowledge of it. Ibn Jurayj on *how ungrateful he is*: none is worse in disbelief than he. Qatadah: none is more cursed than he.

Then the answer, which is his own biography:

**"From what thing did He create him? From a Nutfah He created him, and then set him in due proportion"** — and in that proportioning Allah decreed his life span, his sustenance, his deeds, and whether he would be miserable or happy.

**"Then He made the path easy for him."** Two readings, and Ibn Kathir picks one:

- *The path out of the womb.* Al-`Awfi from Ibn `Abbas — He made his coming out of his mother's belly easy for him. Also `Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak, Abu Salih, Qatadah, As-Suddi; preferred by Ibn Jarir.
- *The path of guidance.* Mujahid, citing **"Verily, We guided him on the path, he is either grateful or ungrateful" (76:3)** — We explained it to him, clarified it, and made it easy to act upon. Al-Hasan and Ibn Zayd say the same. **Ibn Kathir: "This is the most correct view, and Allah knows best."**

**"Then He causes him to die and puts him in his grave"** — He makes him the inhabitant of a grave. **"Then when it is His will, He will resurrect him"** — *Al-Ba`th* and *An-Nushur*. Cited alongside: *"among His signs is that He created you from dust, and then behold, you are human beings scattered"* (30:20), and *"look at the bones, how We bring them together and clothe them with flesh"* (2:259).

And from the Two Sahihs, via Al-A`mash ← Abu Salih ← Abu Hurayrah:

> *All of the sons of Adam will decay except for the bone of the coccyx. From it he was created, and by it he will be reconstructed.*

**"Nay, but he has not done what He commanded him."** Ibn Jarir reads it of man: the disbeliever claims he has discharged what he owed Allah in his person and his wealth — but he has not; he has not fulfilled the obligations imposed on him. **Ibn Kathir offers his own reading** — and Allah knows best — attaching it to the previous ayah instead: *He will resurrect him* — **but He has not done so yet**, not until the appointed term has run out and the full span of human life on earth is complete, according to the lives of every person Allah wrote into existence. The existence of mankind and its duration are decreed; when that is finished, He resurrects the creation and repeats it just as He first made it.

## 4. Look at Your Food (80:24–32)

> *Then let man look at his food.*

Ibn Kathir reads this doubly. It is **a call to reflect on Allah's favour** — and it is **an evidence**: vegetation coming to life out of lifeless earth is the same act as bodies being brought to life out of decayed bones and scattered dust. The proof of resurrection is a meal.

The sequence is deliberately physical. **"We pour forth water in abundance"** — sent down from the sky to the earth. **"And We split the earth in clefts"** — the water settles into it, enters its boundaries, mingles with the seeds lying in the soil, and from that the seeds grow, rise, and appear on the surface.

Then the harvest, item by item:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Habb** | all types of seeds and grains |
| **Grapes** | well known |
| **Qadb** | moist green herbage that animals graze on, also called *Al-Qat* (Ibn `Abbas, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi). Al-Hasan Al-Basri: fodder |
| **Olives** | a food, and its juice a food — eaten at breakfast, used as oil |
| **Date palms** | eaten as *Balah, Busr, Rutab, Tamr*, raw and cooked — the whole range from unripe to dried; its juice pressed into fruit drinks and vinegar |
| **Ghulb Hada'iq** | gardens. Al-Hasan and Qatadah: thick, handsome date-palm gardens. Ibn `Abbas and Mujahid: everything gathered and collected together |
| **Fakihah** | every type of fruit — Ibn `Abbas: everything eaten ripe |
| **Abb** | Ibn `Abbas: what the earth grows that grazing animals eat and people do not; in another narration, **grass for livestock** |

**"A provision and benefit for you and your cattle"** — a means of livelihood for you and your animals, in this life, until the Day of Judgement.

### The two famous statements about *Abb*

Ibn Kathir preserves both, and they belong together.

**Abu Bakr As-Siddiq**, asked about *"fruits and herbage"*, said:

> *"What sky would shade me and what earth would carry me, if I said about the Book of Allah that which I had no knowledge of."*

**`Umar bin Al-Khattab** recited the surah, reached the same ayah, and said: *we already know what Al-Fakihah is — but what is Al-Abb?* Then he caught himself:

> *"By your life, O Ibn Al-Khattab, this is something overburdensome."*

Ibn Jarir records it from Anas, and Ibn Kathir notes the **chain is authentic** and narrated from Anas by more than one person. He also explains what `Umar was actually after: not whether *abb* is a plant — he and everyone reading the ayah could see from the surrounding list that it is — but its exact type, appearance and description. That extra precision is what he judged to be *takalluf*: unnecessary strain, beyond what the ayah was given for.

## 5. The Deafening Shout (80:33–42)

> *Then when there comes As-Sakhkhah.*

**Ibn `Abbas:** As-Sakhkhah is **one of the names of the Day of Judgement**, which Allah has magnified and used to warn His servants. **Ibn Jarir:** perhaps it names the blowing into the Trumpet. **Al-Baghawi:** it is the thunderous shout of that Day, called this because **it deafens the ears** — it pierces the hearing so severely it nearly destroys it.

**"That Day shall a man flee from his brother, and from his mother and his father, and from his wife and his children."** He will *see* them — and flee, and seek to get away from them, because the horror is so great and the matter so weighty.

Ibn Kathir brings the authentic hadith of the intercession to show how far this goes: every one of the great Messengers of firm resolve, asked to intercede for the creation, will say **"O myself! O myself! Today I will not ask You concerning anyone but myself."** Even `Isa bin Maryam will say he will not ask concerning anyone but himself — **not even Maryam, the woman who gave birth to him**.

**Qatadah** puts the order of the ayah exactly: the most beloved, then the next most beloved; the closest of kin, then the next closest — due to the terror of that Day. *The flight begins with those you love most.*

**"Every man that Day will have enough to make him careless of others"** — preoccupied with his own business, distracted from everyone else's affairs.

Ibn Abi Hatim records from Ibn `Abbas that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: *You will all be gathered barefoot, naked and uncircumcised.* A woman asked whether they would then look at one another's nakedness. He replied with this very ayah — **every man that Day will have enough to make him careless of others** — or, in the other wording, *he will be too busy to look*. At-Tirmidhi graded this hadith **Hasan Sahih**.

### The two faces

The people divide into two parties, and the sorting is written on their faces.

**Bright (*Musfirah*) — laughing, rejoicing at good news.** Happy and pleased, from joy that is in their hearts, and the good news showing plainly on their faces. **These are the people of Paradise.**

**Dust-stained — darkness (*Qatarah*) covering them.** Ibn `Abbas: they will be overcome with darkness. **"Such will be the disbelieving, the wicked evildoers"** — meaning **disbelievers in their hearts, evildoers in their actions**. As Allah says: *"And they will beget none but wicked disbelievers"* (71:27).

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**You cannot see who is worth your time.** The one interruption the Prophet ﷺ deprioritised was a believer running towards guidance; the man he prioritised had status and nothing else. *And how can you know that he might become pure?* Warn the noble and the weak, the rich and the poor, the master and the slave, equally — and leave the guidance to Allah.

**You are not responsible for outcomes.** *What does it matter to you if he will not become pure?* Deliver; do not carry the result.

**The proof of the Resurrection is dinner.** Water poured, earth split, dead seed pushed up green — the man who calls raising the dead far-fetched eats the demonstration daily and calls it lunch.

**Say "I don't know."** Abu Bakr would not speak on a word of the Book without knowledge; `Umar caught himself asking for a precision the ayah was not sent to give. Two of the greatest of the Companions, on one small word, choosing restraint.

**On the Day of the Shout, the ties that make us rank each other are the first to break.** A man flees his brother, his mother, his father, his wife, his children — most beloved first. What remains is a face that is bright and laughing, or a face covered in dust and darkness. Nothing else about him is recorded on the door.
