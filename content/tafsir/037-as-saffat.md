*Revealed in Makkah · 182 ayat · "Those Ranged in Ranks" — named for the angels standing in formation in its opening oath*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

As-Saffat opens with angels standing in disciplined rows and closes with the peace of Allah sent upon every Messenger. Between those two poles it argues one case: **there is only One God, and the men who said so were vindicated — every single one of them.**

The method is unusual. Rather than argue abstractly, the surah runs a **roll call of prophets** — Nuh, Ibrahim, Ismail, Ishaq, Musa, Harun, Ilyas, Lut, Yunus — and after each name repeats the same refrain like a drumbeat: *We left for him a good remembrance among later generations · Peace be upon him · Thus do We reward the doers of good · Verily, he was one of Our believing servants.* Each man was mocked, each was outnumbered, each was delivered, and each is still greeted with peace centuries later. The Prophet ﷺ, mocked in Makkah as "a mad poet," is being shown his own future.

Ibn Kathir notes the surah's practical footprint too: **An-Nasa'i records from Abdullah bin Umar that the Prophet ﷺ told them to keep prayers short — and would recite As-Saffat when leading them.** It was his idea of a *short* recitation.

---

## 1. The Ranks (37:1–5)

> *By those ranged in ranks · By those who drive the clouds · By those who bring the Dhikr — verily, your God is One.*

Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah and others agree: **all three oaths refer to angels.** Qatadah: they form ranks in the heavens. They drive the clouds. They carry the revelation down from Allah to mankind.

The oath and its answer fit together deliberately: the beings closest to Allah are the ones who witness His Oneness, and they do it *in formation*.

Two hadiths Ibn Kathir attaches here transfer that formation to us:

- **Hudhayfah (Muslim):** *We have been favoured over the rest of mankind in three ways — our ranks have been made like the ranks of the angels; the whole earth has been made a Masjid for us; and its soil purifies us when there is no water.*
- **Jabir bin Samurah (Muslim):** *"Will you not form ranks as the angels form ranks before their Lord?"* They asked how the angels do it. He said: **they complete the front rows first, and they close the gaps.**

Straightening a prayer row is not a minor point of etiquette. It is imitation of the heavens.

The oath is sworn by *"the Lord of every point of the sun's risings."* Ibn Kathir: mentioning the east suffices, since the west is implied — He controls every star and body that rises and sets, in winter and summer alike.

---

## 2. The Guarded Sky, and the Night It Changed (37:6–10)

The lowest heaven is adorned with stars for those who look up — and the same lights are **missiles against eavesdropping devils.** A Shaytan who tries to listen in on the higher assembly is pelted from every direction, driven off, and awaits a lasting torment besides.

The exception in 37:10 is the whole mechanics of soothsaying: a devil snatches a single word, throws it to the one below him, who throws it to the one below — perhaps burned by the flame before he can pass it on, perhaps not. Whatever survives reaches a fortune-teller, who wraps it in lies.

**Ibn Abbas's report is the striking one.** Before the Prophet ﷺ was sent, the Shayatin had seats in the heavens; the stars did not move and no one was struck. To every stolen word they would add nine of their own. Then the flames began hitting without fail. The devils complained to Iblis, who said, *"Something must have happened."* He sent out his troops, and they found the Messenger of Allah ﷺ standing in prayer in the valley of Nakhlah. Iblis said: **"This is what has happened."**

The heavens were sealed the moment the final revelation began.

---

## 3. The Argument for Resurrection (37:11–19)

> *Ask them: are they harder to create, or those whom We have created?*

Ibn Mas'ud: they conceded that the heavens, the earth, the angels, the vast creatures were harder to make than themselves. So why deny the easier thing? And they were made from *sticky clay* — Qatadah: the kind that clings to your hand.

Their objection is quoted exactly: *when we are dead and become dust and bones, shall we be resurrected? And our fathers of old?* The answer is one word plus a warning: **"Yes — and you shall be humiliated."**

And the mechanism is almost dismissively simple: **a single Zajrah** — one shout, one command — and they are standing, staring at the horrors of the Day.

---

## 4. Assembled With Your Own Kind (37:20–37)

The angels are told: *assemble those who did wrong, together with their companions.*

An-Nu'man bin Bashir, Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Mujahid and others read "companions" as **counterparts — those who are like them.** Those who committed Zina gathered with the like, those who dealt in Riba with the like, those who drank with the like. Your resurrection cohort is the crowd you chose.

Then: *stop them — they are to be questioned.* Ibn Abbas: **detain them, for they are to be brought to account.** And Uthman bin Za'idah's chilling line, preserved by Abdullah bin Al-Mubarak: **"The first thing about which a man will be asked is the company that he kept."** Then comes the mockery: *what is the matter with you — why do you not help one another?*

What follows is the great argument in Hell. Followers turn on leaders: *you used to come at us from the right side* — Qatadah reads it as the good side, the side of righteousness, blocking every good deed before it could be done; As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd: you made falsehood attractive and stood between us and Islam. The leaders answer: **"We had no authority over you"** — no proof, no power. *You were a transgressing people yourselves.*

Both are right, and both burn. *That Day they will all share in the torment.*

The root cause is named in 37:35: when they were told *La ilaha illallah*, **they puffed themselves up with pride.** Not confusion — arrogance. They asked, *shall we abandon our gods for a mad poet?* Allah answers: **he has come with the truth, and he confirms the Messengers.**

---

## 5. Paradise, Described Concretely (37:40–49)

The exception clause arrives: **"Save the chosen servants of Allah."** Ibn Kathir places it alongside Surah Al-Asr and At-Tin — the same structure of universal loss with a rescued remnant. Their bad deeds are overlooked; each good deed is multiplied from ten to seven hundred, or beyond.

The details are physical and deliberate:

- **Known provision · fruits of every kind · honoured** — served, in luxury.
- **Facing one another on thrones.** Mujahid: no one ever sees another's back. Nobody is turned away from.
- **A cup of pure wine, white, delicious** — no *Ghawl* and no intoxication. Ibn Abbas listed what earthly wine does: intoxicates, gives headache, causes vomiting and urine. **Paradise's wine is refined of all four.** Zayd bin Aslam: bright and shining, unlike the repulsive reds, blacks and yellows of this world's drink. Its taste matches its colour, and from a stream that never runs dry.
- **Qasirat At-Tarf** — companions who restrain their gaze, looking at no one but their husbands; wide and beautiful of eye.
- **As if they were Bayd Maknun** — a well-protected egg. The scholars debate which part is meant: Ibn Abbas, hidden pearls; As-Suddi, the egg preserved in its nest; Ata' Al-Khurasani, the membrane beneath the shell; another, the white beneath the shell. Ibn Jarir's reading of *Maknun*: **the outside of the egg is touched by the bird's wing, the nest, people's hands — the inside is touched by nothing.**

## 6. The Man Who Looked Down (37:50–61)

Reclining and talking, one of the people of Paradise says: **"I had a companion."** A man who used to ask him mockingly, *do you actually believe in this? That when we're dust and bones we'll be brought to account?*

Then: **"Will you look down?"** He looks over the edge and sees him in the middle of Hell. Al-Hasan Al-Basri: **like a burning star.** And he says: *By Allah, you nearly ruined me. Had it not been for the grace of my Lord, I would have been among those brought forth.*

Then the joy that Al-Hasan Al-Basri says completes everything — because they know that death ends every pleasure: *are we then not to die? Except our first death? And we shall not be punished!* **Truly, this is the supreme success.**

Ibn Jarir reads the final line as Allah's own comment: **"For the like of this, let the workers work."** For *this*, not for anything here.

### The two partners

Ibn Jarir preserves an illustrative story of two Israelite business partners who split eight thousand Dinars. One bought a house for a thousand; his friend admired it, then went out and said: *O Allah, my companion bought a house for a thousand Dinars — I ask You for a house in Paradise* — and gave a thousand in charity. The first married with a dowry of a thousand; the friend asked for a wife among Al-Hur Al-'Iyn and gave a thousand. The first bought two gardens for two thousand; the friend asked for two gardens in Paradise and gave two thousand.

Then the angel came for their souls. The believer was shown a house, a woman so radiant the ground shone beneath her, and two gardens. He said, *this is like a man who had such and such.* The angel said: **"That is exactly what it is. This house, these gardens and this wife are all for you."**

## 7. Zaqqum (37:62–70)

> *Is that better entertainment, or the tree of Zaqqum?*

The question is posed as a straight comparison, immediately after the description of Paradise's food and drink.

Qatadah records that Zaqqum was **a test the Makkans failed publicly.** They scoffed: *your companion tells you there's a tree in the Fire — but fire burns trees.* The answer came: **it springs from the very bottom of Hell** — nourished by fire, created from it. Abu Jahl's response was to joke that "Zaqqum" meant dates and butter, which he was eating.

Its fruit stalks are *like the heads of Shayatin* — an image that works, Ibn Kathir notes, precisely because nobody has seen a devil, yet everyone is certain they are hideous.

They will eat and fill their bellies, not from appetite but because there is nothing else — then be given boiling *Hamim* on top of it. Sa'id bin Jubayr's description is the harshest passage in the surah: the skin of their faces falls away as they eat; then thirst is sent upon them; the drink is like oil heated to the ultimate degree, and brings the heat near enough that the exposed flesh is baked and their insides melt. Then they are returned to the Fire, rotating between the two.

The cause is given plainly: **they found their fathers on the wrong path, and rushed after them.** Mujahid: this is like running. Sa'id bin Jubayr: they followed ignorance and foolishness. Not evidence — inheritance.

## 8. The Roll Call

Then Allah begins the parade of Messengers who stood alone and were vindicated.

### Nuh (37:75–82)
A thousand years less fifty among people who turned further away each time he called. He finally prayed *"I have been overcome, so help me!"* — and Ibn Kathir's phrasing is arresting: **Allah became angry because Nuh was angry with them.** The flood came. Ibn Abbas and Qatadah: **all of humanity descends from Nuh's offspring** — Sam father of the Arabs, Ham of the Ethiopians, Yafith of the Romans. The refrain lands for the first time: *We left for him a good remembrance · Peace be upon Nuh among all creatures.* Mujahid: an honourable mention on the tongue of every Prophet after him.

### Ibrahim (37:83–98)
He came to his Lord with a *Salim* heart. Ibn Abbas: one that bears witness to Allah alone. Al-Hasan: **one free of Shirk.** Muhammad bin Sirin: one that knows Allah is true, that the Hour is coming, and that Allah will raise the dead.

He looked at the stars and said *"I am sick"* — so his people would leave him behind on their festival day. Ibn Kathir handles this carefully, quoting the hadith of Abu Hurayrah about Ibrahim's "three lies" (this one; *the biggest of them did it*; and *she is my sister*) and then explaining: **this is not the kind of lie a man is condemned for.** Two were purely for the sake of Allah, and one calls them lies only for lack of a better word — they were oblique speech serving a religious purpose. And he *was* sick — sick at the implications of what they worshipped.

Then, alone in the empty city, he addressed the idols before the food laid out for their blessing: *Will you not eat? What is the matter with you that you speak not?* Al-Farra': and then he began striking them with his right hand — the stronger hand. He left them shattered except the biggest.

When they rushed at him, he turned the accusation around: **"Do you worship what you yourselves carve, when Allah created you and what you make?"** They built a furnace. Allah made them the lowest.

### The sacrifice (37:99–113)
Ibrahim emigrated — *I am going to my Lord, He will guide me* — and asked for righteous offspring. The glad tidings came: **a forbearing boy.**

Ibn Kathir argues at length, and firmly, that the son was **Ismail**. His reasons:

- Ismail was the firstborn (born when Ibrahim was 86; Ishaq at 99), and the People of the Book's own text says *only son* / *firstborn* — which cannot describe Ishaq.
- The name Ishaq, he says, was inserted out of tribal jealousy, since Ismail is the ancestor of the Arabs — and *"only son"* cannot be said of a man who has another.
- **Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi's textual argument:** the surah gives Ishaq's glad tidings *after* the sacrifice narrative is finished, and includes the promise that Ishaq would himself have a son — Allah would not command the sacrifice of a boy already promised descendants.
- The chain of transmission: Ibn Abbas (*"the Jews claimed it was Ishaq, but they lied"*), Ibn Umar, Mujahid, Ash-Sha'bi, Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ali, Abu Hurayrah, and Imam Ahmad when asked directly by his son: **"Ismail."**
- And a physical trace: the ram's horns hung in the Ka'bah, inherited by the Quraysh generation after generation, until the Prophet ﷺ sent for Uthman bin Talhah to have them covered — *there should be nothing in the House that distracts a worshipper.* They hung there until the Ka'bah burned, and burned with it.

The exchange itself is the heart of it. *"O my son, I have seen in a dream that I am slaughtering you — so look what you think."* 'Ubayd bin Umayr: **the dreams of the Prophets are revelation.** He told his son partly to ease it for him, partly to test his patience. And the boy answered: **"O my father, do that which you are commanded. If Allah wills, you shall find me of the patient."**

*Then, when they had both submitted.* Ibn Kathir: both pronounced the Shahadah and remembered Allah — Ibrahim about to sacrifice, Ismail about to die. He laid him face down so he would not have to see his face.

Ibn Abbas's account adds the detail that survives in the Hajj: the Shaytan appeared at the Masa, then at Jamrat Al-Aqabah, then at Al-Jamrah Al-Wusta, and was driven off with seven pebbles each time. And Ismail in his white shirt said: **"O my father, I have no garment to be shrouded in but this — take it off me so you can shroud me in it."** As he began to remove it, the call came.

*"O Ibrahim! You have fulfilled the dream."* The purpose was accomplished the moment he laid his son down. As-Suddi: the knife passed over the neck and did not cut, a sheet of copper between them. Ibrahim turned and saw a fine white horned ram. Ibn Abbas: **a ram that had grazed in Paradise for forty years.**

Ibn Kathir draws the legal point out too — some scholars of Usul cite this as proof that **a ruling can be abrogated before anyone acts on it**, since the purpose was the obedience, not the blood.

And the general principle: *thus do We reward the doers of good* — meaning, when someone obeys Us in what is hard for him, **We make for him a way out.**

### Musa and Harun (37:114–122)
Prophethood, rescue from Fir'awn's persecution, victory, the inheritance of their oppressors' lands and wealth, the clear Scripture, and guidance to the straight path — *in words and deeds*. Then the refrain.

### Ilyas (37:123–132)
He called his people away from the idol **Ba'l** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah: the word means *lord*; Ikrimah says it is Yemeni dialect; Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd says it names the idol of Ba'labak (Baalbek), west of Damascus. Wahb bin Munabbih's account: their king believed and then apostatised, and **not one person among them believed.** He prayed and rain was withheld three years; they begged, promised faith, got rain, and went straight back. Ibn Kathir passes the more fantastical parts of Wahb's report along from the People of the Book and comments: **"Allah knows best how true it is."** Then the refrain — peace be upon Ilyasin.

### Lut (37:133–138)
Saved with his family, except the old woman who stayed behind. The rest destroyed, their place left as a foul, stinking lake on a well-travelled route — **the Dead Sea, on the highway between Arabia and Syria.** *Verily you pass by them in the morning, and at night — will you not then reflect?* The ruins are on the trade road the Quraysh used. They drove past the evidence twice a day.

### Yunus (37:139–148)
The ship in a storm, the lots drawn three times, and each time his name. **They did not want to throw him overboard** — he took off his garment to jump, and they tried to stop him. The fish came from the Green Sea, commanded to swallow him without cutting flesh or breaking bone. When he had been inside a while he thought he had died; then he moved his limbs and knew he was alive, and prayed: **"O Lord, I have taken as a place of worship to You a place no other person has reached."** (How long: three days per Qatadah, seven per Ja'far As-Sadiq, forty per Abu Malik, from morning to evening per Ash-Sha'bi. Ibn Kathir does not decide.)

*Had he not been of those who glorify Allah, he would have remained in its belly until the Day of Resurrection.* The interpretation Ibn Jarir favoured, held by Ad-Dahhak, Abu Al-'Aliyah, Wahb, and Qatadah: **the glorifying that saved him was the righteous deeds he had done in his time of ease.** The hadith attached is the one worth carrying: **"Remember Allah in times of ease, and He will remember you in times of difficulty."**

The narration from Anas makes it vivid: his cry from the darkness — *La ilaha illa Anta, You are glorified, truly I have been of the wrongdoers* — rose and hovered around the Throne. The angels said, **"O Lord, this is a voice that is weak, but known — from a faraway strange land."** Allah said, *"My servant Yunus."* They said, *"Your servant Yunus — from whom acceptable deeds and answered supplications kept coming?"* And they interceded: *will You not have mercy on him for what he did in his time of ease?* He said: **"Of course."**

Cast on a bare shore, sick and weak, shaded by a gourd plant — squash, per an unusually long list of authorities, praised because it grows fast, its wide smooth leaves give real shade, flies avoid it, and every part of it nourishes. Ibn Kathir adds that the Prophet ﷺ liked it and would search for it in the dish.

Then he was sent back to the same hundred thousand or more — **and all of them believed.** The only town in the Qur'an whose faith arrived in time.

Also: *"It is not right for any person to say I am better than Yunus bin Matta."*

## 9. Demolishing the Daughters of Allah (37:149–160)

The Makkans said the angels were Allah's daughters. Ibn Kathir dismantles it in stages.

**First, the hypocrisy:** they said this while their own faces darkened at the news of a baby girl. *Are there daughters for your Lord and sons for you?* They assigned to Allah the share they refused for themselves.

**Second, the evidence:** *did We create the angels female while they were witnesses?* They did not observe the creation. Their testimony will be recorded and they will be questioned about it.

Ibn Kathir counts **three compounding falsehoods**, each alone sufficient for ruin: that Allah has offspring; that the offspring are female; and that they then *worshipped* them.

**Third, the challenge:** *bring your Book, if you are truthful.* Produce revealed evidence. There is none — *what you say is totally irrational.*

And Mujahid preserves the exchange that ends the argument: the idolaters said the angels were Allah's daughters, and **Abu Bakr asked, "Then who are their mothers?"** They said: the daughters of the leaders of the Jinn.

The angels themselves answer the slander in their own voice (37:164–166): *there is not one of us but has his known place; we stand in rows; we are those who glorify.* A'ishah reports the Prophet ﷺ saying: **there is no place in the lowest heaven without an angel standing or prostrating.** Ibn Mas'ud: no handspan without a forehead or a foot on it.

And Umar, when the Iqamah was given, would turn to face the people: **"Make your rows straight, for Allah wants you to follow the ways of the angels."** Move back, so-and-so. Move forward. Then *Allahu Akbar*.

The surah's opening oath has become an instruction for how to stand behind an imam.

## 10. The Promise, and the Waiting (37:171–179)

> *Our Word has gone forth of old for Our servants, the Messengers: that they would be made triumphant, and that Our hosts would be the victors.*

Ibn Kathir: **this was already decided in the first decree.** The Messengers and their followers prevail, in this world and the next. It is not a hope; it is a settled matter awaiting its date.

Which is why the instruction to the Prophet ﷺ is simply: *turn away from them for a while, and watch.* **Bear the annoyance patiently and wait for the appointed time.**

They ask for the punishment to be hurried. *Then when it descends in their courtyard, evil will be the morning for those who were warned.*

And Anas reports the morning it happened literally: at **Khaybar**, the people came out with their tools for the day's work, saw the Muslim army, and ran back shouting *"Muhammad, by Allah — Muhammad and the army!"* The Prophet ﷺ said: **"Allahu Akbar! Khaybar is destroyed."** And then recited this very verse.

## 11. The Closing Three (37:180–182)

> *Glorified be your Lord, the Lord of Al-'Izzah, above what they attribute to Him · And peace be upon the Messengers · And all praise be to Allah, Lord of all that exists.*

*Al-'Izzah* — might and power that nothing can resist. The peace is sent on the Messengers because **what they said about their Lord was sound, correct and true** — the exact opposite of the fabrications just refuted. And the praise, Ibn Kathir notes, pairs naturally with the glorification: *Tasbih* declares Him free of all deficiency, *Hamd* affirms all perfection. Together they are complete.

Two practices are attached:

- **Qatadah:** *"When you send Salam upon me, send Salam upon all the Messengers — for I am one of the Messengers."*
- **Ali:** whoever wants a greater measure of reward on the Day of Resurrection, let him **say these three verses at the end of any gathering.**

The surah that began by swearing on the ranks of angels ends by handing you three lines to say as you stand up to leave a room.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Every prophet in this surah was outnumbered, mocked, and right.** Nuh preached 950 years to almost no one; Ibrahim's people built him a furnace; Ilyas converted not a single soul; Lut's own wife stayed behind; Yunus fled. And after each one: *We left for him a good remembrance · Peace be upon him · Thus do We reward the doers of good.*

The disbelievers' error was never a failure of evidence. It was two things: **pride** — *when it was said to them La ilaha illallah, they puffed themselves up* — and **inheritance** — *they found their fathers on the wrong path and rushed after them.*

And the practical residue of a surah about angels is startlingly small and physical:

- **Straighten the row.** *Make your rows straight, for Allah wants you to follow the ways of the angels.*
- **Watch the company you keep.** *The first thing about which a man will be asked is the company that he kept* — and you will be assembled with your counterparts.
- **Bank your good deeds now.** *Remember Allah in times of ease, and He will remember you in times of difficulty.* Yunus was recognised in the dark of the fish's belly because of what he had done in the light.
- **Say the last three verses when you leave a gathering.**

And the line Ibn Jarir says is Allah's own commentary on Paradise, placed right after a man in the Garden looks down at his old friend in the Fire:

> **For the like of this, let the workers work.**
