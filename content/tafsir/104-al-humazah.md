*Revealed in Makkah · 9 ayat · "The Slanderer" — named for the man condemned in its first verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Humazah is nine verses long and does one thing: it takes two habits that look unrelated — **sneering at people** and **counting your money** — and shows that they are the same illness, then describes the punishment built precisely to answer it.

The sequence is the argument. Verse 1 names a man who belittles others. Verse 2 tells you what he does with his days: he piles up wealth and tallies it. Verse 3 tells you what he believes: that the pile will make him permanent. Read backwards, the causation is obvious. He thinks his wealth makes him last forever, so he thinks he is a different order of creature from the people around him, so he mocks them. **Contempt is what a man's face does when he has quietly appointed himself immortal.**

Then comes *Kalla* — **no** — and everything reverses. The verbs of the second half of the surah are the verbs of the first half turned against him:

| What he did | What is done to him |
|---|---|
| **Crushed** people's reputations | Thrown into **Al-Hutamah** — the Crusher |
| **Gathered and enclosed** his wealth | **Enclosed**, the gates shut upon him |
| Sneered with **eye, hand and tongue** | A fire that goes past all of that and reaches the **heart** |
| Raised himself up on his heaps | **Columns** stretched over him |

The surah never says what he actually said about anyone. It doesn't need to. It is interested in the engine, not the exhaust.

---

## 1. Woe to Every Humazah Lumazah (104:1)

> *Woe to every Humazah Lumazah.*

Ibn Kathir gives the standard distinction: **Al-Hammaz** is defamation by **speech**, **Al-Lammaz** is defamation by **action** — the gesture, the look, the imitation behind someone's back. The single description covers a man who **finds fault with people and belittles them**, in whichever medium is available to him.

Both words are intensive forms — not a man who once mocked someone, but a man for whom this is a settled habit and a personality.

Ibn Kathir points back to the same charge levelled elsewhere:

> *Hammaz, going about with slander* (68:11)

Two of the earliest readings are preserved:

- **Ibn Abbas:** it means one who **reviles and disgraces** others.
- **Mujahid:** *Al-Humazah* is with **the hand and the eye**; *Al-Lumazah* is with **the tongue**.

Mujahid's split is worth pausing on, because it removes every hiding place. The raised eyebrow across a room and the hand gesture behind a back are named in the Qur'an alongside the spoken insult. Silence is not innocence if the face is doing the work.

---

## 2. He Gathered Wealth and Counted It (104:2–3)

> *Who has gathered wealth and counted it. He thinks that his wealth will make him last forever!*

Ibn Kathir reads the two verbs literally and unglamorously: he **piles some of it on top of the rest**, and then he **tallies it up**. As-Suddi and Ibn Jarir link it to the man described elsewhere who *collects and hides* (70:18).

**Muhammad bin Ka'b** gives the most vivid line in the surah's commentary — a whole life in one sentence. The man's wealth occupies his entire day, moving him from this thing to that thing; and then when night comes, **he sleeps like a rotting corpse**.

That is the image: motion all day, and at the end of it a body that is already dead. The wealth was supposed to make him last forever, and it has instead reduced him to something that decays.

On verse 3, Ibn Kathir's gloss is exact: he thinks **the gathering itself** will make him permanent **in this abode** — the worldly life. Not that money buys medicine or safety, but that the accumulation *is* a form of not-dying. This is not a calculation anyone makes out loud. It is what a life looks like when nobody has ever tested the assumption underneath it.

And then:

> *Kalla* — **But no!**

Ibn Kathir: the matter is **not as he claims, nor as he reckons**.

---

## 3. Al-Hutamah: The Crusher (104:4–5)

> *But no! Verily, he will be thrown into Al-Hutamah. And what will make you know what Al-Hutamah is?*

The one who gathered and counted is **thrown in** — the passive verb, applied to a man who spent his life as the one doing things to others.

**Al-Hutamah** is one of the descriptive names of the Hellfire. Ibn Kathir gives the reason for the name in a single clause: **because it crushes whoever is in it.**

The man who spent his life crushing people's standing is handed to the thing whose entire nature is crushing.

---

## 4. The Fire That Reaches the Heart (104:6–7)

> *The fire of Allah, Al-Muqadah, which leaps up over the hearts.*

*Al-Muqadah* — kindled, set ablaze. And what it climbs to is not the skin but the **hearts**, which is the surah's most deliberate stroke: the sin began in the heart as contempt, and the fire is directed at the same place.

Two witnesses, both unbearable:

- **Thabit Al-Bunani:** it will burn them all the way to their hearts **while they are still alive**. Then he said that the torment will indeed reach them — and then he wept.
- **Muhammad bin Ka'b:** it will consume every part of the body until it reaches the heart and rises to the level of the throat — **and then return to the body** and begin again.

Note what Muhammad bin Ka'b is describing: not a fire that finishes, but a circuit. There is no point at which the burning arrives somewhere and stops.

That Thabit Al-Bunani broke off and cried in the middle of the explanation tells you how the early generations received these verses. They were not commentary to them.

---

## 5. Shut In, Under Columns (104:8–9)

> *Verily, it shall be Mu'sadah upon them, in pillars stretched forth.*

**Mu'sadah** means **covering** — closed over them, sealed. Ibn Kathir refers back to his treatment of the same word in Surah Al-Balad (90:20).

On the **pillars stretched forth**, three reports:

- **Atiyah Al-'Awfi:** pillars of **iron**.
- **As-Suddi:** made of **fire**.
- **Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas:** they are made to enter them — meaning there will be **columns over them**, **chains on their necks**, and **the gates of Hell shut upon them**.

Ibn Abbas's picture completes the reversal the surah has been building. The man's life was an exercise in enclosure: gathering wealth inward, sealing it off, counting it in private, keeping other people outside and beneath him. He is given exactly that, made permanent — walls over him, the doors closed, and nothing left to count.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Mockery and hoarding are one disease, not two.** The surah puts them in consecutive verses because the second produces the first.
- **You do not have to say anything to be a Humazah.** Mujahid: the hand and the eye count.
- **Nobody announces the belief that kills him.** *He thinks his wealth will make him last forever* is not a doctrine anyone professes — it is a conclusion visible only in how someone spends his days.
- **Muhammad bin Ka'b's diagnostic:** a day spent moving from this to that on account of your wealth, ending in sleep like a corpse. If that is the shape of the week, the surah is describing you, whatever your bank balance.
- **The fire is aimed at the heart** — because that is where the contempt was kept.
- **He crushed, so he is crushed. He enclosed, so he is enclosed.** In this surah the punishment is not merely severe; it is the sin returned to the sinner in his own currency.
