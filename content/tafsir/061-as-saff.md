*Revealed in Madinah · 14 ayat · "The Row" — named for the battle ranks at 61:4*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

As-Saff is a short surah about **the gap between what a person says and what he does** — and about closing it.

It opens by shaming a specific failure: claiming a commitment you do not honour. It then names the deed Allah loves most — fighting in His cause *in rows, as if a solid structure* — and spends the rest of its length showing what happens to people who fail that standard (Musa's people, who wore him down; the Children of Israel, who split over 'Isa) and what is offered to those who meet it (a "trade" that buys forgiveness, Paradise, and victory in this life too).

The unifying image is the **row**: parts cemented to each other, no gaps, no one out of line. The surah's diagnosis of failure is exactly this image inverted — words detached from deeds, a community fractured into sects, a Prophet left standing alone.

---

## 1. Its Virtue

Ibn Kathir opens with a narration from **`Abdullah bin Salam** (recorded by Imam Ahmad): the Companions wanted to ask the Messenger ﷺ which actions Allah loves most, but none of them volunteered to go and ask. The Messenger ﷺ sent a man to them, who gathered them and **recited this entire surah** — the answer arriving before the question was put.

That framing matters: the whole surah is an answer to *"what does Allah love most?"*

---

## 2. Saying What You Do Not Do (61:2–3)

> *O you who believe! Why do you say that which you do not do? Most hateful it is with Allah that you say that which you do not do.*

Ibn Kathir reads this first as a **refutation of those who fail to fulfil their promises**, and cites it as evidence for the position of several of the Salaf: **a promise must be kept**, whether or not it involves money owed to the person promised.

He supports this from the Sunnah with two hadiths from the Sahihs:

- **The three signs of a hypocrite** — when he promises, he breaks his promise; when he speaks, he lies; when he is entrusted, he betrays.
- **The four characteristics** — whoever has all of them is a pure hypocrite; whoever has one has a trait of hypocrisy until he abandons it. Breaking a promise is among them.

Then the narration that gives the point its domestic edge (Ahmad and Abu Dawud, from **`Abdullah bin `Amir bin Rabi`ah**): as a boy he was called in from playing by his mother — *"Come, I want to give you something."* The Prophet ﷺ asked her what she intended to give. She said dates. He said:

> *If you had not given them to him, it would have been written as a lie in your record.*

An unkept promise to a child is a lie on your record. That is the standard being set.

---

## 3. The Deed Allah Loves: Rows Like a Solid Structure (61:4)

> *Verily, Allah loves those who fight in His cause in rows as if they were a solid structure.*

**Muqatil bin Hayyan**: the believers had said, *"If we only knew which good deeds are dearest to Allah, we would do them."* So Allah told them — and then they were **tested at Uhud**, where they retreated and fled, leaving the Prophet ﷺ behind. It was about *that* failure, Muqatil says, that 61:2 was revealed. *The dearest of you to Me is the one who fights in My cause.*

Two further readings Ibn Kathir preserves:

- Some said it concerns **boasting about battle** — claiming to have fought and endured when one did not.
- **Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak**: it was sent down about people who claimed they had killed, fought and stabbed in battle, having done none of it.

On the *rows* themselves, **Sa`id bin Jubayr**: before engaging an enemy the Prophet ﷺ liked to **line his forces up in ranks**, and in this surah Allah teaches the believers to do the same.

And on *solid structure* — **Muqatil**: firmly connected to each other. **Ibn `Abbas**: *"like a firm structure that does not move, because its parts are cemented to each other."*

---

## 4. Musa and the Cost of Wearing Down a Prophet (61:5)

> *O my people! Why do you annoy me while you know certainly that I am the Messenger of Allah to you?*

Ibn Kathir reads this as **consolation for Muhammad ﷺ** for what his own people did to him, and as an instruction to be patient. Hence the Prophet's ﷺ own words:

> *May Allah have mercy on Musa: he was annoyed more than this, yet he was patient.*

And, by implication, believers are **forbidden from harming or bothering the Prophet ﷺ in any form** — as at 33:69: *Be not like those who annoyed Musa.*

Then the verse's severe second half:

> *So, when they turned away, Allah turned their hearts away.*

**Deviation is punished with deviation.** When the Jews turned from guidance *while knowing it*, Allah turned their hearts from it and left doubt, suspicion and failure in place of it. Ibn Kathir strings this together with 6:110 (*We shall turn their hearts and vision away… and leave them wandering blindly*) and 4:115 (*whoever opposes the Messenger after guidance is clear… We shall keep him in the path he has chosen*).

---

## 5. 'Isa's Announcement of Ahmad (61:6)

> *…giving glad tidings of a Messenger to come after me, whose name shall be Ahmad.*

Ibn Kathir's paraphrase of 'Isa's position: *the Tawrah announced my coming, and my coming confirms the Tawrah — and I announce the Prophet who comes after me*, the unlettered Makkan Arab Prophet, **Ahmad**. 'Isa is the **last messenger from among the Children of Israel**; after Muhammad ﷺ there is no prophethood at all.

**The names** (Al-Bukhari and Muslim, from Jubayr bin Mut`im):

| Name | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Muhammad** and **Ahmad** | The praised one |
| **Al-Mahi** | The eraser — through whom Allah eliminates disbelief |
| **Al-Hashir** | The gatherer — first to be resurrected, with people gathered after him |
| **Al-`Aqib** | The last — no Prophet after him |

**The three announcements of his advent.** Asked by his Companions to tell them about himself, he ﷺ said he was:

1. **The invocation of Ibrahim** (2:129 — *Our Lord! Send among them a Messenger of their own*),
2. **The glad tidings of 'Isa**,
3. **His mother's dream** — a light emanating from her that lit the palaces of Busra in Ash-Sham.

And to Al-`Irbad bin Sariyah: *I was written with Allah as the Last of the Prophets when Adam was still clay.*

### The Court of An-Najashi

Ibn Kathir inserts here the long narration of **Ibn Mas`ud** — the Muslim emigration to Abyssinia — because it is the moment the Christian world was formally shown the announcement.

Eighty men, including Ja`far bin Abi Talib and Ibn Mas`ud himself, went to An-Najashi. Quraysh sent `Amr bin Al-`As and `Umarah bin Al-Walid with gifts, who prostrated before the king and accused the Muslims of abandoning their people and religion.

Ja`far spoke for the Muslims and **did not prostrate**. Asked why: *"We only prostrate for Allah."* Asked about 'Isa, he answered only with what Allah said of him — **Allah's Word, and a soul created by Him**, sent to an honourable virgin untouched by any man.

An-Najashi picked up a straw and said that what they had said about 'Isa did not differ from what he himself held **by so much as that straw** — that he bore witness Muhammad ﷺ was the Messenger foretold in the Injil, and that were he not burdened with kingship he would go and carry the Prophet's ﷺ sandals and his water for ablution. He returned the idolaters' gifts. When news of his death reached the Prophet ﷺ, he asked Allah to forgive him.

And yet, when Ahmad came with clear proofs, the response was: **"This is plain magic."**

## 6. The Light They Try to Blow Out (61:7–9)

- **61:7** — None is more unjust than one who invents lies against Allah, calls on rivals and associates partners with Him — *while he is being invited to Tawhid and to sincere worship*. The injustice is compounded by the invitation standing open.
- **61:8** — *They intend to put out the Light of Allah with their mouths.* Ibn Kathir's image: they try to contradict truth with falsehood the way a man tries to **extinguish the sun by blowing at it**. It is not resisted; it is simply impossible.
- **61:9** — He sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth **to make it victorious over all religions**, however much the idolaters hate it.

---

## 7. The Trade That Never Fails (61:10–13)

> *Shall I guide you to a trade that will save you from a painful torment?*

This is the direct answer to the Companions' question at the start of the surah. Note the structure Ibn Kathir highlights: Allah **asks** before He tells, framing obedience as commerce — a transaction that *earns what you want and saves you from what you dread*.

**The price you pay:**
- Faith in Allah and His Messenger
- Striving in Allah's cause **with your wealth and your lives**

*That will be better for you, if you but know* — better, Ibn Kathir says, than the trade of this life, the striving for it and the amassing of it.

**What you receive:**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Forgiveness of sins** | 61:12 |
| **Gardens under which rivers flow** | Exalted residences, high positions |
| **Pleasant dwellings in `Adn** | *That is indeed the great success* |
| **"Another which you love"** | Help from Allah and **a near victory** |

That last item is the surah's most generous touch: an **extra**, unasked for, delivered *in this life* — victory that arrives sooner and then continues on to become the delight of the Hereafter. Its condition is stated plainly at 47:7 and 22:40: *if you help Allah, He will help you.*

## 8. Be the Ansar (61:14)

> *Be you helpers of Allah as said 'Isa to the disciples: "Who are my helpers in the cause of Allah?" The Hawariyyun said: "We are Allah's helpers."*

An order to be Allah's supporters **at all times, in all statements and actions, sacrificing self and wealth**. 'Isa then sent his disciples across Ash-Sham to call the Greeks and the Israelites.

Ibn Kathir places the parallel exactly: during the days of Hajj, the Prophet ﷺ went from group to group asking,

> *Who will support me in conveying the Message of my Lord? Quraysh have prevented me from conveying the Message of my Lord.*

**Al-Aws and Al-Khazraj** answered, pledged to protect him from mankind and Jinn if he came to them, and kept the vow. This is why Allah and His Messenger named them **Al-Ansar** — *the Supporters* — and the name has belonged to them ever since.

### The Split Over 'Isa

*Then a group of the Children of Israel believed and a group disbelieved.*

Ibn Kathir sorts the response to 'Isa into three:

1. **Those who believed** and accepted his guidance.
2. **Those who rejected him**, denied his prophethood and invented terrible slanders about him and his mother.
3. **Those who exaggerated**, raising him above the prophethood Allah gave him — splitting into factions saying he was the son of God, or one of three, or God Himself.

Then the striking narration of **Ibn `Abbas** (via Ibn Jarir, also in An-Nasa'i's Sunan): when Allah willed to raise 'Isa, he came to twelve companions and told them some would disbelieve in him. He asked who would volunteer to be **made to resemble him and killed in his place**, and be with him in Paradise. The youngest man present volunteered — three times — and was told, *"Then it will be you."* 'Isa's likeness was cast upon him; 'Isa was raised through an opening in the roof; the young man was taken and crucified.

Those who remained divided into three parties — **Al-Ya`qubiyyah** (*God stayed with us then ascended*), **An-Nasturiyyah** (*God's son stayed with us then was raised*), and one group who said, *Allah's servant and Messenger remained with us as much as Allah willed, then Allah raised him to Himself.* **The last group was the Muslim group.** The other two combined against it and wiped it out, and Islam remained unjustly concealed — **until Allah sent Muhammad ﷺ**.

> *So, We gave power to those who believed against their enemies, and they became the victorious.*

The verse, on this reading, is not fulfilled in 'Isa's lifetime at all. It is fulfilled **centuries later**, by the Prophet ﷺ, on behalf of a group that had already been destroyed. Ibn Kathir closes with the consequence: the Ummah of Muhammad ﷺ **will always be prevalent upon the truth** until Allah's command comes, and the last of them will fight Ad-Dajjal alongside 'Isa, peace be upon him.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Allah does not merely dislike hypocrisy of speech — He calls it the thing most hateful to Him.** A promise unkept to your own child is recorded as a lie.

**What He loves most is the opposite: people whose deeds match their words, standing shoulder to shoulder like a wall with no gaps.** Not individual heroism — *cemented parts*.

**And He frames the whole thing as a trade, not a demand.** You give faith, wealth and life. You receive forgiveness, `Adn, and — thrown in, because He knows you love it — help and a victory that arrives before you die.

**The surah's warning and its promise are the same mechanism run in two directions:** turn away, and He turns your heart away; help His cause, and He helps you. Musa's people and the Ansar are the two outcomes.
