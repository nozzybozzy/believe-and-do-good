*Revealed in Makkah · 15 ayat · "The Sun" — named for the first of its seven oaths*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ash-Shams builds the longest run of oaths in the Qur'an — **seven of them, one after another** — and then spends them all on a single sentence about you.

The oaths climb in a deliberate order: sun, moon, day, night, heaven, earth, **soul**. Six of them are the largest things in existence; the seventh is the smallest thing in the list and the only one being addressed. Everything vast is sworn by in order to arrive at something interior:

> *Indeed he succeeds who purifies it. And indeed he fails who Dassaha.*

That is the whole claim. The sun and the moon are running their courses exactly as they were made to; the question the surah puts is whether **you** are.

Then, instead of arguing, it produces a case file. **Thamud** — a nation that was shown the truth in the form of a she-camel brought out of solid rock, and killed it. Ibn Kathir notes what makes them the perfect illustration: the deed was done by one man, but the punishment fell on everyone equally, because everyone had signed on to it. A soul can be buried by the crowd it belongs to.

There is a quiet piece of craftsmanship holding the two halves together. In verse 7 Allah **Sawwaha** — proportioned the soul, made it balanced. In verse 14 He **Fasawwaha** — levelled them, made the punishment fall on all alike. The same root opens the surah's gift and closes its judgement.

## 1. Where You'll Hear It: The Mu'adh Incident

Ibn Kathir opens not with the text but with a hadith of Jabir in the Two Sahihs. Mu'adh had led the night prayer with a long recitation, and the Prophet ﷺ asked him why he hadn't prayed with:

- **Al-A'la (87)** — *Glorify the Name of your Lord the Most High*
- **Ash-Shams (91)** — *By the sun and Duhaha*
- **Al-Layl (92)** — *By the night as it envelops*

The point is practical: this is a surah the Prophet ﷺ named as fit for leading ordinary people in ordinary prayer. Short, weighty, and not a burden on the congregation.

## 2. The Seven Oaths (91:1–7)

### By the sun and Duhaha

- **Mujahid:** by its light.
- **Qatadah:** *Duhaha* means the whole day.
- **Ibn Jarir**, resolving it: Allah swears by the sun **and by its daytime** — because the clear light of the sun *is* daytime.

### By the moon as it Talaha (follows it)

- **Mujahid:** it follows the sun.
- **Ibn Abbas** (via Al-Awfi): it follows the day.
- **Qatadah**, with the most vivid reading: this is the night of the **Hilal** — the new crescent. When the sun goes down, the crescent becomes visible. The moon literally comes out behind it.

### By the day as it Jallaha (displays it)

**Mujahid:** when it illuminates — parallel to *By the day as it Tajalla* (92:2).

### By the night as it Yaghshaha (covers it)

When it covers the sun — the moment the sun disappears and the horizons go dark.

### By the heaven and Ma Banaha

Here Ibn Kathir flags a genuine ambiguity in the word **Ma**, and refuses to collapse it:

| Reading | Meaning | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| *Ma* = "what" (descriptive) | "By the heaven **and its construction**" | Qatadah |
| *Ma* = "Who" | "By the heaven **and its Constructor**" | Mujahid |

He then says the two views are **interrelated** — and glosses *building* as **raising**, citing: *With Hands did We construct the heaven, and verily We are able to extend the vastness of space thereof. And We have spread out the earth: how excellent a spreader are We!* (51:47–48) — where "with Hands" means *with strength*.

### By the earth and Ma Tahaha

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Al-Awfi): what He created in it.
- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah): He proportioned it.
- **Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi, Ath-Thawri, Abu Salih, Ibn Zayd** — all: **He spread it out.**

### By Nafs, and Ma Sawwaha

**He created it sound and well-proportioned, upon the correct nature — Al-Fitrah.** Ibn Kathir immediately reaches for 30:30: *So set your face towards the religion, Hanif — Allah's Fitrah with which He has created mankind. No change let there be in the Khalq of Allah.*

And the famous hadith (Bukhari and Muslim, from Abu Hurayrah):

> **"Every child that is born, is born upon the Fitrah, but his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Zoroastrian. This is just as the animal is born complete with all its parts. Do you notice any mutilation in it?"**

And in Sahih Muslim, from Iyad bin Himar Al-Mujashi'i, Allah says:

> **"Verily I created My servants Hunafa' — but then the devils came to them and distracted them from their religion."**

The soul is not delivered damaged. Damage is done to it afterwards.

## 3. The Verse the Surah Turns On (91:8)

> *Then He showed it its Fujur and its Taqwa.*

He showed the soul **its own transgression and its own God-consciousness** — clarified them for it, and guided it to what was ordained for it.

- **Ibn Abbas:** He explained the good and the evil to it. (**Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Ath-Thawri** all concur.)
- **Sa'id bin Jubayr:** He gave it *inspiration* to see good and evil.
- **Ibn Zayd:** He made its Fujur and its Taqwa **inside of it.**

### The Qadar exchange this verse provoked

Ibn Jarir records Abul-Aswad Ad-Dili being cornered by **'Imran bin Husayn**: are people's deeds preordained, or only recorded once the Message reaches them? Abul-Aswad said: preordained. 'Imran pressed: *"Is that an injustice?"* — and Abul-Aswad, badly frightened, gave an answer worth memorising:

> *"There is nothing except that He created it and possesses it in His Hand. He is not asked about what He does, while they will be asked."*

'Imran then revealed he had only been testing him, and told the underlying story: a man of Muzaynah or Juhaynah asked the Prophet ﷺ the same question. The reply: **"Rather it is something preordained for them."** So the man asked the obvious follow-up — *then what is the point of our actions?* And the Prophet ﷺ said:

> **"Whoever Allah created for one of the two positions, He makes it easy for him to attain."**

Then he recited, as proof, the two verses that stand at the centre of this surah: *By Nafs, and Ma Sawwaha. Then He showed it its Fujur and its Taqwa.* (Recorded by Ahmad and Muslim.)

The answer, in short: destiny is not a reason to sit still. **Ease is the evidence.** Which way you find yourself moving easily is itself the sign.

## 4. The Answer to All Seven Oaths (91:9–10)

> *Indeed he succeeds who purifies it. And indeed he fails who Dassaha.*

Ibn Kathir gives **two complementary readings**, and endorses neither exclusively:

**1. The servant's act.** He succeeds who purifies his own soul through obedience to Allah — **Qatadah:** *"He cleanses it from the lowly and despicable characteristics."* (Similarly from Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr.)

**2. Allah's act.** He succeeds whose soul **Allah** purifies, and he fails whose soul Allah corrupts — reported by Al-Awfi and Ali bin Abi Talhah from Ibn Abbas.

**Dassaha** — literally *to conceal it, to bury it*. Ibn Kathir's gloss is precise and uncomfortable: he **makes it dull**, and disregards it by neglecting to let it receive guidance — treating it that way until he is committing acts of disobedience and has abandoned obedience altogether. It is not a single crime. It is a long process of covering something over.

### The two du'as attached to this verse

**At-Tabarani, from Ibn Abbas** — the Messenger of Allah ﷺ used to **stop** whenever he recited *By Nafs, and Ma Sawwaha. Then He showed it its Fujur and its Taqwa*, and say:

> **"O Allah! Give my soul its good. You are its Guardian and Master, and the best to purify it."**

**Ahmad, from Zayd bin Arqam** (also in Muslim) — the fuller version, which Zayd said the Prophet ﷺ taught them and which they in turn taught on:

> **"O Allah! I seek refuge with You from weakness, laziness, senility, cowardliness, stinginess and the torment of the grave.**
>
> **O Allah! Give my soul its Taqwa and purify it, for You are the best to purify it. You are its Guardian and Master.**
>
> **O Allah! I seek refuge with You from a heart that is not humble, a soul that is not satisfied, knowledge that does not benefit, and a supplication that is not answered."**

Note what the surah has done here. Verse 9 says *he succeeds who purifies it* — and the Prophet ﷺ, on reaching it, does not congratulate himself on the effort. **He asks.**

## 5. The Case Study: Thamud (91:11–15)

> *Thamud denied through their transgression. When their most wicked went forth.*

**Mujahid, Qatadah and others:** Thamud rejected their Messenger **because of** the injustice and transgression they were already practising. The order matters — the wrongdoing came first, and it produced the rejection of guidance in their hearts. Denial is often downstream of behaviour, not upstream of it.

**"Their most wicked"** — Qudar bin Salif, the man who hamstrung the she-camel, cross-referenced to *But they called their comrade and he took a sword and killed her* (54:29). Ibn Kathir stresses that he was no outcast: mighty, respected, of noble lineage, a leader who was obeyed. Ahmad records from Abdullah bin Zam'ah that the Prophet ﷺ described him in a sermon as:

> **"A strong and mighty man who was invincible among his tribe, like Abu Zam'ah, went forth to her."**

(Bukhari in his Book of Tafsir; Muslim in his Book of the Description of the Hellfire; Tirmidhi and Nasa'i in their Books of Tafsir.)

### The warning and the killing

*"Be cautious! That is the she-camel of Allah — and its drink!"* — beware of touching her with any harm, and do not transgress against her **on her drinking day**. She had her allotted day at the water; they had theirs. They knew the arrangement.

They denied him, and they killed her — a she-camel Allah had brought **out of solid rock** as a sign for them and a proof against them.

### Fasawwaha — the levelling

*So their Lord destroyed them because of their sin, Fasawwaha.* He became angry with them and annihilated them, **making the punishment descend upon them all equally.**

**Qatadah** explains why that was just:

> *"The leader of Thamud did not kill the she-camel until their youth, their elderly, their males and their females had all pledged allegiance to him. So when the people cooperated in killing her, Allah destroyed them all with the same punishment due to their sin."*

One hand held the blade. Every hand had signed for it.

### And He feared not the consequences (91:15)

Ibn Kathir notes an alternate recitation — *Fala yakhafu*, "So He feared not." Either way:

**Ibn Abbas:** *"Allah does not fear any consequences from anyone else."* (Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Bakr bin Abdullah Al-Muzani and others said the same.)

The surah that began by swearing on the sun ends by noting that the One who made it answers to no one.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Allah swore by everything you can see in order to make one point about the thing you cannot: your soul was delivered to you sound, balanced, and already knowing the difference between right and wrong.**

Both roads were shown to it (91:8). Nothing was hidden from you. Which means success is not about acquiring information — it is about **not burying** what you already have.

- **Zakkaha** is active: cleaning the soul of lowly characteristics through obedience.
- **Dassaha** is passive and slow: letting it go dull, neglecting it, declining to let guidance in — until disobedience is simply what you do.

Thamud is what *dassaha* looks like at national scale, and its lesson is that the crowd does not dilute responsibility. Everyone had pledged; everyone fell.

And the Prophet ﷺ, arriving at the verse about purifying the soul, stopped and asked Allah to do it:

> **"O Allah! Give my soul its Taqwa and purify it — for You are the best to purify it. You are its Guardian and Master."**

The effort is yours. The outcome is His. Both halves are in the surah, and the du'a is where they meet.
