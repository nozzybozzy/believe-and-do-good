*Revealed in Makkah · 5 ayat · "The Elephant" — named for the army that came to demolish the Ka'bah in the year the Prophet ﷺ was born*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Fil is five verses long and almost entirely made of **questions and short verbs**. *Have you not seen? Did He not make their plot go astray?* Then three quick strokes: He sent, they struck, He made them like chewed straw. The surah assumes you already know the story — it is not narrating history, it is **reminding a living audience of something that happened to their fathers, in their city, within memory.**

Ibn Kathir opens by naming what it is: **one of the favours Allah did for the Quraysh.** He saved them from an army that had come to pull the Ka'bah down stone by stone and erase every trace of it. Allah destroyed that army, defeated it, thwarted its plan, wasted its effort, and sent it home broken.

But the tafsir immediately complicates the picture in a way that is easy to miss, and it is the theological heart of the surah:

**The invaders were Christians. The defended city was pagan.** By religion, Abrahah's army was closer to the truth than the idol-worshipping Quraysh. So the victory was not a verdict on who was more righteous. Ibn Kathir puts the meaning into the mouth of destiny itself: *We are not helping you, O Quraysh, because you rank above the Abyssinians. We are defending the Ancient House — which We intend to honour by sending the unlettered Prophet, the last of them all.*

That is the frame for everything else. **The House was protected for the sake of what was about to be born in it**, in that same year, by the most widely held account.

---

## 1. The Text

> **105:1** Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with the Owners of the Elephant?
> **105:2** Did He not make their plot go astray?
> **105:3** And He sent against them birds, in flocks (Ababil).
> **105:4** Striking them with stones of Sijjil.
> **105:5** And He made them like `Asf, Ma'kul.

---

## 2. How the Abyssinians Came to Be in Yemen

The story starts one surah's-worth of history earlier, with the **People of the Ditch**.

- **Dhu Nuwas**, the last king of Himyar and a polytheist, ordered the burning of the People of the Ditch — some twenty thousand Christians. **One man escaped**: Daws Dhu Thalaban, who fled to Ash-Sham and sought the protection of Caesar.
- Caesar, being far away, wrote instead to **An-Najashi**, king of Abyssinia, whose lands were nearer. An-Najashi sent two commanders with a large army — **Aryat** and **Abrahah bin As-Sabah Abu Yaksum**.
- The army took Yemen house by house hunting for Dhu Nuwas, who **died by drowning in the sea**.

So Yemen fell under two Abyssinian governors who could not stand each other. Rather than let their armies bleed, they agreed to settle it in single combat, with a water channel behind each man so neither could run. **Aryat struck first**, splitting Abrahah's nose and mouth and slashing his face — which is why history knows him as **Al-Ashram**, the split-faced. But Abrahah's guard **Atawdah** killed Aryat, and Abrahah, wounded, healed and took sole command.

An-Najashi was furious, and swore he would **tread on Yemeni soil and cut off Abrahah's forelock**. Abrahah's answer was a masterpiece of flattery: gifts, treasures, **a sack of Yemeni earth for the king to walk on**, and **his own forelock hair, cut and sent**. Let the oath be fulfilled without the march. The king was pleased.

---

## 3. The Church, and the Insult

To secure his position further, Abrahah promised to build An-Najashi a church in Yemen unlike any before it. He raised it in San'a' — tall, ornate, decorated on every side. The Arabs called it **Al-Qullays**, from its height: look up at it and **your cap would fall off your head**.

Then he overreached. He decreed that the Arabs should make their **pilgrimage to his church instead of the Ka'bah**. The tribes of Adnan and Qahtan rejected it outright, and the Quraysh were enraged.

Ibn Kathir preserves two accounts of what happened next:

- A man of Quraysh entered the church at night, **relieved himself inside it**, and fled. Its custodians reported to Abrahah that this was Qurayshi anger over the House his church was meant to replace.
- **Muqatil bin Sulayman**: a group of Qurayshi youths got inside and **set a fire on a fiercely windy day**. The church burned and collapsed.

Either way, Abrahah swore an oath of his own: he would go to Makkah and **take the Ka'bah apart stone by stone**.

---

## 4. The March

He assembled an army large enough that nothing could stop it, and at its centre an enormous elephant named **Mahmud**, sent by An-Najashi specifically for this expedition — a beast the like of which had not been seen. Some reports add eight more elephants; others say twelve, plus Mahmud. **Allah knows best.**

The plan was mechanical and precise: **chain the pillars of the Ka'bah to the elephant's neck and have it pull the walls down all at once.**

Three encounters mark the road north, and each one shows the Arabs' calculation:

| Who | What they did | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Dhu Nafr**, noblest chief of Yemen | Called his people and any Arab who would answer, to fight in defence of the Sacred House | Defeated — *by Allah's will, and His intent to honour the Ka'bah* |
| **Nufayl bin Habib Al-Khath'ami**, with the Shahran and Nahis tribes | Fought Abrahah at Khath'am | Defeated and captured; Abrahah spared him and **made him his guide to the Hijaz** |
| **Thaqif**, at At-Ta'if | Did not fight — they **appeased** him, afraid for their own shrine, Al-Lat | Spared, and gave him a guide named **Abu Righal** |

Ibn Kathir's structure here is quiet but pointed: **every human attempt to defend the House failed.** The ones who fought lost. The ones who bargained kept their idol by helping the invader. Nothing was left between Abrahah and the Ka'bah.

Camping at **Al-Mughammas** near Makkah, he sent a raiding party under **Al-Aswad bin Mafsud** which seized the Makkans' grazing herds — including **two hundred camels belonging to Abdul-Muttalib**. Ibn Ishaq notes that Arab poets later satirised Al-Aswad for his part in it.

---

## 5. Abdul-Muttalib and Abrahah

Abrahah sent an emissary, **Hanatah Al-Himyari**, to summon the chief of Quraysh and deliver a message: *I have not come to fight you. I have come for the House. Do not stand in the way and no blood need be spilled.*

Hanatah was directed to **Abdul-Muttalib bin Hashim**, the Prophet's ﷺ grandfather, whose reply already contains the entire theology of the surah:

> We have no wish to fight him and no power to. This is the Sacred House of Allah, and the house of His Khalil, Ibrahim. If He wishes to stop him, it is His House and His to defend. And if He lets him reach it — by Allah, we have no means to keep him from it.

Then came the meeting. Abrahah was so struck by Abdul-Muttalib's bearing — a large, handsome man — that he **came down from his throne and sat with him on a carpet on the ground.** Through his translator he asked what he wanted.

**"I want the king to return my two hundred camels."**

Abrahah's reaction is the most famous exchange in the story. *I admired you when I saw you, and I think less of you now that you have spoken. You ask me about camels, and say nothing about a House that is the foundation of your religion and your fathers' religion, which I have come to destroy?*

Abdul-Muttalib answered:

> **I am the lord of the camels. The House has a Lord who will defend it.**

*"I cannot be prevented,"* said Abrahah. *"Then do so,"* he replied.

It is also reported that Arab chiefs with him offered Abrahah **a third of the wealth of Tihamah** to turn back. He refused — but he returned the camels.

Abdul-Muttalib then ordered Makkah **evacuated to the mountaintops**, took hold of the **metal ring on the door of the Ka'bah**, and prayed with a group of Quraysh:

> No man defends anything more fiercely than his own property. **O Lord, defend Your property.** Do not let their cross and their scheming overcome Your plan by morning.

**Muqatil bin Sulayman** adds a haunting detail: they deliberately left **a hundred camels tethered at the Ka'bah**, hoping some of the soldiers would steal them and so **draw Allah's vengeance down on themselves.**

Then they let go of the ring, and climbed.

---

## 6. The Elephant That Would Not Walk

In the morning Abrahah dressed Mahmud for the assault and turned the army toward the city. And **Nufayl bin Habib — the captured guide, the man leading them in — walked up to the elephant, took hold of its ear**, and said:

> **Kneel, Mahmud. Turn around and go straight back where you came from. You are in the Sacred City of Allah.**

He let go. The elephant knelt. Nufayl ran for the mountains.

What follows is one of the strangest passages in the tafsir, and its force is in the repetition. They beat the elephant. It refused. They struck its head with **axes**. It refused. They drove **hooked staffs** into it to lever it up. It refused.

Then:

- Turn it toward **Yemen** — it rose and walked briskly.
- Turn it toward **Ash-Sham** — the same.
- Turn it toward **the east** — the same.
- Turn it toward **Makkah** — it knelt.

The animal was not immobilised. It was **obedient in every direction except one.**

---

## 7. The Birds (105:3)

Then Allah sent the birds **from the sea**, like swallows and herons, **each carrying three stones the size of chickpeas and lentils** — one in each claw and one in its beak. Everyone struck was destroyed, **though not everyone was struck**.

Ibn Kathir gathers the scholars on the word **Ababil**, and they converge on plurality and succession rather than a single flock:

- **Abdullah (ibn Mas'ud) and Abu Salamah bin Abdur-Rahman**: in groups.
- **Ibn Abbas and Ad-Dahhak**: some following after others.
- **Al-Hasan Al-Basri and Qatadah**: many.
- **Mujahid**: various, successive groups.
- **Ibn Zayd**: different, coming from here and there — **they came at them from every direction.**
- **Ishaq bin Abdullah bin Al-Harith bin Nawfal**: in divisions, the way camels march in herds.
- **Al-Kasa'i** heard grammarians say the singular is *Ibil*; **Ibn Hisham** says the Arabs have no singular for it at all.

And on what they looked like — three descriptions, all with **authentic chains**:

- **Ibn Abbas**: snouts like birds' beaks, paws like dogs' paws.
- **Ikrimah**: green birds out of the sea, with heads like beasts of prey.
- **Ubayd bin Umayr**: black sea-birds carrying stones in beaks and claws.

---

## 8. Stones of Sijjil (105:4)

On the word itself, **Ibn Hisham** reports from Yunus An-Nahwi and Abu Ubaydah that in Arabic it means **something hard and solid**. Some commentators held it to be **two Persian words fused into one**: *Sanj* (stone) and *Jil* (clay) — the two materials the rocks were made of.

**Ubayd bin Umayr's** account of the strike itself is the fullest:

The birds came from the sea, three stones apiece — two in the feet, one in the beak. They **gathered in rows directly over the army's heads**, gave a single loud cry, and released. **No stone landed on a man's head without passing straight through him and out the other side**; none struck any part of the body without exiting the opposite side. Then Allah sent a **violent wind that struck the stones and multiplied their force**, and the destruction was total.

The army broke and fled along the road, **calling out for Nufayl** to show them the way home. Nufayl was on the mountaintop with the Quraysh, watching. He said:

> Where do you flee, when the One True God is the pursuer? **Al-Ashram is the defeated, not the victor.**

Ibn Ishaq records his longer verses too — a man thanking Allah at the sight of the birds, fearing the stones might fall on him as well, and remarking wryly that the whole army is now asking after him **as though he owed the Abyssinians a debt**.

---

## 9. Like Devoured Straw (105:5)

The final image is agricultural, and every explanation lands in the same place — **chewed-up plant waste**:

- **Sa'id bin Jubayr**: straw, what people call *Habbur*; elsewhere, **the leaves of wheat**. *Al-'Asf* is straw and *Al-Ma'kul* is **fodder cut for animals**. **Al-Hasan Al-Basri** said the same.
- **Ibn Abbas**: the husk of the grain, the covering of wheat.
- **Ibn Hisham**: *Al-'Asf* is the leaves of crops left ungathered.
- **Ibn Zayd**, most graphically: the leaves of vegetation — **after the cattle have eaten it and passed it out as dung.**

An army with elephants and chains and a king's ambition, reduced to **the part of the plant nobody keeps.**

---

## 10. How It Ended

Not everyone died at once. **Ata' bin Yasar** and others report that some were destroyed on the spot while **others came apart limb by limb as they fled**. Abrahah was of the second kind. **Ibn Ishaq**: they were struck down along every road and at every water source, and Abrahah's body rotted from the stones as his men carried him back to San'a', **falling to pieces as they went**. By the time they arrived he was like a baby bird — and he did not die until **his heart fell out of his chest.** *So they claim.*

The aftermath, briefly: his son **Yaksum** ruled after him, then Yaksum's brother **Masruq bin Abrahah**. Then **Sayf bin Dhi Yazan Al-Himyari** went to **Kisra**, the Persian king, who sent troops to help him drive the Abyssinians out. **Yemen was returned to the Arabs**, and delegations came to congratulate him.

---

## 11. Why It Sits Beside Surah Quraysh

Ibn Ishaq notes that when Muhammad ﷺ was given prophethood, **this was one of the favours he would recount to the Quraysh** — that Allah had turned back the Abyssinians so they could go on living safely in Makkah. Which is exactly why the next surah follows:

> *For the Ilaf of the Quraysh… So let them worship the Lord of this House, Who has fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear.* **(106:1–4)**

Ibn Kathir's gloss is generous and slightly sad: **Allah would not have changed their situation — He wanted good for them, if only they would accept Him.** Al-Fil is the safety. Quraysh is the bill for it.

---

## 12. The Elephant, Twice More, in the Prophet's ﷺ Life

The surah's central image returns at two of the most important moments of the Sirah.

**At Hudaybiyyah**, the Prophet's ﷺ she-camel **Al-Qaswa'** knelt on the road to the Quraysh and would not stand. The people said she had turned stubborn. He answered:

> Al-Qaswa' has not turned stubborn — that is not in her nature. **She has been stopped by the One who restrained the Elephant.**

And then, in the same breath, the treaty terms: he swore that the Quraysh would not ask him for **anything honouring the sacred things of Allah** without his agreeing to it. Then he signalled the camel, and she rose. *(Recorded by Al-Bukhari alone.)*

**On the day Makkah was conquered** (in the Two Sahihs):

> Allah restrained the Elephant from Makkah, and gave His Messenger and the believers authority over it. **Its sacredness has returned exactly as it was sacred yesterday.** Let whoever is present inform whoever is absent.

The same city, the same protection, the opposite verdict — because now the man entering it comes **on Allah's terms, not against them.**

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Abdul-Muttalib's line is the surah in seven words: *I am the lord of the camels; the House has a Lord who will defend it.*** He was not being flippant about the Ka'bah. He was being exact about jurisdiction — asking for what was his to ask for, and leaving what was Allah's to Allah.

Everything the surah shows follows from that division of labour. **Every human defence of the House failed**: Dhu Nafr fought and lost, Nufayl fought and was captured, Thaqif surrendered and bought safety, the Quraysh withdrew to the hills and left the door unguarded. Then the defence arrived — **not an army, but birds; not swords, but pebbles the size of lentils.** The disproportion is the message. Allah did not need a fair fight.

And note the timing. This was **the year the Prophet ﷺ was born.** The House was not saved because its custodians deserved it — they were idolaters, and their attackers were Christians. It was saved because of **what Allah had already decided to do with it**. Protection sometimes arrives for reasons that have nothing to do with the one being protected, and only become visible later.

The plot was *made to go astray* before it was ever punished. Abrahah's elephant knelt before a single stone fell. **The scheme was already ruined while it still looked unstoppable.**
