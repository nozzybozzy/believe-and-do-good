*Revealed in Makkah · 26 ayat · "The Overwhelming" — one of the names of the Day of Judgement*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Ghashiyah opens with a question that is really a summons: *Has there come to you the narration of Al-Ghashiyah?* Ibn Kathir explains the name plainly — it is called this **because it will overwhelm the people and overcome them**. Nobody stands outside it.

The surah then does three things in sequence, and the order is the argument.

First it shows you **two faces**. Faces that are humiliated on that Day, and faces that are joyful. Between them sits the most disturbing verse in the surah — *laboring, weary* — describing people who **worked hard and got nothing for it**. Not the idle. The exhausted.

Second, having described a destination, it turns to **evidence**, and the evidence is deliberately unimpressive: a camel, the sky, a mountain, the ground. No cosmic spectacle. Just the four things a Bedouin can see without turning his head — the animal under him, the sky over him, the mountain in front of him, the earth beneath him. Ibn Kathir's point is that the proof was never hidden; it was only unexamined.

Third, it releases the Prophet ﷺ from the outcome: *you are only one who reminds; you are not a Musaytir over them.* He cannot manufacture faith in anyone's chest. That belongs to Allah — and so does the reckoning, which is the surah's final word.

---

## 1. Its Place in the Friday and `Id Prayers

This is the surah the Prophet ﷺ used in the largest gatherings of the year.

- **From An-Nu`man bin Bashir:** he used to recite **Al-A`la (87)** and **Al-Ghashiyah (88)** in the `Id and Friday prayers.
- **Imam Malik** records that Ad-Dahhak bin Qays asked An-Nu`man directly: *what else did he recite on Friday, alongside Surah Al-Jumu`ah?* An-Nu`man answered with one word: **Al-Ghashiyah**.

The narration is also in Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i, Muslim and Ibn Majah.

Worth noticing: this is a surah about the Day of Judgement, chosen weekly for the sermon congregation, and paired with a surah about purification and the Hereafter. The pairing is not decorative.

---

## 2. The First Face: Humiliated (88:2)

> *Some faces that Day will be Khashi`ah.*

- **Qatadah:** humiliated.
- **Ibn `Abbas:** *"They will be humble — but this action will be of no benefit to them."*

Ibn `Abbas's gloss is the sharp one. Humility arrives at last, and arrives too late to count. The posture is right; the moment for it has passed.

---

## 3. The Hardest Verse: Laboring, Weary (88:3)

> ***`Amilatun Nasibah*** — *Laboring, weary.*

Ibn Kathir's explanation: **they did many deeds and became weary in performing them — and will still be cast into a blazing Fire.** Effort without acceptance. Religion without truth.

### `Umar and the monk

Al-Hafiz Abu Bakr Al-Burqani narrates from Abu `Imran Al-Jawni: `Umar bin Al-Khattab passed by a monk's monastery and called out to him. The monk came out. `Umar looked at him — and **wept**.

He was asked why. He said:

> *"I remembered the statement of Allah in His Book — **`Amilatun Nasibah**, laboring, weary — **They will enter into Fire, Hamiyah**. So that is what has made me cry."*

He is not weeping at the man's sin. He is weeping at his **discipline**: a life of visible austerity, and the verse says it may lead nowhere.

### Who is meant

- **Al-Bukhari, from Ibn `Abbas:** *"The Christians."*
- **`Ikrimah and As-Suddi:** labouring in this world **in disobedience**, and wearied in the Fire by torment and destruction — i.e. the labour is in this life, the weariness in the next.

Ibn Kathir preserves both readings without collapsing them. Either way the warning lands in the same place: **exertion is not evidence.** The question is what you exerted yourself for.

## 4. The Food and Drink of the Fire (88:4–7)

| Ayah | Term | Ibn Kathir's gloss |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | Fire, **Hamiyah** | Hot with **intense** heat — Ibn `Abbas, Al-Hasan, Qatadah |
| **5** | An **Aniyah** spring | Its heat has reached **its maximum limit and boiling point** — Ibn `Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, As-Suddi |
| **6** | **Dari`** | Their only food |
| **7** | Neither nourishes nor avails against hunger | It fails at both things food exists to do |

### On Dari`

The early authorities are unusually specific, and it reads like botany:

- **Ibn `Abbas** (via `Ali bin Abi Talhah): *"A tree from the Hellfire."*
- **Ibn `Abbas, Mujahid, `Ikrimah, Abu Al-Jawza' and Qatadah:** it is **Ash-Shibriq**, a plant.
- **Qatadah:** the Quraysh called it *Ash-Shabraq* in spring and *Ad-Dari`* in summer.
- **`Ikrimah:** a **thorny** tree that reaches down along the ground.
- **Al-Bukhari, from Mujahid:** the people of the Hijaz call it Ad-Dari` **when it has dried out — and it is poisonous.**
- **Qatadah** again, via Sa`id: *"the worst, most disgusting and loathsome of foods."*

The detail matters because it is drawn from what the Arabs already knew. This is not an exotic torment; it is the dry, thorny, useless scrub they walked past every summer — the one thing even livestock will not eat.

Then verse 7 closes the trap. Ibn Kathir: **the intent in eating it will not be achieved, and nothing harmful will be repelled by it.** Food has two purposes — to nourish and to stop hunger. This does neither. It is eating that accomplishes nothing at all.

## 5. The Second Face: Joyful (88:8–9)

> *Faces that Day will be joyful, glad with their endeavour.*

Ibn Kathir: **pleasure will be noticeable in them** — *and this will only occur due to their striving.*

**Sufyan:** *"They will be pleased with their deeds."*

Set this beside verse 3 and the surah's spine appears. Both groups **worked**. One group is wearied by its labour; the other is *pleased* with it. The difference is not quantity of effort. It is whether the effort was owed to the right One.

---

## 6. Paradise, Furnished (88:10–16)

Ibn Kathir works through it item by item, and the striking thing is how **domestic** the imagery is — not thrones of power but a room made comfortable.

**A lofty Paradise (10)** — elevated and brilliant, and its people are **secure in their dwellings**.

**No Laghw heard therein (11)** — no foolish or worthless speech. Ibn Kathir stacks three parallel verses:
- *They shall not hear therein any Laghw, but only Salam* (19:62)
- *Free from any Laghw, and free from sin* (52:23)
- *No Laghw will they hear therein, nor any sinful speech. But only the saying of: "Salam! Salam!"* (56:25–26)

A place defined partly by **what is absent from the conversation**. No idle talk, no lie, no sin in anything said.

**A running spring (12)** — flowing freely. Ibn Kathir makes a grammatical point with a spiritual payoff: the singular is **for emphasis, not limitation**. It does not mean there is only one spring; it refers to springs *collectively*. Paradise has flowing springs.

> **Ibn Abi Hatim, from Abu Hurayrah**, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: *the rivers of Paradise spring forth from beneath hills — or mountains — of **musk**.*

**Thrones raised high (13)** — lofty, numerous couches with elevated ceilings. And a detail Ibn Kathir passes on: **whenever the friend of Allah wishes to sit, the thrones lower themselves for him.** Elevation that bends to serve you rather than making you climb.

**Cups set at hand (14)** — drinking vessels already **prepared and presented**, waiting for whoever wants them. Nothing has to be asked for.

**Namariq set in rows (15)** — *"An-Namariq are pillows."* (Ibn `Abbas, and likewise `Ikrimah, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi, Ath-Thawri.)

**Zarabi, Mabthuthah (16)** — *"Az-Zarabi are carpets."* (Ibn `Abbas, Ad-Dahhak.) **Mabthuthah** means **spread here and there** for whoever would like to sit on them.

Read together: cushions, carpets, cups already poured, seats that come down to you, and no stupid conversation. It is the picture of a house where nothing is left for you to arrange.

## 7. The Four Things in Front of You (88:17–20)

> *Do they not look at the camels, how they are created? And at the heaven, how it is raised? And at the mountains, how they are rooted? And at the earth, how it is outspread?*

**The camel** gets the longest treatment, and Ibn Kathir's admiration is unguarded. It is *an amazing creation, strangely fashioned* — **extremely powerful and strong, yet gentle**. It carries heavy loads and allows itself to be led by a **weak rider**. It is eaten; its hair is used; its milk is drunk. And it is named first for a simple reason: it was **the most common domestic animal of the Arabs**. The proof was tied up outside the tent.

**The heaven** — Shurayh Al-Qadi used to say: *"Come out with us, so that we may look at the camels and how they were created, and at the sky and how it has been raised."* Cross-referenced to **50:6**: *Have they not looked at the heaven above them, how We have made it and adorned it, and there are no rifts in it?*

**The mountains** — *how they are rooted*: erected and firmly affixed **so that the earth does not sway with its dwellers**, and made to contain benefits and minerals.

**The earth** — spread out, extended, made smooth.

Ibn Kathir then names what he is doing:

> He directs the Bedouin to consider **what he himself witnesses** — the camel he rides, the sky above his head, the mountain that faces him, the earth under him. All of it is proof of the power of the Creator. These things should lead him to see that He is the Lord, the Most Great, the Owner and Controller of everything — and therefore **the God other than Whom none deserves to be worshipped**.

The argument runs from *creation* to *ownership* to *worship*. Nothing supernatural is required at any step.

## 8. The Story of Dimam bin Tha`labah

Ibn Kathir attaches a long narration here, because these are **the very things Dimam swore by**. Imam Ahmad, from Thabit, from Anas:

> *"We were prohibited from asking the Messenger of Allah ﷺ anything. So it used to amaze us when an intelligent man from the desert people would come and ask him while we listened."*

A man from the desert came and said: *O Muhammad — your messenger came to us and claims that you claim Allah sent you.*
The Prophet ﷺ: **"He told the truth."**

Then the questioning:

- *Who created the heaven?* — **"Allah."**
- *Who created the earth?* — **"Allah."**
- *Who erected these mountains and placed in them what is in them?* — **"Allah."**
- *By the One who created the heaven, the earth, and erected these mountains — did Allah send you?* — **"Yes."**

He then put the same oath behind each obligation in turn — the five daily prayers, the Zakah on wealth, the Hajj to the House for whoever can find a way — and each time: *"He told the truth."* … *"By He who has sent you, did Allah command you with this?"* … **"Yes."**

Then the man turned to leave, saying:

> *"By He who has sent you with the truth, **I will not add anything to these things, and I will not decrease anything from them.**"*

The Prophet ﷺ said:

> **"If he has spoken truthfully, he will certainly enter Paradise."**

Recorded by Al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah.

Note what Islam looked like at that moment: **four questions about creation, four obligations, and a refusal to add or subtract.** Nothing else.

## 9. The Messenger's Job — and Its Limit (88:21–22)

> *So remind them — you are only one who reminds. You are not a **Musaytir** over them.*

Ibn Kathir: *O Muhammad, remind the people with what you have been sent with.* Cross-referenced to **13:40** — *your duty is only to convey the Message, and on Us is the reckoning.*

On **Musaytir**:

- **Ibn `Abbas and Mujahid:** *"You are not a dictator over them."* Ibn Kathir's clarification is precise — **you cannot create faith in their hearts.**
- **Ibn Zayd:** *"You are not the one who can force them to have faith."*

### The hadith the Prophet ﷺ attached to this verse

**Imam Ahmad, from Jabir** — the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> *"I have been commanded to fight the people until they say **La ilaha illallah**. So if they say that, they have safeguarded their blood and their wealth from me — except for what is rightfully due from it — and **their reckoning is with Allah**."*

And then **he recited 88:21–22**.

This is Ibn Kathir's most important placement in the surah. The hadith is about an outward limit — what a state may compel is the public profession, no more. The verse he recites immediately afterwards is about an **inward** one: *you are not a dictator over them.* The two together mark the boundary precisely. **Conduct can be governed. Belief cannot be installed.** Muslim records it in his Book of Faith; At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i in their Books of Tafsir; it is found in both Sahihs.

---

## 10. The Exception, and the Return (88:23–26)

> *Save the one who turns away and disbelieves. Then Allah will punish him with the greatest punishment.*

Ibn Kathir: he **turns away from acting upon its pillars**, and **disbelieves in the truth with his heart and his tongue** — action and creed both refused. Compare **75:31–32**: *So he neither believed nor prayed! But on the contrary, he belied and turned away.*

Then the two closing verses, which hand everything back:

> *Verily, to Us will be their return* — Ibn Kathir: **their place of return and their resort.**
>
> *Then verily, for Us will be their reckoning* — *"We will reckon their deeds for them and requite them for those deeds."*

And his final sentence on the surah:

> **If they did good, they will receive good; and if they did evil, they will receive evil.**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Effort is not evidence.** The most frightening two words in this surah are *laboring, weary* — describing people who exhausted themselves in worship and arrived at the Fire. `Umar wept at a monk not because the man was lazy but because he was **disciplined**. The joyful faces in verse 9 are also described by their striving. Both groups worked. What separates them is not how hard, but **for Whom, and on what terms.**

**The proof was never far away.** A camel, the sky, a mountain, the ground. Ibn Kathir insists the Bedouin needed nothing he did not already have in his line of sight. Whatever is missing in a person's faith, it is not usually information.

**And no one can be forced into it.** *You are not a Musaytir over them* — you cannot put faith inside another chest, and you were never asked to. Remind, convey, and leave the reckoning where the surah leaves it: **to Us will be their return; for Us will be their reckoning.**
