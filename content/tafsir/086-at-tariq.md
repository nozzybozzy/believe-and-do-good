*Revealed in Makkah · 17 ayat · "The Night-Comer" — the one who arrives after dark, named in its first verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

At-Tariq is built out of two oaths, and each one is answered by a single hard sentence.

The **first oath** is on the night sky and the star that pierces it — and the answer is that **you are being watched**: *there is no human being but has a protector over him.* The surah then asks you to look at the least impressive fact about yourself, the fluid you came from, and draws the conclusion the Makkans kept refusing: the One who started that can do it again.

The **second oath** is on the sky that sends rain back year after year and the earth that splits open to let plants through — and the answer is that **this Qur'an is a decisive word, not entertainment**. Both oath-objects are things that *return*. Water goes up and comes back down; seed goes into the ground and comes back up. The surah is swearing by return in order to establish return.

Between the two halves sits its central image: **a Day when the secrets are examined.** Not the deeds — the secrets. What was concealed becomes the exhibit.

And it closes on the calmest verse in the surah. The disbelievers are plotting; Allah says He is planning too; and the Prophet ﷺ is told simply to **wait a little.** No threat is spelled out. The waiting is the threat.

There is a quiet fitness in the name. *Tariq* is the one who knocks at night, and Ibn Kathir cites the hadith warning a man against returning to his family **Taruq** — arriving unannounced in the dark. A surah about being watched, about secrets opened, and about a punishment that comes when it comes, is named after the unexpected knock.

## 1. Its Virtues

**An-Nasa'i, from Jabir:** Mu'adh led the Maghrib prayer and recited **Al-Baqarah** and **An-Nisa'**. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Are you putting the people to trial, O Mu'adh! Was it not sufficient for you to recite **As-Sama'i wat-Tariq** (86) and **Ash-Shamsi wa Duhaha** (91), and something like them?"*

The rebuke is not against long recitation as such — it is against imposing it on a congregation. And it doubles as a recommendation: this is a surah the Prophet ﷺ named as fit for leading people in prayer.

---

## 2. The Oath: The Star That Pierces the Dark (86:1–3)

> *By the heaven, and At-Tariq; And what will make you know what At-Tariq is? The star, Ath-Thaqib.*

Allah swears by the sky and by the radiant stars He placed in it. The construction is one the Qur'an uses for weight: a name is given, the listener is told he does not know what it means, and only then is it explained.

**Why "Tariq"?** — **Qatadah** and others: *the star was named Tariq because it is only seen at night and is hidden during the day.* Ibn Kathir supports this from language rather than astronomy: the authentic hadith that prohibits a man coming to his family **Taruq**, i.e. **unexpectedly, at night**. The root is arrival after dark.

**What is "Ath-Thaqib"?**
- **Ibn Abbas:** the illuminating.
- **Ikrimah:** it illuminates — **and it burns the Shaytan.**

---

## 3. No One Is Unwatched (86:4)

> *There is no human being but has a protector over him.*

Ibn Kathir's reading: **every soul has a guardian appointed by Allah, protecting it from calamities.** The verse is placed immediately after the oath on the night sky, so the sequence is: *the dark is not empty, and neither are you alone in it.*

He anchors it in Surah Ar-Ra'd:

> *For Him, there are angels in succession, before and behind him. They guard him by the command of Allah.* **(13:11)**

Note what this verse is **not** yet saying. Here the watcher is a *protector*. Five verses later, secrets will be examined. The same attendance that shields you is the attendance that knows.

---

## 4. Look at What You Came From (86:5–8)

> *So, let man see from what he is created!*

This is a command to inspect your own origin, and Ibn Kathir states the purpose plainly: **it alerts man to the weakness of the thing he was made from, in order to bring him to accept the Hereafter** — because *whoever is able to begin the creation is able to repeat it in the same way.*

> *And He it is Who originates the creation, then He will repeat it; and this is easier for Him.* **(30:27)**

**"A water gushing forth" (86:6)** — the sexual fluid emitted by the man and the woman; from both of them the child is produced, by Allah's permission.

**"From between the backbone and the ribs" (86:7)** — the **backbone (loins) of the man** and the **ribs of the woman**, meaning her chest.

**Shabib bin Bishr ← Ikrimah ← Ibn Abbas**, on this verse: the backbone of the man and the ribs of the woman; the fluid is **yellow and fine in texture**, and *the child will not be born except from both of them.*

**"Verily, He is Able to bring him back!" (86:8)** — able to return this man who was made from a gush of fluid: to repeat his creation and raise him for the final abode. Ibn Kathir notes that Allah advances this same proof — *He who began it can repeat it* — in more than one place in the Qur'an.

---

## 5. The Day the Secrets Are Tested (86:9–10)

> *The Day when all the secrets will be examined.*

Ibn Kathir: on the Day of Judgement the secrets are **tested** — meaning **exposed and made manifest**. What was hidden becomes open; what was concealed becomes well known.

The hadith he brings is precisely about hidden things being labelled in public. **Ibn Umar**, in the Two Sahihs, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> *"Every betrayer will have a flag raised for him behind his back, and it will be said: 'This is the betrayal of so-and-so, the son of so-and-so.'"*

Then the two words that finish the scene:

- **"no power"** — Ibn Kathir: *within himself.*
- **"nor any helper"** — *from other than himself.*

Between them they close every exit: **he cannot save himself from the torment of Allah, and no one else can save him either.**

---

## 6. The Sky That Returns, the Earth That Splits (86:11–14)

> *By the sky which gives rain, again and again. And the earth which splits.*

**Ar-Raj'** (literally "the return"):
- **Ibn Abbas:** it means **rain**. Also narrated from him: **the clouds that hold the rain**. And again: *it rains, and then it rains again.*
- **Qatadah:** it **returns the sustenance of the servants every year** — were it not for this, they and their cattle would all be destroyed.

**The earth "which splits" (As-Sad')** — **Ibn Abbas:** splitting open **to bring forth plant growth**. Ibn Kathir lists the agreement: Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ikrimah, Abu Malik, Ad-Dahhak, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others.

The oath lands on the Qur'an itself:

> *Verily, this is the Word that separates.*

- **Ibn Abbas** on *Fasl*: **"True."** **Qatadah** said the same.
- Another view: **"a just ruling."**

> *And it is not a thing for amusement.* — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, **rather it is serious and true.**

---

## 7. Two Plans, and an Instruction to Wait (86:15–17)

> *Verily, they are but plotting a plot. And I am planning a plan.*

Their plot is defined narrowly and accurately: **they scheme against the people, calling them to oppose the Qur'an.** The reply is left deliberately bare — Allah plans too, and no detail is offered.

> *So, give a respite to the disbelievers; deal gently with them for a while.*

- **"give a respite"** — *wait for them; do not be in haste concerning them.*
- **"for a while"** — *a little while.*

Ibn Kathir supplies what the verse withholds: **you will see what befalls them of torment, punishment and destruction.**

> *We let them enjoy for a little while, then in the end We shall oblige them to (enter) a great torment.* **(31:24)**

*And with that the tafsir of Surah At-Tariq ends — "and unto Allah is all praise and thanks."*

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**A guard is standing over you, and He who made you out of a gush of water can make you again.**

At-Tariq argues resurrection from the humblest possible starting point. It does not point to the mountains or the seven heavens. It points at the fluid you began as — *let man see from what he is created* — and says: that was the hard part, and it is already done.

Everything else in the surah follows from that. If you can be remade, you can be judged; if you can be judged, the secrets matter more than the surface; and if the secrets are what will be examined, then the watcher appointed over you is not an intrusion but a witness you have had all along.

And the surah's final word to anyone waiting for justice that has not arrived is not *fight* or *despair.* It is: **wait a little.**
