*Revealed in Makkah · 4 ayat · "Sincerity" / "Purity of Faith" — the shortest surah to be called a third of the Qur'an*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Ikhlas is **an answer to a question about Allah's lineage** — and the answer refuses the question's premise entirely.

The idolaters asked the Prophet ﷺ to describe his Lord's ancestry, the way one describes a tribe: who is He descended from, who came out of Him. Four verses came back, and each one closes a door. **He is One** — not a member of a category. **He is As-Samad** — the One everything else needs, who needs nothing. **He did not father and was not fathered** — no line runs into Him or out of Him. **Nothing is comparable to Him** — there is no second item to place beside Him for measurement.

Note the shape: two verses of affirmation, then two of negation. The surah tells you what Allah is, then removes everything that could be smuggled in alongside it. That is why it is called *Al-Ikhlas* — the making-pure — and why Ibn Kathir's commentary keeps returning to the same point in different words: **He has no coequal, and nothing is like Him.**

---

## 1. Why It Was Revealed

**Imam Ahmad, from Ubayy bin Ka'b:** the idolaters said to the Prophet ﷺ, *"O Muhammad! Tell us the lineage of your Lord."* So Allah revealed the surah.

At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Jarir record the same, with an addition that unlocks the second verse:

> **As-Samad** is the One Who does not give birth nor was born — because nothing is born except that it will die, and nothing dies except that it leaves behind an inheritance. And Allah does not die, and He leaves behind no inheritance.

That is the whole argument in a sentence: **birth implies death, and death implies succession.** A God with a lineage is a God who can be inherited from.

**Ikrimah** gives a wider setting. The Jews said, *we worship Uzayr, the son of Allah*; the Christians said, *we worship the Messiah, the son of Allah*; the Zoroastrians said, *we worship the sun and the moon*; the idolaters said, *we worship idols.* Against all four at once, Allah revealed: **Say: He is Allah, One.**

## 2. Its Virtues

This is where Ibn Kathir spends most of his space — the commentary on a four-verse surah is mostly a catalogue of what reciting it does.

**Equivalent to a third of the Qur'an.**
- **Al-Bukhari, from Abu Sa'id:** a man heard another repeating the surah over and over through the night, and mentioned it to the Prophet ﷺ the next morning *as though he were belittling it*. The reply: *"By He in Whose Hand is my soul, it is equivalent to a third of the Qur'an."*
- **Al-Bukhari, also from Abu Sa'id:** the Prophet ﷺ asked his Companions, *"Is one of you not able to recite a third of the Qur'an in a single night?"* They found this hard and asked who could manage it. He answered: *"Allah is the One, As-Samad" is a third of the Qur'an.*

**Loving it earns Allah's love.**
**Al-Bukhari, from Aishah** (via Amrah bint Abdur-Rahman): a man leading a war expedition ended every recitation in prayer with this surah. Asked why, he said: *because it is the description of Ar-Rahman, and I love to recite it.* The Prophet ﷺ said, *"Inform him that Allah the Most High loves him."*

**Loving it earns Paradise.**
**Al-Bukhari, from Anas:** an Ansari leading prayer at the Masjid of Quba' began every rak'ah with this surah, then added another after it. His congregation objected — either recite it alone or replace it. He refused to drop it, offering instead to stop leading them altogether; they wanted no one else. Asked by the Prophet ﷺ what made him hold to it, he said simply, *"I love it."* The reply: **"Your love of it will cause you to enter Paradise."**

**Its recitation obligates Paradise.**
**Imam Malik, from Abu Hurayrah:** walking with the Prophet ﷺ, they heard a man reciting it. He said, *"It is obligatory."* Abu Hurayrah asked what was obligatory. He answered: **"Paradise."**

**It suffices, morning and evening.**
**From Abdullah bin Khubayb:** on a dark, thirsty night the Prophet ﷺ took him by the hand and said only, *"Say."* Then, again, *"Say."* Asked what to say, he replied: recite this surah and the two surahs of refuge (Al-Falaq and An-Nas) three times each, morning and evening — *"they will be sufficient for you."* Another chain has: **"They will suffice you against everything."**

**It contains the Greatest Name.**
**An-Nasa'i, from Buraydah:** a man was heard supplicating — *You are the One, the Self-Sufficient Sustainer of all, Who does not give birth, nor were You born, and there is none comparable to Him.* The Prophet ﷺ said he had supplicated with **Ism al-A'zam**, the Greatest Name of Allah, and that supplication made with it is answered.

**It is a cure.**
**Al-Bukhari, from Aishah:** every night before bed the Prophet ﷺ would cup his palms, blow into them, recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas into them, then wipe what he could reach of his body — beginning with his head and face and the front of his body — **three times.**

## 3. The Four Verses

**112:1 — *Say: He is Allah, One.***

Ibn Kathir: He is the One, the Singular — **who has no peer, no assistant, no rival, no equal, and none comparable to Him.** He notes a point of language: *Al-Ahad* cannot be used affirmatively of anyone but Allah, *because He is perfect in all of His attributes and actions.*

**112:2 — *Allah As-Samad.***

Three readings, each adding a layer:

| Source | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ibn Abbas** (via Ikrimah) | The One Whom all of creation depends upon for their needs and their requests |
| **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah) | The Master perfect in sovereignty, the Most Noble perfect in nobility, the Magnificent perfect in magnificence, the Forbearing perfect in forbearance, the All-Knowing perfect in knowledge, the Most Wise perfect in wisdom — perfect in every aspect of nobility and authority |
| **Abu Wa'il** (via Al-A'mash ← Shaqiq) | The Master **Whose control is complete** |

Ibn Abbas closes his own explanation the way the surah does: *these attributes are not befitting anyone other than Him. He has no coequal and nothing is like Him.*

**112:3 — *He begets not, nor was He begotten.***

Ibn Kathir's gloss is three words wide and closes three doors at once: **He has no child, no parent, and no spouse.**

**112:4 — *And there is none comparable to Him.***

**Mujahid:** this means He has no spouse. Ibn Kathir supports it with 6:101 — *He is the Originator of the heavens and the earth. How can He have children when He has no wife? He created all things.* His comment: **He owns everything and created everything — so how could He have, among His own creatures, a peer equal to Him or a relative resembling Him?**

## 4. Allah Is Above Children and Procreation

Ibn Kathir then stacks the Qur'an's harshest language on this one error:

- **19:88–95** — *And they say: Ar-Rahman has begotten a son.* Whereby **the heavens are almost torn, the earth is split asunder, and the mountains fall in ruins.** There is none in the heavens and the earth but comes to Ar-Rahman **as a slave** — each one known, counted in a full counting, and coming alone on the Day of Resurrection.
- **21:26–27** — the angels are honoured servants who *speak not until He has spoken, and act on His command.*
- **37:158–159** — they invented a kinship between Him and the Jinn, *but the Jinn know well that they have indeed to appear before Him.*

Two hadiths in Al-Bukhari close the surah, and they are the most striking passages here:

> **"There is no one more patient with something harmful that he hears than Allah. They attribute a son to Him, while it is He Who gives them sustenance and cures them."**

And, from Abu Hurayrah, Allah says:

> *"The Son of Adam denies Me, and he has no right to do so; and he abuses Me, and he has no right to do so.*
>
> *His denial of Me is his saying: 'He will never re-create me as He created me before' — but the re-creation of him is easier than his original creation.*
>
> *His cursing of Me is his saying: 'Allah has taken a son.'*
>
> ***But I am the One, the Self-Sufficient Master. I do not give birth, nor was I born, and there is none comparable to Me."***

Note what has happened: the surah's own four verses have become Allah's answer to the two great insults — **denying the resurrection, and giving Him a child.**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

The idolaters asked for a **genealogy**, and were given a **definition** — one that makes genealogy impossible. Nothing came before Him to produce Him; nothing comes after Him to inherit from Him; nothing stands beside Him to be measured against Him. Everything else in existence *needs*; **As-Samad** is the one thing that is needed and needs nothing.

And the surah is treated as a third of the Qur'an because it is the whole of one of the Qur'an's three subjects: **who Allah is.** Which is why the man who could not stop reciting it — *"Verily, I love it"* — was told his love of it would carry him into Paradise. Ibn Kathir's structure makes the point quietly: a surah you can say in eight seconds, guarded by more narrations of virtue than almost any other in the Book.
