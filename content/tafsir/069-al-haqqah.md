*Revealed in Makkah · 52 ayat · "The Reality" — one of the names of the Day of Judgement*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Haqqah is built like a legal case, and it is worth reading it that way.

It opens by naming the Day three times without describing it — *Al-Haqqah! What is Al-Haqqah? And what will make you know what Al-Haqqah is?* — a name held up before the eyes until the question becomes uncomfortable. Then, instead of describing the Day, it does something cleverer: it produces the **historical record**. Thamud, 'Ad, Fir'awn, the overturned cities, the people of Nuh. Five nations who were told a threat was coming and who found out that it was. The argument is not *believe the warning* but **the warning has already been demonstrated, repeatedly, in your own geography**.

Only then does the surah open onto the Day itself — the Trumpet, the earth crushed flat, the sky torn, eight angels bearing the Throne — and sort humanity into **two hands**: the man who cannot stop showing people his record, and the man who wishes death were permanent.

And then, in its final movement, it turns to face the obvious objection: *who says?* The last fifteen verses are a sworn defence of the credentials of the one who brought the report — **not a poet, not a soothsayer, and if he had invented a word of it, his aorta would have been cut**.

The surah opens with *Al-Haqqah*, the Reality, and closes with *haqq al-yaqin*, absolute truth with certainty. Everything between the two is the working.

---

## 1. The Name of the Day (69:1–3)

> *Al-Haqqah! What is Al-Haqqah? And what will make you know what Al-Haqqah is?*

**Al-Haqqah is one of the names of the Day of Judgement** — so called, Ibn Kathir explains, **because during it the promise and the threat will inevitably occur**. It is the Day on which everything that was said would happen, *happens*. It is the moment the words become real.

The tripled question is not rhetorical padding. Ibn Kathir reads it as Allah **declaring the greatness of the matter**: the Day is being placed beyond the reach of description, and the reader is being told so directly.

---

## 2. Thamud: Destroyed by the Taghiyah (69:4–5)

> *Thamud and 'Ad denied the Qari'ah! As for Thamud, they were destroyed by the Taghiyah!*

Two readings of **Taghiyah**, and Ibn Kathir preserves both:

- **The event.** It is *the cry which will silence them, and the quake that will silence them.* **Qatadah**: At-Taghiyah is the shout.
- **The cause.** **Mujahid**: At-Taghiyah means *the sins*. **Ar-Rabi' bin Anas** and **Ibn Zayd**: it means **transgression** — and Ibn Zayd recited 69:4 itself as his proof, *Thamud denied through their transgression*.

Read the second way, the verse says something sharp: they were not destroyed *by* something external. They were destroyed **by their own excess**. The instrument and the offence carry the same name.

---

## 3. 'Ad: Seven Nights and Eight Days (69:6–8)

> *And as for 'Ad, they were destroyed by a wind, Sarsar 'Atiyah!*

**Sarsar** — a **cold** wind (Ad-Dahhak). **'Atiyah** — severe, violent blowing (Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas, Ath-Thawri); Ad-Dahhak adds the crucial nuance: *it blew fiercely upon them **without any mercy or blessing***. Wind normally carries rain and pollination. This one carried nothing.

Two images preserved:

- **Qatadah**: *it blew fiercely upon them until it pierced their hearts.*
- **Ali** and others: *it blew upon their stored harvest until it was brought out worthless.*

**Husum** — the seven nights and eight days — means, per **Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah** and **Ath-Thawri**, *in succession*: continuous, unbroken, no reprieve. **Ikrimah** and **Ar-Rabi' bin Khuthaym**: *unfortunately evil upon them*. Compare *in days of calamity* (41:16).

**Khawiyah** — the toppled bodies like hollow palm trunks. **Ibn Abbas**: ruined. Others: dilapidated. Ibn Kathir's reconstruction is bleak and physical: the wind would drive a man head-first into the ground, his skull would shatter, and he would remain **a lifeless corpse as if it were a palm trunk without branches, motionless**.

And in the Two Sahihs, the Prophet ﷺ:

> *I was helped by an easterly wind, and the people of 'Ad were destroyed by a westerly wind.*

The same element. Two entirely different outcomes.

> *Do you see any remnants of them?* (69:8)

Ibn Kathir: *do you find any one of them left, or anyone who even attributes himself to being from them?* **They are all gone, right down to the last of them, and Allah did not make for them any successors.** Not even a claimed lineage survived.

---

## 4. Fir'awn, and the Cities Overturned (69:9–10)

> *And Fir'awn, and those before him, and the cities overthrown committed Al-Khati'ah.*

A recitation point worth keeping. The word can be read **Qiblahu** (with a kasrah) — *those who were with him in his time*, his followers, the disbelieving Coptic people — or **Qablahu** (with a fathah) — *those nations before him who were like him*. Both readings are valid; between them they cover the whole company of the guilty, contemporaries and predecessors.

**Al-Khati'ah** = their **rejection of what Allah revealed**. **Ar-Rabi'**: disobedience. **Mujahid**: they committed errors.

Then the line that unifies the whole roll-call:

> *And they disobeyed their Lord's Messenger, so He seized them with a punishment that was Rabiyah.*

Ibn Kathir draws out a principle here that reaches well beyond these nations. Allah says elsewhere that *everyone of them denied **the Messengers***, and that Nuh's people, 'Ad and Thamud each *belied **the Messengers*** — plural — **yet only one Messenger came to each nation**. The conclusion:

> **Whoever denies one Messenger has denied all of the Messengers.**

They are all of the same type. Rejecting the messenger sent to you is rejecting the office of messengership itself.

**Rabiyah** — great, severe and painful. **Mujahid**: severe. **As-Suddi**: destructive.

## 5. The Ship, and the Ear That Retains (69:11–12)

> *Verily, when the water rose beyond its limits, We carried you in the ship — that We might make it an admonition for you, and that it might be retained by the retaining ears.*

The one **rescue** in the list of destructions, and it is placed deliberately. The water *rose beyond its limits* — **Ibn Abbas**: it increased abundantly — in answer to **Nuh's supplication** against a people who denied him and worshipped other than Allah. The earth was covered except for those on the ship. Therefore, Ibn Kathir notes, **all humans are from the loins of Nuh and his progeny**. Everyone reading this verse is descended from the survivors.

*"We carried **you**"* is doing real work: the address is to the reader.

**What is "it" that remains as an admonition?** **Qatadah**: Allah preserved that actual ship until the first people of this Ummah saw it. But Ibn Kathir prefers the broader reading as **the most apparent**: the pronoun refers to **ships as a species** — Allah caused this kind of creation to remain, so that you ride the currents of the seas. Every vessel afloat is the reminder. Compare *and has appointed for you ships and cattle on which you ride... that you may remember the favour of your Lord when you mount thereon* (43:12–13), and *We bore their offspring in the laden ship* (36:41–42).

**The retaining ear (udhun wa'iyah)** — the hinge of the surah:

- **Ibn Abbas**: an ear that is **retentive and hearing**.
- **Qatadah**: *an ear that **Allah gives intelligence**, so it benefits by what it hears from Allah's Book.*
- **Ad-Dahhak**: an ear that hears it **and retains it** — the person of sound hearing and correct intellect.

Ibn Kathir closes the point generally: **this is for everyone who understands and retains.** All the history just recited is offered on that condition.

## 6. The Day Itself (69:13–18)

> *Then when the Trumpet will be blown with one blowing...*

Ibn Kathir lays out the **three blasts**: the blowing of **fright**; the blowing of **destruction**, when everyone in the heavens and the earth is struck down except whom Allah wills; and the blowing of **standing before the Lord of all that exists** — resurrection and gathering. **This verse is the third.**

Why *one* blowing? Because **the command of Allah cannot be opposed or prevented, and it does not need to be repeated or stressed.** Once is sufficient. That is the whole theology of the verse.

**The earth and mountains** (69:14) — *crushed with a single crushing*: stretched out flat, and **the earth changed into something other than the earth**.

**The sky** (69:16) — *rent asunder... frail and torn up*. **Ibn Jurayj** compares *the heaven shall be opened, it will become as gates* (78:19). **Ibn Abbas**, startlingly: *it will be torn apart, **and the Throne will be near it***.

**The angels** (69:17) — on its sides, collectively, all of them. **Ar-Rabi' bin Anas**: *standing on what has been ground to powder of the heavens, **looking at the people of the earth***. And **eight** bear the Throne of your Lord above them.

**The exhibition** (69:18) — *That Day shall you be brought to Judgement, not a secret of you will be hidden.* You are presented to **the Knower of the secrets and private counsels**, from Whom none of your affairs are hidden.

Imam Ahmad, from **Abu Musa** (also in Ibn Majah and At-Tirmidhi):

> *The people will be exhibited three times on the Day of Judgement. The first two presentations will be arguing and offering excuses. During the third, the pages will **fly into their hands** — some in their right hands, some in their left.*

Two rounds of talking. Then the paperwork arrives and the talking stops.

## 7. The Right Hand (69:19–24)

> *Then as for him who will be given his Record in his right hand, he will say: "Here! Read my Record!"*

The first instinct of the saved man is to **hand the book to strangers**. Ibn Kathir notes he says this *to everyone that he meets* — because he knows what is in it is good and purely virtuous, and because **he is among those whose bad deeds Allah replaced with good ones**.

A grammatical aside worth the space: **'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd** treated the suffix *Um* in *hā'umu* as a grammatical addition; Ibn Kathir's own reading is that **it means "you all"** — he is calling out to a crowd.

Two narrations here are the emotional centre of the surah.

**From Ibn Abi Hatim**, via 'Abdullah bin 'Abdullah bin Hanzalah — *his father being the Companion washed by the angels*:

> Allah stops His servant, and **makes his sins appear on the outside of his Book**. He says: *Did you do this?* — *Yes, my Lord.* — *"I will not expose you for it, for verily, I have forgiven you."*
>
> And **then** the man says: *Here, all of you — read my Book!*

The invitation to read is only possible *after* the concealment. He is not showing off a clean life; he is showing off a covered one.

**And in the Sahih**, from **Ibn 'Umar**, asked about the private counsel (*najwa*):

> *Allah will bring the servant close on the Day of Judgement and make him confess all of his sins, until the servant thinks he is about to be destroyed. Then Allah will say: "**Verily, I concealed these sins for you in the worldly life, and I forgive you for them today.**" Then he will be given his Book of good deeds in his right hand.*

Whereas for the disbeliever and the hypocrite, the witnesses proclaim publicly: *These are those who lied against their Lord — the curse of Allah is on the wrongdoers.* The contrast is exact: **one file is read in private, the other in open court.**

> *Surely, I did believe that I shall meet my account!* (69:20)

Ibn Kathir: *I used to be **certain** in the worldly life that this day would definitely come.* Compare *those who are certain that they are going to meet their Lord* (2:46). The whole difference was certainty.

**The reward** (69:21–24): a life *well-pleasing* — pleasant; a **lofty Paradise** — elevated castles, wide-eyed maidens, pleasant stations, eternal joy. In the Sahih: *Paradise has one hundred levels, and between each level is a distance like that between the earth and the sky.*

**The fruits in bunches, low and near.** **Al-Bara' bin 'Azib**: *close enough for one of them to reach while he is **lying on his bed***.

*Eat and drink at ease for that which you have sent on before you in days past* — an invitation of blessing, favour and goodness. And immediately Ibn Kathir places beside it the hadith that prevents the wrong conclusion:

> *Work deeds, strive, seek to draw near, and know that **none of you will be admitted into Paradise because of his deeds**.*
> "Not even you, O Messenger of Allah?"
> *Not even me — except if Allah covers me with mercy from Himself and grace.*

The verse says *for what you sent on before*. The hadith says *and even that is not the currency*. Both are true.

---

## 8. The Left Hand (69:25–37)

> *"I wish that I had not been given my Record! And that I had never known how my account is! Would that it had been my end!"*

Three wishes, all of them for **less knowledge and less existence**. **Ad-Dahhak** on *would that it had been my end*: **a death which is not followed by any life** — annihilation, not transition. Likewise Muhammad bin Ka'b, Ar-Rabi' and As-Suddi. And **Qatadah**, with the observation that lands hardest:

> *He will hope for death — even though in the worldly life it was **the most hated thing** to him.*

> *My wealth has not availed me; my power has gone from me.*

Ibn Kathir: *my wealth and my honour did not protect me... **now the matter has ended with me alone**, and I have no helper nor anyone to save me.*

**The sentence** (69:30–32) — *Seize him and fetter him; then throw him in the blazing Fire; then fasten him on a chain whereof the length is seventy cubits.* The guardians of Hell remove him forcibly from the gathering, collar his neck, and submerge him.

On the chain: **Ka'b Al-Ahbar** — *every ring of it will equal the entire amount of iron found in this world.* **Ibn Abbas** and **Ibn Jurayj** — *each cubit is the forearm's length of **an angel***, so "seventy cubits" is not a human measure at all. Ibn Abbas's further descriptions of how the chain is threaded through the body are among the most physically graphic passages in the tafsir, and Ibn Kathir records them without softening.

And the hadith of **'Abdullah bin 'Amr** (Ahmad; At-Tirmidhi, who graded it *hasan*):

> *If a drop of lead the size of this — and he pointed to a skull bone — were sent from the heaven to the earth, a distance of **five hundred years' travel**, it would reach the earth before nightfall. And if it were sent from **the head of the chain**, it would travel **forty autumns, night and day**, before it reached the bottom.*

**Why he is there** (69:33–34) — and this is the doctrinal heart of the passage:

> *Verily, he used not to believe in Allah, the Most Great, and urged not on the feeding of the poor.*

Two failures, and Ibn Kathir names them as **two distinct rights**:

- **Allah's right upon the servants** — that they worship Him alone and associate nothing with Him. This man *did not establish the right of Allah upon him*.
- **The servants' right upon each other** — good treatment and assistance in righteousness and piety. He *did not benefit Allah's creation nor give them their rights*.

*For this reason,* Ibn Kathir writes, *Allah commanded **the prayer** and the **payment of Zakah***. And the last instruction of the Prophet ﷺ as death approached was exactly this pair:

> *The prayer — and those whom your right hands possess.*

Note the second failure carefully: not *he did not feed the poor*, but **he did not urge others to**. Indifference propagated is itself the crime.

**The consequence** (69:35–37) — *no friend has he here this Day*: no close friend and no intercessor whose request would be honoured. And no food except **Ghislin**. **Qatadah**: the worst food of the people of the Fire. **Ar-Rabi'** and **Ad-Dahhak**: a tree in Hell. **Ibn Abbas**, in two reports: the **blood and fluid that flows from their flesh**; and, the **pus of the people of the Fire**.

The one who withheld food is left with food no one would take.

## 9. The Oath: Who Brought This Report (69:38–43)

> *So I swear by whatsoever you see, and by whatsoever you see not, that this is verily the word of an honoured Messenger.*

Allah swears **by the visible creation**, in which His signs are seen and which indicates the perfection of His Names and Attributes — **and by the unseen**. The whole of reality, in both halves, is placed behind one claim: **the Qur'an is His Speech, His inspiration, His revelation.**

Then Ibn Kathir handles the phrase that has confused readers:

**"An honoured Messenger" here means Muhammad ﷺ.** The description carries **the meaning of conveying** — that is the entire function of a messenger, to transmit from the sender. Which is why the *same* description is applied in Surah At-Takwir to **Jibril**: *the Word of a most honourable messenger, owner of power with the Lord of the Throne, obeyed and trustworthy* (81:19–21) — and there the surah goes on to say *and your companion is not a madman*, meaning Muhammad ﷺ, and *indeed he saw him in the clear horizon* — Muhammad ﷺ seeing Jibril **in his true form as Allah created him**.

So: in one place the title is given to the angelic messenger, in another to the human one, **because both are conveying from Allah what has been entrusted to them**. The Qur'an belongs to neither of them.

Hence the two denials: *not the word of a poet — little is that you believe; nor the word of a soothsayer — little is that you remember.* These were the two available categories in Makkah for a man producing extraordinary Arabic. The Qur'an refuses both, and 81:25 adds the third refusal: *it is not the word of the outcast Shaytan.*

## 10. If He Had Forged It (69:44–47)

> *And if he had forged a false saying concerning Us, We surely would have seized him by his right hand, and then We certainly would have cut off Al-Watin from him — and none of you could have prevented it.*

The argument is a hostage of the most direct kind. Ibn Kathir spells it out: *if Muhammad ﷺ had forged something against Us as they claim, or added or removed anything from the Message, or said anything from himself while attributing it to Us, then **We would surely be swift in punishing him**. And of course, he did not.*

**Seized by the right hand** — one explanation: *because it is stronger in grabbing.*

**Al-Watin** — **Ibn Abbas**: *the artery of the heart, the vein that is attached to the heart* (likewise 'Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Al-Hakim, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Muslim Al-Batin, Abu Sakhr). **Muhammad bin Ka'b**: *the heart, its blood, and whatever is near it.* The aorta.

**And none of you could have prevented it** — *none of you would be able to come between Us and him.*

The point of the whole passage, in Ibn Kathir's words: **he is truthful, righteous and guided, because Allah determined what he is to convey from Him**, and supports him with clear miracles and definite proofs. The continued survival of the messenger is itself submitted as evidence.

## 11. The Close (69:48–52)

> *And verily, this is a Reminder for those who have Taqwa.*

The Qur'an — but selectively received. Compare: *Say: "It is for those who believe, a guide and a healing. And as for those who disbelieve, there is deafness in their ears, and it is blindness for them."*

> *And verily, We know that there are some among you that deny.*

Ibn Kathir: **with all this explanation and clarification, there will still be among you those who reject it.** Stated flatly, in advance, without dismay. Clarity has never been the variable.

> *And indeed it will be an anguish for the disbelievers.*

**Ibn Jarir** (and similarly **Qatadah**): *the rejection itself* will be their anguish on the Day of Judgement. Ibn Kathir allows a second reading: the pronoun may refer to **the Qur'an**, so that the Qur'an and belief in it are a source of anguish to them — compare *Thus have We caused it to enter the hearts of the criminals; they will not believe in it* (26:200–201), and *a barrier will be set between them and that which they desire* (34:54).

> *And verily, it is an absolute truth with certainty* — **the right and truthful news in which there is no doubt, suspicion or confusion.**

> *So glorify the Name of your Lord, the Most Great.*

Ibn Kathir's last line of the surah: **He Who sent down this magnificent Qur'an.**

Notice where the surah has arrived. At 69:33 the condemned man's defining failure was that *he used not to believe in Allah, **Al-'Azim***. At 69:52, the final instruction to everyone still listening is to glorify the Lord, **Al-'Azim**. The same Name closes both files.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Haqqah means "the Reality" — the Day on which the promise and the threat inevitably occur.** The name itself is the argument: it is the moment words stop being words. Everything the surah does afterwards is designed to make that name credible before it arrives.

**The proof offered is history, not prediction.** Thamud, 'Ad, Fir'awn, the overturned cities, the flood — five populations who were warned and are now, as 69:8 asks, without a single remnant or even anyone who claims descent from them. The Day is not an unprecedented event. It is the same event, scaled.

**Three things worth carrying:**

- **The two hands are two ways of being seen.** The saved man's first act is to hand his book to strangers — but only because Allah has *just* shown him his sins in private and covered them. *"I concealed these for you in the world, and I forgive you today."* The disbeliever's file, by contrast, is read aloud by witnesses. Both men are fully known. Only one is exposed.
- **Two rights, not one.** The man in the chain failed twice: he did not believe in Allah, *and* he did not urge the feeding of the poor. Ibn Kathir makes these the two halves of religion — Allah's right to be worshipped alone, and creation's right to good treatment — which is why Salah and Zakah are commanded together, and why the Prophet's ﷺ dying words were *the prayer, and those whom your right hands possess*. Note also that the second charge is not failing to feed but **failing to urge**.
- **The condition is a retaining ear.** At 69:12 the whole recital is made conditional on *udhun wa'iyah* — Qatadah: *an ear that Allah gives intelligence, so it benefits from what it hears of Allah's Book.* The information in this surah has been public for fourteen centuries. What it does depends entirely on the receiver.

And the quiet architectural signature: the surah opens by naming **Al-Haqqah**, the Reality, and closes by calling the Qur'an **haqq al-yaqin** — absolute truth with certainty. The Day is real; the report of the Day is real; and the report survived because the one carrying it never added a word to it — *had he done so, We would have cut his aorta, and none of you could have stopped Us.*
