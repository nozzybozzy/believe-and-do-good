*Revealed in Makkah · 37 ayat · named for the kneeling of every nation on the Day of Resurrection, 45:28*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Jathiyah is one of the seven "Ha Mim" surahs, and it is built as a **case against a particular kind of person**: not the confused seeker, but the man who *hears* the evidence and shrugs. The surah opens by piling up signs — the heavens, your own body, the animals, the night and day, the rain, the winds — and then immediately introduces the one who listens to all of it and "persists with pride as if he heard them not."

From there it moves through three refusals and their answer:

- The refusal to **credit** — attributing everything to *Ad-Dahr*, time, rather than to the One who moves time.
- The refusal to **anticipate** — "we know not what the Hour is; we only conjecture."
- The refusal to **submit** — taking one's own desire as one's god.

Against all three the surah sets a single image, the one that gives it its name: **every nation on its knees**, records opened, nothing added and nothing deleted. The proud man who would not bend his neck in this world bends it there, along with everyone else.

---

## 1. Ascending Signs, Ascending Audiences (45:1–5)

The opening is a sweep through creation: heavens and earth, and everything in them — angels, jinn, humans, animals, birds, beasts, insects, sea creatures; night and day chasing each other in unbroken succession, one bringing dark and one bringing light; rain sent down from the clouds *when it is most needed*.

Ibn Kathir notes that Allah calls the rain **"provision"** (*rizq*) because it is the resource from which all other provisions grow.

Then the winds — and he lingers here, because the variety is the point: southerly and northerly, easterly and westerly, some off the sea and some off the land, some by night and some by day; **some bring rain, some pollinate, some simply revive the soul, and some carry no benefit at all**.

But the structural observation is the beautiful one. The three verses end on three different audiences, and they climb:

| Verse | Ends for… |
|---|---|
| 45:3 | **the believers** (*mu'minin*) |
| 45:4 | **those who have faith with certainty** (*yuqinun*) |
| 45:5 | **those who understand** (*ya'qilun*) |

Ibn Kathir: this is an ascent "from one honorable stage to what is more honorable and higher in grade." The same signs are available to everyone; what differs is the depth of eye brought to them. He pairs the passage with the great sign-verse of Al-Baqarah (2:164), which gathers the identical list — creation, alternation of night and day, ships, rain, creatures, veering winds and clouds — and ends the same way.

---

## 2. The Sinful Liar (45:6–11)

> *Woe to every sinful liar — who hears the Ayat of Allah recited to him, yet persists with pride as if he heard them not.*

The portrait Ibn Kathir draws: one who lies in his speech, **swears often**, is worthless, commits and utters sinful acts, and disbelieves in the signs. The damning detail is *as if he heard them not* — the words physically reached him; nothing else did.

And when he does pick up some fragment of the Qur'an, he makes it a **jest**. The punishment is matched to the crime: a *humiliating* torment, "as recompense for ridiculing the Qur'an and jesting about it."

Ibn Kathir attaches here a hadith from **Abdullah bin Umar** (Muslim): the Messenger ﷺ **prohibited travelling with the Qur'an into enemy territory**, for fear it would be desecrated. The reverence owed to the Book is not sentimental; it is protected in law.

Then the accounting:

- **Hell is "in front of them"** — waiting, already positioned.
- Their **wealth and children** will not avail them.
- Nor will the **false gods** they took as protectors — not in the least.

And the verdict on the Qur'an itself: *This is guidance.* For those who reject it, a painful torment of *Rijz* — "agonizing and severe."

---

## 3. All of It Is From Him (45:12–13)

The sea is subjected so ships may run through it **by His command** — Ibn Kathir stresses that phrase: it is Allah who ordered the sea to carry ships. The purpose is twofold: *that you may seek of His bounty* (commerce and trade) *and that you may be thankful* — thankful for provisions carried to you "from far away provinces and distant areas."

Then the sentence widens to include everything: the stars, the mountains, the seas, the rivers, all of it subjected — **"all of it from Him."**

**Al-Awfi ← Ibn Abbas** on that phrase:

> "Everything is from Allah, and that is a Name from His Names. So it all comes from Him without rivals to dispute His authority; surely, this fact is completely certain."

Cross-referenced to 16:53 — *whatever blessing you have, it is from Allah; then when harm touches you, to Him you cry aloud.* The reflex in crisis exposes what the mind knew all along.

Signs, here, are for **those who think deeply** (*yatafakkarun*).

---

## 4. Forgive Those Who Do Not Expect the Days of Allah (45:14–15)

> *Say to the believers to forgive those who hope not for the Days of Allah.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as a command to **absorb harm**: let the believers pardon the disbelievers and endure what is directed at them.

He places it historically. In the early period, Muslims were ordered to be patient under the oppression of the idolaters and the People of the Scripture, **so that hearts might incline toward Islam**. When the disbelievers persisted in stubbornness, Jihad was legislated. (From **Ibn Abbas** and **Qatadah**.)

**Mujahid** glosses "those who hope not for the Days of Allah" simply as: *they do not appreciate Allah's favors.*

And the reassurance underneath the restraint: forgiving them now does not mean the account is closed — *that He may recompense people according to what they have earned.* Then the principle that removes every excuse from everyone:

> *Whosoever does a good deed, it is for himself; whosoever does evil, it is against himself. Then to your Lord you will be made to return.*

---

## 5. The Warning Inside a Compliment (45:16–20)

Allah lists what was given to the Children of Israel — **the Scripture, the understanding of it and its laws (*Hukm*), the Prophethood**, good provisions of food and drink, kingship, and preference "above the nations" (Ibn Kathir specifies: of *their time*), plus clear proofs in matters, "evidences, proofs and unequivocal signs."

And then the fracture: *they differed not until after the knowledge came to them* — **out of envy among themselves**. Not ignorance. The proof had already been established against them; the division was transgression, not confusion.

Ibn Kathir is explicit that this passage is a **warning aimed at the Muslim Ummah**: not to take the path they took, nor adopt their ways. That is exactly why the next verse pivots to *you*:

> *Then We have put you on a Shari'ah of the commandment. So follow it, and follow not the desires of those who know not.*

Follow what was revealed to you from your Lord; turn away from the idolaters. As for their solidarity with one another — *the wrongdoers are protecting friends of one another* — Ibn Kathir asks what such loyalty is actually worth: "all they will earn from their protecting friends is **more loss, destruction and demise**."

The contrast is drawn in light and dark:

- **Allah is the Wali of those with Taqwa** — He brings them out of darkness into light.
- The false deities are the *awliya* of those who disbelieve — taking them **out of the light into the darkness**.

The Qur'an itself is *Basa'ir* — clear insight for mankind, guidance and mercy.

---

## 6. Not Equal, in Life or in Death (45:21–23)

> *Or do those who earn evil deeds think that We shall hold them equal with those who believe and do righteous good deeds, in their life and their death? Worst is the judgement that they make.*

Ibn Kathir reads the rebuke as being about **what they assume of Allah**: "worst is the thought that they have about Us and about Our justice, thinking that We will ever make the pious and the wicked equal in the Hereafter or this life." He pairs it with 59:20 — *not equal are the dwellers of the Fire and the dwellers of Paradise.*

Then a small, arresting report (**At-Tabarani**, via Shu'bah ← Amr bin Murrah ← Abu Ad-Duha ← Masruq):

> **Tamim Ad-Dari** once stood in voluntary prayer through the entire night until morning, reciting **only this one ayah**.

The heavens and earth were created *with truth* — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, **in justice** — so that each person is recompensed for what he earned, and no one is wronged.

### The god of one's own desire

> *Have you seen him who takes his own lust as his god?*

The definition is behavioural, not theoretical: he **abides by his desire** — whatever it presents as good, he does; whatever it presents as bad, he abandons. Desire has taken the seat where revelation should sit.

*And Allah left him astray **with knowledge*** — Ibn Kathir gives two readings:

1. Allah **knew** this person deserved misguidance, and so left him astray.
2. Allah led him astray **after knowledge had reached him** and the proof had been established against him.

"The second meaning includes the first, but not the opposite."

The consequence is the sealing of the three faculties — **hearing, heart, sight** — so that "he does not hear what benefits him, understands not what would lead him to guidance, and sees not the evidence with which he can be enlightened." Compare 7:186: *whomsoever Allah sends astray, none can guide him.*

---

## 7. "Nothing Destroys Us but Time" (45:24–26)

This is the surah's confrontation with the *Dahriyyah* — those who denied the Hereafter and said there is only this world: people die, people are born, and **time** is what finishes us. Ibn Kathir identifies two overlapping groups: the Arab idolaters who denied resurrection, and the atheist philosophers who denied creation *and* resurrection — including those who held that the world returns to its original state **every thirty-six thousand years** and begins its cycle again, endlessly, "thus contradicting sound reason and divine revelation."

Allah's answer is one line: *they have no knowledge of it; they only presume.* They speculate and guess.

### The hadith about cursing time

> "The Son of Adam annoys Me when he curses **Ad-Dahr**, while **I am Ad-Dahr**. In My Hand are all matters; I cause the alternation of his days and nights."
>
> And: *"Do not curse Ad-Dahr, for Allah is Ad-Dahr."*

**Ash-Shafi'i, Abu Ubaydah**, and other Imams of Tafsir explain it — and Ibn Kathir calls this "the best explanation for this subject":

> In Jahiliyyah, when a calamity struck, the Arabs would say *"Woe to Ad-Dahr!"* — blaming time and cursing it. But it is Allah who causes these things to happen. So in cursing time, they were in effect cursing **Him**, since He is the true cause of every event. That is why the abuse was forbidden.

He then adds a correction: **Ibn Hazm and the Zahiriyyah erred** in using this hadith to count *Ad-Dahr* among Allah's Most Beautiful Names.

### "Bring back our fathers"

Their only argument against the clear signs is a taunt: *bring our dead fathers back, if you are truthful.* The reply reframes the whole question — *Allah gives you life, then causes you to die, then assembles you on the Day of Resurrection.* You are yourselves the witnesses that He brought you into existence from non-existence (2:28); and **He who initiates creation can restart it more easily** (30:27).

As for their demand for a private demonstration now: the gathering has a fixed day, not an on-demand one — the Day of Gathering (64:9), the Day of Sorting Out (77:12–13), delayed only for a term already fixed (11:104).

Ibn Kathir closes the section on the psychology of it (70:6): **they see it as far off; the believers see its arrival as easy and imminent.**

---

## 8. Every Nation on Its Knees (45:27–29)

> *And you will see each nation kneeling (Jathiyah), each nation called to its Record.*

Kneeling, Ibn Kathir says, out of **fear of the enormity of the events** — and he preserves the tradition that this happens when **Hellfire is brought forth**: she exhales *once*, and everyone falls to their knees.

Including **Ibrahim al-Khalil**, who will say: *"Myself, myself, myself! Today I will not ask You but about myself."*

And **'Isa**, who will say: *"Today I will only argue before You on my own behalf — I will not ask You even about Maryam, who gave birth to me."*

Then the Records. Compare 39:69 — *the Book will be presented, and the Prophets and the witnesses brought forward* — and 18:49, where the criminals cry out at a book that leaves **"neither a small thing nor a big thing"** unrecorded. And 75:13–15: man will be told what he sent forward and what he left behind, and will be **a witness against himself**, however many excuses he offers.

**Ibn Abbas and others** on *"We were recording what you used to do"*:

> The angels record the servants' deeds and ascend with them to heaven, where they meet the angels entrusted with records sent down from **Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz** on each Night of Al-Qadr — containing what Allah wrote would occur from the servants long before He created them. They compare the two, and find **not a single letter added or deleted**.

## 9. The Two Ends (45:30–37)

**For the believers** — "those whose hearts believed and whose limbs performed good deeds, in sincerity to Allah and in conformity with the legislation" — their Lord admits them **into His mercy**, which Ibn Kathir identifies as Paradise itself, citing the hadith in which Allah says to Paradise:

> *"You are My mercy, with which I grant mercy to whom I will."*

That, and nothing else, is "the evident success."

**For the disbelievers**, the interrogation: *Were not Our Ayat recited to you? But you were proud* — you heard them and turned away — *and you were a people who were criminals*, by your actions and by the denial your hearts contained.

Their old excuse is quoted back to them: when believers told them Allah's promise is true and the Hour is coming without doubt, they answered, *we don't recognise what you're talking about; we only remotely think it might come; we have no firm belief in it.* Now the evil they did **appears** to them, and what they mocked **encircles them from every direction**.

> *This Day We will forget you as you forgot the meeting of this Day of yours.*

Ibn Kathir: We will *treat* you as though forgotten — casting you into Jahannam. And he brings the hadith that makes it personal. Allah will ask a servant:

> *"Have I not given you a spouse, honoured you, subjected the camels and horses to you? Have I not allowed you to be a chief and a master?"*
> — "Yes, O Lord."
> *"Did you think that you would ever meet Me?"*
> — "No."
> *"Then this Day, I will forget you as you forgot Me."*

The cause is named plainly: *you took the revelations of Allah in mockery, and the life of the world deceived you.* No exit from the Fire, no return to the world — and, Ibn Kathir adds, **no apologies will be sought from them**; the punishment comes without limit or delay, just as a group of believers enters Paradise without limit or delay.

### The closing

The surah ends where the argument has been pointing the whole time — praise to the **Lord of the heavens, the Lord of the earth, the Lord of all that exists**, the Owner of them and of everything in them.

*And His is the majesty (Al-Kibriya') in the heavens and the earth* — which **Mujahid** glosses as **kingship**. Everything and everyone is subservient, in complete need of Him and dependent on Him. And the hadith that seals the theme of the proud man from verse 8:

> **"Glory is My robe and pride is My garment; whoever rivals Me for either of them, I will make him reside in My Fire."**

Two final Names:

- **Al-'Aziz** — the Almighty, "who is never resisted or rivalled"
- **Al-Hakim** — all-wise in His statements, actions, legislation, and the decree He appoints

*All glory and praise are due to Him; none has the right to be worshipped but He, Alone.*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Jathiyah is about pride, and the posture that ends it.**

The surah's villain is not an atheist who lacked evidence. He is a man who had the evidence recited to him and **persisted with pride as if he heard nothing** — who blamed time for what only God can cause, who dismissed the Hour as guesswork, and who took his own appetite as the thing he obeyed. Ibn Kathir shows every one of those refusals collapsing at the same moment: when Hellfire exhales once and **every nation, without exception, is on its knees** — Ibrahim and 'Isa among them, each asking only for themselves.

Between now and then, the surah gives the believer three instructions and one comfort:

1. **Look properly.** The same night, day, rain and wind are signs for the believer, for the certain, and for the one who understands — three ascending grades of the same eye.
2. **Credit correctly.** All of it is from Him — the sea that carries your trade, the stars, the mountains, the rivers. "Without rivals to dispute His authority."
3. **Absorb the harm.** Forgive those who do not expect the Days of Allah; their account is not yours to settle.

And the comfort: *not a single letter was added or deleted.* Nothing you did is lost, and nothing you did not do will be written against you.
