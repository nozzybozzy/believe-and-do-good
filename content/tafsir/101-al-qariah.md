*Revealed in Makkah · 11 ayat · "The Striking Hour" — one of the names of the Day of Judgement*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Qari`ah is eleven ayat long and does only two things, in sequence: it makes the Day **unimaginable**, and then it tells you **exactly what will be measured on it**.

The first half refuses to explain. It names the Day, asks what it is, then asks again what could possibly make you understand what it is — three ayat that give no information at all, only pressure. When the description finally arrives, it is not of the Day but of *what happens to solid things*: people become scattered moths, and mountains — the heaviest, most permanent objects a human being has ever seen — become **carded wool**, fibres pulled apart until they drift.

Then the surah pivots without warning to something very small and very precise: **a balance**. Everything that has just been dissolved — people, mountains, the whole visible order — is replaced by a single scale with your deeds on it. The surah that began by insisting nothing could be described ends by describing the one thing that will matter, in two short verdicts.

And it does not close on Paradise. It closes on the Fire, and on a word for heat that Ibn Kathir spends the rest of the tafsir unpacking with hadith after hadith — because the Prophet ﷺ evidently thought people did not understand what was being said to them.

---

## 1. The Day With Many Names (101:1–3)

> *Al-Qari`ah. What is Al-Qari`ah? And what will make you know what Al-Qari`ah is?*

**Al-Qari`ah is one of the names of the Day of Judgement.** Ibn Kathir lists its siblings — **Al-Haqqah** (the Reality), **At-Tammah** (the Overwhelming), **As-Sakhkhah** (the Deafening Blast), **Al-Ghashiyah** (the Overwhelming Event) — and others besides.

This is worth pausing on. The Qur'an does not have one name for that Day; it has a dozen, and each one is a different angle of attack on the same event. A day named for what it *strikes*, a day named for what it *deafens*, a day named for what it *covers over*. No single name is sufficient, which is itself part of the argument.

Ibn Kathir's note on the third ayah is one line and explains the entire structure: **Allah intensifies concern and fright for it by saying, *"And what will make you know what Al-Qari`ah is?"*** The question is not seeking an answer. It is doing work on the listener.

## 2. Moths, and Carded Wool (101:4–5)

> *It is a Day whereon mankind will be like moths scattered about.*

Ibn Kathir explains the comparison as being about **their scattering, their dividing, their coming and their going** — and gives the cause: **all due to being bewildered at what is happening to them**.

That is the precise point of the image. Moths around a flame do not move randomly because they are stupid; they move that way because their navigation has been overwhelmed by something they were never built to process. The surah is describing disorientation on a species-wide scale.

He cross-references the parallel image:

> *As if they were locusts spread abroad.* (54:7)

> *And the mountains will be like wool, carded.*

Not merely soft — **carded**. Ibn Kathir: they will become like wool **that has begun to wear out and be torn apart**. Carding is the process of pulling a fleece into loose separated fibres so it can be spun; it is the moment before a thing becomes something else, when it has lost all structure but not yet gained a new one.

On the word `Ihn (عِهْن) — that it means **wool** — Ibn Kathir stacks up eight authorities in agreement: **Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa`id bin Jubayr, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, `Ata' Al-Khurasani, Ad-Dahhak and As-Suddi**.

**The two images together are the whole cosmology of the Day**: the lightest thing that exists (a moth) and the heaviest thing you know (a mountain) end up in the same condition — weightless, structureless, blown about.

## 3. The Balance (101:6–8)

Having dissolved everything, the surah introduces the only thing that will still have weight.

> *Then as for him whose Balance will be heavy, he will live a pleasant life.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is deliberately plain, with no mysticism attached: **his good deeds are more than his bad deeds** — and the pleasant life **means, in Paradise**.

> *But as for him whose Balance will be light...*

**His bad deeds are more than his good deeds.**

Note what has just happened structurally. Mountains lost their weight in ayah 5. Deeds acquire weight in ayah 6. The surah has performed a transfer: **on that Day, the only mass in the universe is moral.**

Ibn Kathir also frames this as Allah **informing about the results received by those who performed the deeds, and the honour and disgrace they will experience based upon their deeds** — the outcome is presented as *earned*, not arbitrary.

## 4. "His Mother Will Be Hawiyah" (101:9–10)

> *His mother will be Hawiyah. And what will make you know what it is?*

This is the strangest phrase in the surah, and Ibn Kathir preserves **two readings** without forcing a choice.

**Reading one — the head.** He will be **falling and tumbling headfirst into the fire of Hell**, and *"his mother"* is used to refer to **his brain** — because it is the mother of his head (*umm ar-ra's*, the membrane enclosing the brain, is an established Arabic idiom). Reported similarly from **Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Abu Salih and Qatadah**.

- **Qatadah:** "He will fall into the Hellfire on his head."
- **Abu Salih:** "They will fall into the Fire on their heads."

**Reading two — the destination.** *Hawiyah* is **one of the names of the Hellfire**, and it is the mother he **returns to and ends up with in the Hereafter**.

- **Ibn Jarir:** *"Al-Hawiyah is only called his mother because he will have no other abode except for it."*
- **Ibn Zayd:** *"Al-Hawiyah is the Fire, and it will be his mother and his abode to which he will return, and where he will be settled."* — then reciting *Their abode will be the Fire* (3:151).
- **Qatadah**, via Ibn Abi Hatim: *"It is the Fire, and it is their abode."*

The second reading is the more devastating of the two, and Ibn Jarir's sentence is the reason why. A mother is where you come from and where you can always return; **the word is used because for this man there is nowhere else left to go.** It is the vocabulary of shelter turned inside out.

## 5. A Fire, Hamiyah (101:11)

> *A fire Hamiyah!*

**Meaning, extreme heat** — Ibn Kathir specifies, **a heat accompanied by a strong flame and fire.** He then closes the surah not with commentary but with four narrations, because the word needs calibrating against something the reader has actually felt.

**The seventy parts** — from Abu Hurayrah, recorded by **Al-Bukhari and Muslim**:

> *The fire of the Children of Adam that you all kindle is one part of the seventy parts of the fire of Hell.*

The Companions asked whether that alone would not be sufficient. He ﷺ replied: ***It is more than it by sixty-nine times*** — and in another wording, ***each of them is like the heat of it.***

**The lightest punishment** — from Abu Hurayrah:

> *Verily, the person who will receive the lightest torment of the people of the Hellfire will be a man who will have two sandals that will cause his brain to boil.*

The *lightest*. Note also how it lands on the same image as ayah 9 — the head, boiling.

**The two breaths** — in the Two Sahihs:

> The Hellfire complained to its Lord: *"O Lord! Some parts of me devour other parts of me."* So He permitted it two breaths — one in winter, one in summer. **The most severe cold you experience in winter is from its cold, and the most severe heat you experience in summer is from its heat.**

**And the practical ruling that follows from it** — in the Two Sahihs:

> *When the heat becomes intense, pray the prayer when it cools down, for indeed the intense heat is from the breath of Hell.*

That last narration is the quiet masterstroke of the whole section. An eschatological warning about a Fire you cannot imagine ends up **changing what time you pray Dhuhr in July**. The Hereafter is not being described as a distant place; it is being described as something whose edge you have already brushed against, every summer of your life, without recognising it.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Al-Qari`ah is one of many names for one Day** — Al-Haqqah, At-Tammah, As-Sakhkhah, Al-Ghashiyah. No single name can hold it, which is why the surah opens by asking twice and answering neither time.
- **The threefold naming is not information — it is pressure.** Ibn Kathir: Allah *intensifies concern and fright* by asking what could make you know.
- **Moths and mountains end in the same state.** The lightest and heaviest things you know both become weightless and structureless — the moths from bewilderment, the mountains torn into carded wool.
- **Weight transfers from matter to deeds.** Mountains lose their mass in ayah 5; the Balance acquires it in ayah 6. Heavy = good deeds outweigh bad → Paradise. Light = bad outweigh good.
- **"His mother will be Hawiyah"** carries two readings: he falls headfirst (his *mother* = his brain), or **the Fire is the only home he has left**. Ibn Jarir: it is called his mother *because he will have no other abode except for it.*
- **Worldly fire is one part in seventy.** And the *lightest* punishment in Hell is a pair of sandals that boil the brain.
- **You have already felt its breath.** The worst heat of summer and the worst cold of winter are its two exhalations — which is why the Prophet ﷺ told us to delay the prayer until the heat cools. The surah's warning ends as a change to your daily timetable.
