*Revealed in Makkah · 3 ayat · "The Help" / "The Victory" — the last complete surah of the Qur'an to be revealed*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

An-Nasr is three verses long, announces the greatest victory in the Prophet's ﷺ life, and is — read correctly — **a death notice**.

That is the whole surah, and it is the reason Ibn Kathir titles his commentary on it *"This Surah informs of the Completion of the Life of Allah's Messenger."* On the surface it reads as congratulation: the help of Allah has come, Makkah has fallen, the tribes are pouring in. Underneath, it is telling the man who received it that his work is finished, and that finished work means a finished life.

And the instruction it gives at the summit of triumph is the last thing anyone would expect. Not *celebrate*. Not *consolidate*. **Glorify your Lord and ask His forgiveness** — *istighfar*, at the exact moment of total success. The surah closes on the word **Tawwaba**, the One Who accepts repentance, which is a strange note on which to end a victory and the entire point of it.

The structure is: *a gift you did not earn* (nasr — **help**, from Him) → *a result you did not engineer* (people entering in crowds) → *therefore, humility and repentance*. Victory is not a reason to stand taller. It is a reason to see how little of it was yours.

---

## 1. Its Virtue, and Its Place at the End

Ibn Kathir notes what he has already established elsewhere: **An-Nasr is equivalent to one-fourth of the Qur'an** — as Surah Az-Zalzalah is.

Then the anecdote that fixes its position in the revelation, recorded by **An-Nasa'i** from Ubaydullah bin Abdullah bin Utbah. Ibn Abbas tested him:

> *"Do you know the last Surah of the Qur'an that was revealed?"*
> — *"Yes. It was 'When there comes the help of Allah and the Conquest.'"*
> — *"You have spoken truthfully."*

So the reader is being handed the **closing statement of the revelation**: the last complete surah sent down, and its subject is the ending of the messenger's task.

---

## 2. Umar's Gathering, and Ibn Abbas's Answer (110:1)

The centrepiece of the tafsir, recorded by **Al-Bukhari** — and Ibn Kathir notes Bukhari was alone in recording it.

Umar used to bring the young Ibn Abbas into the council of the veterans of Badr, and one of the old men resented it: *why do you bring this boy to sit with us when we have children his age?* Umar answered only, *he is among those whom you know*. Then one day Umar convened them and put a question to the room — Ibn Abbas says he believes the whole invitation was staged to make a point:

> *"What do you say about Allah's statement, 'When there comes the help of Allah and the Conquest'?"*

Some of the elders gave the plain answer: **we were commanded to praise Allah and seek His forgiveness when He helps us and gives us victory.** Others said nothing at all.

Then Umar turned to the youngest man present. *Is this what you say, O Ibn Abbas?* — **"No."**

> *"It was the end of the life of Allah's Messenger ﷺ that Allah was informing him of.* **That is a sign of the end of your life** *— so glorify the praises of your Lord, and ask His forgiveness. Verily, He is the One Who accepts the repentance and Who forgives."*

Umar bin Al-Khattab's verdict closed the discussion:

> *"I do not know anything about it other than what you have said."*

Two things are being taught at once. The **meaning** — that the completion of the mission announces the completion of the messenger. And the **method** — that a verse can carry a message it never states outright, and that reading the Qur'an properly means hearing what the wording implies, not only what it says.

---

## 3. "My Death Has Been Announced to Me"

**Imam Ahmad** records from Ibn Abbas — again, alone in recording it — that when the surah was revealed, the Prophet ﷺ said:

> **نُعِيَتْ إِلَيَّ نَفْسِي** — *"My death has been announced to me."*

And, Ibn Abbas adds, **he died during that year.**

Ibn Abbas's reading in Umar's council was not clever inference after the fact. It was what the Prophet ﷺ himself had understood on the day the words came down.

---

## 4. How He ﷺ Lived the Verse (110:3)

The most practical material in the surah is not exegesis but **imitation**: Ibn Kathir shows the Prophet ﷺ obeying the third verse, out loud, for the rest of his life.

**Aishah** (Al-Bukhari, and the rest of the group except At-Tirmidhi) reports that he began saying often in his **bowing and prostration**:

> **سُبْحَانَكَ اللَّهُمَّ رَبَّنَا وَبِحَمْدِكَ، اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِي**
> *Glory to You, O Allah, our Lord, and praise be to You. O Allah, forgive me.*

Ibn Kathir's comment is the striking part: **he did this as his interpretation of the Qur'an** — his *tafsir* of the verse was to perform it. Tasbih and istighfar, exactly as commanded, folded into the ruku' and sujud of every prayer.

**Masruq from Aishah** (Ahmad, and Muslim) records the second formula, used often towards the end of his life:

> **سُبْحَانَ اللهِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ، أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللهَ وَأَتُوبُ إِلَيْهِ**
> *Glory to Allah, and praise be unto Him. I seek Allah's forgiveness and I repent to Him.*

And when asked, he explained himself by quoting the surah:

> *"Verily, my Lord has informed me that I will see **a sign in my Ummah**, and He has commanded me that when I see it, I should glorify His praises and seek His forgiveness, for He is the One Who accepts repentance. **And indeed I have seen it**"* — then he recited all three verses.

The sign was the crowds. He saw them, recognised what they meant for him, and increased his istighfar.

---

## 5. Al-Fath Is Makkah — and Why the Arabs Were Waiting (110:2)

On the identity of the Conquest, Ibn Kathir is unusually flat: **it is the conquest of Makkah, and there is only one view concerning it.**

He then explains the mechanism behind *"the people enter Allah's religion in crowds."* The scattered Arab regions had been holding back deliberately, treating Makkah as the test case:

> *"If he is victorious over his people, then he is a true Prophet."*

**Al-Bukhari** records the same from `Amr bin Salamah: when Makkah fell, the people **rushed** to the Messenger ﷺ to profess Islam; the various regions had been delaying precisely until that outcome was known. Their saying was, *"Leave him and his people alone. If he is victorious over them, he is a true Prophet."*

The result, in Ibn Kathir's summary: **two years did not pass** after the conquest before the peninsula of the Arabs was **laden with faith**, and not a tribe remained that had not professed Islam. (For the military detail he refers the reader to his own *As-Sirah*.)

Hence *afwaja* — in crowds, in throngs, tribe after tribe — a word describing something no strategy produced. That is why the verse before it calls the whole thing **nasrullah**, *the help of Allah*.

---

## 6. Jabir's Tears: They Will Leave in Crowds Too

Ibn Kathir ends the surah on a sombre note that most readers do not expect. **Imam Ahmad** records from Abu `Ammar that a neighbour of Jabir bin `Abdullah met him after a journey and began talking about the **divisions among the people** and what they had started doing.

Jabir **began to cry**, and said he had heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say:

> **إِنَّ النَّاسَ دَخَلُوا فِي دِينِ اللهِ أَفْوَاجًا، وَسَيَخْرُجُونَ مِنْهُ أَفْوَاجًا**
> *"Verily, the people have entered into the religion of Allah in crowds, and they will also leave it in crowds."*

The same word — *afwaja* — running in both directions. The surah's happiest image is quoted back as a warning: **a crowd is not a proof.** People who arrive in throngs because a movement is winning can depart in throngs when the wind changes. Which is, again, why the response commanded in verse three is repentance rather than confidence.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **A victory can be a summons.** The surah announcing the conquest of Makkah was, to the one who received it, the announcement of his own death: *"My death has been announced to me."* Completion of the work means completion of the time.
- **The correct response to success is *istighfar*.** At the highest point of his mission, the Prophet ﷺ was commanded to glorify and seek forgiveness — and did so in every bowing and prostration until he died.
- **Read what the words imply, not only what they say.** Ibn Abbas out-read a room full of Badr veterans, and Umar conceded on the spot. Depth of understanding is not a function of age.
- **Nasr is His, not yours.** The tribes came in *afwaja* — floods of people no campaign could have produced. Naming the victory "the help of Allah" is the surah removing your claim to it.
- **He ﷺ interpreted the Qur'an by obeying it.** Aishah's report is a definition of tafsir: the meaning of *"glorify and seek forgiveness"* is a man saying *Subhanaka Allahumma… Allahumma-ghfir li* in sujud.
- **Crowds arrive and crowds leave.** Jabir wept over it. Numbers are not the measure of the religion's health, and neither is momentum.
- **The last word of the last surah revealed is forgiveness** — *Tawwaba*, the One Who constantly accepts repentance. The revelation closes not on triumph but on the door that stays open.
