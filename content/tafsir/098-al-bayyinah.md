*8 ayat · "The Clear Evidence" — named for the word repeated in its opening verses · the surah the Prophet ﷺ was commanded to recite to a named Companion*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Bayyinah answers a quiet objection: **why did a new Messenger need to come at all, to people who already had Scripture?**

Its answer is a short chain of statements, each one closing off an excuse. First: the disbelievers among the People of the Scripture and the idolaters **were not going to be dislodged** from where they stood until clear evidence reached them. Then it names the evidence — not an argument, but a *person*: **a Messenger reciting purified pages**, containing books that are upright and contain no error. Then it removes the possibility of misunderstanding: the People of the Scripture **did not split until after the evidence arrived**. Their division was not caused by confusion. It came after clarity.

And then the surah states what was ever asked of them, and it is startlingly small: **worship Allah alone, sincerely, as people of pure monotheism; pray; give.** Four things. Ibn Kathir calls this *the upright, just religion* — the same instruction given to every Messenger before.

The surah then closes by sorting humanity into two named groups and using the same grammatical shape for both: **the worst of creatures** and **the best of creatures**. What separates them is not information — both received it — but what they did once it arrived.

---

## 1. The Surah Allah Named a Man In

Before a word of exegesis, Ibn Kathir places a scene. **Imam Ahmad recorded from Anas bin Malik** that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to Ubayy bin Ka'b:

> *Verily, Allah has commanded me to recite to you: "Those who disbelieve from among the People of the Scripture."*

Ubayy asked: **"He mentioned me by name to you?"**

The Prophet ﷺ replied, *Yes.*

**So Ubayy cried.**

Al-Bukhari, Muslim, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i all recorded this from Shu'bah. The report is doing something the rest of the surah then explains: revelation is not an abstraction floating above history. It arrives, addressed, at a specific person, on a specific day — and it can bring a grown man to tears simply by being *for him*.

---

## 2. They Would Not Have Moved Without It (98:1)

> *Those who disbelieve from among the People of the Scripture and the idolators, were not going to leave until there came to them the Bayyinah.*

**Who is meant.** The People of the Scripture are **the Jews and the Christians**. The idolaters are **the worshippers of idols and of fire, among the Arabs and the non-Arabs** alike — the surah is not addressing one region.

**The disputed word** is *munfakkin* — "to leave," "to be parted from." **Mujahid** explains it as: *they will not be finished until the truth becomes clear to them.* **Qatadah** said the same. The picture is of people fixed in place, and the Bayyinah as the only thing capable of moving them.

**And the Bayyinah is the Qur'an.**

---

## 3. The Evidence Turns Out to Be a Man (98:2–3)

> *A Messenger from Allah reciting purified pages. Wherein are upright Books.*

Allah does not leave the term undefined. He explains it immediately, and the explanation is **Muhammad ﷺ and the Magnificent Qur'an he recites** — a Qur'an written down among the highest gathering, on pages that are pure.

Ibn Kathir sets alongside it the description in Surah 'Abasa (80:13–16): records held in honour, exalted and purified, in the hands of scribes — angels who are **honourable and obedient**. The chain of transmission is clean at every link before it ever reaches a human ear.

**Ibn Jarir** on *upright Books*: within those purified pages are Books from Allah that are **upright, just and straight** — *they have no mistakes in them, because they are from Allah, the Mighty and Majestic.*

---

## 4. The Division Came *After* the Clarity (98:4)

> *And the People of the Scripture differed not until after there came to them the Bayyinah.*

This is the surah's sharpest point. Ibn Kathir reads it as a statement about **the earlier nations**: after Allah established the proofs and evidences against them, they divided and differed over what Allah had intended in their own Scriptures — *and their differences were many.*

The parallel verse (3:105) makes the warning explicit: **be not as those who divided and differed among themselves after the Bayyinat came to them** — for them there is an awful torment.

And then the hadith, reported through many routes:

> *Verily, the Jews differed until they became seventy-one sects. And verily, the Christians differed until they became seventy-two sects. And this Ummah will divide into seventy-three sects, and all of them will be in the Fire except one.*

They asked, "Who are they, O Messenger of Allah?" He replied:

> ***What I and my Companions are upon.***

The verse describes a pattern; the hadith says the pattern does not exempt us.

---

## 5. What Was Actually Commanded (98:5)

> *And they were commanded not, but that they should worship Allah, making religion purely for Him alone, Hunafa', and that they perform Salah and give Zakah, and that is the right religion.*

Ibn Kathir unpacks it clause by clause:

- **Worship Allah, sincerely for Him alone** — the single message of every Messenger. *We did not send any Messenger before you but We revealed to him: there is no god but Me* (21:25).
- **Hunafa'** — turning away from Shirk and being **truly devout to Tawhid**. Compare 16:36: *We have sent among every Ummah a Messenger: worship Allah, and avoid the Taghut.* (He notes that *Hanif* was already discussed in Surah Al-An'am, and declines to repeat himself.)
- **Salah** — *the best of the physical forms of worship.*
- **Zakah** — *doing good to the poor and the needy.*
- **The right religion** — the upright and just religion; or, the nation that is **straight and balanced**.

Sincerity inward, prayer upward, charity outward. That is the whole requirement.

---

## 6. The Worst and the Best of Creation (98:6–8)

The surah ends by naming two groups in identical grammar, so the contrast cannot be missed.

**The worst of creatures** (98:6) — the wicked disbelievers among the People of the Scripture and the idolaters, who **opposed Allah's revealed Books and the Prophets He sent**. They will be in the Fire of Hell on the Day of Judgement, abiding forever: *they will remain in it, they will have no way out of it, and they will not cease being in it.*

**The best of creatures** (98:7) — those who **believed in their hearts and performed righteous deeds with their bodies**. Ibn Kathir notes that **Abu Hurayrah and a group of the scholars used this very Ayah** as proof that the believers hold a rank among creation **better than the angels** — because Allah calls them, without qualification, the best of creatures.

**Their reward** (98:8) — Eternal Gardens with rivers running beneath, abiding forever: *having no end, no break and no conclusion.* And then the line Ibn Kathir singles out:

> *Allah will be pleased with them, and they well-pleased with Him.*

**His pleasure with them is more illustrious than all of the everlasting delights they have been given** — the Gardens are not the summit; His being pleased is. And their pleasure with Him is due to the comprehensive favours He has poured on them.

**And who gets it?** *That is for him who fears his Lord* — meaning those who **revere Allah and fear Him as He deserves to be feared**: the person who worships Allah **as if he sees Him, knowing that though he does not see Him, Allah sees him.**

---

## 7. The Closing Hadith: Best and Worst, Made Concrete

Ibn Kathir ends the surah with a hadith from **Imam Ahmad, from Abu Hurayrah**, in which the Prophet ﷺ turns the abstraction into three portraits.

*Shall I not inform you of the best of creation?* — "Of course, O Messenger of Allah!"

> **A man who takes the reins of his horse in the way of Allah, and whenever there is a fearful cry from the enemy, he climbs upon it.**

*Shall I not inform you of the best of creation?* — "Of course!"

> **A man who has a flock of sheep, and he establishes the prayer and gives the obligatory charity.**

*Shall I not inform you of the worst of creation?* — "Of course."

> **The person who is asked by Allah and does not give for His sake.**

The first two are the surah's fifth verse in human form: one man ready at a moment's notice, one man quietly praying and paying from what he owns. Neither is spectacular. The worst of creation, likewise, is not defined by some monstrous crime — but by being asked **in Allah's name** and refusing.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Clear evidence was sent, and it was a Messenger.** Not a theory — a man reciting purified pages containing books with no error in them.
- **Division came after clarity, not before it.** The earlier nations split *once* the proof had reached them; the Prophet ﷺ warned that this Ummah would do the same, and named the one exception: what he and his Companions were upon.
- **The whole demand is four words long** — worship Allah alone and sincerely, be *Hanif*, pray, give. Ibn Kathir calls that *the right religion*, and it is what every Messenger before brought.
- **Believers are called the best of creatures** — a phrase some scholars, including Abu Hurayrah, read as ranking them above the angels.
- **Allah's pleasure outranks Paradise itself.** The Gardens are eternal, but Ibn Kathir says His being pleased with them is *more illustrious* than every delight in them.
- **The measure of it all is *ihsan*:** worshipping Allah as if you see Him, knowing that He sees you.
