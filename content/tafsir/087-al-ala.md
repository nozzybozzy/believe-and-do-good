*Revealed in Makkah · 19 ayat · "The Most High" — the surah of the two `Ids, of Jumu`ah, and of the short prayer the Prophet ﷺ told Mu`adh to lead with*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-A`la is a surah about **things being made easy**. Every claim in it is a claim about Allah doing the difficult part.

He creates and then proportions. He measures and then guides. He brings the pasture up out of dead ground and later turns it to dark stubble. He makes His Prophet ﷺ recite so that he will not forget. He makes the path itself easy. And having done all that, He asks for something small in return: **purify yourself, remember His Name, pray.**

The surah then names the one thing that stands between a person and that small return — not disbelief in the abstract, but a preference. *Rather, you prefer the life of this world.* Ibn Kathir's comment is almost gentle: you prefer it because of what it contains of usefulness and benefit for you, your livelihood, your returns. It is a rational-looking choice made on a false accounting.

And at the end, the surah does something remarkable: it says this same message — purify, remember, pray, and stop preferring the passing thing — **was already in the scrolls of Ibrahim and Musa.** Ibn Kathir sides with At-Tabari that "this" refers to the four verses immediately preceding. Nothing new is being asked. It is the oldest instruction there is.

---

## 1. Its Place in Prayer (Introduction)

Ibn Kathir establishes the Makkan origin from **Al-Bara' bin `Azib** (Bukhari), whose account is also a small history of how the Qur'an reached Madinah: Mus`ab bin `Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum came first and taught them; then `Ammar, Bilal and Sa`d; then `Umar with twenty; then the Prophet ﷺ himself — and Al-Bara' notes that he had already learned to recite *Sabbih isma Rabbika-l-A`la* and its likes **before** the Prophet ﷺ arrived. He also records the joy: even the children in the streets saying, *This is the Messenger of Allah who has come.*

Then the surah's liturgical life:

- **To Mu`adh** (in the Two Sahihs), after he lengthened the prayer for his people: *Why didn't you recite* Al-A`la (87), Ash-Shams (91), Al-Layl (92)? Three short surahs offered as the model for leading others.
- **The two `Ids**: from An-Nu`man bin Bashir (Ahmad, Muslim, Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah) — the Prophet ﷺ recited **Al-A`la and Al-Ghashiyah** in the `Id prayers, and if `Id fell on a Friday, he recited them in both prayers.
- **Al-A`la, Al-Kafirun, Al-Ikhlas** together — from Ubayy bin Ka`b, Ibn `Abbas, `Abdur-Rahman bin Abza and `A'ishah; `A'ishah added the **Mu`awwidhatayn** (Al-Falaq and An-Nas).

---

## 2. The Response the Verse Asks For (87:1)

> *Glorify the Name of your Lord, the Most High.*

The verse is a command, and the Prophet ﷺ **answered it out loud**. Ahmad records from Ibn `Abbas that whenever he recited it he would say: **Subhana Rabbiyal-A`la** — *Glory to my Lord, the Most High.* Qatadah reports the same.

Ibn `Abbas kept the habit, and had another like it: on reaching the end of Al-Qiyamah — *Is He not able to give life to the dead?* (75:40) — he would answer, **"Glory to You, of course."**

A small, transferable practice: recitation is not only reading. Some verses are addressed to you and expect a reply.

---

## 3. Created, Proportioned, Measured, Guided (87:2–5)

> *Who has created, and then proportioned it. And Who has measured; and then guided.*

**Created and proportioned** — He fashioned every creation in the best of its forms.

**Measured and guided** — **Mujahid**: He guided man to distress and to happiness, and He guided the cattle to their pastures. Ibn Kathir pairs this with Musa's answer to Fir`awn: *Our Lord is He Who gave to each thing its form and nature, then guided it aright* (20:50). A decree set, and the creature guided towards it.

He then cites Sahih Muslim, from `Abdullah bin `Amr:

> **Allah ordained the measure of all creation fifty thousand years before He created the heavens and the earth, and His Throne was over the water.**

**The pasturage** (87:4–5) — all types of vegetation and crops, brought up green, and then made *ghutha'an ahwa*: **Ibn `Abbas** glosses it as *dried up and altered* (Mujahid, Qatadah and Ibn Zayd similarly). The same hand does both. Growth and decay are one act, not two.

---

## 4. "You Shall Not Forget" (87:6–7)

> *We shall make you recite, so you shall not forget, except what Allah may will.*

This is Allah **informing and promising** the Prophet ﷺ that He will teach him a recitation he will not lose. **Qatadah**: he forgot nothing except what Allah willed.

Ibn Kathir preserves a second reading: that *fala tansa* is a prohibition — *do not forget* — and the exception covers **abrogation**. Meaning: do not let go of what We teach you, except what Allah wills to remove, and there is no sin on you for not retaining that.

The clause that closes it — *He knows what is apparent and what is hidden* — Ibn Kathir applies to all creatures, not just to revelation: He knows what they do openly and what they conceal, in word and deed. None of it is hidden from Him.

---

## 5. The Easy Path (87:8)

> *And We shall make easy for you the easy.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as covering both the person and the Law:

- **`We will make good deeds and good statements easy for you'** — the doing is eased, not just the knowing.
- **`And We will legislate for you a Law that is easy, tolerant, straight and just — with no crookedness, difficulty or hardship in it.'**

A one-line description of the Shari`ah from a fourteenth-century commentator, and worth holding onto whenever religion is presented as a burden.

---

## 6. Remind — But Know Whom You Are Talking To (87:9)

> *Therefore remind, in case the reminder profits.*

**Remind where reminding is beneficial.** From this, Ibn Kathir says, we derive **the etiquette of spreading knowledge: that it should not be wasted upon those who are not suitable or worthy of it.**

He supports it with two statements of **`Ali** — both of which are about the *listener's* capacity, not his worth:

> *"You do not tell people any statement that their intellects do not grasp except that it will be a Fitnah for some of them."*

> *"Tell people that which they know. Would you like for Allah and His Messenger to be rejected?"*

Teaching above someone's level is not generosity. It manufactures rejection.

---

## 7. Who Takes It, and Who Turns Away (87:10–13)

> *The reminder will be received by him who fears — but it will be avoided by the wretched, who will enter the great Fire. There he will neither die nor live.*

The one who benefits is the one **whose heart fears Allah and knows that he is going to meet Him.**

The one who avoids it enters a state Ibn Kathir describes precisely: he will not die and be allowed to rest, nor will he live a life of any benefit to him. His living *is* the harm — it is the very thing by which he feels the pain of the punishment.

Then two hadiths from **Abu Sa`id** (Ahmad; the second also in Muslim) that pull the sentence apart into two groups:

- **The people of the Fire who are its deserving dwellers** — they neither die nor live.
- **Those Allah wants mercy for** — the Fire burns them for their sins until they are like burnt coal; **He causes them to die in it**; then intercession is permitted, they are brought out group after group and scattered over the rivers of Paradise (*the River of Al-Haya*), and it is said: *"O people of Paradise, pour down upon them."* Then **they sprout like the seed that grows on the moist bank of a flowing stream.**

The Prophet ﷺ followed it with a question: *Haven't you all seen the tree that is green, then turns yellow, then turns green again?*

One of those present remarked that it was as if he ﷺ had lived in the desert wilderness — his images come from watching things grow. Note that the same picture from 87:4–5 returns here: green, then withered, then green. In the surah it described plants. In the hadith it describes people coming out of the Fire.

---

## 8. Whoever Purifies Himself (87:14–15)

> *Indeed whosoever purifies himself shall achieve success. And remembers the Name of his Lord, and performs Salah.*

**Purifies himself** — from despised characteristics, and follows what Allah revealed to the Messenger ﷺ.

**Remembers and prays** — he establishes the prayer **in its appointed time**, seeking Allah's pleasure, obeying His command, implementing His Law.

Ibn Kathir also records a second, concrete application that generations acted on:

- **`Umar bin `Abdul-`Aziz** used to command people to give **Sadaqat Al-Fitr** and recite this very ayah — purification (*tazakka*) read as the Zakah given *before* the `Id prayer, remembrance and Salah following it, exactly in the order of the verse.
- **Abu Al-Ahwas**: if a beggar comes to one of you and you want to pray, give the charity first — *for Allah says: Indeed whosoever purifies himself shall achieve success, and remembers the Name of his Lord, and prays.*
- **Qatadah**, in one line: **"He purifies his wealth and pleases his Creator."**

---

## 9. The Preference That Costs Everything (87:16–17)

> *Rather you prefer the life of this world. Although the Hereafter is better and more lasting.*

Ibn Kathir does not call this stupidity; he calls it **precedence**. You give this world priority over the matter of the Hereafter, and you prefer it for what it contains of usefulness and benefit to you — livelihood, income, gain.

Then the correction: the reward of the final abode is better *and* longer. **This life is lowly and temporal; the Hereafter is noble and eternal.** And so the questions:

> How can an intelligent person prefer the short-lived over the eternal? How can he give importance to what will soon pass away from him, while ignoring the abode of infinity?

Ahmad records from **Abu Musa Al-Ash`ari**:

> **Whoever loves his worldly life will suffer in his Hereafter, and whoever loves his Hereafter will suffer in his worldly life. So choose that which is everlasting over that which is temporal.**

---

## 10. This Was Always the Message (87:18–19)

> *Verily, this is in the former Scriptures — the Scriptures of Ibrahim and Musa.*

Two readings are given:

- **Abu `Aliyah**: the story of this Surah is in the earlier Scriptures.
- **Ibn Jarir At-Tabari** (whom Ibn Kathir prefers, calling the view **"good and strong"**, with Qatadah and Ibn Zayd reported similarly): *this* refers to the **four verses just before** — whoever purifies himself succeeds; remembers his Lord's Name and prays; yet you prefer this world; and the Hereafter is better and more lasting.

Ibn Kathir sets it beside An-Najm's near-identical appeal to the same two prophets (53:36–42): *that no bearer of burdens shall bear another's burden; that man shall have nothing but what he strives for; that his striving will be seen; then he will be fully recompensed; and that to your Lord is the final end.*

So the surah ends by removing the excuse of novelty. Purification, remembrance, prayer, and the refusal to be bought by the short-term — this is not a new demand made of the Quraysh. It is what was handed to Ibrahim and to Musa, arriving again.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**He does the hard part.** Created and proportioned, measured and guided, made to recite and not forget, made easy. Everything the surah asks of you sits on top of work already done.

**Answer the verse.** The Prophet ﷺ heard *Glorify the Name of your Lord, the Most High* and replied, aloud: *Subhana Rabbiyal-A`la.* Some ayat are instructions addressed to the reciter in the moment of reciting.

**Green, withered, green again.** The pasture He raises and turns to dark stubble is the same image He uses for people pulled out of the Fire and planted by a river. Nothing He has ended is beyond His restarting.

**Match the message to the mind in front of you.** *Remind, in case the reminder profits.* `Ali: telling people what their intellects cannot grasp turns knowledge into a trial for them. Restraint is part of teaching.

**The Law is meant to be liveable.** Ibn Kathir on *We shall make easy for you the easy*: easy, tolerant, straight and just — no crookedness, difficulty or hardship in it.

**The real disease is preference, not denial.** *Rather, you prefer the life of this world.* Most people never reject the Hereafter; they simply keep ranking it second, for perfectly sensible reasons, until the ranking is final.

**Purify, remember, pray — that is the whole résumé of success.** And it is old. It was in the scrolls of Ibrahim and Musa before it was recited in Makkah.
