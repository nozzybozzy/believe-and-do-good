*Revealed in Makkah · 7 ayat · "Small Kindnesses" — named for the last word of the last verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Ma'un is seven short verses that trace a single line from the deepest belief a person holds to the smallest object he refuses to lend — and the line never breaks.

It opens with a question about **creed**: have you seen the one who denies the Recompense? It then answers, immediately, with **behaviour**: *that is he who shoves the orphan away.* No argument is offered for the connection, because the surah treats it as self-evident. A man who is certain no reckoning is coming will treat the powerless exactly as his convenience dictates.

Then the surah does something unexpected. Having condemned the outright denier, it turns and pronounces **woe on people who pray** — and the charge against them is almost identical. They are heedless of their prayer, they perform for an audience, and they will not part with a bucket. The praying man and the denying man have arrived at the same place by different roads.

Its final word is the surah's argument in miniature. *Al-Ma'un* is not wealth, not Zakah, not charity — it is, on Ibn Mas'ud's reading, **an axe, a pot, a bucket**: things you lend and get back, undamaged. The surah measures a man not at the limit of his generosity but at the point where generosity costs him nothing at all. That is where it finds him refusing.

---

## 1. The One Who Denies Ad-Din (107:1)

> *Have you seen him who denies Ad-Din?*

**Din** here means **the Hereafter, the Recompense, and the Final Reward.** The address is to the Prophet ﷺ — *have you seen this man?* — as though pointing him out in a crowd, since the denial is invisible but its symptoms are not.

---

## 2. The Two Symptoms: The Orphan and the Miskin (107:2–3)

> *That is he who repulses the orphan, and urges not the feeding of Al-Miskin.*

**Repulsing the orphan** is not only pushing him away. Ibn Kathir spells it out as a whole disposition: he **oppresses** the orphan, **withholds his just due**, does not feed him, and is not kind to him. The orphan is chosen because he has no one to argue his case — which makes how you treat him an exact measure of what you believe about being answerable.

**And urges not the feeding of Al-Miskin** — note the verb. He is not merely failing to feed the poor man himself; he does not **encourage** anyone else to. Ibn Kathir pairs it with Al-Fajr:

> *Nay! But you treat not the orphans with kindness and generosity! And urge not one another on the feeding of Al-Miskin!* (89:17–18)

The **Miskin** is the destitute man who has nothing to sustain him or meet his needs.

---

## 3. The Turn: Woe to Those Who Pray (107:4–5)

> *So, woe unto those performers of Salah, those who with their Salah are Sahun.*

The verse is famously alarming, and Ibn Kathir keeps its edge. **Ibn Abbas and others:** this means **the hypocrites, who pray in public but do not pray in private.**

The wording matters. Allah says *woe to those who pray* — these are people who **pray and keep to the prayer, yet are heedless of it.** The problem is not the absence of prayer but the absence of the person inside it.

**What *Sahun* covers.** Ibn Kathir lists the readings rather than choosing between them:

- **The act itself entirely** — as Ibn Abbas held.
- **Its legislated time** — praying it completely outside the time (Masruq and Abu Ad-Duha).
- **The first time of the prayer** — habitually delaying until the very end of its window.
- **Its pillars and conditions** — not fulfilling them in the required manner.
- **Its humility** — praying without *khushu'* or any contemplation of what is being said.

His verdict: **the wording of the Ayah comprises all of these meanings.** Whoever has one of these traits, a portion of the verse applies to him; whoever has all of them has **completed his share of it, and the hypocrisy of actions is fulfilled in him.**

### The distinction that should be read carefully

**Ata' bin Dinar** noticed the preposition, and his comment is the most consoling line in the tafsir:

> *"All praise is due to Allah, the One Who said, 'with their Salah are Sahun' — and He did not say, 'those who are absent-minded in their prayer.'"*

The verse condemns being heedless **about** the prayer — treating it as a thing of no consequence — not the wandering thought that visits a person **inside** a prayer he is sincerely trying to hold on to. Every worshipper has the second. The surah is not about him.

### The prayer of the hypocrite

Confirmed in the Two Sahihs, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> **"This is the prayer of the hypocrite, this is the prayer of the hypocrite, this is the prayer of the hypocrite.** He sits watching the sun until it is between the two horns of Shaytan. Then he stands and pecks four (Rak'ahs), and he does not remember Allah in them except very little."

Ibn Kathir unpacks the scene: this is the **end of the time for 'Asr** — the middle prayer, established by text — and the moment it reaches is the disliked time. The man has waited out the entire window doing nothing, then stands and **pecks like a crow**, with no tranquillity and no humility in it at all. Hence: *he does not remember Allah except very little.* And the diagnosis of why he stood up at all — **he prays so people will see him praying, not seeking the Face of Allah** — which, Ibn Kathir says, **is as if he had not prayed at all.**

The Qur'an describes the same man:

> *Verily, the hypocrites seek to deceive Allah, but it is He Who deceives them. And when they stand up for Salah, they stand with laziness and to be seen of men, and they do not remember Allah but little.* (4:142)

## 4. Those Who Do Deeds to Be Seen (107:6)

> *Those who do good deeds only to be seen.*

**Imam Ahmad** records from Amr bin Murrah: they were sitting with Abu Ubaydah and the people mentioned showing off. A man called Abu Yazid said he heard Abdullah bin Amr report that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> **"Whoever tries to make the people hear of his deed, Allah — the One Who hears His creation — will hear it and make him despised and degraded."**

**And the essential qualification.** Ibn Kathir immediately distinguishes *riya'* from something that resembles it:

> Whoever does a deed **solely for Allah**, but the people come to know about it, and he is pleased with that — **this is not considered showing off.**

The sin is in the intention at the moment of acting, not in the deed becoming known afterwards.

---

## 5. And Withhold Al-Ma'un (107:7)

> *And withhold Al-Ma'un.*

Ibn Kathir reads the final verse as the summary of both failures at once: **they do not worship their Lord well, nor do they treat His creation well.**

Then he makes the point as sharp as it will go. These are people who **will not even lend the things others benefit from — items that will come back intact and undamaged.** Not given away. Borrowed and returned. And if that is where they stand, then **they are stingier still when it comes to Zakah and the kinds of charity that bring a person closer to Allah.**

**Al-Mas'udi** narrated from Salamah bin Kuhayl, from Abu Al-Ubaydin, that he asked **Ibn Mas'ud** about Al-Ma'un, and he said:

> **"It is what the people give to each other — like an axe, a pot, a bucket, and similar items."**

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Belief in the Recompense is visible in how you treat people who cannot answer back.** The surah proves the first by pointing at the second, and offers no other proof.
- **The orphan and the Miskin are the test case**, and the charge is not only failing to feed them — it is failing to **urge** anyone else to.
- **"Woe to those who pray"** is directed at people who keep the prayer while being heedless *about* it: outside its time, delayed to its edge, missing its pillars, empty of humility, or performed for an audience.
- **Ata' bin Dinar's relief:** Allah said heedless **of** their prayer, not heedless **in** it. The wandering mind of a sincere worshipper is not what is being condemned.
- **Riya' is about intention, not publicity.** A deed done purely for Allah that later becomes known — and pleases you — is not showing off.
- **The last word is the smallest one.** The surah ends its indictment not at Zakah but at an axe, a pot, and a bucket: things lent and returned unharmed. A religion that cannot survive the loan of a bucket has already failed at the reckoning it claims to believe in.
