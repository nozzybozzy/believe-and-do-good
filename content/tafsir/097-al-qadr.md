*Revealed in Makkah · 5 ayat · "The Decree" / "The Measure" — named for the night on which the Qur'an came down*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Qadr is five verses about **one night**, and the whole surah turns on a single claim: that a stretch of hours can be worth more than a lifetime.

It opens without naming what was sent down — *Verily, **We** have sent **it** down* — because the pronoun needs no antecedent. Then it asks the reader a question it knows he cannot answer: *what will make you know what the Night of Al-Qadr is?* The answer given is not a description but a **conversion rate**: better than a thousand months. Roughly eighty-three years. More than most people get.

The rest is what happens on it. The sky opens and the angels come down in numbers Ibn Kathir compares to pebbles on the earth, led by Ar-Ruh. Every matter of the coming year is measured out — provisions, deaths, decrees. And Shaytan is locked out of it entirely, so that from sunset to Fajr the night is **only good, with no evil in it at all**.

The name itself carries the two meanings the surah runs on. *Qadr* is **decree** — the night things are determined. And *Qadr* is **worth** — the night's own value, and the value it confers on whoever finds it.

Then, having established all of that, the tafsir spends most of its length on the question the surah deliberately never answers: **which night?** Ibn Kathir assembles the entire disagreement — the twenty-first, third, fifth, seventh, ninth, every Ramadan, a moving target — and lands, with the majority of the imams, on the position that it *shifts from year to year* through the last ten. The night is hidden so that you look for it, and looking for it costs you ten nights of standing. That is the point of the concealment.

---

## 1. "We Sent It Down" (97:1)

> *Verily, We have sent it down in the Night of Al-Qadr.*

Allah informs that He sent the Qur'an down during this night, which He describes elsewhere as **blessed**:

> *We sent it down on a blessed night.* (44:3)

And He fixes it inside Ramadan:

> *The month of Ramadan in which was revealed the Qur'an.* (2:185)

**The two descents.** Ibn Abbas and others resolve how a book revealed over twenty-three years can be said to have come down on a single night:

- Allah sent the Qur'an down **all at once** from **Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz** (the Preserved Tablet) to **Baytul-'Izzah** (the House of Might) in the heaven of this world.
- From there it descended **in parts** to the Messenger ﷺ, according to the events that occurred, over twenty-three years.

So the Night of Al-Qadr is the night of the Qur'an's *complete* descent — and the honour of the night comes from what was placed in it. Allah magnified the status of this night precisely because He chose it for the revelation of the Mighty Qur'an.

---

## 2. Better Than a Thousand Months (97:2–3)

> *And what will make you know what the Night of Al-Qadr is? The Night of Al-Qadr is better than a thousand months.*

**The month it sits inside.** Imam Ahmad and An-Nasa'i record from Abu Hurayrah that when Ramadan came, the Prophet ﷺ would say:

> *Verily, the month of Ramadan has come to you all. It is a blessed month, which Allah has obligated you all to fast. During it the gates of Paradise are opened, the gates of Hell are closed and the devils are shackled. In it there is a night that is better than one thousand months. **Whoever is deprived of its good, then he has truly been deprived.***

That last line is the surah's sharpest edge. The night is not merely an opportunity — missing it is described as **deprivation**, a loss suffered rather than a bonus foregone.

**Two separate gifts.** Ibn Kathir is careful to note that the thousand-month equivalence is only one of the night's virtues. *Aside from* the fact that worship on it equals worship performed for a thousand months, it is confirmed in the Two Sahihs from Abu Hurayrah:

> *Whoever stands (in prayer) during the Night of Al-Qadr with faith and expecting reward (from Allah), he will be forgiven for his previous sins.*

Note the two conditions attached: **Imanan** (with faith — believing in the promise) and **ihtisaban** (seeking the reward from Allah alone, not the appearance of piety). The night rewards intention, not attendance.

---

## 3. The Angels Descend (97:4)

> *Therein descend the angels and the Ruh by their Lord's permission with every matter.*

**Why so many.** The angels descend *in abundance* on this night because its blessings are abundant. Ibn Kathir gives the principle behind it: **angels descend with the descent of blessing and mercy**. It is the same pattern by which they come down when the Qur'an is recited, surround the circles of *Dhikr*, and — in one of the most quietly beautiful images in the tafsir — **lower their wings in true respect for the student of knowledge**.

Abu Hurayrah's narration (recorded by Ahmad alone, with a sound chain) gives the scale: **the angels on the earth that night are more numerous than the number of pebbles.**

**Who is Ar-Ruh.** It is said to mean **Jibril**. He is named separately from "the angels" as a mark of distinction — singling out the particular from the general, a form the Qur'an uses to honour.

**"With every matter."** Mujahid reads it as *peace concerning every matter*. Qatadah and others read it as the decreeing itself: **the matters are determined during it, and the times of death and provisions are measured out.** Allah confirms this in the parallel verse:

> *Therein is decreed every matter of decree.* (44:4)

---

## 4. Peace Until Fajr (97:5)

> *There is peace until the appearance of dawn.*

Three readings, and they fit together rather than compete:

- **Mujahid** (via Sa'id bin Mansur ← Isa bin Yunus ← Al-A'mash): it is **security — a night in which Shaytan cannot do any evil or any harm.**
- **Ash-Sha'bi**: it is the **angels greeting with peace** those standing in the Masjids, until the coming of Fajr.
- **Qatadah and Ibn Zayd**: **all of it is good, and there is no evil in it** until dawn breaks.

So the night has a hard boundary. It begins at sunset and ends at Fajr — not at some point of achievement, and not once you feel something. The clock is the only measure.

---

## 5. Which Night? — The Whole Disagreement

Ibn Kathir lays out every position with its evidence, and this section is really a lesson in how the scholars weighed reports.

**Everyone agrees on the frame.** From Ubadah bin As-Samit (Ahmad):

> *The Night of Al-Qadr occurs during the last ten. Whoever stands for them seeking their reward, Allah will forgive his previous sins and his latter sins. It is an odd night: the ninth, or the seventh, or the fifth, or the third, or the last night.*

Beyond that, the candidates:

| Position | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Any night of any Ramadan** | Abu Dawud's chapter, from Ibn Umar. The men of the chain are reliable — but Shu'bah and Sufyan both treated it as **Ibn Umar's own statement**, not the Prophet's ﷺ |
| **The 21st** | The mud-and-water hadith below. Ash-Shafi'i: *"the most authentic of what has been reported"* |
| **The 23rd** | 'Abdullah bin Unays, in Sahih Muslim |
| **The 25th** | Al-Bukhari, from Ibn Abbas |
| **The 27th** | Muslim, from Ubayy bin Ka'b — who swore to it |
| **The 29th** | Reported; and Abu Hurayrah's hadith pairs it with the 27th |
| **It moves each year** | Abu Qilabah (At-Tirmidhi) — and this is where Ibn Kathir lands |

**The mud-and-water hadith** (Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri; Al-Bukhari and Muslim). The Prophet ﷺ made I'tikaf the first ten nights; Jibril came and said *what you seek is ahead of you*. He made I'tikaf the middle ten; Jibril said it again. On the morning of the twentieth he stood and said:

> *Whoever performed I'tikaf with me, let him come back — for verily I saw the Night of Al-Qadr, and **I was caused to forget it**, and indeed it is during the last ten. It is an odd night, and I saw myself as if I were prostrating in mud and water.*

The Masjid roof was dried palm leaves and there was not a cloud in the sky. Then a patch of wind-driven cloud came and it rained; he led them in prayer, and the Companions saw **the traces of mud and water on his forehead** — the dream confirmed that same night. One narration specifies the morning of the twenty-first.

**Ibn Abbas's counting hadith** (Al-Bukhari):

> *Seek it in the last ten of Ramadan. In the ninth it still remains, in the seventh it still remains, in the fifth it still remains.*

Most have explained this as referring to the **odd nights**, and Ibn Kathir calls that the most apparent and most popular reading.

**Ubayy bin Ka'b's certainty** (Ahmad, Muslim). Zirr told Ubayy that Ibn Mas'ud said whoever stands in prayer the entire year will catch the Night of Al-Qadr. Ubayy replied: *"May Allah have mercy upon him. Indeed he knows that it is during Ramadan and that it is the twenty-seventh night"* — and then swore by Allah. Asked how he knew, he said: **by the sign the Prophet ﷺ told us of — the sun rises the next morning with no rays on it.**

*(Ibn Mas'ud's position is worth noting for what it implies: standing the whole year guarantees you catch it — which is an argument for concealment, not a claim about a date.)*

**Ibn Kathir's conclusion.** The view that it **moves from year to year through the last ten nights** — held by Malik, Ath-Thawri, Ahmad bin Hanbal, Ishaq bin Rahuyah, Abu Thawr, Al-Muzani, Ibn Khuzaymah and others, and related from Ash-Shafi'i — is, in his words, **"most likely. And Allah knows best."**

## 6. Its Signs

From Ubadah bin As-Samit — a chain Ibn Kathir grades **good**, while noting frankly that **the text contains some oddities and some objectionable wordings**:

- The night is **pure and glowing**, as if a bright, tranquil, calm moon were in it
- **Neither cold nor hot**
- **No shooting star** is permitted until morning
- The next morning **the sun rises smooth, with no rays** — like the moon on a full-moon night
- Shaytan is not permitted to come out with the sun that day

The sun-without-rays sign is the one corroborated independently by Ubayy bin Ka'b, which is why it is the most cited of the group.

## 7. The Supplication of the Night

It is recommended to supplicate often at all times, **especially in Ramadan, more so in the last ten nights, and most of all on the odd nights** among them.

'A'ishah asked: *"O Messenger of Allah, if I find the Night of Al-Qadr, what should I say?"* He ﷺ replied:

> **اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ عَفُوٌّ تُحِبُّ الْعَفْوَ فَاعْفُ عَنِّي**
>
> *"O Allah! Verily, You are the Oft-Pardoning, You love to pardon, so pardon me."*

Recorded by At-Tirmidhi (**Hasan Sahih**), An-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah; and by Al-Hakim in the *Mustadrak* through a different chain, which he graded authentic by the criteria of Al-Bukhari and Muslim.

Consider what is being asked for. Given a night worth eighty-three years and one question to ask, the answer taught is not wealth, not knowledge, not even Paradise directly — it is **'afw**: erasure. Not merely forgiveness of a sin, but the wiping away of the record of it. And the du'a argues its case before it asks: *You are the One who pardons; You **love** to pardon* — the request is grounded in what Allah is, not in what the asker deserves.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **The night is honoured by what came down in it.** The Qur'an descended complete to the lowest heaven on this night, then in installments over twenty-three years. Value the night the way you value the Book.
- **Better than a thousand months** — over eighty-three years of worship, available annually, in one night. And missing it is called *deprivation*.
- **Two conditions on the forgiveness**: faith, and seeking the reward from Allah. Standing without either is just a late night.
- **Angels outnumber the pebbles**, led by Ar-Ruh — Jibril — and Shaytan is locked out. From sunset to Fajr the night contains **no evil at all**.
- **Your year is measured out in it**: provisions, deaths, every matter of decree. Which is a reason to be awake and asking.
- **It is hidden on purpose**, and it most likely **moves each year** within the last ten. The concealment is the instruction: search all ten, weight the odd nights, and give the twenty-seventh its due without staking everything on it.
- **One du'a above all others**: *Allahumma innaka 'Afuwwun, tuhibbu-l-'afwa, fa'fu 'anni.* You are asking the One who loves to pardon to do the thing He loves.
