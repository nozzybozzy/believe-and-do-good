*18 ayat · "The Dwellings/Chambers" — named for the private rooms of the Prophet's ﷺ wives, from behind which the Bedouins shouted (49:4)*

> *A note on the header: the PDF copy is labelled "Revealed in Makkah," but Al-Hujurat is unanimously Madani — every occasion of revelation Ibn Kathir cites (the Bani Tamim delegation, the Aws and Khazraj, the Ansar, the Bedouin tribes) belongs to Madinah.*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Hujurat is the Qur'an's charter of manners — and it builds outward in a single, deliberate arc.

It begins with **how you behave toward Allah and His Messenger** (don't get ahead of him, don't shout at him, don't summon him like a neighbour). It moves to **how you handle information** (verify what a corrupt source tells you). Then to **how you handle conflict** (reconcile fighting believers; they remain brothers even while fighting). Then to **how you speak of and about one another** (no mockery, no slurs, no suspicion, no spying, no backbiting). Then to **how you rank one another** (you don't — lineage confers nothing; taqwa is the only currency). And it closes with **how you rank yourself** (the Bedouins claimed *iman* when they had only reached *islam*).

The logic is unbroken: reverence at the top produces restraint in speech, which produces peace in the community, which dissolves the hierarchies people invent, which finally turns the mirror on the claimant himself. **A surah about how to talk ends by telling you not to overstate what you are.**

---

## 1. Do Not Get Ahead of Allah and His Messenger (49:1)

> *O you who believe! Make not (a decision) in advance before Allah and His Messenger.*

Meaning, says Ibn Kathir: **do not rush to decide before him — follow his lead in all matters.**

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah): "Do not say anything that contradicts the Qur'an and Sunnah."
- **Qatadah**: some people used to say, *"Revelation should be sent down about such and such,"* and *"such and such should be made permissible."* Allah disliked this attitude.

Then: *have Taqwa of Allah* — in what He has ordered. *Verily, Allah is Hearing* (your statements), *Knowing* (your intentions).

---

## 2. Lower Your Voice (49:2–3)

> *Raise not your voices above the voice of the Prophet... lest your deeds should be thwarted while you perceive not.*

**The occasion.** Al-Bukhari, from Ibn Abi Mulaykah: *"The two righteous ones, Abu Bakr and Umar, almost earned destruction"* when the delegation of Bani Tamim arrived. Abu Bakr recommended one man as their leader, Umar another (Al-Aqra bin Habis). Abu Bakr said, *"You only wanted to contradict me."* Umar: *"I did not intend to contradict you."* Their voices rose — and the ayah came down.

**The aftermath.** Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr: after that, **Umar's voice was so low that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had to ask him to repeat himself** to understand what he was saying.

**Thabit bin Qays.** The most moving story in the surah. Thabit — a man with a naturally loud voice — concluded he was doomed, shut himself in his house, and stopped coming. He told the man sent to check on him: *"An evil matter! My deeds have been rendered useless and I am among the people of the Fire."* The Prophet ﷺ replied:

> *"Go back to him and tell him: you are not among the people of the Fire. Rather, you are among the dwellers of Paradise."*

Anas adds the ending: *"We used to see Thabit walk among us knowing that he was to be among the dwellers of Paradise."* At the battle of **Yamamah**, when the Muslim lines broke, Thabit appeared **having already put on his burial perfume and shrouds**, said *"the worst habit is the one you acquire from your enemy,"* and fought until he was martyred.

**Why the warning is so severe.** Angering the Prophet ﷺ angers Allah — and so the deeds of the one who caused it can be erased **without him knowing**. Ibn Kathir cites the Sahih:

> *A man might utter a word that pleases Allah, not recognising its significance, and Paradise is written for him on account of it. And a man might utter a word carelessly that angers Allah, and on its account he is cast into the Fire farther than the distance between the heavens and the earth.*

Compare 24:63 — *Make not the calling of the Messenger among you as your calling one of another.*

**And the reward (49:3):** those who lower their voices are *"the ones whose hearts Allah has tested for Taqwa"* — hearts purified by Allah and made a dwelling place for taqwa. For them: forgiveness and a great reward.

> **Umar was asked**: who is better — a man who feels no desire to sin, or one who feels it and does not act? He answered: *the one who feels the desire and does not commit it* — and recited this ayah.

---

## 3. Don't Shout at the Door (49:4–5)

Uncivilised Bedouins would stand outside the chambers of the Prophet's ﷺ wives and call out — *"O Muhammad! O Muhammad!"* — and be told by Allah Himself that **most of them have no sense**. Had they waited until he came out, *it would have been better for them* — better in this life and the next.

Then, immediately: *And Allah is Forgiving, Most Merciful.* Ibn Kathir reads this as an invitation to repentance, tacked onto the rebuke. More than one source says the ayah concerned **Al-Aqra bin Habis At-Tamimi**.

---

## 4. Verify the News (49:6)

> *If a wicked person comes to you with any news, ascertain the truth, lest ye harm people unwittingly, and afterwards become full of repentance.*

If the word of a sinner is taken at face value and acted upon, **the authorities end up being led by the corrupt**. Ibn Kathir draws the direct methodological consequence:

> This is why groups of the scholars of Hadith **refuse to accept narrations from narrators whose reliability is unknown** — for they might, in reality, be from among the wicked.

One ayah about a rumour becomes the foundation of an entire science of transmission.

---

## 5. The Messenger's Judgement Is Better Than Yours (49:7–8)

> *And know that among you there is the Messenger of Allah. If he were to obey you in much of the matter, you would surely be in trouble.*

Honour him, be polite with him, obey him — **for he knows what benefits you better than you do, and is more concerned for you than you are for yourself.** (Cf. 33:6: *The Prophet is closer to the believers than themselves.*)

And if the truth followed people's desires, 23:71 answers what would happen: *the heavens and the earth and whoever is in them would be corrupted.*

**Then the gift:** *But Allah has endeared the faith to you and has beautified it in your hearts, and has made disbelief, Fusuq and 'Isyan hateful to you.* Faith made dear to the soul; kufr, major and minor sins, and all disobedience made repellent. Ibn Kathir: *"This statement transfers us from one level to a better level, to perfect Allah's bounty."* Such are the rightly guided — **a grace from Allah and His favour**, not an achievement you can invoice Him for.

He records the du'a the Prophet ﷺ made at Uhud, which contains these very words:

> *O Allah! There is none who can withhold what You send, or send what You withhold, or guide whom You send astray, or misguide whom You guide... O Allah! Make faith dear to us and beautify it in our hearts, and make disbelief, Fusuq and 'Isyan hateful to us, and make us among the rightly guided.*

---

## 6. When Believers Fight Each Other (49:9–10)

> *And if two parties among the believers fall to fighting, then make peace between them both... The believers are but a brotherhood.*

**The doctrinal point Ibn Kathir highlights:** Allah calls both warring groups **believers, while they are fighting**. Al-Bukhari and others relied on this as proof that **committing a sin does not nullify faith, however major** — refuting the Khawarij, and the Mu'tazilah who followed them in this.

**The prophecy fulfilled.** The Prophet ﷺ on the minbar, looking between Al-Hasan bin Ali and the people:

> *"Verily, this son of mine is a Sayyid, and may Allah make peace between two great groups of Muslims through him."*

And it happened: **Al-Hasan brought peace between the people of Ash-Sham and Iraq** after enormous wars.

**Fight the aggressor** — until it returns to the command of Allah and His Messenger. Which is a form of help, not hostility:

> *"Help your brother, whether he is an oppressor or he is oppressed."*
> *"O Messenger of Allah, how do I help him if he is an oppressor?"*
> *"By preventing him from oppressing others — this is how you help him."*

**Occasions:** Sa'id bin Jubayr — a scuffle between **Aws and Khazraj** using date-palm branches and slippers. As-Suddi — a domestic dispute: a man named Imran locked his wife Umm Zayd in an upper room to stop her visiting her family; her family came to take her; his clan intervened; it descended into a brawl with sandals. **The Prophet ﷺ sent someone to reconcile them, and both sides submitted to Allah's judgement.**

**Then be equitable** — *Verily, Allah loves those who are equitable.* And: *those who are equitable in this life will be on podiums of pearls before Ar-Rahman, on account of their fairness.*

**Brotherhood, in four hadiths:**
- *The Muslim is the brother of the Muslim: he is not unjust with him, nor does he forsake him.*
- *Allah helps the servant as long as the servant helps his brother.*
- *When a Muslim supplicates for his absent brother, the angel says: "Amin — and for you the same."*
- *The believers, in their mutual kindness, mercy and compassion, are like one body: when one organ falls ill, the whole body responds with fever and sleeplessness.* And: *like a building whose parts reinforce each other* — at which the Prophet ﷺ interlaced his fingers.

## 7. Mockery, Slander, Nicknames (49:11)

> *Let not a group scoff at another group, it may be that the latter are better than the former.*

The reason is left explicit: **the one being belittled may be more honoured and dearer to Allah than the one belittling.** You are laughing at a rank you cannot see. The prohibition is stated for men, then repeated for women.

> *Arrogance is refusing the truth and belittling people.* (Another version: *and despising people.*)

**"Nor defame yourselves."** Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah and Muqatil: *none of you should defame each other* — the community is one self, exactly as *nor kill yourselves* (4:29) means do not kill one another. Cf. 104:1, *Woe to every Humazah, Lumazah* — **Hamz is defamation by action, Lamz by words**; and 68:11, *Hammaz — going about with slander.*

**"Nor insult one another by nicknames."** Abu Jabirah bin Ad-Dahhak: *"This ayah was revealed about us, Banu Salamah. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came to Al-Madinah, every man among us had two or three nicknames. When he called a man by one of them, people would say: 'O Messenger of Allah, he hates that name.'"*

*Evil is the name of wickedness after faith* — i.e. to carry on using the labels of Jahiliyyah **after you have embraced Islam and understood it**. And whoever does not repent of this is named, by Allah, a wrongdoer.

---

## 8. Suspicion, Spying, Backbiting (49:12)

Three sins, in escalating order of intimacy: what you *think*, what you *seek out*, and what you *say*.

**Suspicion.** Doubts about the conduct of family, relatives, and people generally — to be avoided where there is no foundation.

> **Umar bin Al-Khattab**: *"Never think ill of the word that comes out of your believing brother's mouth, as long as you can find a good excuse for it."*

> *Beware of suspicion, for suspicion is the worst of false tales.* Then: do not spy; do not look for others' faults; do not be jealous; do not envy; do not hate; do not shun one another. **And be brothers, O servants of Allah.** (With the added ruling elsewhere: *no Muslim may shun his brother for more than three days.*)

**Spying.** Al-Awza'i's distinction: **Tajassus** is searching out (usually with ill intent — hence *jasus*, a spy); **Tahassus** is listening to people without permission or eavesdropping at their doors; **Tadabur** is shunning one another. Tahassus can be innocent — Ya'qub used the word: *Go and inquire about Yusuf and his brother* (12:87) — but both can carry evil connotations, and the Prophet ﷺ forbade both.

**Backbiting — defined.** Asked what ghibah is, he ﷺ said: *Mentioning about your brother in a manner that he dislikes.* — *"And if it is true of him?"* — *If he is as you said, you have backbitten him. If he is not, you have slandered him.*

**Backbiting — depicted.** *Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother? You would hate it.* Ibn Kathir: **hate backbiting by your religion just as you hate that by your nature — and the punishment for the former is worse.**

Two images seal it:
- **Ma'iz.** After he was stoned, the Prophet ﷺ overheard two men disparaging him. Passing the carcass of a donkey, he told them: *"Dismount and eat from this."* They recoiled. He said: *"What you took from your brother's flesh is a fouler meal than this. By Him in whose Hand is my soul — he is now swimming in the rivers of Paradise."*
- **Jabir bin Abdullah**: a rotten odour blew over them, and the Prophet ﷺ said, *"Do you know what this odour is? It is the odour of those who backbite people."*

And from the Farewell Hajj: *Your blood, your wealth and your honour are as sacred among you as this day, in this month, in this town.*

**Repentance.** *Allah is the One Who forgives and accepts repentance, Most Merciful.* The majority hold that repenting from backbiting means resolving never to return to it. Scholars differ on whether remorse is required, and whether you must seek the victim's pardon — many say **you should not tell him**, since knowing would wound him more than the offence did. Better: **praise him in the same gatherings where you disparaged him, and defend him against backbiting as far as you can.**

## 9. The Abolition of Lineage (49:13)

> *O mankind! We have created you from a male and a female, and made you into nations and tribes that you may know one another. Verily, the most honourable of you with Allah is the one with the most Taqwa.*

Note the address: not *O you who believe* but **O mankind**. All descend from Adam and Hawwa; all share this honour equally. *Shu'ub* and *qaba'il* are nations and tribes (one view: nations = non-Arabs, tribes = Arabs). Their purpose is **identification, not ranking** — Mujahid: so one may say *"so-and-so, son of so-and-so, of such-and-such tribe."* Sufyan Ath-Thawri notes the Himyar organised themselves by province, the Arabs of the Hijaz by tribe.

Ibn Kathir's placement is deliberate: **after forbidding backbiting and belittling, Allah alerts mankind that they are all equal in their humanity.** The only real difference is obedience to Allah and following His Messenger.

**"Who is the most honourable of people?"** — *The one with the most Taqwa.* — *"We did not ask about this."* — *Then Yusuf: a Prophet, son of a Prophet, son of a Prophet, son of the Khalil of Allah.* — *"We did not ask about this."* — *Then you're asking about Arab lineage? Those who were best in Jahiliyyah are the best in Islam — if they attain religious understanding.*

> *Verily, Allah does not look at your shapes or your wealth, but He looks at your hearts and your actions.*

**And on the day Makkah was conquered**, from atop Al-Qaswa':

> *O people! Allah has removed from you the slogans of Jahiliyyah and its boasting of forefathers. Men are of two types: a righteous man, fearful of Allah and honourable to Him — and a vicious man, miserable and of little worth to Him.* Then he recited this ayah, and added: *"I say this and ask Allah's forgiveness for me and for you."*

**A legal consequence:** several scholars relied on this ayah and these hadiths to hold that **kafa'ah (social compatibility) is not a condition of a valid marriage contract** — the only condition in this regard is adherence to the religion.

## 10. "We Have Submitted" — Islam vs Iman (49:14–18)

> *The Bedouins say: "We believe." Say: "You do not believe. But say, 'We have submitted,' for Faith has not yet entered your hearts."*

**The core teaching:** according to Ahl us-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, **Iman is a higher grade than Islam** — as shown here and in the hadith of Jibril, which moves from the general (Islam) to the more specific (Iman) to the most specific (Ihsan).

Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas once pressed the Prophet ﷺ about a man passed over in a distribution: *"He is a believer."* Three times the Prophet ﷺ corrected him: **"Or say, a Muslim."** Then he explained: *I give to some and withhold from others who are dearer to me — I withhold for fear they be thrown on their faces into the Fire.*

**Crucially, these Bedouins were not hypocrites.** Ibn Kathir is explicit (with Ibn Abbas, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Qatadah, and Ibn Jarir's preference): they were Muslims in whose hearts faith was not yet firmly established. Their fault was **claiming a higher grade than they had earned** — and they were corrected, not condemned. The verse ends: *if you obey Allah and His Messenger, He will not decrease anything of your deeds' reward* (cf. 52:21), *Verily, Allah is Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

**Who, then, are the real believers? (49:15)** Those who believed in Allah and His Messenger, **then did not doubt** — no wavering, conviction intact — and who **strove with their wealth and their lives**, gladly giving the most precious of both seeking His pleasure. *Those! They are the truthful.*

**(49:16)** *Will you inform Allah of your religion?* — will you inform Him of what is in your own hearts? Nothing in the heavens or earth, down to the weight of a speck, escapes Him.

**(49:17) The favour runs the other way.** They counted their Islam as a favour done to the Prophet ﷺ. Allah reverses it: *Nay, but Allah has conferred a favour upon you that He has guided you to the Faith.* The benefit of your Islam is **yours**.

Ibn Kathir sets beside this the Prophet's ﷺ words to the Ansar at Hunayn: *Did I not find you astray, and Allah guided you through me? Were you not divided, and Allah united you around me? Were you not poor, and Allah enriched you through me?* — to which they answered every time: *"Allah and His Messenger have most favoured us."* **That is the correct posture.** (Al-Bazzar records the ayah's occasion as Banu Asad, who came saying: *"We embraced Islam, and we never fought you as the Arabs did"* — and were told, *"They understand but little, and the Shaytan speaks through their words."*)

**(49:18)** And it closes where it must: *Verily, Allah knows the Unseen of the heavens and the earth. And Allah is the All-Seer of what you do.* — He sees the claim, and He sees whether it is true.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Manners are not the decoration on faith — they are the evidence of it.** Al-Hujurat spends eighteen ayat establishing that a man's deeds can be erased by his volume, that his rank is invisible to everyone who mocks him, that his brother's flesh is on the menu every time he gossips, and that his tribe buys him nothing at all.

The surah's two hinges are worth memorising together:

> *Verily, the most honourable of you with Allah is the one with the most Taqwa.* (49:13)
> *Say: "You do not believe. But say, 'We have submitted.'"* (49:14)

The first removes every external ladder people climb. The second removes the internal one. **What is left is a heart Allah has tested for taqwa, a tongue that is careful, and a community that is one body.**

And the pattern of mercy runs throughout: Abu Bakr and Umar *"almost"* earned destruction — but didn't. Thabit thought he was in the Fire — and was told he was in Paradise. The Bedouins overclaimed — and were taught, not cast out. Ma'iz was disparaged in death — and was swimming in the rivers of Paradise. **Every rebuke in this surah arrives with a door left open.**
