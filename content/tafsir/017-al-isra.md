*Revealed in Makkah · 111 ayat · "The Night Journey" — also called Bani Isra'il, "The Children of Israel"*

---

## 0. The Shape of the Surah

The surah is bracketed by two journeys: one man taken up through the heavens at the start, and at the end, the Qur'an itself brought down in stages. Between them sits the reason for both.

1. **The Night Journey** (1)
2. **Musa's Book, and the two corruptions of his people** (2–8)
3. **The Qur'an, time, and the record of deeds** (9–14)
4. **Justice: no burden carried for another, no punishment before a warning** (15–21)
5. **The Charter** — eighteen ayat of law and manners (22–39)
6. **Against the idolators' theology** (40–48)
7. **Resurrection, speech, and the sovereignty of Allah** (49–60)
8. **Iblis, and the limits of his reach** (61–65)
9. **Sea, land, and the honouring of Adam's children** (66–72)
10. **The Prophet: protected, commanded, promised** (73–84)
11. **The Ruh, the challenge, and the demands of Quraysh** (85–100)
12. **Musa's nine signs, and the Qur'an sent down piece by piece** (101–111)

---

## 1. Its Standing

Ibn Mas'ud said of this surah, together with Al-Kahf and Maryam, that they are among the earliest and most beautiful surahs — and he called them **his treasure**.

A'ishah described the Prophet's ﺺ habit of reciting **Bani Isra'il and Az-Zumar every night**.

---

## 2. The Night Journey (17:1)

> *Glorified be He Who took His servant by night from Al-Masjid Al-Haram to Al-Masjid Al-Aqsa, the neighbourhood whereof We have blessed, in order that We might show him of Our signs.*

Ibn Kathir takes the ayah word by word before touching a single hadith:

| Phrase | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| **Glorified be He** | Allah glorifies Himself for doing what none but He can do |
| **His servant** | Muhammad ﺺ — and *'abd* means body and soul together, not soul alone |
| **by night** | in the depths of the night |
| **to Al-Masjid Al-Aqsa** | Jerusalem, the home of the Prophets since Ibrahim — where he ﺺ **led them all in prayer, in their own homeland**, proof that he is the greatest of them |
| **blessed neighbourhood** | its crops and fruits |
| **All-Hearer, All-Seer** | He hears every servant, believer and disbeliever, and gives each what he deserves |

**The journey itself.** Al-Buraq was brought — a white animal larger than a donkey, smaller than a mule, whose single stride reached the horizon. He ﺺ tethered it at the hitching post of the Prophets, prayed two rak'ahs, and was offered vessels. He chose the milk over the wine, and Jibril said: *You have chosen the Fitrah.* One version adds the warning — had he taken the wine, his Ummah would have gone astray.

Then the ascent, heaven by heaven, with the same exchange at every gate — *Who is this? Jibril. Who is with you? Muhammad. Has his Mission started? It has started.* — and in each, a Prophet:

- **Adam**, with the souls of his descendants massed to his right and left; he smiles looking right, weeps looking left
- **Yahya and 'Isa**, the maternal cousins
- **Yusuf**, given "the beautiful half"
- **Idris**, of whom Allah said *We raised him to a high station*
- **Harun**
- **Musa**, who wept — because a young man sent after him would send more of his Ummah to Paradise than Musa's own
- **Ibrahim**, leaning against Al-Bayt Al-Ma'mur, which seventy thousand angels enter daily and never return to

Then **Sidrat Al-Muntaha**, leaves like elephants' ears, fruits like jugs, veiled by Allah's command in colours no creature can describe. Four rivers: two hidden (of Paradise), two visible (the Nile and the Euphrates). He rose until he could **hear the scratching of the pens** that write what is decreed.

**The fifty prayers.** Fifty were enjoined. On the way down Musa stopped him: *Go back — your Ummah will not manage. I tested the Children of Israel and I know.* He returned, and returned, and returned — until five, with the reward of fifty, and the ruling that a good intention unacted is written as one good deed, an acted one as ten; an evil intention unacted is written as nothing, an acted one as one.

Musa sent him back even then. His answer:

> *I have asked my Lord until I feel too shy. I accept this and submit.*

And a voice called out: **My order has been decreed, and I have lightened the burden on My servants.**

**What he saw on the way.** People with copper nails tearing their own faces and chests — *these are those who ate the flesh of the people*, the backbiters. People eating rotten meat, for the same sin. The man who hamstrung Salih's she-camel. Malik, the keeper of Hell, who greeted him first. And Bilal, whose footsteps he heard ahead of him in Paradise.

Asked whether he saw his Lord, he ﺺ answered only: **I saw light — how could I see Him?**

## 3. The Morning After

Quraysh disbelieved. Some Muslims apostatised over it. When they demanded a description of the sanctuary, Bayt Al-Maqdis was raised up before his eyes so he could describe it detail by detail — and those who had travelled there admitted: *by Allah, he has got it right.*

Abu Bakr was told what his companion was claiming. He said: **if he said it, he is speaking the truth** — I believe him about revelation from heaven, which is more than this. From that day he was As-Siddiq.

Abu Jahl, hoping to expose him publicly, called the people together to hear it — and gained nothing.

Ibn Kathir also records a striking non-Muslim witness: when Abu Sufyan raised the Isra' before Heraclius as proof of a lie, the **Patriarch of Jerusalem**, standing beside the emperor, said he knew that night. One door of the sanctuary would not close, immovable as a mountain; in the morning there was a hole bored in the corner stone and the marks of a tethered animal. *This door was not held open last night except for a Prophet who prayed in our sanctuary.*

**Ibn Kathir's verdict:** the reports of the Isra' reach the level of **mutawatir**, narrated from more than twenty Companions. The Muslims are unanimous that it happened; only heretics and apostates rejected it.

**Body or dream?** Awake, in body and soul. His evidence: *Subhan Allah* is said for grave matters — a dream would not have astounded anyone, Quraysh would not have rushed to call him a liar, and no Muslim would have abandoned the faith over it. *'Abd* means the whole person. *The sight turned not aside* (53:17) — sight is a physical faculty. And Al-Buraq is a mount; souls do not need transport.

## 4. Musa, and the Grateful Servant (17:2–3)

Allah pairs Muhammad ﺺ with Musa, and the Qur'an with the Tawrah, immediately after the Isra'. The Tawrah was made a guide, with one instruction: *take none other than Me as your Wakil* — you have no protector or god besides Me.

Then: *O offspring of those whom We carried in the ship with Nuh — verily, he was a grateful servant.*

The address is a reminder of rescue. **Follow your father's footsteps: he was grateful.** Ibn Kathir attaches the hadith that Allah is pleased with a servant who praises Him over a mouthful of food or a sip of drink — and the scene on the Day of Resurrection where mankind comes to Nuh saying: *Allah called you a grateful servant, so intercede for us.*

## 5. The Two Corruptions (17:4–8)

Allah had already told the Children of Israel in their own Book that they would corrupt the earth **twice** and become extreme tyrants.

- **First:** servants of terrible warfare were unleashed, entering the innermost parts of their homes — a promise fulfilled. Then the tide turned back: wealth, children, greater numbers.
- **Second:** faces disgraced, the Masjid entered as before, everything within reach destroyed utterly.

Between the two, the principle: *If you do good, you do good for yourselves; if you do evil, it is against yourselves.*

On the identity of the invaders Ibn Kathir makes a deliberate refusal:

> Many Israiliyyat were narrated about this, but I did not want to lengthen this book with them, because some are fabricated by their heretics, and others may be true but **we have no need of them**. What Allah has told us in His Book is sufficient.

He records only the sound report from Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib about **Nebuchadnezzar** — who destroyed Jerusalem, killed the nobles and scholars, and left no one alive who knew the Tawrah by heart.

The closing warning is open-ended: *if you return, We shall return* — and **Hell is a prison (Hasir)**, a place of permanent detention with no escape.

## 6. The Qur'an, Time, and the Record (17:9–14)

**The Qur'an guides to that which is most upright** (*aqwam*) — the best and clearest of ways.

**Man's haste** (11): he prays against himself, his children, his wealth, in anger — and if Allah answered, he would be destroyed. *Do not pray against yourselves or your wealth, lest it coincide with an hour when prayers are answered.* Ibn Kathir traces the trait to Adam himself, who tried to stand before the soul had reached his feet.

**Night and day, two signs** (12): the night's sign obliterated, the day's made illuminating — so that people may rest, earn, travel, and count debts, worship, contracts, rents. Without change, none of it could be measured.

**The bird on the neck** (13–14): *Ta'irah* is what flies from a man — his deeds, good and bad. It is tied to the **neck** specifically, Ibn Kathir notes, because the neck is the one part of the body with no counterpart; bound by it, there is no escape.

Al-Hasan Al-Basri's words on this — which Ibn Kathir calls among the best he ever spoke:

> O son of Adam, your Book has been opened for you and two noble angels entrusted to you, one on your right, one on your left. So do whatever you want, a lot or a little, until you die; then I will fold up your Book and tie it to your neck with you in your grave. Then when you come out on the Day of Resurrection you will find it wide open — so read your Book. By Allah, the One Who makes you account for your own deeds is being perfectly just.

*Read your Book. You yourself are sufficient as a reckoner against you this Day* — and everyone will be able to read it, literate or not.

## 7. No One Carries Another's Load (17:15)

Three principles in one ayah:

1. **Guidance and misguidance return to the one who chooses them.**
2. **No bearer of burdens bears another's burden.** The apparent counter-verses — that some carry *other loads besides their own* (29:13) — mean those who called others to evil carry the sin of their own deviation *plus* the sin of misleading, **without the smallest amount being taken off the followers**. This is justice and mercy at once.
3. **No punishment until a Messenger has been sent.** Allah does not punish before establishing proof.

**Then the hard case.** What of children who die young to disbelieving parents, the insane, the deaf, the senile, and those who lived in a *fatrah* between messengers?

Ibn Kathir gathers the hadiths. Four will present their case; each will say what he never received. Allah will take their pledge of obedience and then send word for them to **enter the Fire** — and by the One in Whose Hand is Muhammad's soul, whoever enters it will find it **cool and safe**; whoever refuses will be dragged in.

Alongside these: *every newborn is born upon the Fitrah*, and asked about those who die little, he ﺺ said only, **Allah knows best what they would have done.** Also reported: the children of the Muslims are in Paradise in Ibrahim's care; the children of the idolators too; prophets, martyrs, infants, and the baby girls who were buried alive are in Paradise.

**And then Ibn Kathir counsels silence.** Some of the Salaf — Ibn Abbas among them — disliked discussing this at all, on the hadith: *this Ummah will be fine so long as they do not talk about children and the divine decree.*

## 8. How a Town Is Destroyed, and Two Kinds of Seeker (17:16–21)

The word **Amarna** in 17:16 is disputed. It cannot mean Allah commanded immorality, since He commands no such thing. The readings offered: We sent Our decree upon them; We gave power to their evil people; We commanded them to obey and they rebelled; We increased their numbers.

Then the two seekers:

- **Whoever desires the quick-passing world**: he gets what Allah wills, for whom Allah wills — not automatically. Then Hell, entered *disgraced* (blamed for choosing the transient over the eternal) and *rejected* (cast far from mercy).
- **Whoever desires the Hereafter, strives for it as it deserves, and is a believer**: his striving is appreciated. Ibn Kathir notes the three conditions — the wanting, the striving **in the right way, which is following the Messenger**, and the faith in the heart.

*See how We prefer one above another* — rich, poor, beautiful, plain, long-lived, short-lived. And the differences in the Hereafter will be **greater**. There are a hundred levels in Paradise, and the distance between two of them is like the distance between heaven and earth; the people of the highest will see the people of 'Illiyin as distant stars on the horizon.

## 9. The Charter (17:22–39)

Eighteen ayat that Ibn Kathir treats as a single revealed code — and Allah closes them by saying so: *This is part of the wisdom which your Lord has revealed to you.* What is commanded is good manners; what is forbidden is evil qualities.

**Worship none but Him** (22). Do not admit a partner into your worship, or you will sit *reproved* — blamed for the shirk — and *forsaken*, left to the one you worshipped, who can neither benefit nor harm.

**And your parents** (23–24). *Qada* here means *commanded*; Ubayy, Ibn Mas'ud and Ad-Dahhak read it as *wassa* — enjoined. The prohibition begins at the smallest unit of contempt: not even **"Uff"**. Not a hand raised (Ata' bin Rabah). Then the positive command — *address them in terms of honour*: gently, politely, with appreciation. *Lower to them the wing of humility through mercy* — be humble in your actions. And the du'a: *My Lord, bestow on them Your mercy as they brought me up when I was young* — said when they grow old, and when they die.

The hadiths Ibn Kathir attaches are severe. Jibril came to the Minbar and said *say Amin* three times: **doomed** is the one who hears you mentioned and does not send Salla upon you; **doomed** is the one who sees Ramadan come and go unforgiven; **doomed** is the one whose parents, one or both, reach old age and do not cause him to enter Paradise. And to the man who came asking permission to go and fight: *Do you have a mother? Then stay with her — Paradise is at her feet.*

**And your lapses toward them** (25). For the man who said something to his parents he did not mean badly. *Your Lord knows best what is in your souls; if you are righteous, He is Ever Most Forgiving to the Awwabin* — those who repent and return, again and again.

**Kin, the poor, the wayfarer — and no waste** (26–28). Giving is commanded and extravagance forbidden in the same breath. *The spendthrifts are brothers of the Shayatin* — sharing the trait of squandering and disobedience. Mujahid's precise line: spend all your wealth on what is right and you are no spendthrift; spend a little wrongly and you are. And if you have nothing to give the one asking, and turn away hoping for provision from your Lord — **then speak to them a soft, kind word**: a promise.

**Neither hand tied nor fully outstretched** (29–30). The miser is blamed and abandoned; the one who spends past his means ends *worn out*, like an animal too weak to walk. The image Ibn Kathir chooses is the hadith of the two iron cloaks: the almsgiver's expands until it covers his fingertips and wipes out his tracks; the miser's rings lock in place and will not widen, however he pulls. Two angels descend every morning — *O Allah, compensate the giver* / *O Allah, destroy the withholder.* And: *wealth never decreases because of charity.*

**Do not kill your children fearing poverty** (31). Allah puts **their** provision before yours in the wording. And in the hadith of the worst sins: rivals set up with Allah; then *to kill your child lest he eat with you*; then adultery with your neighbour's wife.

**Do not come near zina** (32). Not merely *do not commit* — *do not approach*, and nothing that leads to it. The young man who came asking permission to commit it, whom the crowd shouted down and the Prophet ﺺ called closer, and sat down, and asked: *Would you like it for your mother? Your daughter? Your sister? Your paternal aunt? Your maternal aunt?* Each time: *No, by Allah.* Each time: *Neither do people like it for theirs.* Then his hand on the young man's chest — *O Allah, forgive his sin, purify his heart, and guard his chastity* — and after that the young man never looked at anything of that nature again.

**Do not kill unjustly** (33). Muslim blood is forbidden except in three cases: a life for a life, the married adulterer, and one who leaves his religion and deserts the community. Where killing is wrongful, the heir is given **authority** — retaliation, blood money, or free pardon — but *let him not exceed limits*: no mutilation, no revenge on anyone but the killer.

**The orphan's property** (34) — approach it only to improve it. Ibn Kathir includes the Prophet's ﺺ advice to Abu Dharr: *I see you are weak; do not take charge of two people, and do not take charge of an orphan's property.*

**Fulfil every covenant** (34) — every promise will be asked about.

**Full measure, straight scales** (35). *That is good, and better in the end* — good in your life here, better in your ultimate end. Ibn Abbas: you are entrusted with the two things for which the people before you were destroyed — weights and measures.

**Do not follow what you have no knowledge of** (36). Do not say it; do not accuse with it; do not bear false witness with it. Qatadah's gloss is the plainest: do not say *I saw* when you did not see, *I heard* when you did not hear, *I know* when you do not know. *The hearing, the sight, and the heart — each of those will be questioned.*

**Do not walk the earth with conceit** (37). You cannot split the earth by stamping on it, nor grow as tall as the mountains.

**All of it** (38–39): *all the bad aspects of these are hateful to your Lord.* And the closing threat is aimed through the Prophet ﺺ at the Ummah, since he is protected: set up no other god, or be thrown into Hell blamed — by yourself, by Allah, by creation — and cast out.

## 10. Against Their Theology (17:40–48)

**Angels are not His daughters** (40). Ibn Kathir counts three errors stacked: making the angels female, calling them Allah's children, and worshipping them. And the sting — *you assign to Him what you do not want for yourselves, and even bury alive.*

**If there were other gods** (42–43), those gods would themselves be seeking a way to the Lord of the Throne. The intermediary you invent would need an intermediary. So worship Him directly: *you have no need of a deity between you and Him* — He hates it and has forbidden it through every Messenger.

**Everything glorifies Him** (44). *There is not a thing but glorifies His praise, but you understand not their glorification* — you do not understand them because it is not your language. This covers animal, mineral, and plant alike. Ibn Mas'ud: *we used to hear the tasbih of the food as it was being eaten.* And the Prophet ﺺ to men sitting talking on their stationary mounts: *ride them safely and leave them safely — do not use them as chairs, for the one who is ridden may be better than the rider, and may remember Allah more.*

*He is Ever Forbearing, Oft-Forgiving* — He does not hasten. But: *Allah will let the wrongdoer carry on until, when He seizes him, He never lets him go.*

**The veil** (45–48). Over the hearts of those who reject the Hereafter — *Akinnah* over hearts, deafness in ears — and when Allah is mentioned as One, they turn away in extreme dislike. Asma' bint Abi Bakr's account: when Umm Jamil came hunting for the Prophet ﺺ with a stone pestle in her hand, he recited this ayah, and she stood next to Abu Bakr and could not see him.

They called him *bewitched*. And Ibn Ishaq preserves the scene that answers them: **Abu Sufyan, Abu Jahl, and Al-Akhnas bin Shurayq**, each creeping out alone to listen to him ﺺ praying at night, each unaware of the others, meeting on the road at dawn and scolding each other — and doing it again the next night, and the next. Al-Akhnas asked Abu Jahl afterward what he really thought. Abu Jahl's answer had nothing to do with truth:

> We and Banu 'Abd Manaf competed for honour: they fed people, we fed people; they gave, we gave — until we were neck and neck like race horses. Then they said, *we have a Prophet among us who receives revelation from heaven.* How could we compete with that? By Allah, we will never believe in him.

## 11. Bones and Fragments (17:49–52)

*When we are bones and fragments, shall we really be resurrected?*

The answer refuses the premise: **Be you stones, or iron, or any creation greater in your breasts** — meaning, on Ibn Abbas's reading, *death itself*: assume you become the very opposite of life, He will still raise you. *Say: He Who created you the first time* — He made you from nothing, and can do it again.

*They will shake their heads at you* — Ibn Kathir dwells on the verb *yunghidun*, an up-and-down motion, the same root used for an ostrich chick bobbing its head as it runs and for a tooth working loose. It is a gesture of mockery, and the Arabic carries it.

*Perhaps it is near.* And on that Day, having risen, they will think they stayed here only a little while — a day, part of a day, an hour.

## 12. Say the Best Words (17:53)

Tell My servants to say **that which is best**. If they do not, Shaytan sows discord between them; words become actions, and evil and fighting follow. His enmity is old and open — he refused to prostrate to your father.

Ibn Kathir applies it to something as small as a gesture: *no one should point at his brother with a weapon, for he does not know whether Shaytan will cause him to strike with it.*

## 13. Why No Miracle Was Sent (17:56–60)

The idols you call on **cannot remove harm from you, nor even shift it elsewhere** (56). And those very beings you call on — angels, the Messiah, 'Uzayr, and jinn who later became Muslim while their human worshippers kept worshipping them — are themselves **competing for nearness to their Lord, hoping for His mercy and fearing His punishment** (57). Ibn Kathir notes the pairing: worship is not complete without both. Fear stops you doing the forbidden; hope makes you do more good.

Then the reason Makkah never got its sign (59–60). Quraysh asked him ﺺ to turn As-Safa into gold, to move the mountains, to bring back their dead forefathers. The answer came:

> If you wish, I will do what they ask — but if they then disbelieve, the punishment will come down upon them, because after the sign is sent there is no room for delay. Or if you wish, I will open for them the gate of repentance and mercy.

He ﺺ said: **Rather, the gate of repentance and mercy.**

*And We send not the signs except to make them afraid.* Qatadah: Allah frightens people with whatever signs He wills so they may take heed. When Kufah shook, Ibn Mas'ud said, *your Lord is rebuking you — pay heed.* And the eclipse hadith: the sun and moon do not eclipse for anyone's birth or death; Allah uses them to frighten His servants — so pray, call on Him, seek forgiveness.

*The vision We showed you* was made **a trial for mankind** — the Isra', which some could not hold and abandoned Islam over; and the cursed tree, Zaqqum, which Abu Jahl mocked by calling for dates and butter.

## 14. Iblis and the Limits of His Reach (17:61–65)

The old hatred: *Shall I prostrate to one You created from clay?* Then the bargain — *give me respite and I will seize his offspring, all but a few.*

Allah's reply is a permission that is also a sentence: **Go — and whoever of them follows you, Hell is your recompense, an ample recompense.** Then the list of his instruments:

| *"Fool them with your voice"* | Idle entertainment and singing (Mujahid); every caller who calls to disobedience (Ibn Abbas) |
| *"Your cavalry and infantry"* | Everyone who rides or walks to commit sin |
| *"Share with them in wealth"* | Every dirham spent in disobedience |
| *"…and children"* | Children of zina; children killed in ignorance; children raised in any religion but Islam |
| *"And make promises to them"* | *But Shaytan promises them nothing but deceit* |

Ibn Kathir adds that the mention of wealth and children is not a limit: **anything in which a man obeys Shaytan rather than Allah is a share given away.**

Then the boundary line: *Verily, over My servants you have no authority. And All-Sufficient is your Lord as a Guardian.*

## 15. At Sea, and On Land (17:66–72)

Ships are a mercy — He drives them so you may seek His bounty (66). Then the pattern of human sincerity: at sea, when harm strikes, **everything you worship besides Him vanishes from your heart** (67); safe on land, you turn away.

Ibn Kathir's illustration is perfect: **Ikrimah bin Abi Jahl**, fleeing to Ethiopia after the conquest of Makkah, caught in a storm, hearing the sailors say *none can save you but Allah alone* — and saying to himself, *if none benefits at sea but Allah, then none benefits on land but Allah either.* He vowed that if delivered he would put his hand in Muhammad's ﺺ hand. He was, and he did.

Then the questions (68–69): do you think that stepping onto dry land makes you safe? That the land will not swallow you, that no stone-carrying wind will come, that He will not send you back to sea and drown you?

**And then the honour** (70): *We have honoured the Children of Adam* — created in the best form, walking upright and eating with the hand while other creatures walk on four and eat with their mouths; carried on land and sea; provided with good things; **preferred above many of those We created.** Ibn Kathir notes this ayah indicates preference over the angels also.

**Called by your Imam** (71). Each people summoned by their record of deeds — Ibn Kathir judges this the strongest reading, against "by their Prophet," on the strength of 36:12 and 45:28–29, though the Prophet ﺺ will inevitably be a witness. The one given his record in his right hand *reads it* — out of joy at what is in it.

*And whoever is blind in this world* — blind to the signs and proofs — *will be blind in the Hereafter, and further astray from the path.*

---

## 16. Protected, Commanded, Promised (17:73–84)

**They nearly tempted you** away from what was revealed, to fabricate something else against Us — and *had We not made you stand firm, you would nearly have inclined to them a little* (73–74). The warning is proportional to the station: had it happened, *a double portion in this life and a double portion after death.* This is Allah advertising His own protection of His Prophet ﺺ.

**And they nearly drove you out** (76–77) — but had they, they would not have remained behind him long. Ibn Kathir dates the fulfilment: a year and a half after the Hijrah, Badr, without appointment. And *you will not find any alteration in Our Sunnah* — this is what always happens to a people who expel their Messenger. Were he not the Messenger of Mercy, vengeance unlike anything seen would have come.

**The prayers** (78–79). *From the decline of the sun till the darkness of the night* covers Zuhr, 'Asr, Maghrib and 'Isha; *the recitation of dawn* is Fajr. And Fajr's recitation is *ever witnessed* — by the angels of the night and the angels of the day, who overlap at Fajr and 'Asr. When they ascend, their Lord asks — though He knows best — *how did you leave My servants?* They say: **we came to them while they were praying, and we left them while they were praying.**

**Then Tahajjud**, an extra for him specifically, because his sins were already forgiven, whereas for the rest of the Ummah optional prayers expiate.

**Al-Maqam Al-Mahmud** — the station of praise. Ibn Kathir gives the great intercession hadith in full: mankind gathered, the sun brought close, the sweat rising, and the search for someone to intercede. Adam: *my Lord is angry today as never before* — go to Nuh. Nuh: go to Ibrahim. Ibrahim: go to Musa. Musa: go to 'Isa. 'Isa — who mentions no sin at all — says: **go to Muhammad.** And he ﺺ says:

> I will stand and come before the Throne and fall prostrate, and Allah will inspire me with words of praise such as no one has been inspired with before. Then it will be said: *Raise your head. Ask, and it will be granted. Intercede, and your intercession will be accepted.*

Ibn Kathir then adds, in his own voice, the honours reserved for him ﺺ alone: the first for whom the earth opens; the banner under which Adam and everyone gathers; the Hawd; the first whose Ummah is judged; the first across the Bridge; the first to enter Paradise; Al-Wasilah, its highest station, which befits no one else.

**The du'a of entry and exit** (80), revealed when he was commanded to emigrate: *My Lord, let my entry be good and my exit be good, and grant me from You a helping authority.* Qatadah's note on that last phrase is worth keeping: he knew he could not establish the Book and the laws without authority — *and authority is a mercy Allah places among His servants, otherwise some would attack others and the strong would consume the weak.*

**Truth has come** (81). He ﺺ entered Makkah at the Conquest with three hundred and sixty idols around the House, striking them with a stick in his hand and reciting this ayah.

**The Qur'an as cure and mercy** (82) — it removes doubt, hypocrisy, shirk and confusion from the heart. But *it increases the wrongdoers in nothing but loss*. Ibn Kathir is careful about where the fault lies: **the problem is in the disbeliever, not in the Qur'an.**

**Man in ease and in calamity** (83). Given health, wealth and ease, he turns away and becomes arrogant. Touched by evil, he despairs utterly — thinking he will never have anything good again.

**Shakilah** (84) — *each does according to his shakilah*: his inclination (Ibn Abbas), his nature (Mujahid), his intention (Qatadah), his religion (Ibn Zayd). All close in meaning, and the ayah is a warning: everyone will be repaid for what he actually is.

## 17. The Ruh, the Challenge, and the Night Behind the Ka'bah (17:85–100)

**The Ruh** (85). The Jews prompted each other to ask; some warned against it. He ﺺ fell silent, and Ibn Mas'ud, standing with him on the farm, knew revelation was coming. The answer: *the Ruh is of the affair of my Lord, and of knowledge you have been given only a little.*

They objected that they had been given the Tawrah, which is *hikmah*, and whoever is given hikmah is given abundant good. The reply came in 31:27 — the trees as pens, the sea as ink with seven more behind it. Whatever knowledge saves you from the Fire is great and good; **measured against Allah's knowledge it is very little.**

Ibn Kathir also relays As-Suhayli on Ruh and Nafs: the Ruh is the origin and essence; it is called Nafs only once joined to a body and coloured by it — as water is the life of a vine, but once mixed and pressed it is no longer called water. So they are the same in one sense and not in another.

**If We willed, We could take away what We revealed to you** (86–87) — and you would find no protector against Us. It remains with you *only as a mercy from your Lord.* Ibn Mas'ud's chilling gloss: a red wind will come at the end of time, and there will be nothing left of the Qur'an in a man's copy or in his heart, not one ayah.

**The challenge** (88): if mankind and the jinn combined and helped one another, they could not produce the like of it. How could the words of created beings be like the Words of the Creator?

**The demands** (90–93), preserved in Ibn Ishaq's long account. The nobles of Quraysh gathered behind the Ka'bah after sunset and sent for him. Their opening offer: wealth to make him the richest among them, or leadership, or kingship, or medicine if he was possessed. His answer:

> I have not brought this because I want your wealth, or to be your leader or king. Allah sent me to you as a Messenger and revealed to me a Book. I have conveyed my Lord's Messages and advised you. If you accept it, it is your good fortune here and in the Hereafter; if you reject it, I will wait patiently for the command of Allah until He judges between me and you.

They asked next for the mountains moved and rivers made to run and their forefathers resurrected — Qusayy bin Kilab among them, to be cross-examined. Same answer. Then: at least get a treasure and a palace for yourself, so you needn't work the markets like us. Same answer. Then: bring the sky down on us. *That is for Allah to decide.*

Finally Abdullah bin Abi Umayyah, his own cousin, followed him out and told him: even if you climbed a ladder to heaven in front of me and came back with an open book and four angels to testify, **I think I still would not believe you.** He ﺺ went home to his family grieving.

Ibn Kathir's comment: had Allah known they were asking in order to be guided, they would have been granted it. He knew they were asking out of stubbornness. And the mercy in the refusal shows in the sequel — several of the men at that gathering, **including Abdullah bin Abi Umayyah**, later became sincere Muslims.

**A human Messenger** (94–95). What kept most people from believing was that a *man* was sent. But: *if there were angels walking about the earth in peace, We would have sent them an angel from heaven as a messenger.* You get a messenger of your own kind because that is the only kind you could speak with and learn from — a kindness, not a deficiency.

**Man is ever miserly** (100). *If you possessed the treasures of my Lord's mercy, you would hold them back for fear of spending* — for fear of poverty, though such treasures could never run out. This is human nature, contrasted against the hadith: *Allah's Hand is full and never decreases by His giving night and day. Look how much He has given since He created the heavens and the earth — and what is in His right hand has not decreased.*

## 18. Nine Signs, and a Book Sent Down in Pieces (17:101–111)

**Musa's nine signs** (101–104) — the staff, the hand, the years of famine, the crop failure, the flood, the locusts, the lice, the frogs, the blood. Fir'awn witnessed every one and called him *bewitched*. The point is made against Quraysh: signs were given in full to a people once before, and they disbelieved anyway.

Fir'awn resolved to drive them out of the land; he was drowned, and the Children of Israel were told to **dwell in the land**. Ibn Kathir reads this as glad tidings of the Conquest of Makkah, revealed in Makkah before the Hijrah — the Prophet's ﺺ people also tried to expel him, and he inherited their city, entered it, and then let them go out of generosity.

**Revealed in stages** (105–106). *Faraqnahu* — brought down whole from Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz to the lowest heaven, then revealed to the Messenger ﺺ piece by piece as events required, over twenty-three years. Or read *Farraqnahu*: revealed ayah by ayah, explained and made clear. *In order that you might recite it to men at intervals* — and *We have revealed it by stages*, little by little.

**Believe in it or do not believe** (107–109). It is the same; the Qur'an is true in itself. But those who were given knowledge before it — the righteous of the People of the Book who kept their Books undistorted — **fall down on their chins in prostration** when it is recited, saying *Glory to our Lord! The promise of our Lord is fulfilled.* They are thanking Him for letting them live to meet this Messenger. And they fall weeping, *and it increases their humility.*

**The closing** (110–111). An idolator heard him ﺺ saying in prostration *O Most Gracious, O Most Merciful* and complained that he claims to pray to One but prays to two. The answer: *Call upon Allah, or call upon Ar-Rahman — by whichever name you call, to Him belong the Most Beautiful Names.*

**Neither loud nor low.** Ibn Abbas explains the balance precisely: not so loud that the idolators hear and insult the Qur'an and the One Who revealed it; not so quiet that your own companions cannot hear it and learn it from you. And in another account: not so quiet that the man listening in secret, hiding from his own people, hears nothing at all — *perhaps he will catch some of it and benefit.*

Then the last ayah of the surah, which answers everyone at once — the Jews and Christians who claimed a son, the Arabs who kept a partner in their talbiyah, the Sabians and Magians who thought Allah would be weak without helpers:

> *All praise be to Allah, Who has not begotten a son, and has no partner in dominion, nor is He low as to need a supporter. And magnify Him with all magnificence.*

## What to Carry Away

**The surah opens with a journey and closes with a Book.** In between it explains why the journey was necessary: a man had to be taken up and shown, so that what he brought down could be trusted.

**The Charter is the surah's floor.** Everything cosmic in this surah — the seven heavens glorifying, the pens scratching, the intercession at the Throne — comes to rest in eighteen ayat about not saying *uff* to your mother, not shortchanging a scale, not repeating what you did not hear.

**Justice is stated as a system.** No load carried for another. No punishment before a warning. A record you write yourself and then read yourself. And within that system, mercy chosen deliberately at every fork: five prayers instead of fifty, the gate of repentance instead of As-Safa turned to gold, patience with Makkah instead of the vengeance its expulsion of a Prophet deserved.

**Shaytan has instruments but no authority.** He can call, ride, walk, share, and promise. Over Allah's servants he has none — and the promise is deceit either way.

**And man is described without flattery.** Hasty, ungrateful, miserly, sincere at sea and forgetful on land, arrogant in ease and despairing in loss — *except* those Allah protects. The surah names the exception every time it names the flaw.
