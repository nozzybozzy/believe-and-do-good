*Revealed in Makkah · 85 ayat · the first of the seven **Ha Mim** surahs · also known as **Al-Mu'min**, "The Believer," after the unnamed man of Fir'awn's own court whose speech occupies its centre*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ibn Kathir opens not with the surah but with the family it belongs to. Ibn Abbas: *"Everything has an essence, and the essence of the Qur'an is the family of Ha Mim."* Mis'ar bin Kidam: *"They used to be called 'the brides'."* Ibn Mas'ud: *"When I reach the family of Ha Mim, it is like reaching a beautiful garden, so I take my time."* A parable narrated from Abdullah compares the rest of the Qur'an to the first traces of rainfall, and the Ha Mims to the gardens that rainfall produces.

The surah has one governing subject: **disputation** — *jidal*, the attempt to refute clear truth with clever falsehood. It opens with it (40:4), returns to it at the midpoint (40:35), diagnoses it (40:56), and closes on it (40:69). Around that spine Ibn Kathir sets three long movements: the angels who pray for believers, the believer who spoke up in a tyrant's court, and the disbelievers arguing with each other in the Fire when arguing is finally useless.

A hadith recorded by Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi, whose chain Ibn Kathir calls Sahih: *"When you go to bed tonight, recite **Ha Mim, La Yunsarun**"* — a reference to this surah's opening.

---

## 1. The Four Names at the Door (40:1–3)

> *The Forgiver of sin, the Acceptor of repentance, the Severe in punishment, the Bestower. La ilaha illa Huwa, to Him is the final return.*

Ibn Kathir reads the pair of forgiveness-names as covering both directions of time: **He forgives sins already committed, and accepts repentance for sins yet to come** from whoever turns back to Him. "Severe in punishment" is aimed at a specific person — the one who *persists*, prefers this world, and stubbornly turns from the commands of Allah.

The two attributes stand together deliberately: *"These two are often mentioned together in the Qur'an, so that people will remain in a state of both hope and fear"* — as in 15:49–50, *Declare unto My servants that truly I am the Oft-Forgiving... And that My torment is indeed the most painful torment.*

**Dhi at-Tawl** ("the Bestower") — Ibn Abbas: *"He is Generous and Rich, Independent of means."* The blessings are ongoing and uncountable (16:18).

---

## 2. Don't Be Impressed by How Well They're Doing (40:4–6)

Only disbelievers dispute the signs of Allah after clear proof has come. Then the warning that follows it:

> *So let not their ability of going about here and there through the land deceive you!*

Ibn Kathir: this means **their wealth and their luxurious life**. Cross-referenced to 3:196–197 (*a brief enjoyment; then their ultimate abode is Hell*) and 31:24 (*We let them enjoy for a little while, then... a great torment*).

Then the consolation to the Prophet ﷺ: Nuh was the first Messenger sent to forbid idol worship, and every nation since has *plotted against their Messenger to seize him* — meaning they tried to kill him, and some succeeded. *"So I seized them, and how was My punishment!"* Qatadah's comment is one line: **"It was terrible, by Allah."**

The final note is bleak and logical: the Word of punishment is even *more* justified against Quraysh, because *if they have disbelieved in you, there is no certainty that they will believe in any other Prophet*.

---

## 3. The Bearers of the Throne Are Praying for You (40:7–9)

This is the surah's first great consolation, and one of the most tender passages in the tafsir. The angels closest to Allah — those who carry the Throne and those around it — combine **Tasbih** (glorification: He is free of shortcomings) with **Tahmid** (praise: an affirmation), believe in Him, and then *ask forgiveness for those who believe*: for the people of earth, whom they have never seen, who believe in the unseen.

Ibn Kathir draws out the implication with a hadith from Sahih Muslim:

> *When a Muslim prays for his brother in his absence, the angel says, "Amin, and may you have something similar to it."*

Shahr bin Hawshab describes the eight bearers: four say *"Glory and praise be to You, O Allah; to You be praise for Your forbearance after Your knowledge,"* and four say *"...for Your forgiveness after Your power."*

Their du'a is worth reading as a model:

- **"Our Lord! You comprehend all things in mercy and knowledge"** — *Your mercy encompasses their sins and Your knowledge encompasses all their deeds.*
- **"...and make them enter the 'Adn Gardens... and the righteous among their fathers, their wives, and their offspring"** — Ibn Kathir: *bring them together so that they may find delight in one another in neighbouring dwellings.* Sa'id bin Jubayr: the believer entering Paradise asks after his father, son, brother; he is told *they did not reach your level*; he says, *but I did it for my sake and for theirs* — and they are raised to join him, **the lower raised to the higher, with nothing deducted from anyone** (52:21).

Mutarrif bin Abdullah bin Ash-Shikhkhir summed the passage up: *"The most sincere of the servants of Allah towards the believers are the angels."* And then: *"The most treacherous of the servants of Allah towards the believers are the Shayatin."*

---

## 4. The Regret That Comes Too Late (40:10–14)

In Hell the angels will call out that **Allah's aversion toward them was greater than their own self-hatred is now** — Qatadah: Allah's hatred for them *when Faith was presented and they turned away* exceeds their hatred of themselves *when they see the punishment with their own eyes*.

Their plea — *You have made us die twice and given us life twice* — Ibn Mas'ud (and Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah) matches to 2:28: dead, then alive, then dead, then raised.

Ibn Kathir then stacks the escalating requests to be sent back, each refused: at the standing (32:12), then held over the Fire (6:27–28), then inside it (35:37), then the final answer, *Remain you in it with ignominy! And speak you not to Me!* (23:108). The reason given is decisive — *if you were to go back, this is how you would be* (6:28). Return is refused not out of harshness but because their nature would reject the truth again.

The section closes constructively: He shows you His signs, sends provision from the sky — one kind of water producing every colour, taste and fragrance — and *none remembers but those who turn in repentance*. So: **worship Him with sincerity, however much the disbelievers hate it.** Ibn Az-Zubayr reported that the Prophet ﷺ said this Tahlil after *every* prescribed prayer: *There is no God but Allah alone, no partner with Him... we worship Him in all sincerity even though the disbelievers hate that.*

---

## 5. The Day of Mutual Meeting (40:15–17)

**Yawm at-Talaq** — Ibn Abbas: one of the names of the Day of Resurrection, *the Day when everyone will find out about his deeds, good and bad*. Nothing of them will be hidden; there will be nothing to give them shelter or shade.

> *Whose is the kingdom this Day? It is Allah's, the One, the Irresistible!*

Ibn Kathir cites the hadith of Allah rolling up the heavens and the earth in His Hand: *"I am the King, I am the Compeller, I am the Proud — where are the kings of the earth? Where are the tyrants? Where are the proud?"* In the hadith of the Trumpet, He asks *"Whose is the kingdom today?"* three times, and answers Himself.

Then the standard of justice: not a speck of dust's weight of injustice; **tenfold reward for a good deed, one-for-one for a bad one**. Anchored to the hadith qudsi from Abu Dharr in Sahih Muslim: *"O My servants, I have forbidden injustice for Myself and made it unlawful for you... these are your deeds, I record them for you then I will recompense you for them. Whoever finds something good, let him praise Allah; whoever finds otherwise, let him blame no one but himself."*

---

## 6. Hearts at the Throats (40:18–20)

**Yawm al-Azifah**, "the Day drawing near" — so named because it is close (53:57–58; 54:1; 21:1; 16:1).

*Kazimin* — Qatadah: the hearts reach the throats out of fear, *and they will neither come out nor go back to their places*. Ibn Kathir adds a second sense: **silent**, because no one speaks without permission (78:38). Ibn Jurayj: weeping.

Then the verse that reaches furthest into private life:

> *Allah knows the fraud of the eyes, and all that the breasts conceal.*

Ad-Dahhak: **a wink**; a man claiming he saw what he didn't, or denying what he did. Ibn Abbas: *Allah knows when the eye looks at something, whether it wants to commit an act of betrayal or not.* And on the second half, Ibn Abbas again: *He knows, if you were able to, whether you would commit Zina with a woman or not* — i.e. He judges the intention that circumstance never let you act on. As-Suddi: insinuating whispers.

---

## 7. Musa, Fir'awn, and the Man Who Spoke (40:21–46)

Ibn Kathir identifies the cast: **Fir'awn**, king of the Copts; **Haman**, his adviser; **Qarun**, the richest trader of his time. The order to kill the sons of the believers is a *second* command — the first had been precautionary, against the emergence of someone like Musa; this one is purely to humiliate, so the people would *regard Musa as a bad omen* (7:129).

Fir'awn's line — *Leave me to kill Musa, and let him call his Lord!* — Ibn Kathir calls **the utmost in offensive stubbornness**, with *let him call his Lord* meaning simply *"I do not care."* Musa's answer is refuge: *I seek refuge in my Lord and your Lord from every arrogant one.* The Prophet ﷺ, when afraid of a people, said the same in his own words: *"O Allah, we seek refuge in You from their evil, and we seek Your help in repulsing them."*

### The Believer of Fir'awn's Family (40:28–45)

The well-known view: a Copt, of Fir'awn's own household; As-Suddi says his cousin. Ibn Abbas: **no one from the family of Fir'awn believed except three** — this man, the wife of Fir'awn, and the man who came running to warn Musa (28:20).

He had concealed his faith his whole life, and revealed it on the day the killing was ordered. Ibn Kathir: *the best of Jihad is to speak a just word before an unjust ruler, and there is no greater example of this than the words this man said to Fir'awn.* Al-Bukhari records the same sentence in Muslim history: when Uqbah bin Abi Mu'it strangled the Prophet ﷺ with his own garment in the courtyard of the Ka'bah, Abu Bakr pushed him away saying, ***"Would you kill a man because he says: My Lord is Allah?"***

His argument is a masterpiece of restraint — he does not begin by professing belief, he begins by conceding the other side:

- **If he is lying**, the sin of the lie is on him, and Allah will deal with it. So leave him alone; it costs you nothing.
- **If he is telling the truth**, then some of what he warns you of will land on you. So leave him alone; it costs you everything.
- **And a liar claiming prophethood would be visibly inconsistent** — *Allah guides not one who is a transgressor, a liar.* But this man's words and deeds hold together.

Ibn Kathir notes the same request for non-interference in 44:17–21 (*if you believe me not, then keep away from me and leave me alone*), and in the Prophet's own appeal to Quraysh through ties of kinship (42:23) — which, he says, became the basis of the truce at **Al-Hudaybiyyah, a manifest victory**.

Then the warnings, in order: the fate of the confederates — Nuh's people, 'Ad, Thamud (40:30–31); **the Day of Mutual Calling** (40:32); the reminder that **Yusuf** had already been sent to Egypt with clear signs, and they doubted him alive and despaired after his death, saying *No Messenger will Allah send after him* (40:34).

Fir'awn's counter-move is the tower — Haman is to bake bricks and build a fortress *that I may look upon the God of Musa* — a stunt to persuade his people Musa was lying. Ibn Abbas and Mujahid on *the plot of Fir'awn led to nothing but loss*: **"Meaning nothing but ruin."** Fir'awn's claim *I show you only that which I see* was a lie, and he knew it (17:102; 27:14). Ibn Kathir attaches the hadith: *There is no leader who dies having cheated his people, but he will never smell the fragrance of Paradise — and its fragrance can be detected from a distance of a five-hundred-year journey.*

The believer's closing speech turns the whole scene inside out:

> *And O my people! How is it that I call you to salvation while you call me to the Fire?*

**La Jarama** — "Truly" (As-Suddi, Ibn Jarir); "No lie" (Ad-Dahhak) — *the thing you call me to has no claim in this world or the Hereafter.* Qatadah: idols possess no power to benefit or harm. As-Suddi: they do not respond, here or there (46:5–6; 35:14).

And the last line before he vanishes from the narrative: ***and my affair I leave to Allah*** — *I put my trust in Allah and seek His help, and I renounce you utterly.* Allah saved him from everything they plotted: in this world alongside Musa, and in the Hereafter to Paradise.

---

## 8. The Torment of the Grave (40:46)

> *The Fire, they are exposed to it, morning and afternoon.*

Ibn Kathir flags this as **one of the major proofs used by Ahlus-Sunnah for punishment during Al-Barzakh** — and then honestly raises the objection: the ayah is Makkan, so how is it being used this way? His answer: the ayah speaks of the **souls** being exposed morning and evening; the effect on bodies in the grave is established separately, by the Sunnah.

He gives the sequence from A'ishah. A Jewish woman serving her used to say *"May Allah save you from the torment of the grave."* The Prophet ﷺ, asked, initially said: *"No — who said that? The Jews are lying."* Then, after Allah willed a time to pass, he came out at midday wrapped in his robe, eyes reddening, calling at the top of his voice:

> *"The grave is like patches of dark night! O people, if you knew what I know, you would weep much and laugh little. O people, seek refuge with Allah from the torment of the grave, for the torment of the grave is real."*

Qatadah on *morning and afternoon*: every morning and every evening, for as long as this world remains, *it will be said to them by way of rebuke: O people of Fir'awn, this is your position.* Ibn Zayd: **"They are there today."** And Ibn Umar's hadith: when one of you dies, his place is shown to him morning and evening, *"this is your place until Allah resurrects you to go to it."*

---

## 9. The Argument in the Fire (40:47–50)

The weak followers turn on the arrogant leaders: *we followed you — can you take from us some portion of the Fire?* Meaning, Ibn Kathir says, *can you carry a part of our burden?* The answer: **"We are all in this together"** — *our own punishment is enough for us to bear.* Allah has already apportioned to each what he deserves (7:38).

So they go to the keepers of Hell instead, asking only for *one day* of relief — knowing Allah has already told them not to speak to Him. The keepers ask whether Messengers didn't come with clear evidence. They say *Yes*. The keepers say: **"Then call."** Ibn Kathir: *you are on your own; we will not pray for you or listen to you; and we tell you it makes no difference whether you supplicate or not.*

---

## 10. The Promise: They Will Be Made Victorious (40:51–56)

As-Suddi states the principle: Allah never sends a Messenger who is killed, or believers who are killed, without sending afterwards someone *who will support their call and seek vengeance for their blood* — so the messenger may die, but **the call prevails in this world**.

Ibn Kathir then narrates it as history: Al-Madinah, Badr and the humiliation of the Quraysh nobility, the ransom of the captives, the conquest of Makkah, then the Yemen and the whole Peninsula, then the Prophet's death and the Khalifahs conveying the religion outward, *"until the call of Muhammad spread throughout the world, east and west. This religion will continue to prevail until the Hour begins."*

The instructions attached: **be patient; ask forgiveness for your fault** (which, Ibn Kathir notes, *encourages the Ummah to seek forgiveness*); **and glorify your Lord in the 'Ashi and the Ibkar** — the end of the day and beginning of night, the beginning of the day and end of the night.

The diagnosis of the disputers is final: *there is nothing in their breasts except pride — they will never attain it.* They are too proud to follow the truth and submit to the one who brought it, and their attempts to suppress it will fail.

---

## 11. The Argument from Creation (40:57–68)

> *The creation of the heavens and the earth is indeed greater than the creation of mankind; yet most of the people do not know.*

The resurrection is the *smaller* of the two acts. Ibn Kathir points out the incoherence in the Arabs' position: they acknowledged that Allah created the heavens and the earth, then denied the resurrection — **"they acknowledged something which was greater than that which they denied"** (46:33).

The blind and the seeing are not equal; nor the believer who works righteousness and the one who does evil. *Little do you remember.*

### "Call upon Me, I will answer you" (40:60)

Sufyan Ath-Thawri's prayer: *"O You Who love most those who ask of You, and O You Who hate most those who do not ask of You — and there is no one like that apart from You, O Lord."* And the poet's line: *"Allah hates not to be asked, and the son of Adam hates to be asked."*

Ka'b Al-Ahbar: three things were given to this Ummah that were given only to Prophets before — being made witnesses over mankind; being told *He has not laid upon you in religion any hardship* (22:78); and being told, as a whole Ummah, ***Call upon Me, I will answer you.***

An-Nu'man bin Bashir reported the Prophet ﷺ saying: ***"Indeed, the supplication is the worship,"*** and then reciting this ayah. Which is why refusing to ask is described as being *too proud for My worship*. The consequence, from 'Amr bin Shu'ayb's chain: *the proud will be gathered on the Day of Resurrection like ants in the image of people, everything stepping on them, until they enter a prison in Hell called **Bulas**.*

The signs then come in sequence: night for rest and day for seeing; the earth made a dwelling place, steadied with mountains; the sky a canopy; *He gave you shape and made your shapes good*; the stages of a human life — dust, Nutfah, clot, infant, full strength, old age, *though some among you die before* — and *when He decides upon a thing He says to it only "Be," and it is.*

---

## 12. The Closing Warning (40:69–85)

The disputers again — collars and chains, dragged into boiling water, then burned (55:43–44; 56:41–56; 44:43–50). Asked *where are those you associated with Allah?*, they answer *they have vanished from us*, and then deny ever having worshipped them at all — which Ibn Kathir matches to 6:23, *By Allah, our Lord, we were not those who joined others in worship with Allah.*

The stated cause: ***That was because you had been exulting in the earth without any right.***

Then the last great mercy of the surah, framed as a sign: the **cattle**. Ridden and eaten; their milk drunk; used to plough and to carry heavy burdens *to distant lands*; their hair and wool made into tents, clothing, furnishings. *And He shows you His Ayat. Which, then, of the Ayat of Allah do you deny?* — Ibn Kathir: *you cannot deny any of them, unless you are stubborn and arrogant.*

The surah ends where it began, with people who travelled the earth and did not look. Those before were *more in number, mightier in strength, greater in their traces* — and when the Messengers came with clear proofs, *they were glad with that which they had of knowledge*. Mujahid: **"They said, we know better than them; we will not be resurrected and we will not be punished."** As-Suddi: *"In their ignorance, they rejoiced in what they had of worldly knowledge."*

And the final ruling, the surah's last word:

> *Then their Faith could not avail them when they saw Our punishment.*

Like Fir'awn drowning (10:90–91), whose belated belief was refused because Allah had already answered Musa's prayer (10:88). This is **Sunnat Allah** — His unvarying way with all who repent only once the punishment is in sight. The window, per the hadith, closes narrowly but not before:

> *Allah will accept the repentance of His servant so long as the death rattle is not sounding in his throat.*

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Ghafir is a surah about argument — and about who is still arguing when it no longer matters.**

Its first named attribute of Allah is *Forgiver of sin*, and its last statement is that forgiveness has a deadline. Between the two, it shows you four kinds of speech: angels who carry the Throne and spend their speech asking forgiveness for people they have never met; a courtier who spent a lifetime silent and then said one true sentence in front of a man who could kill him; a tyrant who built a tower to make a point he knew was false; and a crowd in the Fire blaming each other, then begging the guards for a single day.

The believer of Fir'awn's family is the model the surah is built around, and his last line is the one to carry: **and my affair I leave to Allah.** He said the true thing, took no responsibility for the outcome, and was saved from *the evils that they plotted* — plots Ibn Kathir says had already been laid.

And the one command that answers all of it: ***Call upon Me, I will answer you.*** Not permitted — *commanded*. The supplication **is** the worship, and the only people described in this surah as too proud for it end up in Bulas.
