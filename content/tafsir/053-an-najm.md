*Revealed in Makkah · 62 ayat · named for the star of its opening oath · the first surah in which a prostration was revealed*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

An-Najm is built on a single opposition, and once you see it the whole surah snaps into shape: **what the Prophet ﷺ actually saw, against what the idolaters merely invented.**

The first half is testimony. It swears that the man they call deluded is neither astray nor deceived, that nothing he says comes from his own appetite, and then — extraordinarily — it *describes the encounter*: an immense figure on the highest horizon, an approach to within two bow-lengths, a revelation delivered, a lote-tree at the boundary of creation covered in something no eye had seen. The sight, it says, neither wandered nor overreached.

The second half is the audit. Against that firsthand seeing it sets Al-Lat, Al-'Uzza and Manat — and asks what, exactly, anyone ever *saw* of them. The verdict is devastating in its plainness: **they are but names which you have named**, you and your fathers. Not gods. Names. Behind them stands nothing but guesswork and inherited habit, and *conjecture is no substitute for the truth.*

From there the surah tightens onto the individual: no one carries another's burden, no one has anything but what he worked for, wishing is not the same as earning, and no amount of self-praise moves the needle with the One who knew you when you were a fetus. It ends with the Hour called *Al-Azifah* — "the imminent" — and a single command: **fall down in prostration.**

And they did. The Prophet ﷺ recited it in Makkah and prostrated, and the entire assembly went down with him — Muslims and idolaters together, before anyone had time to remember they were enemies.

## 1. The Oath, and the Man It Defends (53:1–4)

> *By the star when it goes down. Your companion has neither gone astray nor has he erred. Nor does he speak of desire. It is only a revelation revealed.*

Ibn Kathir opens with a principle from Ash-Sha'bi that governs every Qur'anic oath: **the Creator swears by whatever He wills of His creation; the created may swear only by the Creator.**

On *the star*: Mujahid (via Ibn Abi Najih) took it as the Pleiades as it sets at Fajr. Ad-Dahhak read it as the star used to drive off the Shayatin. The parallel Ibn Kathir cites is the great oath of Surah Al-Waqi'ah (56:75–80) — sworn by the setting of the stars, and answered by *that is indeed an honourable recitation, in a Book well-guarded.*

The subject of the oath is 53:2, and Ibn Kathir's reading of it is precise and worth sitting with. There are two ways to miss the truth:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Gone astray** (*dall*) | The error of the **ignorant** — one who walks no path with knowledge at all |
| **Erred / deceived** (*ghawa*) | The error of the **knowledgeable** — one who knows the truth and deliberately deviates from it |

The verse denies both. And Ibn Kathir immediately maps them: the first is the misguided way of the Christians, the second the erring path of the Jews — knowing the truth, concealing it, and holding to falsehood anyway. The Prophet ﷺ and his message stand clear of both, on **the perfect straight path**.

### "Every word that comes out of me is the Truth"

53:3–4 assert that nothing he utters is appetite or wish — he transmits what he was commanded, *in its entirety, without addition or deletion*. Ibn Kathir supports this with two reports:

- **Abu Umamah** heard the Prophet ﷺ say that numbers like the two tribes of Rabi'ah and Mudar — or one of them — will enter Paradise through the intercession of **one man who is not a Prophet**. A man objected that Rabi'ah was only a subtribe of Mudar. The reply: *"I said what I said."*
- **Abdullah bin 'Amr** used to write down everything he heard from the Prophet ﷺ. The Quraysh discouraged him — *he is human, and sometimes speaks when angry.* He stopped, then asked. The answer: ***"Write! By He in Whose Hand is my soul, every word that comes out of me is the Truth."***

That second hadith is one of the foundation stones of the entire written Sunnah, and it sits here, in this surah, as commentary on a single verse.

## 2. The Encounter (53:5–18)

> *He has been taught by one mighty in power. Dhu Mirrah, then he Istawa. While he was in the highest part of the horizon. Then he approached and came closer, and was at a distance of two bows' length or less.*

**The teacher is Jibril**, peace be upon him. Ibn Kathir anchors this with the parallel in At-Takwir (81:19–21) — *the word of a most honourable messenger, owner of power, with the Lord of the Throne, obeyed and trustworthy.* Mujahid, Al-Hasan and Ibn Zayd all read *Dhu Mirrah* as "mighty in power."

*(A useful aside Ibn Kathir slips in: the same word appears in the hadith that charity is not lawful for a rich person, nor for a **Dhu Mirrah** — a person sound and strong in body — which is where the fiqh of who may accept zakat partly rests.)*

Al-Hasan, Mujahid, Qatadah and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas all take *he Istawa* as Jibril rising to the highest horizon. Ikrimah: *"the highest horizon, where the morning comes from."* Mujahid: the place of sunrise. Qatadah: that from which the day comes.

### Jibril in his own shape

Two reports from Ahmad, both remarkable:

- **Ibn Mas'ud**: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ saw Jibril in his original form with **six hundred wings**, each wing filling the side of the horizon, a colourful array, and pearls and rubies falling from every wing *as much as only Allah knows*.
- **Ibn Abbas**: the Prophet ﷺ asked Jibril to show himself as he truly is. Jibril told him, *"Invoke your Lord."* He did — and a vast figure rose from the east, rising and spreading until it filled everything. The Prophet ﷺ **was knocked unconscious**. Jibril came down, revived him, and wiped the saliva from his cheeks.

The tenderness of that last detail is the whole point. The being who fills the horizon is also the one who kneels down and cleans your face.

### "Or less"

*Two bows' length or less* — Mujahid and Qatadah: the span of two bows extended to full length (another view: the distance between a bow's string and its wooden centre).

Ibn Kathir then does something linguistically careful with the phrase *or less*. It does not introduce vagueness; it **excludes the possibility of more**. He gives three parallels where the same construction appears:

- *hearts became as stones, or even worse in hardness* (2:74) — not softer than rock, but that hard or harder.
- *they fear men as they fear Allah, or even more* (4:77).
- *We sent him to a hundred thousand, or even more* (37:147) — not fewer than a hundred thousand.

So the measurement is fixed and honest: that near, or nearer. **No room left for doubt or refutation.**

## 3. Did He See His Lord? (53:11–18)

> *The heart lied not in what he saw. Will you then dispute with him about what he saw?*

This is the surah's most contested passage, and Ibn Kathir handles it by laying both positions side by side without forcing a verdict.

**Ibn Abbas** (recorded by Muslim), and following him Simak, Ikrimah, Abu Salih and As-Suddi: *"He saw Allah twice in his heart."*

**A'ishah** (رضي الله عنها) — flatly the opposite. Masruq asked her whether Muhammad ﷺ saw his Lord. Her reply: *"You said something that caused my hair to rise!"* She then named **three claims, any of which is a lie**:

1. That Muhammad ﷺ saw his Lord — and she recited *No vision can grasp Him, but He grasps all vision* (6:103) and *it is not given to any human being that Allah should speak to him except by revelation, or from behind a veil* (42:51).
2. That he knew what tomorrow brings — *Verily, Allah, with Him is the knowledge of the Hour, He sends down the rain, and knows what is in the wombs* (31:34).
3. That he concealed any part of the Message — *O Messenger, proclaim what has been sent down to you from your Lord* (5:67).

What he *did* see, she said, was **Jibril — twice, in his real form**: once at Sidrat Al-Muntaha, and once at Ajyad in Makkah, six hundred wings covering the horizon. She added that she was the first of this Ummah to ask the Prophet ﷺ about the verse, and he told her plainly: *that was Jibril.*

And **Abu Dharr** asked him directly, "Have you seen your Lord?" The answer, in two narrations: *"How can I see Him, since there was a light?"* and *"I only saw a light."*

Ibn Kathir notes at the end of the section that scholars of Ahl us-Sunnah lean on 53:18 itself — *indeed he saw of the greatest signs of his Lord* — to conclude he did not see Allah on the Isra': had he seen Him, that news would have been told, and the Prophet ﷺ would have related it.

## 4. Sidrat Al-Muntaha (53:14–17)

> *Near Sidrat Al-Muntaha. Near it is the Paradise of Abode. When that covered the lote tree which did cover it!*

**Ibn Mas'ud** (via Ahmad, collected by Muslim): on the Isra' journey the Prophet ﷺ ascended to Sidrat Al-Muntaha in the seventh heaven. It is the **terminus in both directions** — everything ascending from the earth stops there and is held; everything descending from above stops there and is held. As for what covered it: *"Golden butterflies."* Elsewhere, angels, Allah's light, and spectacular colours.

And there he was given three things:

- the **five daily prayers**
- the **closing verses of Surah Al-Baqarah** (2:284–286)
- **remission of the grave sins** of those of his Ummah who associate nothing with Allah

### The sight that did not wander

> *The sight turned not aside, nor did it transgress beyond the limit.* (53:17)

Ibn Abbas: his gaze went neither right nor left, and did not exceed what had been ordained for it.

Ibn Kathir calls this **a tremendous quality** — proof of perfect obedience. At the most overwhelming moment ever granted to a human being, standing at the edge of creation, he *looked only at what he was permitted to look at, and asked for nothing beyond what he was given.* Discipline in the presence of wonder is a rarer virtue than discipline under hardship.

## 5. Three Names and Nothing Behind Them (53:19–23)

> *Have you then considered Al-Lat, and Al-'Uzza, and Manat, the other third?*

Ibn Kathir gives the field report on each, because the argument depends on knowing how ordinary these "gods" actually were.

| Idol | What it actually was |
|---|---|
| **Al-Lat** | A **white stone with inscriptions**, in a house at At-Ta'if with curtains, servants and a sacred courtyard, in imitation of the Ka'bah. Worshipped by Thaqif, who boasted of it to every Arab tribe but Quraysh. Ibn Jarir: the name was **derived from "Allah" and feminised**. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Ar-Rabi': *Al-Lat was a man* who mixed **Sawiq** (barley mash) with water for pilgrims in Jahiliyyah — when he died they camped by his grave and worshipped him. |
| **Al-'Uzza** | A **tree** at Nakhlah, between Makkah and At-Ta'if, dressed with a monument and curtains. The name derived from Allah's Name **Al-'Aziz**. Revered by Quraysh. |
| **Manat** | A site at **Mushallal near Qudayd**, between Makkah and Madinah. Revered by Khuza'ah, Aws and Khazraj, who used to declare their Hajj from beside it. |

At Uhud, Abu Sufyan called out, *"We have Al-'Uzza, but you do not have Al-'Uzza."* The Messenger ﷺ instructed the reply: ***"Allah is our Supporter, and you have no support."***

### Khalid and Al-'Uzza

An-Nasa'i, from Abu At-Tufayl. After the conquest of Makkah, Khalid bin Al-Walid was sent to Nakhlah. He cut down the three trees, demolished the house built around them, and returned. The Prophet ﷺ said: ***"Go back and finish your mission, for you have not finished it."***

He went back. The custodians saw him coming and began calling on Al-'Uzza. And what came out was **a naked woman with wild hair, throwing sand over her head**. Khalid killed her with his sword and returned. The Prophet ﷺ said: ***"That was Al-'Uzza."***

*(The rest of the Arabian idol-map, from Ibn Ishaq and Ibn Hisham: Al-Lat destroyed by Al-Mughirah bin Shu'bah and Abu Sufyan, with a Masjid built on the site; Manat by Abu Sufyan or 'Ali; **Dhul-Khalasah** — called the Southern Ka'bah, to Makkah's Northern — by Jarir bin Abdullah Al-Bajali; **Fals** of Tay' by 'Ali, who found two swords in its treasury and was given them as spoils; **Riyam** in San'a', which had a black dog in it; **Ruda'**; **Dhul-Ka'bat** at Sindad.)*

### The verdict

Then the two blows. First the absurdity — *is it for you the males and for Him the females?* If you would not accept that split between yourself and another human being, **that indeed is a division most unfair** — so how do you impose it between yourselves and Allah?

Then the definition, which is one of the most quoted verses in the Qur'an on the subject:

> ***They are but names which you have named — you and your fathers — for which Allah has sent down no authority.***

No *sultan*, says Ibn Kathir: no proof. What they have instead is exactly two things — **inherited habit**, the path their forefathers took, and **desire**: the appetite for leadership, honour and reverence that comes from keeping the ancestors' religion alive. *They follow but a guess and that which they themselves desire* — while **the guidance has already reached them from their Lord.**

## 6. Wishing Is Not Earning (53:24–26)

> *Or shall man have what he wishes?*

Not everyone who claims guidance is guided; not everyone gets the good he imagines for himself. Ibn Kathir cites *It will not be in accordance with your desires, nor those of the People of the Scripture* (4:123).

Then a hadith from Abu Hurayrah with a strange edge to it:

> ***"When one of you wishes for something, let him be careful with what he wishes for, because he does not know what part of his wish will be written for him."***

And *to Allah belongs the last and the first* — He owns both worlds and does as He wills in both.

On intercession (53:26): even **the angels closest to Him** cannot intercede until He permits it, and only for one He is pleased with (compare 2:255 and 34:23). Ibn Kathir's rhetorical turn: if that is the position of the angels, on what basis would an idolater expect a stone or a tree to intercede for him — with no permission, and no revealed licence to worship it in the first place?

---

## 7. Conjecture Is No Substitute for Truth (53:27–30)

> *Verily, those who believe not in the Hereafter name the angels with female names. But they have no knowledge thereof. They follow but conjecture, and verily, conjecture is no substitute for the truth.*

The theological point is simple and Ibn Kathir makes it with 43:19 — *did they witness their creation?* They are stating as fact something they cannot possibly have observed.

But the epistemic point is broader than idolatry, and this is why the verse travels so far beyond its context. **Guesswork does not become knowledge by being repeated confidently.** The hadith he attaches:

> ***"Beware of suspicion, for suspicion is the most lying speech."***

Then the instruction that follows (53:29) — **withdraw from him who turns away from Our Reminder and desires nothing but the life of this world.** Ibn Kathir defines that man precisely: one whose *aim and knowledge are both concentrated on this life*. And *that is what they could reach of knowledge* — worldly striving is **the ceiling of what they ever managed to learn**.

Which produces one of the most quietly frightening du'as in the tafsir:

> ***"O Allah, make not this life the greatest of our concerns, nor the limit of the knowledge we attain."***

---

## 8. The Good-Doers, and the Sins That Get Forgiven (53:31–32)

> *Those who avoid great sins and Al-Fawahish except Al-Lamam — verily, your Lord is of vast forgiveness.*

**Al-Lamam** = small faults and minor errors. The mechanism is the one stated in 4:31: *if you avoid the great sins which you are forbidden, We shall expiate from you your small sins, and admit you to a Noble Entrance.* Avoiding the major covers the minor.

Ibn Abbas said he had never seen anything that explains *Al-Lamam* better than Abu Hurayrah's hadith (in both Sahihs):

> *Allah has decreed for the son of Adam his share of Zina, and he will inevitably meet it. The **Zina of the eye** is the look; the **Zina of the tongue** is speech; the heart wishes and desires — **and the private parts either confirm all of that, or deny it.***

Ibn Mas'ud extends the list: the eyes by looking, the lips by kissing, the hands by seizing, the feet by walking towards it — *and the private parts confirm or deny*. If the act occurs, it is Zina. If not, **it was Lamam**. Abu Hurayrah, asked directly, gave the same: kissing, winking, looking, embracing — *when the act itself occurs, Ghusl is obligatory, and that is Zina.*

The pastoral shape of this is important: minor lapses are not treated as nothing, but they are not treated as ruin either — and the line where they stop being minor is drawn very clearly.

## 9. Do Not Certify Yourself (53:32)

> *He knows you well when He created you from the earth, and when you were fetuses in your mothers' wombs. So ascribe not purity to yourselves.*

He knew you when Adam was still clay, when your ancestors were drawn from his loins *as small as ants* and divided into two groups; He knew you when the angel was commanded to record your provision, lifespan, deeds, and whether you would be among the happy or the wretched. Given that — **whom exactly is the self-praise for?**

Ibn Kathir gathers three episodes:

- Muslim records that **Zaynab bint Abi Salamah** had been named *Barrah* — "the pious one." The Prophet ﷺ forbade it: *"Do not ascribe purity to yourselves; Allah knows best who among you is pious."* They asked what to call her instead. *"Call her Zaynab."*
- A man praised another man in the Prophet's presence. ﷺ: ***"Woe to you, you have cut your friend's neck!"*** — repeated. Then the permitted form: *"I reckon so-and-so is thus and so; Allah knows best about him, and I do not certify anyone before Allah"* — **and only if you actually know him to be as you describe.**
- A man praised **Uthman bin Affan** to his face, and **Al-Miqdad bin Al-Aswad started throwing sand in the praiser's face**, saying the Messenger of Allah ﷺ ordered them to throw dirt in the faces of the flatterers.

The corrective for despair, though, is placed right beside it: *your Lord is of vast forgiveness* — and Ibn Kathir reaches for 39:53, *O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, despair not of the mercy of Allah.*

## 10. The Man Who Gave a Little and Stopped (53:33–41)

> *Did you observe him who turned away, and gave a little, then stopped? Is the knowledge of the Unseen with him, so that he sees?*

Ibn Abbas: *gave a little, then stopped giving* — and Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ikrimah and Qatadah agree. Ikrimah and Sa'id offer an image for it: **people digging a well who strike rock, announce "we're finished," and walk away from the work.**

Then the piercing question. This man stopped spending out of **fear of poverty** — so: does he have access to the Unseen? Has he seen the ledger? Does he know that if he keeps giving, his wealth runs out? He has cut off charity and cut off kinship on the strength of a forecast he cannot make.

Against him:

> ***"O Bilal, spend, and fear no reduction from the Owner of the Throne."***

and *whatsoever you spend of anything, He will replace it; and He is the Best of providers* (34:39).

### What was in the scrolls of Musa and Ibrahim

> *Or is he not informed of what is in the Suhuf of Musa, and of Ibrahim who fulfilled?*

On *fulfilled* (*waffa*): Sa'id bin Jubayr and Ath-Thawri — he conveyed everything he was ordered to convey. Ibn Abbas — he delivered all that Allah commanded. Qatadah — he obeyed Allah and delivered His message to creation; this is the reading Ibn Jarir preferred. Compare 2:124: *when his Lord tried Ibrahim with commands and he fulfilled them, He said: I am making you an Imam for mankind.* **The leadership was the wage of the completion.**

And the content of those ancient scrolls turns out to be four principles that have never been superseded:

1. **No burdened soul bears the burden of another** (compare 35:18 — even a close relative cannot carry any of your load).
2. **Man shall have nothing but what he strove for.**
3. **His striving will be seen** — on the Day of Resurrection (compare 9:105).
4. **He will be recompensed with the fullest recompense.**

### The apparent exception, resolved

If a man gets nothing but his own work, what of the famous hadith that **when a person dies his deeds cease except three** — ongoing charity, knowledge benefited from, or a righteous child who prays for him?

Ibn Kathir's answer is elegant: **all three are his own work**, arriving later.

- The child — *"the best of what a man consumes is from his own earning, and his child is from his earning."*
- The endowment — a trace of his own action, and *We record what they sent before them and their traces* (36:12).
- The knowledge — *"whoever calls to guidance has a reward like the rewards of those who follow him, without diminishing their rewards in the least."*

Nothing is credited to you that you did not set in motion. The three exceptions are not exceptions at all; they are **long-tailed instalments of your own effort**.

## 11. He Is the One Who — (53:42–55)

A run of short, hammering attributions, each opening *and that it is He who*:

- **To your Lord is the End** — the final return. Mu'adh bin Jabal stood before his tribe and said: *"O children of Awd, I am the emissary of Allah's Messenger to you. Know that the return is to Allah — either to Paradise or to the Fire."*
- **He makes laugh and makes weep** — He created in His creatures the capacity for both, and the causes of both.
- **He causes death and gives life** — compare 67:2.
- **He creates the pairs, male and female, from a Nutfah when it is emitted** — with the full sequence at 75:36–40: a drop, then a clinging clot, then shaping and proportioning, then two sexes. *Is He not able to give life to the dead?*
- **Upon Him is another bringing forth** — He who originated it can repeat it.
- **He gives much (Aghna) and gives little (Aqna)** — Mujahid: He gives wealth, and He gives servants. Ibn Abbas and Mujahid elsewhere: He grants, and He grants **contentment**.
- **He is the Lord of Ash-Shi'ra** — Sirius, Mirzam Al-Jawza', a star **that a group of Arabs actually worshipped**. The line is aimed: the thing you prostrate to has an Owner.

Then the record of what He has done to those who refused:

- **The former 'Ad** — people of Hud, descendants of 'Ad son of Iram son of Sam son of Nuh; *Iram of the lofty pillars, the like of which was never created in the land* (89:6–8). The strongest and most defiant of peoples, destroyed by *a furious violent wind, imposed on them for seven nights and eight days in succession* (69:6–7).
- **Thamud — He spared none.**
- **The people of Nuh, before them** — *more unjust and more rebellious* than any who came after.
- **The overthrown cities** — Lut's people, turned upside down and rained with stones of Sijjil; *and how evil was the rain of those who had been warned* (26:173).

And the refrain that closes the sequence: ***then which of the graces of your Lord will you doubt?*** Qatadah reads it as addressed to man generally — which of Allah's favours to you do you dispute? Ibn Jurayj read it as addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, but Ibn Kathir prefers the first, as did Ibn Jarir.

## 12. The Imminent Thing (53:56–62)

> *This is a warner from the warners of old. The Azifah draws near. None besides Allah can avert it.*

He is not a novelty — *say: I am not a new thing among the messengers* (46:9). And the Hour is called **Al-Azifah**, "the one drawing near." Nobody but Allah can hold it back; nobody but Allah knows when it lands. Compare 54:1, the opening of the next surah: *the Hour has drawn near.*

Ibn Kathir explains the *urgency* of a warner with a phrase from the hadith — ***"I am the naked warner."*** A man who sees a raid coming and runs to his people so fast he does not stop to dress. That is the posture the surah is describing.

And a hadith from Sahl bin Sa'd on the sins nobody bothers to count:

> ***"Beware of belittled sins. The likeness of belittled sins is a people who camp in the bottom of a valley: one brings a stick, and another brings a stick, until they have cooked their bread. Belittled sins, when a man is taken to account for them, will destroy him."***

Two twigs are nothing. The fire is not nothing.

### Laughing at it

> *Do you then wonder at this recitation, and laugh and weep not, while you are Samidun?*

*Wonder* here means doubting its truth; *laugh* means mockery. The believers' response is the opposite — *they fall down on their faces weeping, and it increases their humility* (17:109).

**Samidun**: Ibn Abbas (via Sufyan Ath-Thawri from his father) gives a Yemeni usage — *ismid lana* means "sing for us," so **Samidun = singing**, drowning the recitation in entertainment. Ikrimah agrees. In another narration from Ibn Abbas, and from Mujahid and Ikrimah, it means simply **turning away**.

### And then the ground

> ***So fall down in prostration to Allah, and worship Him.***

With submission, sincerity and **Tawhid**.

Al-Bukhari records from Ibn Abbas: **the Prophet ﷺ prostrated on reciting An-Najm, and those present prostrated with him — Muslims, idolaters, jinn and men.**

The scene has a coda. **Al-Muttalib bin Abi Wada'ah** was there, still an idolater, and he alone raised his head and refused. He said later that ever since becoming Muslim, he never heard anyone recite this surah to its end without prostrating with whoever was prostrating.

He spent the rest of his life making up one sujud.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**An-Najm is the surah that asks what you have actually seen.**

One man is described as having *seen* — a real figure, at a real distance, at the boundary of creation, with a gaze that neither wandered nor grasped for more. Everyone else in the surah is dealing in **names their fathers gave them, guesses they cannot verify, and appetites they have dressed up as convictions**. *They are but names which you have named, for which Allah has sent down no authority.*

And the practical residue, once the idols are cleared away, is a short and unsentimental list:

- **Nobody carries your load, and nobody's load lands on you.**
- **You get what you worked for** — and even the deeds that keep paying after you die are your own work, arriving late.
- **Wishing is not earning**, and be careful what you wish, because you do not know which part of it will be granted.
- **Do not certify your own purity** — He knew you when you were a fetus.
- **Do not stop giving out of a forecast you are not qualified to make.**
- **Small sins are twigs, and twigs cook bread.**

Then the last word, which is not an argument at all. After sixty-one verses of evidence, testimony and dismantling, the surah does not conclude with *therefore believe*. It says **go down**. And the first people to hear it did — including the ones who had come to laugh.
