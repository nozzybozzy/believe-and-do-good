*Revealed in Makkah · 8 ayat · "The Opening Forth" / "The Relief" — also called Alam Nashrah, after its first words*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ash-Sharh is eight verses long and does only one thing, but it does it in an unusual order.

It does not begin by promising relief. It begins by **listing relief already given**. Four verses of completed favours — *We opened your chest, We removed your burden, the one that weighed down your back, We raised your fame* — all in the past tense, all phrased as questions the Prophet ﷺ cannot answer with anything but *yes*.

Only after that record is established does the surah state the principle:

> *Verily, along with every hardship is relief.*

And it says it **twice**, back to back.

The order is the argument. The general rule is not offered as consolation in the abstract; it is offered as a summary of what has already happened to him. *Look at the pattern of your own life — now here is the law behind it.*

Then, having settled him, the surah gives him work: **when you are done, stand up again.** Relief is not a place to stop.

---

## 1. "Have We Not Opened Your Breast?" (94:1)

Ibn Kathir's gloss is three words wide and entirely physical in its imagery:

> `We illuminated it, and We made it spacious, vast and wide.`

Light and room. Not comfort — **capacity**.

He then shows this is a standing divine act, not a one-off miracle for the Prophet ﷺ alone, by citing:

> *And whomsoever Allah wills to guide, He opens his breast to Islam.* **(6:125)**

The same verb, the same gift, available to anyone Allah wills to guide.

And then the observation that turns the verse outward:

**Just as Allah expanded his chest, He also made His Law vast, wide, accommodating and easy — containing no difficulty, hardship or burden.**

The shape of the Messenger's inner life and the shape of the religion he brought are the same shape. A wide chest was given a wide Law.

---

## 2. The Burden That Was Removed (94:2–3)

> *And removed from you your burden, which weighed down your back.*

Ibn Kathir reads *wizr* here through another verse addressed to the Prophet ﷺ:

> *That Allah may forgive you your sins of the past and the future.* **(48:2)**

On *anqada* — "weighed down" — he notes it refers to **the sound**: the creaking a back makes under a load too heavy for it. More than one of the Salaf explained the verse simply as `its burden weighed heavy upon you.`

The point is not that the burden was light. It is that it was crushing, audibly so, **and it was lifted anyway**.

---

## 3. "And Raised High Your Fame" (94:4)

Two of the shortest and most far-reaching comments in the tafsir:

- **Mujahid:** "It means whenever I (Allah) am remembered, you will also be remembered."
- **Qatadah:** "Allah elevated the remembrance of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ both in this world as well as in the hereafter."

Mujahid's reading is the one you live inside without noticing. The *shahadah*, the *adhan*, the *iqamah*, the *tashahhud* in every prayer — the Name of Allah and the name of His Messenger are bound together in the same breath, five times a day, everywhere on earth, permanently. The verse is not a prediction. It is a description of something Allah built into the structure of the religion itself.

---

## 4. The Verse Repeated Twice (94:5–6)

> *Verily, along with every hardship is relief.*
> *Verily, along with every hardship is relief.*

Ibn Kathir's explanation has two halves, and the second is the one worth memorising.

**First:** whatever troubles are encountered by the creation, **Allah always provides a relief with it.**

**Second — and he is emphatic:**

> **The relief does not merely come after the difficulty: it is provided *with* it.**

Not *after*. *With*. The Arabic preposition is *ma'a* — accompaniment, not sequence. The relief is already inside the hardship, travelling alongside it, present in the same moment even when it is not yet visible.

This is why the verse is repeated rather than stated once. The doubling is the reassurance: the same guarantee, said again immediately, for someone who did not have room to believe it the first time.

---

## 5. When You Finish, Begin (94:7–8)

> *So when you have finished, devote yourself to Allah's worship.*
> *And to your Lord turn intentions and hopes.*

Ibn Kathir:

**"When you are free from difficulties and blessed with relief, then turn towards your Lord for His worship, and turn to Him for all hopes."**

The surah refuses to end on the promise. Having given four favours and one guarantee, it asks for a response — and the response is not gratitude in words but **redirection**: the moment your hands are empty, fill them with worship; the moment your circumstances ease, aim your wanting at Him rather than at the ease.

Verse 7 governs the body. Verse 8 governs the heart. *Fansab* — exert yourself. *Farghab* — desire, long for, set your hope on. Effort and appetite, both pointed the same way.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Ease is not on the other side of your hardship. It is inside it.** That is Ibn Kathir's one insistence in this surah, and he makes it against the reading everyone instinctively reaches for: the relief does not merely come *after* the difficulty — it is provided *with* it.

And the surah proves the claim before it makes it. It shows the Prophet ﷺ a chest that was opened, a back that was unloaded, a name that was raised — and only then tells him the rule those things were examples of.

Then it removes the one thing that could go wrong next. When the relief does arrive, the danger is that you rest in it. So: **finish, and stand up. Turn to your Lord for worship, and turn to Him for everything you want.**
