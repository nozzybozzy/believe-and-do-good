*Revealed in Makkah · 45 ayat · named for the disjointed letter that opens it · the first surah of the Mufassal*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Qaf is the surah the Prophet ﷺ recited **every Friday from the minbar**, and at both `Id prayers. Ibn Kathir tells us exactly why, and his sentence is effectively a table of contents for the whole surah: it contains *the beginning of creation, Resurrection, the Return, the Standing before Allah, the Reckoning, Paradise, the Fire, reward and punishment, and lessons of both encouragement and discouragement.* It is the entire arc of a human being's existence, compressed into forty-five ayat, delivered to the largest gathering of the week.

The surah has one adversary throughout: the man who says **"That is a far return"** — resurrection is too implausible to take seriously. Qaf answers him not with argument but with **proximity**. Everything he thought was distant is shown to be near: the sky he never looks at, the earth that is already digesting the dead, the angel closer to him than his own jugular vein, the scribe recording the sentence he just spoke, the death that will find him in his own home, the caller who will summon him **from a near place**, and Paradise, brought forward until it is **not far off**.

The surah closes by releasing the Prophet ﷺ from the burden of results: *you are not the one to force them.*

---

## 1. The First Surah of the Mufassal

Ibn Kathir opens with a technical point he considers settled. Qaf is **the first surah of the Mufassal** section of the Qur'an — the correct view. It is also said the Mufassal begins with Al-Hujurat. What he rejects outright is the popular notion that it begins at `Amma An-Naba (78), because **no respected scholar ever held it**.

The evidence is a report from Aws bin Hudhayfah, recorded by Ibn Majah and Imam Ahmad. He asked the Companions how they divided the Qur'an, and they answered: **three, five, seven, nine, eleven, thirteen, and the Mufassal as one.** Counting them out — 2–4, 5–9, 10–16, 17–25, 26–36, 37–49 — brings you to forty-eight surahs after Al-Fatihah. The next one is Qaf.

**Its virtues:**

- **Umm Hisham bint Harithah**, whose family oven and the Prophet's ﷺ oven were one and the same for a year or two, said she memorised Qaf **from his tongue** — because he recited it every Friday on the minbar. (Muslim)
- The daughter of Al-Harith bin An-Nu`man said the same: Qaf was the only surah she memorised from his mouth, from the Friday khutbah. (Muslim, An-Nasa'i)
- **Umar bin Al-Khattab** ؓ asked Abu Waqid Al-Laythi what the Prophet ﷺ recited at `Id: **Qaf and Al-Qamar.** (Ahmad, Muslim, the Four Sunan)

## 2. The Oath, and the Objection (50:1–5)

> *Qaf. By the Glorious Qur'an.*

**Qaf** is one of the disjointed letters, like Sad, Nun, Alif Lam Mim, Ha Mim, Ta Sin — Mujahid and others said so, and Ibn Kathir declines to repeat what he already covered at the start of Al-Baqarah.

The oath is by the Qur'an *which falsehood cannot approach from before it or behind it* (41:42). But notice what is missing: **the subject of the oath is never stated in words.** Ibn Kathir explains that it is carried in the meaning — the affirmation of prophethood and resurrection — exactly as in *Sad. By the Qur'an full of reminding. Nay, those who disbelieve are in false pride and opposition* (38:1–2).

Then the two objections that generate the rest of the surah:

**First, they wondered that the warner was a human being** from among themselves. The answer is that this is not strange at all: Allah chooses Messengers from angels and from humans — *Is it a wonder for mankind that We sent revelation to a man from among themselves?* (10:2).

**Second, the resurrection itself**: *When we are dead and have become dust — that is a far return.* They meant: once the body has disintegrated and the organs are torn apart, restoration is not merely difficult but **implausible**.

Allah answers with knowledge, not power: ***We know that which the earth takes of them.*** Ibn Abbas (via Al-`Awfi), and similarly Mujahid, Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak: what the earth consumes of **their flesh, skin, bones and hair**. Nothing about where a body went or what it became has ever left Allah's knowledge — and beyond knowledge, *with Us is a Book preserved*, in which everything is already precisely recorded.

Their real problem is diagnosed in 50:5: they denied the truth when it came, **so they are in a Marij state** — *disarray, confusion*, defying the very characteristics of truth. Ibn Kathir's principle: this is the condition of everyone who rejects the truth; **whatever they say afterwards is false.** Compare *Certainly you have differing ideas* (51:8–9).

## 3. Look Up, Look Down (50:6–11)

The argument now moves outdoors. *Have they not looked at the heaven above them* — how it was built, how it was adorned, and that **there are no Furuj in it**: no clefts (Mujahid), no rifts or cracks. Cross-referenced to the famous challenge of 67:3–4, where the eye is sent back twice and returns **exhausted, having found no flaw**.

Then the earth: spread out and made spacious, with **Rawasi** — mountains fixed in place so it does not shake with its inhabitants — and *every lovely pair* (**Bahij**, a beautiful scene) of plant, fruit and vegetation; *of everything We created pairs, that you may reflect* (51:49).

All of this is described as **an insight and a Reminder for every servant who turns to Allah** (*munib*) — one who submits in humility and repentance, in awe of Him. The signs are public; the benefit is not. It goes to the penitent.

Then **blessed water** from the sky, producing gardens, grain that is reaped, and **date palms Basiqat** — tall and towering, per Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and As-Suddi — with fruit in arranged clusters. All of it *a provision for the servants*.

And then the pivot the whole passage was built for:

> *And We give life therewith to a dead land. **Thus will be the Resurrection.***

Barren ground, rain, and the land stirs, swells, and produces beauty where there was nothing. Ibn Kathir's point is that this **already happens, visibly, every year** — and it is a greater sign than the denial of those who find resurrection far-fetched. Reinforced three ways: *The creation of the heavens and the earth is greater than the creation of mankind* (40:57); *was He not wearied by their creation?* (46:33); *He Who gives life to the earth surely gives life to the dead* (41:39).

## 4. The Roll Call of Ruined Nations (50:12–15)

A warning to Quraysh, delivered as a list of people who used the same argument and are no longer here: **the people of Nuh** (drowned in a flood that reached every people on earth), **the Dwellers of Ar-Rass**, **Thamud, `Ad, Fir`awn**, **the brethren of Lut** (Sadum, whose land was overturned into a reeking lake — as foul, Ibn Kathir says, as the disbelief and defiance they held), **the Dwellers of Al-Aykah** (Shu`ayb's people), and **the people of Tubba`**, king of Yemen.

*Everyone of them denied the Messengers* — plural, though most received only one. Ibn Kathir's rule: **whoever denies a single Messenger has denied them all**, and had all the Messengers been sent to them, they would have rejected all of them. Compare *The people of Nuh denied the Messengers* (26:105).

*So My threat took effect.* The warning transfers directly: the latter have denied their Messenger just as the former denied theirs.

Then the question that closes the section: ***Were We then tired with the first creation?*** Did the first act of creation exhaust Us, such that repeating it is now in doubt? *Nay, they are in confused doubt about a new creation.*

- *He originates creation, then repeats it, and **that is easier for Him*** (30:27)
- *Who will give life to bones when they are rotten? Say: He will give life to them Who created them the first time* (36:78–79)
- And the hadith Qudsi: **"The son of Adam insults Me when he says, 'He will not resurrect me as He created me before.' But originating creation is not easier for Me than reproducing it."**

## 5. Nearer Than the Jugular Vein (50:16–18)

> *And indeed We created man, and We know what his self whispers to him. And We are nearer to him than his jugular vein.*

Allah knows every thought that crosses the mind — good or evil. Ibn Kathir immediately pairs this with mercy: **"Allah has forgiven my Ummah what they talk to themselves about, as long as they do not utter it or act on it."** (Sahih)

Then comes one of the most careful pieces of theological reasoning in the surah. **What does "We are nearer" mean?**

Some scholars explained "We" as "Our knowledge," specifically to avoid any suggestion of **incarnation or indwelling (hulul)** — creeds Ibn Kathir says are **false by the consensus of Muslims**. But he argues the interpretation isn't necessary, and rests his case on the grammar: Allah did **not** say *"I am closer to him,"* He said ***We*** — the plural pointing to **His angels**, exactly as in *We are nearer to him than you, but you see not* (56:85), spoken of the dying, in reference to the angels who take the soul. He supports it with 15:9 — *We sent down the Dhikr and We will guard it* — where the sending down was carried out by angels **by Allah's leave**.

So: the angel is closer to a man than his own jugular vein, by Allah's power and leave — **just as the devil is as close to him as the blood running in his veins**, as the truthful Prophet ﷺ informed us.

And the two who are nearest are recording. *The two receivers* — one seated **on the right**, one **on the left** — *not a word does he utter but there is a watcher by him, ready.* Al-Hasan and Qatadah: **every word spoken is written**, which Ibn Kathir notes is the apparent meaning of the ayah. Compare *Kiraman Katibin — they know all that you do* (82:10–12).

The hadith that made a Companion afraid to speak, from **Bilal bin Al-Harith Al-Muzani**:

> A man may utter a word pleasing to Allah, not realising how far it will reach — and Allah decrees His pleasure with him until the Day he meets Him. And a man may utter a word angering Allah, not realising how dreadful it is — and Allah decrees His anger against him until the Day he meets Him.

**Alqamah** used to say: *"How many words have I not spoken, because of the hadith Bilal bin Al-Harith narrated."*

## 6. Death, the Trumpet, and the Removed Veil (50:19–22)

> *And the stupor of death will come in truth: "This is what you have been avoiding!"*

Ibn Kathir gives the ayah two readings, and both bite:

1. **What you spent your life fleeing has arrived and moved into your house.**
2. **You never had any way of escaping it in the first place.**

In the Sahih, the Prophet ﷺ — wiping the sweat from his face as death overcame him — said: ***"Glory be to Allah! Verily, death has its stupor."***

At-Tabarani records a parable from Samurah: a fox owed a debt to the earth. Tired, it went into its den to sleep, and the earth said, *"O fox, pay my debt."* It came out howling and continued until its neck gave out and it died. **There is no den.**

Then *the Trumpet will be blown — that will be the Day of the threat.* Ibn Kathir recalls the hadith: **"How can I feel comfort while the angel of the Trumpet has already placed it to his mouth and lowered his forehead, waiting to be commanded?"** They asked what to say. He said: ***Hasbunallahu wa ni`mal Wakil*** — Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best disposer of affairs.

*Every person will come forth with a **Sa'iq** and a **Shahid*** — an angel driving him to the gathering, and an angel bearing witness against his deeds. This is the apparent meaning, preferred by Ibn Jarir, and **Uthman bin Affan** ؓ explained it from the pulpit in exactly those terms.

Then, to every human being: ***Indeed you were heedless of this. Now We have removed from you your covering — and sharp is your sight this Day.*** Everyone's vision clears on that Day, **including the disbelievers' — and it will do them no good**: *How clearly they will see and hear the Day they come to Us* (19:38); *Our Lord, we have now seen and heard, so send us back* (32:12).

## 7. The Dispute Before Allah (50:23–29)

The recording angel testifies: ***"Here is his record, ready with me"*** — complete, with nothing added and nothing deleted.

The command is then issued, apparently to the **Sa'iq and the Shahid** — the one who drove him there and the one who testified: ***"Both of you throw into Hell every stubborn disbeliever"***, described in four terms:

- **Kaffar `anid** — one who knowingly and stubbornly rejected the truth, contradicting it with falsehood
- **Hinderer of good** — who did not fulfil what he was commanded, nor keep ties of kinship, nor give charity
- **Transgressor** — who exceeds the limits; Qatadah: *"a transgressor in his speech, his behaviour and his affairs"*
- **Doubter** — who doubts, and **seeds doubt in others** who examine his conduct

And above all: *who set up another god with Allah.*

Abu Sa`id Al-Khudri ؓ narrated (Ahmad) that **a neck will emerge from the Fire and speak**: *"Today I have been entrusted with three: every obstinate tyrant; everyone who ascribed another god with Allah; and whoever took a life without right."* Then it closes in on them and casts them into the midst of Jahannam.

Now the second **qarin** appears — not the angel this time but **the devil assigned to that man** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah and others). He disowns him on the spot: ***"Our Lord, I did not push him to transgression"*** — *I did not lead him astray* — *"but he was himself in error far astray."*

Ibn Kathir sets beside it Shaytan's speech in 14:22: *Allah promised you truly, and I promised you and betrayed you. **I had no authority over you except that I called you, and you responded.** So blame me not, blame yourselves.*

The man protests that the devil misled him. The devil protests that the man was already lost. Allah closes the case:

> ***"Dispute not in front of Me. I had already sent you the threat in advance."***

`Proof was established against you by the words of the Messengers and the Books sent down — the evidences and signs were all delivered.` And then: ***"The sentence that comes from Me cannot be changed"*** — *"I have made My decision,"* per Mujahid — ***"and I am not unjust to the servants."*** No one is punished except on account of his own sins, and only after the proof reached him.

## 8. "Are There Any More?" (50:30–35)

Hell is asked: ***"Are you filled?"*** and answers: ***"Are there any more?"***

Ibn Kathir takes this at its apparent meaning and supports it with three hadiths:

- **Anas** ؓ (Ahmad, Muslim): people are thrown in and it keeps asking for more, **until the Mighty Lord places His Foot upon it**, its sides are drawn together, and it says: *"Enough! Enough, by Your grace and Your generosity!"* Meanwhile **Paradise still has room**, so Allah creates another creation and settles them in it.
- **Abu Hurayrah** ؓ (Al-Bukhari): the same exchange, ending in *"Qat! Qat!"* — enough, enough.
- **Abu Hurayrah** ؓ (Al-Bukhari): **Paradise and the Fire quarrelled.** The Fire said, *"I have been favoured with the arrogant and the tyrants."* Paradise said, *"What is wrong with me, that only the poor and humble enter me?"* Allah said to Paradise: ***"You are My mercy, by which I show mercy to whom I will."*** And to the Fire: ***"You are My punishment, by which I punish whom I will — and I shall fill both of you."***

Then the counter-image: ***Paradise will be brought near to those who had Taqwa — not far off.*** Qatadah, Abu Malik and As-Suddi: brought close. And Ibn Kathir notes the phrase *not far off* refers to the Day itself — **everything that is bound to come is near.**

Who receives it? Four descriptions:

- **Awwab** — one who returns to Allah in sincere repentance, *intending not to repeat the sin*
- **Hafiz** — one who guards his covenant with Allah and does not break or betray it
- **Who feared Ar-Rahman in the unseen** — who feared Him in private, where **only Allah could see him**. (The Prophet ﷺ, listing those shaded on that Day: *"a man who remembered Allah in seclusion, and his eyes overflowed."*)
- **Who came with a penitent heart** — meeting Allah with a heart turned back to Him, **free of Shirk** and humbled before Him

*Enter therein in peace* — safe from His punishment, and greeted with **Salam by the angels** (Qatadah) — *this is a Day of eternal life*: they will never die, never be moved from it, and never wish to be.

*There they will have all that they desire* — whatever delight they wish for is brought before them — ***and We have more.*** Ibn Kathir reads this with *For those who do good is the best, **and even more*** (10:26), and with the hadith of **Suhayb bin Sinan Ar-Rumi** ؓ in Sahih Muslim: **the "more" is looking upon the Face of Allah.**

## 9. A Heart, or an Attentive Ear (50:36–38)

*How many generations stronger in power did We destroy before them?* — more numerous, mightier, who built more on the earth than Quraysh ever did. *And they went about the land* — Ibn Abbas: **they left their traces across it**; Qatadah: *"they travelled the land for trade and provision more than you do."*

***Could they find any place of refuge?*** No shelter from Allah's decree. Nothing they accumulated averted the punishment when it came. **Neither will yours.**

Then the ayah that names the two kinds of people who actually benefit from any of this:

> *Verily, therein is a reminder for **him who has a heart**, or **gives ear while he is heedful**.*

- **A heart** — a sound understanding with which he comprehends; a good mind (Mujahid)
- **Gives ear while heedful** — he hears, grasps it in his mind, and takes its meaning. Mujahid: *he does not talk to himself, but listens with an attentive heart.* Ad-Dahhak: the Arabs said a man "gave ear" when he heard with his ears **while his heart was present and not absent**. Ath-Thawri said the same.

And the last argument for resurrection: *We created the heavens and the earth and all between them in six Days, **and nothing of fatigue touched Us***. Qatadah records that the Jews claimed Allah created in six days and then **rested on the seventh**, the Sabbath, which is why they keep it as a holiday — and Allah sent this down to deny it. No sleep, no exhaustion, no weariness. Compare 46:33, 40:57, and *Are you harder to create, or the heaven He built?* (79:27).

## 10. Patience, and Two Prayers (50:39–40)

> *So bear with patience what they say, and glorify the praises of your Lord **before the rising of the sun and before its setting**.*

Ibn Kathir places this historically: **before the Isra' journey there were two ordained prayers** — one before sunrise, one before sunset — plus Qiyam Al-Layl, which was obligatory on the Prophet ﷺ and his followers for a time before being lifted for the Ummah. On the night journey, the five daily prayers were ordained, including **Fajr and `Asr** — the same two hours named here.

Which is why **Jarir bin Abdullah** ؓ narrated (Ahmad) that the Prophet ﷺ looked at the full moon and said:

> *"You will be brought before your Lord and will see Him as you see this moon, without crowding or difficulty. **So if you can avoid missing the prayer before sunrise and the prayer before sunset, do so.**"*

— and then recited this very ayah.

*And during a part of the night glorify His praises, and after the prostrations.* Two readings, both from the Salaf:

1. **Tasbih after the prayers** — Ibn Abbas via Mujahid. Supported by the hadith of the poor migrants who complained that the wealthy could give charity and free slaves while they could not. The Prophet ﷺ taught them **SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah and Allahu Akbar, thirty-three times after every prayer**. When the wealthy heard and copied it, he said: ***"That is a favour of Allah, which He grants to whom He wills."***
2. **The two rak`ahs after Maghrib** — from Umar, Ali, Al-Hasan, Ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayrah and Abu Umamah ؓ, and from Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ash-Sha`bi, An-Nakha`i, Al-Hasan Al-Basri and Qatadah.

## 11. The Caller from a Near Place (50:41–45)

*Listen for the Day the caller will call **from a near place*** — the summons to the Judgement; the Day they hear **As-Sayhah in truth**, the blast in the Trumpet that finally delivers what most of them denied. *That will be the Day of coming out* — from the graves.

Ibn Kathir then gives the sequence in full, and it is the most vivid passage in the surah:

**Allah sends down rain from the sky.** From it, the bodies of the dead **grow in their graves like seed grows in soil after rain**. When they have grown to full strength, Israfil is commanded and blows the Trumpet a second time. The souls, held in a hollow of the Trumpet, are released and fly between the heavens and the earth. Allah declares: ***"By My grace and power, let every soul return to the body it used to inhabit."*** And each soul enters its body **the way poison enters a body**. Then the earth is uncovered from above them, and they rise and hasten toward the Reckoning — *Hastening towards the caller; the disbelievers will say: "This is a hard Day"* (54:8); *you will think you stayed but a little while* (17:52).

And in the Sahih, from Anas ؓ: ***"The earth will split open around me first."***

*That will be a gathering, quite easy for Us* — effortless. *Our command is but one, like the twinkling of an eye* (54:50); *your creation and your resurrection are only as that of a single person* (31:28).

The surah ends by turning to the Prophet ﷺ himself:

- ***We know best what they say*** — `Our knowledge encompasses all the denial they confront you with, so do not be distressed.` Compare *We know your breast is straitened by what they say — so glorify your Lord and be of those who prostrate* (15:97).
- ***And you are not the one to force them*** — a *jabbar*, one who compels. Guidance is not your mission's mechanism: *Your duty is only to convey, and on Us is the reckoning* (13:40); *You are only a reminder, not a dictator over them* (88:21–22); *Not upon you is their guidance* (2:272); *You guide not whom you like, but Allah guides whom He wills* (28:56).
- ***But warn by the Qur'an him who fears My threat*** — because those are the only ones who will take the warning: **those who fear His punishment and hope in His promise.**

Ibn Kathir closes the surah with **Qatadah's du`a**, made on this very ayah:

> *"O Allah, make us among those who fear Your threat and hope for Your promise — O **Barr** (the Subtle, Kind, Generous), O **Rahim** (the Most Merciful)."*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Qaf answers "that is a far return" by proving that nothing is far.**

The dead earth greening after rain is the resurrection, performed annually in front of you. The angel recording your speech is nearer than your jugular vein. The death you are avoiding is already in the house. The caller calls from **a near place**. Paradise is brought forward until it is **not far off**.

The only thing genuinely distant is the man who hears all of it and remains, in the surah's word, in **Marij** — disarray.

And the qualification for benefiting is stated with unusual mercy. Not scholarship, not lineage, not strength — the surah has just finished burying seven nations stronger than its audience. Only this: **a heart, or an ear that listens while the heart is present.**

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Qaf. All praise is due to Allah; He is for us the most excellent Trustee.*
