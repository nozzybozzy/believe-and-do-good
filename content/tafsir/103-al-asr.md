*Revealed in Makkah · 3 ayat · "Time" / "The Age" — named for the oath sworn at 103:1*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-`Asr is the shortest complete argument in the Qur'an. In three verses it establishes a **default verdict on the entire human race** — loss — and then names the only four things that lift a person out of it.

Its structure is a courtroom in miniature. An **oath** (by Time). A **judgement** (mankind is in loss). An **exception clause** (except those who…). Nothing is described, nothing is illustrated, no nation is cited as a warning. The surah simply states the finding and the four grounds for appeal, and stops.

Ibn Kathir's treatment reflects that economy. He spends more space on two stories about how the surah was *received* than on the verses themselves — because the reception is the point. A pagan Arab could tell instantly that this was not human speech, and the Companions treated it as the natural thing to say to one another before parting.

---

## 1. The Test That Musaylimah Failed

Before he accepted Islam, `Amr bin Al-`As visited **Musaylimah Al-Kadhdhab** ("the Liar"), the false prophet. Musaylimah asked what had lately been revealed to `Amr's friend Muhammad ﷺ.

`Amr answered that a short, concise surah had come — and recited Al-`Asr.

Musaylimah sat and thought for a while. Then he claimed something similar had been revealed to him too, and produced his own composition: an address to the **wabr** — a small furry animal resembling a cat, all ears and torso and otherwise ugly — telling it that it was nothing but two ears and a chest, and the rest of it was digging and burrowing.

*"What do you think, O `Amr?"*

`Amr's reply is the whole point of the story:

> *"By Allah! Verily, you know that I know you are lying."*

Ibn Kathir notes he saw a chain of narration for part of this account, or something close to its meaning, in volume two of Abu Bakr Al-Khara'iti's *Masawi' ul-Akhlaq*.

The lesson he draws: Musaylimah set out to compose something that would rival the Qur'an, **and it did not even convince an idol worshipper** — a man who had every tribal and political reason to want it to work, and who was not yet a Muslim.

---

## 2. How the Companions Treated It

At-Tabarani records from Abdullah bin Hisn Abi Madinah:

> Whenever two of the Prophet's ﷺ Companions met, they would not part until one had recited Surah Al-`Asr in full to the other, and one had given the other the greetings of peace.

Two men, meeting in the street, part by reminding each other that time is running out and that only four things save a person. It was their equivalent of a farewell.

And **Ash-Shafi`i** on this surah:

> *"If the people were to ponder on this Surah, it would be sufficient for them."*

---

## 3. The Oath: By Al-`Asr (103:1)

> *By Al-`Asr.*

**Al-`Asr is the time in which the movements of the Children of Adam occur, whether good or evil.** That is Ibn Kathir's preferred reading — time itself, understood not as an abstraction but as *the container of human action*. Allah swears by the very medium in which we spend ourselves.

A second view is preserved: **Malik narrated from Zayd bin Aslam** that it means the evening (the time of the `Asr prayer). Ibn Kathir notes this but records that **the first view is the popular opinion**.

The choice matters. If `Asr is time-as-such, then the oath and the verdict are inseparable: *by the thing you are spending — you are spending it at a loss.*

---

## 4. The Verdict: Man Is in Loss (103:2)

> *Verily, man is in loss.*

Allah swears that mankind is in **khusr** — which Ibn Kathir glosses as **loss and destruction**.

Note the grammar: this is a statement about the *species*. The default condition of a human being, left to himself, is not neutral. He is not breaking even. He is running down.

---

## 5. The Exception (103:3)

> *Except those who believe and do righteous deeds, and recommend one another to the truth, and recommend one another to patience.*

Allah makes an exception, from the species of man being in loss, for four things — two inward-facing, two outward-facing.

**1 & 2 — Belief and righteous deeds.**
Ibn Kathir's phrasing is deliberately anatomical: those who **believe in their hearts** and **work righteous deeds with their limbs**. Conviction and action, interior and exterior, neither sufficient alone.

**3 — Recommending one another to the truth.**
Ibn Kathir defines this concretely, not sentimentally: **to perform acts of obedience and to avoid the forbidden things.** It is not merely holding the truth — it is urging it on each other. Salvation here is not a solitary project.

**4 — Recommending one another to patience.**
And patience with *what*, specifically? Ibn Kathir is precise: **with the plots, the evils, and the harms of those who harm people because they command them to do good and forbid them from evil.**

This is the hinge of the surah. The fourth clause exists *because* of the third. Once you begin urging truth on people, some of them will hurt you for it — so the surah supplies the patience needed to survive having done the third thing. The four are a sequence, not a list.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Every human being is running at a loss by default.** Time is not neutral ground you stand on; it is the substance being spent, which is why Allah swears by it before delivering the verdict.

Only four things reverse the account, and they come in order:

1. **Believe** — in the heart.
2. **Act** — with the limbs.
3. **Urge the truth on others** — obey what is commanded, avoid what is forbidden, and press this on one another.
4. **Urge patience on others** — because step three will earn you enemies.

Musaylimah heard these three verses and understood, immediately, that he could not produce anything like them — and so did the pagan standing next to him. The Companions thought them worth reciting in full every time they said goodbye. Ash-Shafi`i thought that if people would simply sit with this surah, it would be enough for them.
