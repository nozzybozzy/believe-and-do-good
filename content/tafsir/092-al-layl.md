*Revealed in Makkah · 21 ayat · "The Night" — named for its opening oath · one of the surahs the Prophet ﷺ named for the ʿIsha' prayer*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Layl is built entirely out of pairs. Night and day. Male and female. The giver and the withholder. The path of ease and the path of hardship. The surah swears by opposites in order to establish an opposition — **"Certainly, your efforts and deeds are diverse"** — and then spends the rest of its length showing what the two divergent efforts look like and where each one lands.

The structure is unusually tidy. Three oaths, one verdict, then two portraits set side by side:

| | **The first man** | **The second man** |
|---|---|---|
| With his wealth | gives | is greedy |
| Toward his Lord | has Taqwa | thinks himself self-sufficient |
| Toward *Al-Husna* | believes in it | denies it |
| What is made smooth for him | **the path of ease** | **the path to evil** |

That word — *made smooth*, *yusr* — is where the surah becomes theologically heavy. It does not say the two men are *forced*. It says each finds his own road easy. And Ibn Kathir, sensing exactly how much weight that carries, stops the commentary at verse 10 and stacks up **four separate hadiths on Qadar**, every one of them recording a Companion asking the same anxious question and receiving the same answer.

The surah then turns to the Fire, names the two acts that earn it (denying in the heart, turning away in the limbs), and closes on a portrait of the man who is kept far from it — a man who gives, owes no one, expects nothing back, and wants only one thing: **the Face of his Lord**. Ibn Kathir, along with most commentators before him, reads that closing portrait as being about **Abu Bakr As-Siddiq**.

---

## 1. Its Place in the Prayer

Ibn Kathir opens by referring back to the hadith of **Mu'adh**, who lengthened the night prayer on his congregation and was asked by the Prophet ﷺ why he had not prayed with:

- **Al-A'la (87)** — *Glorify the Name of your Lord the Most High*
- **Ash-Shams (91)** — *By the sun and Duhaha*
- **Al-Layl (92)** — *By the night as it envelops*

Three short surahs, offered as the model for leading people who have to get up in the morning.

---

## 2. Three Oaths on Opposites (92:1–4)

> *By the night as it envelops. By the day as it appears. By Him Who created male and female. Certainly, your efforts and deeds are diverse.*

- **The night** — when it covers creation with its darkness.
- **The day** — when it appears with its light and radiance.
- **Male and female** — and here Ibn Kathir links two verses that make the point general rather than human: *And We have created you in pairs* (78:8), and *And of everything We have created pairs* (51:49).

Then the logic, stated plainly: **just as the things sworn by are opposites, so is the thing sworn about.** The deeds of the servants are diverse and opposed — there are those who do good and those who do evil. The oath is not decoration; it is the argument in miniature.

---

## 3. The Two Roads (92:5–10)

**The first man** — *he who gives and has Taqwa*: he gives what he has been commanded to give, and he fears Allah in his affairs.

*And believes in Al-Husna* — believes in the compensation for it. **Qatadah** said this; **Khusayf** said it means the reward.

*We will make smooth for him the path of ease* — **Ibn ʿAbbas**: "Meaning for goodness."

**The second man** — *he who is greedy*, meaning with what he has, *and thinks himself self-sufficient*. **Ikrimah from Ibn ʿAbbas**, recorded by Ibn Abi Hatim:

> "This means he is stingy with his wealth and considers himself to be in no need of his Lord, the Mighty and Majestic."

*And denies Al-Husna* — denies the recompense in the abode of the Hereafter. *We will make smooth for him the path to evil* — the path of evil is made easy for him too.

Ibn Kathir brings in **6:110** to show this is a settled pattern in the Qur'an: *And We shall turn their hearts and their eyes away, as they refused to believe therein the first time, and We shall leave them in their trespass to wander blindly.* His conclusion:

> **Allah rewards those who intend good with success, while whoever intends evil is abandoned — and all of this is in accordance with a preordained decree.**

---

## 4. The Qadar Hadiths — The Question Everyone Asked

This is the longest passage in the tafsir, and it exists because the Companions found these verses as unsettling as any reader does. Four narrations, four askings, one answer.

**Abu Bakr** (Imam Ahmad) — *"Do we act according to what has already been decided, or is the matter just beginning?"*
> *"Indeed it is according to what has already been decided."*

Abu Bakr: *"Then what good are deeds?"*
> *"Everyone will find it easy to do such deeds that will lead him to what he was created for."*

**ʿAli bin Abi Talib** (Al-Bukhari) — at a funeral in the cemetery of **Baqiʿ Al-Gharqad**. In the fuller version the Prophet ﷺ sat down, the Companions gathered round, and he lowered his head and began scratching the ground with a stick before speaking:

> *"There is none among you except that his place has already been written — a seat in Paradise and a seat in the Hellfire."*

They asked: should we then just depend on this and give up deeds?

> *"Perform deeds. For everyone will have the deeds of what he was created for made easy for him."*

And then he recited these very verses, 92:5–10, from *As for him who gives and has Taqwa* through *the path to evil*. **The Ayah is the Prophet's ﷺ own commentary on the question.**

**ʿUmar bin Al-Khattab** (Imam Ahmad, via Ibn ʿUmar; At-Tirmidhi graded it *Hasan Sahih*) —
> *"It is something that has already been predetermined. Therefore, work deeds, O son of Al-Khattab! For verily, each person will have things made easy for him."*

**Jabir bin ʿAbdullah** (Ibn Jarir; also Muslim) — Suraqah asks the same thing, and receives:
> *"Everyone who does deeds will have his deeds made easy for him."*

The pattern is worth noticing: **not once is the answer a metaphysical explanation.** Every time, the answer is *aʿmalu* — **work**. The decree is affirmed and the question is redirected, because the only evidence a person has of which road is being smoothed for him is the road he actually finds himself walking.

---

## 5. Wealth at the Edge (92:11)

> *And what will his wealth avail him when he goes down?*

- **Mujahid**: "This means when he dies."
- **Abu Salih and Malik**, narrating from **Zayd bin Aslam**: "When he goes down into the Fire."

Both readings share the same shape — wealth is useful right up until the exact moment it is needed most, and then it is nothing.

---

## 6. Guidance, and What Belongs to Allah (92:12–13)

> *Truly, on Us is (to give) guidance. And truly, unto Us belong the last and the first.*

Two readings of the first:

- **Qatadah**: "We will explain what is lawful and what is prohibited." Guidance as **clarification** — the road is marked.
- **Others** (via Ibn Jarir): whoever travels the path of guidance will **reach Allah** — guidance as **destination**. They compare it to *And upon Allah is the responsibility to explain the Straight path* (16:9).

And the second verse: the Hereafter and this world both belong to Him — *I am in complete control of them*. Which means there is no third place to run to.

---

## 7. The Fire That Blazes (92:14–16)

*Talazza*, says **Mujahid**, means **blazing**.

Ibn Kathir attaches three narrations here, and the first is about **how the warning was delivered**, not its content. **An-Nuʿman bin Bashir**, in a sermon, describing the Prophet ﷺ giving a sermon:

> *"I have warned you of the Fire."*

— said in a voice so loud that a man in the marketplace could have heard it from where the narrator was standing, and with such force that **the garment on his shoulder fell to his feet.**

Then the two hadiths on the lightest punishment, both from An-Nuʿman bin Bashir (Ahmad and Al-Bukhari; Muslim):

> *"The person punished lightest of the people of the Fire on the Day of Judgement will be a man with two coals placed on the soles of his feet that will cause his brain to boil."*

> *"...a man who will have two sandals whose straps are made of fire, causing his brain to boil as a cauldron boils. Yet he will not think that anyone is receiving a torment more severe than him — even though he is receiving the lightest punishment of them all."*

That last clause is the sting. The lightest is unbearable *from the inside*.

**Who enters it** — *none shall enter it save the most wretched*, meaning none will be surrounded by it on all sides except him. And he is defined by two verbs:

- *Who denies* — **in his heart**.
- *And turns away* — **from acting with his limbs**, from performing deeds according to their pillars.

Belief refused inwardly, obedience refused outwardly. Ibn Kathir pairs this with the Abu Hurayrah hadith (Ahmad, Al-Bukhari):

> *"All of my followers will enter Paradise on the Day of Judgement except whoever refuses."*
> They said: who would refuse?
> *"Whoever obeys me will enter Paradise, and whoever disobeys me has refused."*

---

## 8. The Man Kept Far From It (92:17–21)

> *And those with Taqwa will be far removed from it. He who gives of his wealth for self-purification. And who has (in mind) no favor from anyone to be paid back. Except to seek the Face of his Lord, the Most High. He, surely, will be pleased.*

Four clauses, and Ibn Kathir reads each as tightening the definition:

1. **He gives** — spending his wealth in obedience to his Lord in order to purify himself, his wealth, and whatever Allah has bestowed on him of religion and worldly things.
2. **No favor to be repaid** — he is not giving so that someone will owe him and return the good. This is the clause that separates generosity from transaction.
3. **Only the Face of his Lord** — hoping to attain **the blessing of seeing Him** in the final abode.
4. **He will be pleased** — indeed, whoever has these characteristics will be pleased.

---

## 9. Why Most Commentators Read This as Abu Bakr

Ibn Kathir notes that many scholars of tafsir — some claiming **consensus** — hold these ayat were revealed about **Abu Bakr As-Siddiq**. His own position is careful and worth quoting in substance: **the wording is general**, so it is not restricted to him; but there is no doubt he is included, and he is *the most deserving of this Ummah* to be described by it.

Then the evidence, in three pieces.

**The freed slaves** (Ibn Jarir, from ʿAmir bin ʿAbdullah bin Az-Zubayr) — Abu Bakr used to free servants in Makkah when they accepted Islam: the elderly, and the women. His father objected on purely practical grounds:

> *"O my son! I see that you are freeing people who are weak. But if you freed strong men they could stand with you, defend you and protect you."*

Abu Bakr's reply is the verse in a single sentence:

> *"O my father! I only want what is with Allah."*

His family reported that 92:5–7 was revealed about him.

**The debt at Hudaybiyyah** — ʿUrwah bin Masʿud, chief of the Thaqif tribe, said to him: *"By Allah, if I did not owe you a debt which I have not paid back, I would have responded to you."* **Abu Bakr became angry at the suggestion that he was owed anything.** Ibn Kathir's point is precise: if that was his conduct with the chiefs of the Arabs, consider his conduct with everyone else. The man who has *no favor from anyone to be paid back* is not merely someone who doesn't collect debts — he is offended by the idea of holding one.

**The gates of Paradise** (in the Two Sahihs) —

> *"Whoever equipped two riding animals in the way of Allah, the Gatekeepers of Paradise will call to him: 'O servant of Allah! This is good.'"*

Abu Bakr asked whether anyone would be called from **all** the gates. The Prophet ﷺ replied:

> *"Yes — and I hope that you will be one of them."*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Layl says that the road you are on gets easier the longer you walk it — in both directions.**

That is the surah's real claim, and it is neither comforting nor despairing. Giving makes giving easier; withholding makes withholding easier. *Yusr* is granted to the generous and *ʿusra* is granted to the greedy, and each man experiences his own descent or ascent as the natural thing to do.

The Companions heard this and asked the obvious question — *then why act at all?* — and were told, four separate times, in four separate narrations: **work.** Not because deeds overturn the decree, but because deeds are the only place the decree becomes visible. You cannot read the writing; you can only notice which direction you keep finding easy.

And the surah's final portrait tells you what "easy" looks like at its best: a man who gives away his wealth, is owed nothing by anyone, wants nothing returned, and is looking past all of it toward one thing — the Face of his Lord, the Most High.

**He, surely, will be pleased.**
