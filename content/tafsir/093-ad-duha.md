*Revealed in Makkah · 11 ayat · "The Forenoon" — named for the oath sworn at 93:1*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ad-Duha is a **surah of reassurance addressed to one man in a moment of silence**. Revelation had paused. The Prophet ﷺ felt it, his enemies noticed it, and someone said out loud what he most feared: *your Lord has left you.* The surah is Allah's answer — and the answer is not an argument. It is a **swearing of an oath, followed by a list of receipts**.

The structure is unusually clean, and Ibn Kathir's whole reading turns on it:

- **93:1–2 — the oath.** By the morning light, and by the night when it settles.
- **93:3–5 — the denial and the promise.** He has not left you, He does not hate you, what is coming is better, and He will keep giving until you are pleased.
- **93:6–8 — the past tense.** Three things you were: an orphan, unguided, poor. Three things He did: sheltered, guided, enriched.
- **93:9–11 — the imperative.** Three things you must now do, in exactly the same order: do not crush the orphan, do not turn away the one who asks, speak about your Lord's favour.

That mirroring is the surah. **What was done for you is the syllabus for what you do for others** — and Ibn Kathir spells it out three times, phrase by phrase, so the reader cannot miss it.

There is also something quiet in the oath itself. The night is sworn by *alongside* the morning, not against it. The absence of light is part of the same created order as its presence — which is precisely the point being made to a man sitting in the dark.

## 1. The Occasion of Revelation

Three narrations, which Ibn Kathir stacks because they converge:

- **Jundub bin 'Abdullah Al-Bajali Al-'Alaqi** (Imam Ahmad; also Al-Bukhari, Muslim, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Jarir): the Prophet ﷺ fell ill and did not stand for the night prayer for a night or two. A woman came and said she thought his devil had finally abandoned him. The first three ayat were revealed.
- **Jundub again** (via Al-Aswad bin Qays): Jibril was slow in coming, and the idolaters said Muhammad's Lord had abandoned him.
- **Ibn 'Abbas** (via Al-'Awfi): Jibril was delayed for a number of days, the Messenger ﷺ was affected by it, and the idolaters said his Lord had abandoned him *and hated him* — which is why the ayah answers both charges, not one.

Worth noting: the taunt came from a woman who assumed his inspiration was a possessing spirit, and from idolaters who assumed a silent God is an angry God. Both readings of the silence were wrong.

## 2. The Oath: Light and Darkness Are Both His (93:1–2)

> *By the forenoon. By the night when it darkens.*

Allah swears by the forenoon **and by the light He placed in it**.

*Saja* — "when it darkens" — is explained by **Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak and Ibn Zayd** as the night when it **settles, grows still, darkens and overcomes**. Ibn Kathir calls this a clear proof of the power of the One who created both the light and the darkness, and cites two parallels:

> *By the night as it envelops. By the Day as it appears.* (92:1–2)

> *(He is the) Cleaver of the daybreak. He has appointed the night for resting, and the sun and the moon for reckoning...* (6:96)

---

## 3. The Denial: Neither Forsaken Nor Hated (93:3)

Two words, each answering one half of the accusation:

- **Wadda'aka** — *He has not abandoned you.*
- **Qala** — *He does not hate you.*

---

## 4. The Hereafter Is the Better End of the Deal (93:4)

> *And indeed the Hereafter is better for you than the present.*

Ibn Kathir: the abode of the Hereafter is better for you than this current abode — and then he does something characteristic. Rather than leaving it as doctrine, he shows the Prophet ﷺ **living as though it were true**, which he says is known by necessity from his biography:

- He was the most abstinent of people concerning worldly things, and the greatest of them in disregarding them.
- Offered the choice at the end of his life between staying in this world forever and then entering Paradise, or moving on to the company of Allah — **he chose what was with Allah**.
- **Ibn Mas'ud** (Imam Ahmad; also At-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah via Al-Mas'udi — At-Tirmidhi: *Hasan Sahih*): finding the Prophet ﷺ rubbing his side where a straw mat had left marks, he offered to spread something soft over it. The reply:

> *I have nothing to do with this world. The parable of me and this world is like a rider who rests in the shade of a tree, then he passes on and leaves it.*

---

## 5. The Promise: Until You Are Pleased (93:5)

> *And verily, your Lord will give you so that you shall be well-pleased.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as concerning **his followers**: in the final abode, Allah will keep giving him until he is content regarding his Ummah, alongside what has been prepared for him from His generosity. Among it is **Al-Kawthar**, with domes of hollowed pearl on its banks and mud of the strongest-scented musk.

**Ibn 'Abbas** (via Al-Awza'i, recorded by Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim): the Messenger ﷺ was shown what his Ummah would be given after him, treasure upon treasure, and he was pleased — and this ayah was revealed. He would be given a million palaces in Paradise, each containing whatever he wished of family and servants.

Ibn Kathir notes the chain is authentic to Ibn 'Abbas, and adds a methodological point worth keeping: a statement of this kind **can only be said from *Tawqif*** — that is, it must have come from the Prophet ﷺ, since no one could arrive at such detail by reasoning.

---

## 6. The Three Debts (93:6–8)

> *Did He not find you an orphan and gave you a refuge? And He found you unaware and guided you. And He found you poor and made you rich.*

**The orphan (93:6)** — Ibn Kathir gives the biography as evidence:

- His father died while his mother was still pregnant with him.
- His mother, **Aminah bint Wahb**, died when he was six.
- His grandfather **Abdul-Muttalib** took guardianship, and died when he was eight.
- His uncle **Abu Talib** then took responsibility — protecting him, assisting him, raising his standing, honouring him, and holding his own people back from harming him — including after the Prophethood came at forty, even though Abu Talib himself remained on the religion of his people, worshipping idols. Ibn Kathir passes over this without softening it: *all of this took place by the divine decree of Allah, and His decree is most excellent.*
- When Abu Talib died shortly before the Hijrah and the Quraysh turned on him, Allah chose migration for him — to **Al-Aws and Al-Khazraj**, who sheltered him, supported him, defended him and fought alongside him.

The refuge, in other words, was never one arrangement. It was a chain of them, each replaced before the last gave out.

**The unaware (93:7)** — paired with:

> *And thus We have sent to you a Ruh from Our command. You knew not what is the Book, nor what is Faith. But We have made it a light wherewith We guide whosoever of Our servants We will.* (42:52)

**The poor (93:8)** — you were poor with dependents, and Allah made you independent of everyone but Him. Ibn Kathir's observation here is the finest in the surah: Allah thereby **combined in him the two stations** — *the one who is poor and patient*, and *the one who is wealthy and thankful*. Most people only ever qualify for one.

Two hadith on what wealth actually is:

- **Abu Hurayrah** (Two Sahihs): *Wealth is not determined by abundance of possessions, but wealth is the richness of the soul.*
- **'Abdullah bin 'Amr** (Sahih Muslim): whoever accepts Islam, is provided with what he needs, and is made content by Allah with what he has been given — he has succeeded.

## 7. The Three Repayments (93:9–11)

Ibn Kathir opens each of these with *"just as you were..."* — the debts of §6 called in, in order.

**93:9 — *Therefore, treat not the orphan with oppression.***
*Just as you were an orphan and Allah sheltered you.* Do not humiliate him, scorn him or treat him with contempt; be gentle and kind. **Qatadah**: be to the orphan like a merciful father.

**93:10 — *And repulse not the one who asks.***
*Just as you were astray and Allah guided you* — so do not rebuff **the one who asks for knowledge, seeking guidance**. (Note that Ibn Kathir's primary reading of *as-sa'il* here is the seeker of knowledge, matching it to 93:7 rather than to 93:8.) Two supplements:
- **Ibn Ishaq**: do not be oppressive, arrogant, wicked or harsh toward the weak among Allah's servants.
- **Qatadah**: answer the poor with mercy and gentleness.

**93:11 — *And proclaim the grace of your Lord.***
*Just as you were poor and needy and Allah enriched you* — so speak about His favour upon you. Two hadith:
- **Abu Hurayrah** (Abu Dawud; At-Tirmidhi: *Sahih*): *Whoever is not thankful to the people, then he is not thankful to Allah.*
- **Jabir** (Abu Dawud alone): *Whoever overcomes some test and mentions it, then he is indeed thankful. And whoever conceals it, then indeed he was ungrateful.*

The last is a corrective to a common instinct. Speaking about a hardship you were carried through is not boasting — **staying silent about it is the ingratitude**.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The silence was not abandonment.** Revelation paused, and the only two interpretations on offer — a devil that had left, a Lord who had tired of him — were both wrong. The surah does not explain the pause. It simply produces the record: *look at what I have already done with you.*

**Your own biography is the argument.** Orphan → sheltered. Lost → guided. Poor → enriched. Ibn Kathir will not let those three stay in the past tense; each one is converted, within five ayat, into an obligation toward someone currently standing where you once stood. **Mercy received is mercy owed, in kind.**

**And the two stations were combined in him** — poor and patient, then wealthy and thankful — which is Ibn Kathir's way of saying that neither condition is the test. The response is.
