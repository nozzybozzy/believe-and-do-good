*Revealed in Makkah · 54 ayat · named from 41:3, "a Book whereof the Ayat are **explained in detail**" · also called Ha Mim As-Sajdah, for the prostration at 41:37*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Fussilat is a surah about **hearing**. It opens with a Book described as clear, detailed, Arabic, unmistakable — and immediately reports that most people turn away and *do not hear*. The Quraysh even say so out loud: our hearts are wrapped, our ears are deaf, there is a screen between us and you, so go your way and we will go ours.

Everything after that is a response to that refusal, and the response is not another argument. It is a series of **witnesses**. The earth and the heavens, which answered *"We come willingly."* The wind that took 'Ad and the Sa'iqah that took Thamud. The believer's own ears, eyes and skin, which will testify against him on a Day when his mouth is sealed. The sun and moon, which do not want your prostration. The angels, who never tire. The horizons and the self, in which signs will keep appearing until the truth becomes plain.

And in the middle of all this the surah drops the two shortest instructions in it — **stand firm** (41:30) and **repel evil with what is better** (41:34) — which is what a person actually does while waiting for stubbornness to run out.

---

## 1. The Book They Refused to Hear (41:1–5)

*Ha Mim. A revelation from the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.*

Ibn Kathir reads **fussilat ayatuhu** as: its meanings are clear and its rulings are sound and wise. **Qur'anan 'Arabiyyan** — its meanings precise and detailed, its words clear and not confusing. He puts 11:1 beside it (perfected, then explained in detail), calling the Book miraculous *in its wording and in its meanings* both.

It gives glad tidings to believers and warning to disbelievers — and *most of them turn away*, meaning, most of the Quraysh understood nothing of it at all despite how clear it was.

Their answer (41:5) is worth reading as a self-portrait of a closed mind:

- **Hearts under coverings** — wrapped and screened
- **Deafness in our ears** — "we are deaf to the message you bring"
- **A screen between us and you** — "nothing of what you say reaches us"
- **"So work; we are working"** — go your way, we will go ours, and we will not follow you

## 2. Say: I Am Only a Human Being (41:6–8)

The reply is deliberately small. Not a claim of divinity — the opposite of one. *I am a man like you; what I have is that it is revealed to me that your God is One God.* Then three imperatives: **go straight to Him** (worship Him alone, sincerely, as He commanded through His Messengers), **seek His forgiveness** for what is past, and — for those who won't — *woe*, meaning doom and destruction.

### The Zakah problem

41:7 condemns *those who give not the Zakah*. Ibn Kathir preserves a genuine disagreement here:

- **Ibn Abbas and Ikrimah**: it means those who do not bear witness that there is no god but Allah. On this reading Zakah is **purification of the soul** — ridding oneself of bad qualities, the worst of which is Shirk (cf. 91:9–10, 87:14–15, 79:18). Wealth-Zakah is called *zakah* precisely because it purifies wealth and increases it.
- **Qatadah**, and the apparent meaning most preferred by the scholars of tafsir and by **Ibn Jarir**: they withheld the Zakah of their wealth.

Ibn Kathir then raises the objection against the majority view himself: obligatory Zakah was instituted in **year 2 after the Hijrah**, yet this ayah is **Makkan**. His reconciliation: the *principle* of charity was enjoined from the beginning of the mission — *pay the due thereof on the day of harvest* (6:141) — while the Nusub and calculation were specified later in Al-Madinah. His parallel is prayer: prescribed early before sunrise and sunset, with the five daily prayers only fixed on the Night of Isra', a year and a half before the Hijrah.

The believers' reward is *ghayru mamnun* — **Mujahid**: never cut off, never decreased (18:3, 11:108).

## 3. Six Days, Counted Out (41:9–12)

This is the passage where the Qur'an's "six days" (7:54) are itemised, and Ibn Kathir gives the days by name:

| Stage | Days |
|---|---|
| The earth created | Sunday and Monday |
| Mountains fixed, the earth blessed, its sustenance measured | Tuesday and Wednesday |
| The heavens completed as seven, each given its affair | Thursday and Friday |

*Blessed it* means He gave it the potential to be planted and to bring forth produce. *Measured its sustenance* — **Ikrimah and Mujahid**: He placed in every land what is suited to no other land. *Sawa'an lis-sa'ilin* — **Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, As-Suddi**: for whoever wants to ask about it; **Ibn Zayd**: whatever a person in need of provision requires, Allah measures out for him (cf. 14:34).

Then the heaven, which was **smoke** — steam that arose when the earth was created. And the command to both: *Come, willingly or unwillingly.* They said: **"We come willingly"** — meaning, everything You wish to create in us, angels and jinn and men, will be obedient to You.

The lowest heaven was adorned with lamps — the stars — serving two purposes at once: **adornment**, and **guard** against the Shayatin listening to the angels on high.

### The man who was confused by the Qur'an

Ibn Kathir inserts here the long report recorded by **Al-Bukhari**, from **Sa'id bin Jubayr**: a man came to Ibn Abbas saying some things in the Qur'an confused him — verses that seemed to contradict.

1. *No kinship among them that Day, nor will they ask of one another* (23:101) vs. *they will turn to one another and question one another* (37:27).
   **Ibn Abbas**: the first is at the first blowing of the Trumpet, when all swoon away (39:68); the second is after the second blowing.
2. *They will never hide a single fact from Allah* (4:42) vs. *By Allah, our Lord, we were not those who joined others* (6:23).
   **Ibn Abbas**: when Allah forgives the sincere believers, the idolaters will say *let us claim we never associated* — then their mouths will be sealed and their hands will speak, and it will be known that nothing can be hidden.
3. The order of creation: 79:27–30 puts the heaven before the spreading of the earth; 41:9–11 puts the earth first.
   **Ibn Abbas**: the earth was created in two days, then the heavens, then He gave the heaven its shape in two more; the *spreading* of the earth (79:30) — bringing forth its water, pasture, mountains, sands, rocks and hills — came after.
4. *Kana Allahu Ghafuran Rahima* — "was" — as if He no longer is.
   **Ibn Abbas**: this is how He described Himself, and this is how He still is. *Whatever Allah wills comes to pass, so do not be confused about the Qur'an, for all of it comes from Allah.*

## 4. 'Ad and Thamud: Two Ways of Not Listening (41:13–18)

The warning is a **Sa'iqah** like theirs. Messengers came *from before them and behind them* — Ibn Kathir explains via 46:21: in every neighbouring town and city, calling to Allah alone, bringing good news and warning. They were not short of evidence.

- **'Ad** were arrogant, stubborn and disobedient, and boasted: *Who is mightier than us in strength?* — thinking physical power would shield them from Allah. The reply is the whole answer to arrogance: *Allah Who created them was mightier in strength than them* (cf. 51:47).
- Their punishment fits the boast exactly. The **Sarsar** wind: some said strongly blowing, some said intensely cold, some said the wind that makes a noise. **Ibn Kathir: it was all of these** — a strong wind, apt for people deceived by their own strength; bitterly cold (69:6); and roaring (there is a famous river in the east called Sarsar for the noise it makes). *Days of calamity* means **consecutive** days — seven nights and eight days in succession (69:7) — the evil omen beginning on one day and continuing until every last one of them was destroyed.
- **Thamud** had the opposite problem: *We showed them the path.* **Ibn Abbas, Abu Al-'Aliyah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd**: "We explained to them." **Ath-Thawri**: "We called them." They had clarity through Salih, and *preferred blindness to guidance* — then slaughtered the she-camel that was their sign.

And the believers were saved out from among them, *because of their fear of Allah*.

## 5. The Day the Body Testifies (41:19–24)

Driven to the Fire and gathered at its edge, their **hearing, eyes and skins** testify — Ibn Kathir: to all their deeds, earlier and later, *not a single letter concealed*.

They turn on their own limbs: *Why do you testify against us?* The limbs answer: **Allah has caused us to speak, as He causes all things to speak** — He cannot be opposed or resisted.

Two reports carry this section:

**Anas bin Malik** (Al-Bazzar, also Ibn Abi Hatim, Muslim, An-Nasa'i): the Messenger of Allah ﷺ laughed one day and said, *Will you not ask why I laughed?* Then:

> I was amazed at how a servant will dispute with his Lord on the Day of Resurrection. He will say, "My Lord, did You not promise me that you would not treat me unjustly?" Allah will say, "Yes." The man will say, "I will not accept any witness against me except from myself." Allah will say, "Is it not sufficient that I and the angels, the noble scribes, are witnesses?" ... Then a seal will be placed over his mouth and his limbs will speak about what he used to do. Then he will say, **"Away with you! It was only for your sake that I was arguing!"**

**Abu Musa** (Ibn Abi Hatim): the disbeliever or hypocrite denies his record — *by Your glory, this angel has written something I did not do* — and when he does that, his mouth is sealed. **Al-Ash'ari** added: "I think the first part of his body to speak will be his right thigh."

The reason for revelation of 41:22 is **Ibn Mas'ud's** (Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi, Muslim, Al-Bukhari): hiding under the covering of the Ka'bah, he overheard three men — a Qurashi and his two Thaqafi brothers-in-law, or the reverse — men with fat bellies and little understanding. One asked whether Allah could hear them. One said: if we raise our voices, yes; if not, no. The third said: **if He can hear one thing from us, He can hear everything.** The ayah came down.

*And that thought of yours about your Lord has brought you to destruction* — the ruinous belief being precisely that Allah did not know much of what they were doing. And there is no exit either way: **whether they bear it patiently or not, they are in the Fire**, and if they plead, nothing will be accepted (cf. 23:106–108, where Ibn Jarir reads *seek to please* as *ask to return to this world*).

## 6. Companions, and the Noise Strategy (41:25–29)

**Qurana'** — intimate companions appointed from the devils of men and jinn — *made fair-seeming to them what was before them and what was behind them*: their past deeds beautified, their future seen only as good (43:36–37: *We appoint for him a Shaytan as a companion... and they think they are guided aright*).

The disbelievers' own tactic is quoted directly: *Listen not to this Qur'an, and make noise in the midst of it, that you may overcome.* **Mujahid**: whistling and shouting to confuse the Messenger ﷺ while he recited. Ibn Kathir notes that Allah commanded the exact opposite of this instinct: *when the Qur'an is recited, listen to it and be silent* (7:204).

At the end they will ask to see *those who led us astray, that we may crush them under our feet* — to be beneath them in the torment, punished more severely. **Ali** identified the two: **Iblis, and the son of Adam who killed his brother**. As-Suddi's version: Iblis is followed by everyone who commits Shirk, and the son of Adam by everyone who commits a major sin. The hadith is the general principle:

> No soul is wrongfully killed but a share of the burden is upon the first son of Adam, because he was the first one who set the precedent of killing.

But no one escapes their own portion: *For each one there is double torment, but you know not* (7:38).

## 7. "Our Lord Is Allah" — Then Stand Firm (41:30–32)

The most quoted ayat of the surah, and Ibn Kathir builds their meaning out of a single word: **istaqamu**.

- **Abu Bakr As-Siddiq**, asked what he made of it: *"Those are the ones who do not associate anything with Allah."* When others suggested it meant "they shun sin," he replied, *"You have not interpreted it improperly"* — then gave his own: they say Our Lord is Allah, **then they do not turn to any other god besides Him**. Also the view of Mujahid, Ikrimah, As-Suddi.
- **Sufyan bin Abdullah Ath-Thaqafi** asked the Prophet ﷺ for something he could hold on to. He said: *"Say, my Lord is Allah, then stand firm."* (An-Nasa'i's wording: *"Say: I believe in Allah, then stand firm."*) Sufyan then asked what the Prophet ﷺ feared most for him — and he **took hold of the edge of his own tongue** and said, *"This."*

To those people the angels descend. **Mujahid, As-Suddi, Zayd bin Aslam**: at the time of death.

- ***Fear not*** — Mujahid, Ikrimah, Zayd bin Aslam: do not fear what lies ahead in the Hereafter.
- ***Nor grieve*** — for what you leave behind: children, family, wealth, debt. *We will take care of it for you.*
- ***Receive the glad tidings of Paradise.*** As in Al-Bara's hadith: *Come out, O good soul, from the good body in which you used to dwell; come out to rest, and provision, and a Lord Who is not angry.*

**Zayd bin Aslam** offers the reading Ibn Kathir calls good and true because it reconciles all the opinions: they are given glad tidings **at death, in the grave, and at the resurrection**.

And then the line that redefines the whole relationship: ***We have been your friends (awliya') in this world and in the Hereafter*** — protecting and helping you by Allah's command in this life; keeping you from loneliness in the grave, reassuring you at the blowing of the Trumpet, taking you across the Sirat, and bringing you to the Gardens. Paradise itself: *whatever you ask for, it will appear before you as you wish it to be* — **nuzulan**, a welcoming gift from the One who forgave your sins and concealed your faults.

## 8. The Best Speech, and How to Answer an Enemy (41:33–36)

*Who is better in speech than one who invites to Allah, does righteous deeds, and says: "I am one of the Muslims"?*

Ibn Kathir stresses the three-part structure: **he follows what he says himself**. Not among those who enjoin good without doing it, or forbid evil while committing it. He does good, avoids evil, and calls others to their Creator.

The surah's own scholars debated who it refers to — the Messenger ﷺ foremost (Muhammad bin Sirin, As-Suddi, Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam); or the righteous **Mu'adhdhin** (*The Mu'adhdhins will be the ones with the longest necks on the Day of Resurrection*; *the Imam is a guarantor and the Mu'adhdhin is in a position of trust*). Ibn Kathir's verdict is that it is **general**, and he gives a clean historical reason: when this ayah was revealed in Makkah, **the Adhan had not been prescribed at all** — it came in Al-Madinah, shown in a dream to Abdullah bin Abd Rabbihi Al-Ansari and taught to Bilal for his more beautiful voice.

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri** recited it and said: *This is the beloved of Allah, the close friend of Allah, the chosen of Allah, the most beloved of all the people of earth to Allah. He responded to the call of Allah and called mankind to that to which he had responded... This is Allah's Khalifah.*

### Repel with what is better

*The good deed and the evil deed cannot be equal.* **Umar** gives the operative sentence:

> There is no better punishment for one who has disobeyed Allah with regard to you, than your obeying Allah with regard to him.

Treat well the one who treats you badly, and *he between whom and you there was enmity becomes as though he were a close friend*. But Ibn Kathir doesn't pretend it is easy — *none is granted it except those who are patient*, because it is difficult for people to do; and *none is granted it except the owner of a great portion* of happiness in this world and the next. **Ibn Abbas**: Allah commands patience when angry, forbearance when confronted with ignorance, forgiveness when mistreated — and if they do this, He saves them from the Shaytan and subdues their enemies until they become like close friends.

Then the crucial distinction (41:36): **the devils among men can be won over by kindness — the devils among the jinn cannot.** Against their whispering there is only one move: seek refuge with the Creator who gave them power over you. The Prophet ﷺ said before prayer: *I seek refuge in Allah the All-Hearing, All-Knowing, from the accursed Shaytan and his evil insinuations, breath and impurity.* Ibn Kathir notes this pattern occurs only twice elsewhere: 7:199–200 and 23:96–98.

## 9. Do Not Prostrate to the Sun (41:37–39)

Night and day alternate without ceasing; the sun and moon have allotted stages and separate orbits, so that man can measure days, weeks, months, years, and the timings of rights, worship and transactions. Precisely **because they are the most beautiful visible bodies**, Allah points out that they are created things in a state of enthrallment to Him.

*Do not prostrate to the sun nor to the moon, but to Allah Who created them.* And if you are too proud — the sentence turns without pause — **there are those with your Lord who glorify Him night and day, and never tire** (cf. 6:89: if these disbelieve, We have entrusted it to a people who are not disbelievers).

Then the argument from the dead earth: barren, lifeless, nothing growing — until water comes and it stirs, and brings forth every kind of crop and fruit. **He who gives it life is able to give life to the dead.**

## 10. The Book That Cannot Be Corrupted (41:40–45)

**Yulhiduna fi ayatina** — **Ibn Abbas**: *Al-Ilhad* means putting words in their improper places. **Qatadah**: disbelief and obstinate behaviour. *They are not hidden from Us* is a stern threat. And *Do what you will* (Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, 'Ata' Al-Khurasani) **is a threat, not a permission** — do what you like of good or evil, for He sees it all.

Of the Qur'an itself: *an honourable, well-fortified Book* — protected and preserved, and nobody can produce anything like it. **Falsehood cannot come to it from before it or behind it**: there is no way to corrupt it, because it was sent down by the Lord of the worlds, *All-Wise in all He says and does, Praiseworthy in all He commands and forbids*.

To the Prophet ﷺ personally: *Nothing is said to you except what was said to the Messengers before you.* **Qatadah, As-Suddi**: the same rejection, the same insults — *just as they bore their people's insults with patience, so too you must*. And the Lord who holds forgiveness for those who turn back holds painful punishment for those who persist.

On the objection that a foreign Qur'an would have been more convincing: it would not have been. Their disbelief is **the disbelief of stubbornness** — had it come in another tongue they'd have said *why is it not explained in Arabic — a foreign book for an Arab?* (cf. 26:198–199). The Qur'an's effect depends on the receiver: *for those who believe, a guide and a cure* — a cure for the doubt and confusion in people's hearts; for the rest, deafness and blindness (17:82).

**They are called from a place far away.** **Mujahid**: far from their hearts. **Ibn Jarir**: as if the one addressing them is calling from a distance and they cannot make out what he says (cf. 2:171).

Musa too was given the Scripture and it was disputed — the consolation is the same, and the delay in punishment is only a Word that went forth beforehand: they have an appointed time beyond which they will find no escape.

## 11. Man in Ease and in Difficulty (41:46–51)

*Whoever does righteous good deeds, it is for himself; whoever does evil, it is against himself* — and Allah is not unjust to His servants: **He punishes no one except after establishing proof and sending a Messenger.**

Knowledge of the Hour is His alone. Ibn Kathir's proof is the sharpest possible: Muhammad ﷺ, the leader of mankind, said to Jibril, one of the leading angels, when asked about it — ***the one who is asked about it does not know more than the one who is asking*** (cf. 79:44, 7:187). Under the same knowledge: no fruit leaves its sheath, no female conceives or gives birth, without Him knowing (6:59, 13:8, 35:11).

Then the portrait of man that closes the surah, and it is uncomfortably recognisable:

- He **never tires** of asking for good — wealth, health — but at the first touch of hardship he *gives up all hope and is lost in despair*, thinking he will never experience anything good again.
- When relief comes after difficulty, he says ***"This is due to my merit"*** — I deserve this. Careless, arrogant, ungrateful (96:6–7: *man transgresses because he considers himself self-sufficient*).
- He adds: *and I don't think the Hour will come — but if I am returned to my Lord, surely the best will be with Him.* Ibn Kathir: he expects Allah to do him favours **in spite of** his bad deeds and his lack of certain faith.
- When favoured he turns away and is too proud to obey; when harmed, *he has recourse to long supplications* (10:12).

And a definition worth keeping: **long supplications are long on words and short on meaning — the opposite of concise speech, which is brief but full of meaning.**

## 12. Signs in the Horizons and in Yourselves (41:52–54)

> *We will show them Our signs in the horizons (al-afaq) and within themselves, until it becomes manifest to them that this is the truth.*

Ibn Kathir gives two readings and keeps both:

- **In the horizons** — conquests, and the advance of Islam over region after region and over all other religions. **Mujahid, Al-Hasan, As-Suddi** on *within themselves*: Badr, the conquest of Makkah, and the events that befell them, where falsehood suffered humiliating defeat.
- **Within themselves** may also mean **man's own physical composition** — "as detailed in the science of anatomy, which indicates the wisdom of the Creator" — along with the opposite natures and inclinations people have, and the decrees a man is subject to and can neither change nor control.

*Is it not sufficient that your Lord is a Witness over all things?* — sufficient is Allah as Witness over the words and deeds of His servants, and as Witness that Muhammad ﷺ is telling the truth (4:166).

The surah ends where it began, on the failure to take something seriously: they are **in doubt about meeting their Lord** — they do not think about it, strive for it or heed it; they regard it as irrelevant and do not care. *But it will undoubtedly come to pass.* And the last word is **muhit**: He surrounds all things — all creation subject to His control, dominion and knowledge; whatever He wills happens, whatever He does not will does not.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The Book is clear; the obstruction is in the listener.** Fussilat never argues that the message is hard to understand. It says the opposite — detailed, Arabic, unmistakable — and then documents people building screens, stuffing ears, and shouting over the recitation. Clarity is not the problem, and more clarity is not the answer.

**Everything answers Him willingly except man.** The earth and the smoking heaven said *"We come willingly."* The angels glorify night and day without tiring. Even a man's own skin will speak the truth on the Day his tongue is sealed. The one holdout is the person who thought Allah did not hear him whispering at the Ka'bah.

**The whole religion fits in six words.** *"Say: my Lord is Allah, then stand firm."* Abu Bakr's gloss — do not turn to any other god besides Him — and the Prophet's ﷺ warning about what most endangers you, delivered by taking hold of his own tongue.

**Answer badness with goodness; answer whispering with refuge.** Two different enemies, two different weapons. Human hostility can be dissolved by kindness until an enemy becomes a close friend — but only the patient manage it. The Shaytan's whisper cannot be dissolved at all; there, the only move is *a'udhu billah*.

**Do not be a person who says "this is due to my merit."** The surah's final diagnosis of man is that he despairs too fast in hardship and takes credit too fast in ease. Both are the same error: forgetting who is actually measuring out the sustenance, in every land, for whoever asks.
