---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Mumtahinah is the Qur'an's most precise surah on **loyalty** — and its precision is the point. It opens and closes with the same prohibition (do not take the enemies of Allah as intimate allies), but everything in between exists to stop that prohibition from being applied too widely.

The surah was occasioned by a genuinely hard case: a sincere Companion, a veteran of Badr, who leaked military intelligence to Makkah out of anxiety for the family he had left there. His motive was not disbelief. It was fear for his children. The surah does not excuse the act — but the Prophet ﷺ accepts his explanation, and Allah's response is not to condemn Hatib but to legislate for everyone.

From there the surah works through the whole spectrum of relationships across a religious divide:

- **Combatants** who expelled you — no alliance (60:1, 60:9, 60:13)
- **Non-combatants** who did not fight you or drive you out — kindness and equity are *not forbidden* (60:8)
- **Idolatrous mothers** bearing gifts — receive them, keep the tie (Asma' bint Abi Bakr)
- **Enemies today** — who may be brothers tomorrow, because Allah turns hearts (60:7)
- **Marriage** — the one bond that *is* severed by the divide (60:10)

The model held up throughout is **Ibrahim**, who disowned his people entirely — with one carefully marked exception that Ibn Kathir dwells on at length, because the Companions were getting it wrong.

---

## 1. Hatib's Letter (60:1–3)

> *O you who believe! Take not My enemies and your enemies as protecting friends, showing affection towards them...*

The story behind the opening is collected in both Sahihs.

**Hatib bin Abi Balta'ah** was among the earliest emigrants and had fought at Badr. He had children and wealth in Makkah but no blood tie to Quraysh — he was an ally of Uthman, not a kinsman. When the Prophet ﷺ mobilised for the conquest of Makkah after Quraysh broke the treaty, he prayed: *O Allah, keep our news concealed from them.*

Hatib then wrote to the Makkans warning them, and sent the letter with a woman of Quraysh — hoping they would owe him a debt and spare his family.

Allah informed His Messenger, **because He had accepted the Prophet's invocation.**

`Ali narrates the rest: he, Az-Zubayr and Al-Miqdad were sent galloping to Rawdat Khakh. They found the woman. She denied carrying anything. They said: *take out the letter, or we take off your clothes.* She produced it from her braid.

Confronted, Hatib's defence was exactly the anxiety the surah is about:

> *I was not of Quraysh, only an ally of theirs. Every emigrant with you has kinsmen in Makkah who protect their families. I wanted to do them a favour so they might protect my relatives. I did not do this out of disbelief, nor to renegade from my religion.*

The Prophet ﷺ said: **"He has told you the truth."**

Umar asked to strike off the head of "this hypocrite." The Prophet ﷺ answered with the sentence that ends the matter:

> *He attended Badr. What can I tell you — perhaps Allah looked at those who attended Badr and said: "O people of Badr, do what you like, for I have forgiven you."*

## 2. The Prohibition Itself

Ibn Kathir defines the category narrowly. The ayah refers to **the idolaters and disbelievers who are combatants against Allah, His Messenger and the believers** — those whom Allah has decided should be our enemies and should be fought. From these, alliance, support and companionship are forbidden.

He stacks the parallel verses:

| Verse | Force |
|---|---|
| 5:51 | *...and if any among you takes them as such, then surely he is one of them* — "a stern warning and a sure threat" |
| 5:57 | Do not befriend those who take your religion as mockery and fun |
| 4:144 | *Do you wish to offer Allah a manifest proof against yourselves?* |
| 3:28 | Whoever does so *will never be helped by Allah in any way* — except where you genuinely fear danger from them |

And the reason is given in the ayah itself: *they have driven out the Messenger and yourselves — because you believe in Allah, your Lord.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is bare and devastating: **"your only fault is that you believed in Allah, Lord of all that exists."** He pairs it with 85:8 (*they had no fault except that they believed*) and 22:40 (*expelled from their homes unjustly only because they said: Our Lord is Allah*).

Then the counter-argument to Hatib's calculation:

- *Should they gain the upper hand over you, they would behave to you as enemies* — "they would use every type of harm at their disposal, in words and action."
- *And they desire that you should disbelieve* — "they are eager that you do not earn any good. Their enmity is outward **and** inward. So how can you become supporters of this type of people?"

## 3. Neither Your Relatives Nor Your Children (60:3)

> *Neither your relatives nor your children will benefit you on the Day of Resurrection.*

This is the direct answer to Hatib's motive. Family ties will not shield you from Allah if Allah decides harm your way — **and they will not benefit you at all if you pleased them with what angers Allah.**

Those who go along with their family's disbelief in order to please them "will have earned loss and failure, and their deeds will be rendered invalid." Their relation will not benefit them with Allah **even if that relation was with a Prophet.**

Ibn Kathir proves the point with the hadith of Anas: a man asked, *O Messenger of Allah, where is my father?* — *In the Fire.* When the man turned away, the Prophet ﷺ called him back:

> *Verily, my father and your father are in the Fire.*

---

## 4. The Excellent Example of Ibrahim (60:4–6)

> *Indeed there has been an excellent example for you in Ibrahim and those with him, when they said to their people: "Verily, we are free from you..."*

Ibrahim and his followers declare three things at once:

1. **Disownment of the people** — *we disown you*
2. **Disownment of the religion** — *we disbelieve in your religion and way*
3. **Permanent, declared hostility** — *animosity has appeared between us and you from now, and as long as you remain on your disbelief*

But the enmity has an expiry condition written into it: ***until you believe in Allah alone***. It is not tribal, not ethnic, not personal. It ends the moment they abandon the idols.

### The one part you may *not* copy

The ayah carves out an exception — *except the saying of Ibrahim to his father: "I will ask forgiveness for you."*

Ibn Kathir explains why the exception exists. Some believers had been praying for their parents who died as disbelievers, **citing Ibrahim as their precedent.** Allah answered in 9:113–114: the Prophet and the believers may not seek forgiveness for idolaters, even close kin, once it is clear they are dwellers of the Fire — and Ibrahim's request for his father was only **the fulfilment of a promise he had made him**. When it became clear his father was an enemy of Allah, *he dissociated himself from him.*

So: follow Ibrahim in the disownment. **Do not follow him in the prayer.** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Muqatil bin Hayyan, Ad-Dahhak and others.)

### Their du'a

Having cut themselves off from everyone, they turn and say:

> *Our Lord! In You we put our trust, to You we turn in repentance, and to You is the final Return.*

Then: ***Our Lord! Make us not a trial (fitnah) for the disbelievers.*** Ibn Kathir gives three readings of what this fitnah means:

- **Mujahid** — do not punish us at their hands or from Yourself, or they will say: *had these people been on the truth, this would not have struck them.*
- **Qatadah** — do not give them victory over us, for then they would conclude they were given victory **because they are on the truth.** (Ibn Jarir's preferred reading.)
- **Ibn Abbas** — do not give them dominance over us, lest we suffer trials at their hands.

The concern is not merely suffering. It is that their suffering would become **an argument for falsehood**.

And *forgive us, our Lord* — Ibn Kathir reads it as: **cover our mistakes from being exposed to any but You, and forgive what is between us and You.**

## 5. Perhaps Allah Will Make Friendship (60:7)

> *Perhaps Allah will make friendship between you and those whom you hold as enemies. And Allah has power over all things, and Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

Immediately after the hardest verses in the surah comes this. Ibn Kathir reads it as **"affection after animosity, tenderness after coldness, and coming together after parting."**

Allah is able to gather opposites and bring hearts together — and the proof is the audience itself. He cites 3:103 on the Ansar: *you were enemies one to another, but He joined your hearts together, so that by His grace you became brethren* — two tribes who had been at war for generations, standing on the brink of a pit of Fire, and saved from it. And 8:63: *if you had spent all that is in the earth, you could not have united their hearts, but Allah has united them.*

The Prophet ﷺ put it to them directly:

> *Did I not find you misguided, and Allah guided you through me; and divided, and Allah united your hearts through me?*

From this Ibn Kathir draws the surah's most quotable practical counsel:

> *Love your loved one moderately, because one day he might become your enemy.*
> *Hate your hated one moderately, because one day he might become your loved one.*

And *Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful* — He forgives the disbelief of the disbelievers themselves if they repent and surrender to Him, **no matter what type of sin it is.**

## 6. The Line That Is Actually Drawn (60:8–9)

This is the heart of the surah's precision, and the section most often forgotten.

> *Allah does not forbid you to deal justly and kindly with those who fought not against you on account of religion, nor drove you out of your homes. Verily, Allah loves those who deal with equity.*

Ibn Kathir: this means **those who had no role in your expulsion.** Allah does not forbid kindness (*birr*) to disbelievers who do not fight you over religion — "such as women and weak disbelievers." Two words are being permitted: *tabarruhum* (be gentle with them) and *tuqsitu ilayhim* (be fair with them).

**The case of Asma' bint Abi Bakr** is the illustration, and it appears twice:

- Asma': *My mother, an idolatress at the time, came to me during the treaty with Quraysh. I asked the Prophet ﷺ, should I treat her with good relations?* — **"Yes. Keep good relation with your mother."**
- Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr's fuller version: Qutaylah came bearing gifts — dibab, cheese, clarified butter. **Asma' refused the gifts and would not let her enter the house.** A'ishah asked the Prophet ﷺ; this ayah came down; and the Messenger ﷺ ordered Asma' to accept the gifts and let her mother into her home.

The scrupulous daughter was the one who was corrected.

Ibn Kathir attaches the hadith on equity: *the just, who are fair in their decisions, their families and those under their authority, will be on podiums of light, at the right of the Throne.*

### And the other side (60:9)

> *It is only as regards those who fought against you on account of religion, and have driven you out of your homes, and helped to drive you out, that Allah forbids you to befriend them.*

Three conditions, all about **action**: they fought you, they expelled you, they assisted in expelling you. From these, friendship is forbidden and enmity is commanded — and *whoever befriends them, such are the wrongdoers.*

## 7. The Emigrant Women (60:10–11)

The treaty of Al-Hudaybiyyah had contained a clause: *every person who reverts from our side to your side is to be returned to us, even if he follows your religion.*

Then Muslim women began to arrive.

Allah's ruling: **test them.** When you are satisfied they are believers, do not send them back — *they are not lawful for the disbelievers, nor are the disbelievers lawful for them.*

- **What the test was** — Al-`Awfi from Ibn Abbas: they were asked to testify to *La ilaha illallah* and that Muhammad is Allah's servant and Messenger.
- **What it was looking for** — Mujahid: ask why she migrated. If she came out of anger with her husband, or any other motive, and you see she has not embraced the faith, send her back.
- Ibn Kathir's note: this ayah shows **faith can be recognised and affirmed** — it is not wholly hidden.

Whether this **specifies** the Sunnah of the treaty or **abrogates** it is disputed; Ibn Kathir prefers specification, calling it "the best case of understanding." The clause was abolished with respect to women particularly — reported in the biography of Abdullah bin Abi Ahmad bin Jahsh, whose sister-in-faith **Umm Kulthum bint Uqbah** emigrated and whose brothers came demanding her return.

### The marriage bond is the one that breaks

The ayah forbids Muslim women to idolaters — **a marriage that had been lawful in the earliest period of Islam.**

The case Ibn Kathir gives is **Zaynab, the Prophet's own daughter**, married to Abu Al-`As bin Ar-Rabi`, an idolater. Captured at Badr, his ransom sent by Zaynab was a necklace that had belonged to Khadijah. The Prophet ﷺ became very emotional at the sight of it and said to the Companions: *if you decide to set free the prisoner who belongs to her, then do so.* They did — on condition Abu Al-`As send Zaynab to Madinah. He kept his word, and sent her with Zayd bin Harithah. She remained in Madinah from the second year of Hijrah until **her husband embraced Islam in the eighth year — and she returned to their marriage without a renewed dowry.**

### The financial settlement

The surah then handles money with unusual evenhandedness:

- Return to the idolater husbands **the dowries they had paid** to the women who emigrated
- Muslims may marry these women, **giving them their due** (after `iddah, with a guardian)
- Do not keep disbelieving wives — Umar divorced two idolatress wives on this ayah
- Muslims whose wives defected to the idolaters are to be **compensated the equivalent** of what they spent

Az-Zuhri records the outcome: **the believers paid what they owed; the idolaters refused to accept Allah's judgement on what they owed.** So the compensation was to be taken from the funds already held.

*That is the judgement of Allah, He judges between you* — Ibn Kathir: this is a decision Allah made for His creatures, and He knows what benefits His servants.

## 8. The Women's Pledge (60:12)

> *O Prophet! When the believing women come to you pledging that they will not associate anything with Allah, and that they will not steal, nor commit Zina, nor kill their children, nor utter slander they have fabricated, nor disobey you in what is right — then accept their pledge and ask Allah's forgiveness for them.*

A'ishah reports that the Prophet ﷺ examined migrating women by this very ayah. And she adds the detail Al-Bukhari preserves:

> *When any believing woman agreed to these conditions, he would say: "I have accepted your pledge" — but by Allah, he never touched the hand of any woman at all while taking the pledge. He took it only by speech.*

Umaymah bint Ruqayqah's account gives his own words. After taking the pledge he said, ***"As much as you can bear to implement."*** They replied: *surely Allah and His Messenger are more merciful with us than we are with ourselves.* When they asked whether he would shake hands:

> *I do not shake hands with women. My statement to one woman is as sufficient as my statement to a hundred women.*

### The clauses, explained

- **Not steal** — the property of others. Ibn Kathir immediately notes the exception: a wife whose husband fails to maintain her may take what is reasonable, with or without his knowledge. Hind bint `Utbah: *Abu Sufyan is a miser and does not give me enough for the household — may I take from his money without his knowledge?* — **"Take what is reasonable and appropriate for you and your children."**
- **Not commit Zina** — paired with 17:32, *come not near to Zina; it is a Fahishah and an evil way.*
- **Not kill their children** — this covers infanticide, which the people of Jahiliyyah practised out of fear of poverty, **and the killing of the fetus**, "just as some ignorant women do for various evil reasons."
- **Not utter slander fabricated between their hands and feet** — Ibn Abbas and Muqatil: attributing to their husbands children who are not theirs.
- **Not disobey you in Ma`ruf** — Maymun bin Mihran's remark is the sharpest: *Allah did not order obedience to His Prophet in other than Ma`ruf — and Ma`ruf is itself obedience.*

In practice, the women understood *Ma`ruf* to include **not wailing over the dead**. Umm Atiyah's account is oddly human: one woman withdrew her hand mid-pledge because a family had once wailed for her dead relative and she felt she owed them the same; she went, repaid the debt, came back, and gave her pledge. Another narration adds refusals to scratch faces, tear hair, or rend clothes in grief.

Ubadah bin As-Samit records the same conditions taken from the men, with the closing formula:

> *Whoever fulfils it, his reward is with Allah. Whoever deviates and receives the legal punishment in this life, that punishment is expiation for the sin. Whoever deviates and Allah screens him — it is up to Allah to punish or forgive, if He wills.*

And then the ayah ends where the surah's severity finally softens completely: *accept their pledge, and ask Allah's forgiveness for them; verily Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

## 9. The Closing Prohibition (60:13)

> *O you who believe! Take not as friends the people who incurred the wrath of Allah. Surely, they have despaired of the Hereafter, just as the disbelievers have despaired of those buried in graves.*

The surah closes exactly as it opened. The argument here is not about danger but about **futility**: how can you ally with people who have already given up on the only thing that lasts?

The final image admits two readings, and Ibn Kathir gives both:

1. The disbelievers **despair of ever meeting their dead again**, since they do not believe in Resurrection — so on their own creed, there is no reunion to hope for.
2. Or: just as **those already buried** have lost all hope of any good, having now seen the punishment and learned that Resurrection was true.

Ibn Mas`ud (via Al-A`mash ← Abu Ad-Duha ← Masruq) takes the second: *just as the disbeliever despairs when he dies and comes to know his recompense.* Mujahid, `Ikrimah, Muqatil, Ibn Zayd, Al-Kalbi and Mansur agree; Ibn Jarir preferred it.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Loyalty is measured by conduct, not by census.** The surah forbids alliance with those who fought you and drove you out — and in the same breath forbids you from extending that ruling to anyone else. The mother bringing cheese and butter to her daughter's door is not the enemy in 60:1.

**Hatib's mistake was not disbelief — it was managing his own security.** He tried to buy insurance for his family with the Prophet's military secret. The answer given him is the whole surah in one line: *neither your relatives nor your children will benefit you on the Day of Resurrection.* And yet he was forgiven, because Badr was behind him and his account was honest.

**Follow Ibrahim in the break, not in the prayer.** Disown falsehood completely and permanently — *until you believe in Allah alone*. But the one thing he did that you may not copy is the plea for a father who died an enemy of Allah.

**The enmity has an expiry date built in.** *Perhaps Allah will make friendship between you and those whom you hold as enemies* — and He has done it before, to two tribes who had been killing each other for generations. So love moderately, hate moderately, and leave the hearts to the One who turns them.

**And the standard the Prophet ﷺ set for every pledge he took: "as much as you can bear to implement."**
