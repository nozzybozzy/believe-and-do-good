*Revealed in Makkah · 88 ayat · "The Stories" — from 28:25, where Musa arrives in Madyan and **narrates his story** to a stranger who tells him he is safe*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Qasas tells the life of Musa more completely than anywhere else in the Qur'an — but it is not a biography. It is a Makkan surah addressed to a Prophet ﷺ and a small community who are **weak, hunted, and being told that following the truth will cost them their place in the world**. So Allah tells them the story of a baby floated down a river past the palace of the man hunting him, raised in that palace at that man's expense, and returned to his own mother by the very system built to destroy him.

The surah is structured around a single claim, stated in its opening lines before any of the narrative begins: **Allah intends to favour the weak, make them rulers, and make them the inheritors** (28:5–6). Everything after is the demonstration.

It then closes with two figures who represent the two ways of losing: **Fir'awn**, who was destroyed by power, and **Qarun**, who was destroyed by wealth — one from Musa's enemies, one from Musa's own family. And the last word of the surah is the line the Prophet ﷺ called the truest a poet ever spoke: *everything except Allah is false*.

---

## 1. The Promise Made Before the Story Begins (28:1–6)

> *Verily, Fir'awn exalted himself in the land and made its people Shiya'a, weakening a group among them: killing their sons, and letting their females live.*

Ibn Kathir explains **Shiya'a** as classes and factions — Fir'awn divided his people into groups, each used for whatever purpose he chose. The group he ground down was the Children of Israel, who at that time were **the best of people**, forced into the most degrading labour, night and day.

The killing of the boys was a precaution: he feared a boy would arise among them who would end his kingdom. Ibn Kathir's comment is the theme of the whole surah in one sentence:

> *This precaution did not protect him against the divine decree, because when the term of Allah comes, it cannot be delayed.*

Then, before a single event is narrated, Allah states the outcome (28:5–6): the weak will be made rulers and inheritors, and **Fir'awn, Haman and their armies will receive from them the very thing they feared**. It had been written and decreed from past eternity that Fir'awn would meet his doom at the hands of Musa. His entire security apparatus was, unknowingly, the machinery for delivering it.

---

## 2. The Basket on the Nile (28:7–13)

**The system.** Ibn Kathir preserves the mechanics of the persecution. The Copts complained that if all Israelite boys were killed, the labour force would collapse and *they* would have to do the work — so Fir'awn ordered the boys killed in alternate years. **Harun was born in a year of reprieve; Musa was born in a killing year.** Midwives circulated, registering pregnancies; at the birth only Coptic women could attend; a girl was left, a boy was killed on the spot.

Musa's mother showed no signs of pregnancy and was never registered. When he was born, she was terrified — and Ibn Kathir adds a detail that recurs through his life: *no one ever saw Musa but they loved him* — as Allah says, **"And I endued you with love from Me"** (20:39).

**The inspiration.** Her house was on the Nile. She made a box into a cradle and, when someone frightening came, would put him in it and set it in the water with a rope tied to it. One day she put him in, but **forgot the rope**. The water carried him past the palace.

**The palace.** The servants dared not open the box without their mistress. When Asiyah opened it and saw the child, Allah filled her heart with love for him — *because she was blessed, and because Allah wanted to honour her and cause her husband's doom*. She said: **"A comfort of the eye for me and for you."** Fir'awn replied: *"For you he may be, but not for me."* Ibn Kathir: and so it was exactly — Allah guided her because of him and destroyed Fir'awn at his hands. She said *perhaps he may benefit us* — and he did: Allah guided her through him and gave her Paradise because of him.

**The empty heart.** *And the heart of the mother of Musa became empty* — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah and others: she could not hold a single thought in this world except Musa. She nearly gave herself away, and would have, *had We not strengthened her heart*.

**The return.** Allah **forbade** every wet nurse to him — a divine prohibition, so that no one could nurse him but his own mother. His sister, watching "from a far place" (Ibn Abbas: off to the side; Qatadah: *she looked at him as though she were not really interested*), stepped forward with the offer of a household. They grew suspicious and seized her — *how do you know they will be sincere?* — and she answered that they would want the king's favour and a reward. So they released her, and took her to the house, and the baby took his mother's breast.

Asiyah rewarded her generously and never knew. Offered a place in the palace, the mother declined — *I have a husband and children* — and nursed him at home, on a salary, paid by Fir'awn's household.

Ibn Kathir's note on the timing is one of the most quietly devastating lines in the tafsir:

> *There was only a short time between the distress and the way out — a day and a night, or thereabouts.*

And when he was returned to her, she knew he would be a Messenger, **so she raised him both as a child, with kindness, and as a Messenger, with respect**.

*But most of them know not* — they do not know the wisdom of Allah in His actions and their good consequences. As Allah says: **"It may be that you dislike a thing which is good for you"** (2:216).

## 3. The Killing, and the Flight (28:14–21)

At maturity Allah gave him *Hukm and knowledge* — Mujahid: prophethood.

He entered the city *when its people were unaware* — Ibn Abbas is reported both ways: between Maghrib and 'Isha, or at midday. He found an Israelite fighting a Copt, was called for help, and struck the Copt with his fist — Mujahid: *he punched him* — and the man died.

Musa's response is immediate and total: **"This is of Shaytan's doing... My Lord! Verily, I have wronged myself, so forgive me." Then He forgave him.** And then a vow that reads as the moral of the incident: **"For that with which You have favoured me, I will nevermore be a helper of the criminals."**

The next day the same Israelite is fighting another Copt and calls again. Musa tells him: *you are a plain misleader*. When Musa moves to seize the Copt, the Israelite — *out of his own cowardice* — assumes Musa is coming for him and blurts out the secret: **"Is it your intention to kill me as you killed a man yesterday?"** Only two people on earth knew. The Copt heard it, carried it to Fir'awn's gate, and the order for Musa's death was given.

Then a man came running *from the farthest end of the city*. Ibn Kathir notes he is called a *man* because he had the courage to take a different and shorter route than the men sent to arrest Musa, and so arrived first: **"The chiefs are taking counsel about you, to kill you, so escape."**

Musa left Egypt alone — *and he was not used to being alone, because before that he had lived a life of luxury and ease, in a position of leadership*. He prayed: **"My Lord! Save me from the people who are wrongdoers."** It is mentioned that Allah sent him an angel on horseback to show him the way.

---

## 4. Madyan: A Kindness with No Expectation (28:22–28)

Turning toward Madyan he said only: **"It may be that my Lord guides me to the right way."** And Allah did — guiding him in this world and the next, and making him a guide for others.

At the well he found shepherds watering and two women holding their flocks back. *We cannot water until the shepherds finish; and our father is a very old man.* He felt sorry for them, watered their flocks, went to the shade of a tree, and made a du'a of pure, undefined need:

> **"My Lord! Truly, I am in need of whatever good that You bestow on me."**

He asked for nothing specific. Within the hour he had a home, employment, protection, and a wife.

**Manners.** One of the women returns, *walking shyly*. Umar (with a Sahih chain, via 'Amr bin Maymun): *"She came walking shyly, putting her garment over her face. She was not one of those audacious women who come and go as they please."* And her message is phrased with care — she does not invite him herself, but says her **father** invites him, *so that he would have no suspicious thoughts about her*.

**Discernment.** She then says: **"O my father! Hire him! Verily, the best of men for you to hire is the strong, the trustworthy."** Her father asked how she knew. She said: *he lifted a rock ten men could not lift; and when I walked ahead of him he told me to walk behind him and throw a pebble if he took a wrong turn.* Strength and trustworthiness, each proven by one action.

Ibn Mas'ud's famous list follows: **the three people of the greatest discernment** were Abu Bakr, when he judged Umar; the Egyptian who said of Yusuf *"make his stay comfortable"*; and this woman.

**The contract.** Eight years of service for marriage, with two more as a voluntary favour — *"I do not want to put pressure on you."* Musa: **"whichever of the two terms I fulfil, there will be no injustice to me, and Allah is Surety over what we say."**

Which term did he serve? Al-Bukhari records that a Jew of Hirah asked Sa'id bin Jubayr, who went to Ibn Abbas — *the scholar of the Arabs* — and was told: **"He fulfilled the longer and better of them, for when a Messenger of Allah says he will do a thing, he does it."**

And the Prophet ﷺ, narrated by 'Utbah bin An-Nadar: **Musa hired himself out for the purpose of keeping chaste and feeding himself.**

---

## 5. The Fire on the Mountain (28:29–35)

Ten years later, missing home, Musa set out secretly with his family and flocks on a cold, dark, rainy night. They lost the way; he could not get a fire to light. Then he saw a fire on At-Tur — *perhaps I may bring you some information, or a firebrand that you may warm yourselves*. He went looking for directions and warmth, and was called by the Lord of all that exists.

He was called from **the right side of the valley** — meaning he was facing the Qiblah with the western mountain to his right — and the fire was burning in a **green bush**, and he stood amazed.

> **"O Musa! Verily, I am Allah, the Lord of all that exists!"**

**The staff.** Ibn Kathir's description of the snake is vivid: it moved with terrifying speed despite its size, jaws snapping, swallowing every rock it passed — *and every rock that fell into its mouth fell with a sound like a rock falling into a valley*. Musa fled and did not look back, *because it is human nature to flee from such a thing*. Then: **"Draw near, and fear not. Verily, you are of those who are secure."**

**The hand, and a remedy for fear.** The hand emerges white *as if it were a piece of the moon or a flash of lightning*, with no trace of disease. And the second instruction — *draw your hand close to your side to be free from the fear* — Mujahid and Qatadah gloss as freedom from terror. Ibn Kathir then offers something rare, a practical suggestion:

> *Perhaps if a person does this, following the example of Musa, and puts his hand over his heart, his fear will disappear or be lessened, if Allah wills.*

**The request for Harun.** Musa fears being killed, and says his brother is more eloquent. Ibn Kathir explains the speech impediment: as a child, given a choice between a date and a pearl, he mistakenly picked up a **coal** and put it on his tongue. Musa's reasoning is that *the word of two carries more weight in people's minds than the word of one*. Allah answers: **"We will strengthen your arm through your brother... you two, as well as those who follow you, will be the victors."**

One of the Salaf: **no one has ever done a greater favour to his brother than Musa did for Harun — he interceded for him until Allah made him a Prophet and Messenger alongside him.**

---

## 6. Fir'awn's Claim to Godhood, and His End (28:36–42)

The response to the clear signs is stubbornness, not confusion: *This is nothing but invented magic. Never did we hear of this among our fathers of old.* Ibn Kathir is precise — **they saw with their own eyes that it was from Allah**, and rejected it out of arrogance and sin, not doubt.

Fir'awn then announces: **"O chiefs! I know not that you have a god other than me,"** and orders Haman to bake bricks and build him a **Sarh** — a tower — *that I may look at the God of Musa*. Ibn Kathir: it was the tallest structure ever built on earth, and its purpose was theatre — to demonstrate to his people that Musa was lying.

An important clarification: **the dispute was not over whether Allah had sent Musa. Fir'awn did not acknowledge a Creator at all.** He had already told Musa: *If you choose a god other than me, I will put you among the prisoners* (26:29).

*They thought that they would never return to Us* — this is the root: arrogance grows in the soil of denying resurrection.

The end comes in one clause: **We seized him and his armies, and We threw them all into the sea** — Ibn Kathir: drowned in a single morning, and not one of them was left. And then a punishment beyond drowning — *We made them leaders inviting to the Fire*, and *We made a curse to follow them in this world*: cursed by the Prophets and their followers then, and by every believer since.

---

## 7. "And You Were Not There" (28:43–51)

Here the surah turns from Musa to Muhammad ﷺ, and the entire narrative becomes an argument.

> *And you were not on the western side when We made clear to Musa the commandment... And you were not a dweller among the people of Madyan... And you were not at the side of At-Tur when We called.*

Ibn Kathir: Allah is pointing to the proof of his Prophethood — **he told people about the distant past as if he had heard and seen it himself, while being an unlettered man among a people who knew nothing of such matters.** The same argument is made about Maryam (3:44) and about Nuh (11:49).

*But you are sent as a mercy from your Lord* — and Allah gives the reason: so that no one can plead *"Why did You not send us a Messenger?"* when the punishment comes. The same logic as 4:165 and 5:19.

**The double standard exposed.** They demand *"Why is he not given the like of what was given to Musa?"* — the staff, the hand, the flood, the locusts, the lice, the frogs, the blood, the parting of the sea, the manna and quails. Allah's reply is one question: **"Did they not disbelieve in that which was given to Musa of old?"** The signs were given, in overwhelming abundance, and rejected anyway. The demand for miracles was never sincere.

Mujahid: the Jews told Quraysh to say **"two kinds of magic, each helping the other"** — meaning Musa and Harun. Ibn Abbas: it refers to the Tawrah and the Qur'an. Hence the challenge: **"Then bring a Book from Allah which is a better guide than these two, and I will follow it."**

And if they do not answer? **"Then know that they only follow their own lusts. And who is more astray than one who follows his own lusts, without guidance from Allah?"** The refusal was never about evidence.

## 8. The Reward Given Twice (28:52–55)

Of the scholars among the People of the Book who believed, Sa'id bin Jubayr says these ayat were revealed about **seventy priests sent by An-Najashi of Ethiopia**. The Prophet ﷺ recited Ya Sin to them until he finished the surah; **they wept, and they became Muslim.**

*These will be given their reward twice over, because they are patient* — patient, Ibn Kathir notes, *in adhering to the truth, for taking such a thing upon oneself is not easy for people*.

The hadith (Sahih, from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari) names **three who receive a double reward**:
- a man of the People of the Book who believed in his Prophet and then believed in Muhammad ﷺ;
- a slave who fulfils his duty to Allah and to his master;
- a man who educates a slave woman, teaches her good manners, frees her, and marries her.

And the Prophet ﷺ on the day of the Conquest: *whoever of the two Books becomes Muslim has his reward twice, and the same rights and duties as we do.*

Their character is then described in four strokes — **they are patient; they repel evil with good** (they do not respond in kind, they forgive and overlook); **they spend** on family, relatives, Zakah and charity; and **when they hear vain talk they withdraw from it**, saying *"To us our deeds, and to you your deeds. Peace be to you."* Ibn Kathir: when a foolish person addresses them foolishly, they turn away and **never say anything but good words**.

## 9. You Cannot Guide the One You Love (28:56–59)

> **"Verily, you guide not whom you like, but Allah guides whom He wills."**

Recorded in both Sahihs: this was revealed about **Abu Talib**. He protected the Prophet ﷺ, supported him and stood by him, and loved him dearly — *but it was a natural love, born of kinship, not a love born of the fact that he was the Messenger of Allah*.

At his deathbed the Prophet ﷺ said: *O my uncle, say La ilaha illallah, a word which I may use as evidence in your favour before Allah.* Abu Jahl and Abdullah bin Abi Umayyah kept interjecting: *will you leave the religion of Abdul-Muttalib?* At the very end he said he was on the religion of Abdul-Muttalib, and refused. The Prophet ﷺ said, *By Allah, I shall certainly seek forgiveness for you unless I am told not to* — and then both 9:113 and this ayah were revealed.

Ibn Kathir's summary of the principle: **all you have to do is convey the Message.** Guidance is not in the Messenger's hands, however much he loves the person.

**The economic excuse.** Quraysh said: *"If we follow the guidance with you, we would be snatched away from our land"* — we fear the Arab tribes around us will make war on us. Allah's refutation is that they are living in a **secure sanctuary** to which fruits of every kind are brought from At-Ta'if and beyond: *how could this sanctuary be safe for them while they were upon Shirk, and not safe once they follow the truth?*

And a warning that follows directly: *how many a town have We destroyed which was thankless for its means of livelihood* — but never **until He sends to their mother town a Messenger**. Ibn Kathir reads this as proof that the Unlettered Prophet was sent from the Mother of Cities to all cities, Arab and non-Arab alike: **"I have been sent to the red and the black."**

## 10. Two Men, Two Concerns (28:60–75)

> *Whatever you have been given is an enjoyment of the life of the world and its adornment, and that which is with Allah is better and will remain forever.*

The Prophet ﷺ: **the life of this world compared to the Hereafter is as if one of you dipped his finger in the sea — let him see what comes back to him.**

Then the comparison at the heart of the surah's second half: *Is he whom We have promised an excellent promise — which he will find true — like him whom We have made to enjoy the luxuries of the life of the world, then on the Day of Resurrection he will be among those brought up?* It was said to have been revealed about the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Jahl; Ibn Kathir judges the meaning **more general than that**.

**The scenes of the Day.** Three calls are described:

1. *"Where are My partners whom you used to assert?"* — the Shayatin and the leaders of disbelief will disown their followers: *We led them astray as we were astray ourselves. It was not us they worshipped.* They will be told to call on their partners, and no answer will come, and they will see the torment — **"If only they had been guided!"**
2. *"What answer gave you to the Messengers?"* — Ibn Kathir compares this to the questioning in the grave. The believer testifies; the disbeliever says only *"Oh, oh, I do not know"* — *for whoever is blind in this world will be blind in the Hereafter*.
3. *"Bring your proof"* — a witness is brought from every nation (Mujahid: a Messenger), and they will be silent, and everything they invented will vanish.

Between these scenes stands the one door left open: **"But as for him who repented, believed, and did righteous deeds, then perhaps he will be among those who are successful."** Ibn Kathir's note on the word *perhaps* ('Asa) is essential: **when used of Allah, it means the thing will inevitably come to pass.**

**Night and day as proof.** Two questions no idol can answer: if the night were made permanent, which god could bring you light? If the day were made permanent, which god could bring you rest? *It is out of His mercy that He has made for you the night and the day* — and Ibn Kathir adds a practical grace: **whoever misses an act of worship in the night can make it up in the day, and vice versa.**

---

## 11. Qarun: Wealth as a Trial (28:76–84)

Qarun was of Musa's own people — Ibn Abbas, An-Nakha'i, Qatadah and others: **his cousin**. His treasure was such that *the keys alone would burden a body of strong men*. Al-A'mash from Khaythamah: the keys were leather, each the size of a finger, each for a separate storeroom; when he rode out, **sixty mules carried the keys**.

**The advice he was given** (28:77) is one of the most complete statements of Islamic balance in the Qur'an, and Ibn Kathir unpacks it clause by clause:

| The instruction | Ibn Kathir's reading |
|---|---|
| *Seek, with what Allah has given you, the home of the Hereafter* | Use the wealth to worship and draw near, through good deeds |
| *And forget not your portion of this world* | Lawful food, drink, clothing, home, marriage — **"Your Lord has rights over you, your self has rights over you, your family has rights over you, and your visitors have rights over you. So give each of them their due."** |
| *Be generous as Allah has been generous to you* | Be generous to His creatures as He was to you |
| *And seek not mischief in the land* | Do not make corruption and harm to Allah's creation your aim |

**His answer** — *"This has been given to me only because of the knowledge I possess"* — Ibn Kathir reads two ways: either *I earned this by my own expertise*, or *Allah gave it because He knows I deserve it and loves me*. Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam: **"Were it not that Allah is pleased with me and knows my virtue, He would not have given me this."** And Ibn Kathir's diagnosis of the whole attitude: *this is what those of little knowledge say when they see a wealthy man — that if he did not deserve it, Allah would not have given it to him.*

**The procession, and the two audiences.** He went out in full regalia — fine clothes, fine horses, servants and retinue. Those *desirous of the life of the world* said: *would that we had the like of what Qarun has been given.* Those *given knowledge* said: **"Woe to you! The reward of Allah is better for those who believe and do righteous deeds — and this none shall attain except the patient."** Ibn Jarir: the patience meant is **patiently forsaking the love of this world while seeking the Hereafter.**

**The end.** *So We caused the earth to swallow him and his dwelling place.* Ibn Kathir attaches the hadiths of arrogance in dress: the man dragging his lower garment who *will remain sinking down into the earth until the Day of Resurrection*, and the man in two green garments walking proudly whom Allah commanded the earth to swallow.

*Then he had no group to help him against Allah* — wealth, retinue, servants: none of it stood between him and the decree, and he could not save himself either.

**The lesson the onlookers drew** is the theological correction of their own envy: **"Know you not that it is Allah Who expands the provision or restricts it to whomsoever He pleases?"** Ibn Kathir: **wealth does not indicate that Allah is pleased with its owner.** The hadith of Ibn Mas'ud makes the distinction exactly:

> *Allah has allotted character among you just as He has allotted your provision. Allah gives wealth to those whom He loves and those whom He does not love, but **He gives Faith only to those whom He loves**.*

And their second realisation: *had it not been that Allah was Gracious to us, He could have caused the earth to swallow us up* — **because we wanted to be like him.** The wish itself was the danger.

**Who inherits the Hereafter** (28:83): *those who do not want to exalt themselves in the land nor cause corruption.* Ikrimah: haughtiness and arrogance. Ibn Jurayj: arrogance and tyranny. Ali's remarkable standard: *if a man wants the straps of his sandals to be better than the straps of his companion's sandals, he is one of those referred to in this ayah* — understood, Ibn Kathir says, of the intention to **show off and appear better than others**. The balance is set by the hadith: a man asked whether liking a good garment and good shoes was arrogance, and the Prophet ﷺ said, **"No — for Allah is beautiful and loves beauty."**

Then justice and generosity, side by side: *whoever brings good shall have better than it* — multiplied many times over — *and whoever brings evil will only be requited for what they did.*

## 12. The Closing Command (28:85–88)

> *Verily, He Who has given you the Qur'an will surely bring you back to the return.*

Ibn Kathir gives the range of interpretation of **"the return"** without forcing one: Al-Bukhari records Ibn Abbas saying it means **to Makkah** — a promise of return made to a man in the middle of being driven out of it. Mujahid: *back to your place of birth*. Ibn Abbas is also reported as reading it of **death**, of **the Day of Resurrection**, and of **Paradise**. All of them came true.

Then four instructions:
- **Never be a supporter of the disbelievers** — separate from them, oppose them.
- **Let them not turn you away from the Ayat of Allah** — do not let their opposition affect you or discourage you, *for Allah will make your word supreme and His religion prevail*.
- **Invite to your Lord.**
- **Invoke no other god along with Allah.**

And the final ayah, which Ibn Kathir treats as the summation of everything the surah has shown about Fir'awn's throne and Qarun's keys:

> **"Everything will perish save His Face. His is the decision, and to Him you shall be returned."**

The Prophet ﷺ, via Abu Hurayrah in the Sahih: **"The truest word a poet ever spoke was the saying of Labid — indeed, everything except Allah is false."**

## 13. If You Remember Nothing Else

**Allah's plan runs through the enemy's plan, not around it.** Fir'awn's surveillance, his midwives, his killing years, his palace, his treasury — every one of them became an instrument in raising the child who would end him. The precautions *were* the delivery mechanism.

**The distance between distress and relief is often nothing at all.** Musa's mother lost her son to a river and had him back, nursing at her breast, on a salary, under royal protection, in roughly a day and a night. *He is the One Who grants those who fear Him a way out from every worry.*

**Ask without specifying.** *"My Lord! Truly, I am in need of whatever good that You bestow on me."* A stranger, hunted, homeless, sitting under a tree, asked for nothing in particular — and received a home, work, safety and a wife.

**Two ways to be destroyed: by power and by wealth.** Fir'awn said *I know of no god for you other than me*; Qarun said *this was given to me because of my own knowledge*. Both are the same sentence. And Qarun was not an outsider — he was Musa's cousin, from the community of the believers.

**Wealth is not a verdict.** Allah gives it to those He loves and those He does not. **Faith He gives only to those He loves.**

**Guidance is not yours to give — even to the person you love most.** The Prophet ﷺ could not save Abu Talib. Convey, and leave the outcome with Allah.

**And the last word:** everything except Allah is false. Every throne in this surah sank — one into the sea, one into the earth.
