*Revealed in Makkah · 29 ayat · "The Folding Up" — named for the rolling of the sun in its first verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ibn Kathir opens the surah with a hadith that functions as its thesis. Imam Ahmad recorded from Ibn Umar that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Whoever wishes to look at the Day of Judgement as if he is seeing it with his own eyes, then let him read: **When the sun is wound round** (81), **When the heaven is cleft asunder** (82), and **When the heaven is split asunder** (84)."*

At-Takwir is therefore not an argument about the Day of Judgement. It is a **camera pointed at it**. At-Tirmidhi recorded the hadith as well.

The surah divides cleanly in two:

- **81:1–14 — twelve conditions and one consequence.** Twelve clauses beginning *And when… And when…* pile up without a main verb, holding the sentence unresolved, until the fourteenth ayah lands: ***Every person will know what he has brought.*** Ibn Kathir calls this "the conclusive response of the previous statements" — the whole cosmic demolition exists grammatically to deliver that one line about you.
- **81:15–29 — four oaths and the chain of custody.** Having shown the Day, the surah turns to face the obvious question: *who says?* It answers by tracing the report back through its carriers — a trustworthy angel, a sane companion, an eyewitness sighting — and ends by locating the will to accept it somewhere other than in the reader's own hands.

## 1. The Twelve Conditions (81:1–13)

| Ayah | The condition | Ibn Kathir's reading |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The sun is **Kuwwirat** | Ibn Abbas: darkened. Al-Awfi from Ibn Abbas: it will go away. Qatadah: its light will go. Sa'id bin Jubayr: it will sink in. Abu Salih: thrown down. |
| **2** | The stars **Inkadarat** | Scattered — as in *when the stars have fallen and scattered* (82:2). The root *Inkidar* is *Insibab*: to be poured out. |
| **3** | The mountains made to pass away | They will not remain in their places; they will be destroyed, and the earth left a flat, level plain. |
| **4** | The pregnant she-camels (**`Ishar**) neglected (**`Uttilat**) | Their owners will abandon them. |
| **5** | The wild beasts gathered | Mixed together — *there is not a moving creature on earth, nor a bird that flies with its two wings, but are communities like you… then unto their Lord they shall be gathered* (6:38). Ibn Abbas: **"Everything will be gathered, even the flies."** |
| **6** | The seas become blazing fire | See below. |
| **7** | The souls joined with their mates | Every type gathered with its peer. |
| **8–9** | The buried infant girl questioned | See §3. |
| **10** | The pages laid open | Ad-Dahhak: every person given his paper in his right hand or his left. |
| **11** | The heaven **Kushitat** | Mujahid: drawn away. As-Suddi: stripped off. |
| **12** | Hell **Su`irat** | As-Suddi: it is heated. |
| **13** | Paradise brought near | Ad-Dahhak, Abu Malik, Qatadah and Ar-Rabi` bin Khuthaym: brought near **to its inhabitants**. |

### What *Takwir* actually means

This is the surah's title word, and Ibn Kathir defines it with two domestic images: *At-Takwir* is to gather one part of something into another part — **the folding of a turban**, and **the folding of clothes**. So the sun is not merely extinguished; part of it is folded into another part, then rolled up, then thrown away. Its light going out is a *by-product* of being folded and discarded.

Al-Bukhari recorded from Abu Hurayrah that the Prophet ﷺ said: ***The sun and the moon will be rolled up on the Day of Judgement.*** (Al-Bukhari was alone in recording it.)

### The `Ishar: the most precise detail in the surah

Ibn Kathir spends more words on 81:4 than on the destruction of the mountains, and the reason is worth sitting with.

Ikrimah and Mujahid: `*Ishar* are pregnant camels. Mujahid: *`Uttilat* means abandoned and left. Ar-Rabi` bin Khuthaym: **"They will not be milked or tied up."** Ad-Dahhak: **"They will be left with no one to tend to them."**

But the point is *which* camels. The `Ishar is **the best type of camel there is** — specifically the pregnant females in the **tenth month** of pregnancy, each called an *`Ushara'* until she gives birth. This is the single most valuable thing an Arab could own, at the single most valuable moment of its life. And the ayah says nobody will look at her.

> *"So the people will be too busy to tend to her, take care of her or benefit from her, after she used to be the most important thing to them."*

Not that wealth is destroyed — that everyone simply **stops caring**, mid-stride, because of what has appeared in front of them.

### Ubayy bin Ka`b's sequence

Recorded by Ibn Jarir, this narration reads like a chronology of the first six clauses:

1. People will be in their **marketplaces** when the sun's light goes.
2. While they are in that state, the stars scatter.
3. While they are in that state, the mountains fall onto the face of the earth, and the earth moves, quakes, and falls into confusion.
4. **The Jinn flee in fright to the humans, and the humans flee to the Jinn.**
5. Domestic beasts, birds and wild animals mix and surge together in a wave.
6. The Jinn say, *"We come to you with news"* — so they all go to the sea, and find it a blazing fire.

Then the earth splits with one huge crack running from the lowest, seventh earth to the highest, seventh heaven — and a wind comes and kills all of them.

### The seas

Ibn Jarir recorded from Sa`id bin Al-Musayyib that `Ali asked a Jewish man, *"Where is Hell?"* The man said, **"The sea."** `Ali replied, **"I think he is truthful"** — and cited *And by the seas kindled (Masjur)* (52:6).

### The souls joined with their mates

Every type is gathered with its like — *Assemble those who did wrong, together with their companions* (37:22). Ibn Abi Hatim recorded from An-Nu`man bin Bashir that the Prophet ﷺ explained the ayah simply: **those who are alike**. Every man will be placed with the group who performed the deeds he performed. The sorting mechanism is 56:7–10: those on the Right Hand, those on the Left Hand, and the foremost.

## 2. The Pages, and Who Wrote Them

Qatadah's comment on 81:10 is the surah's quietest and sharpest line:

> *"O Son of Adam! It is written in, then it is rolled up, then it will be distributed to you on the Day of Judgement. So let each man look at what he himself **dictated** to be written in his paper."*

Note the verb. The book is not composed about you. It is **taken down from you.**

---

## 3. The Buried Infant Girl (81:8–9)

> *And when the female infant buried alive (Al-Maw'udah) is questioned: for what sin was she killed?*

**Al-Maw'udah** is the infant girl whom the people of the pre-Islamic *Jahiliyyah* would bury in the dirt **out of their hatred of girls**.

Ibn Kathir's reading of the rhetoric here is devastating in its restraint. The murderer is not questioned. **The victim is.** She is asked what crime she committed that warranted her death — and the point of asking her is to terrify him:

> *"For verily, if the one who was wronged is questioned, what does the wrongdoer think then?"*

**A second recitation, a second meaning.** The majority read *Su'ilat* — "she is asked." But Ibn Abbas (via Ali bin Abi Talhah) read it as **"she will ask"**, and Abu Ad-Duha explained: *she will demand restitution for her blood.* As-Suddi and Qatadah reported the same. On this reading, the murdered girl is not a witness called by the court — she is the **plaintiff**.

### The hadith of the hidden burying

Imam Ahmad recorded from A'ishah ← Judamah bint Wahb, the sister of Ukkashah. The Prophet ﷺ was with some people and said he had nearly prohibited intercourse with breastfeeding women, until he saw that the Romans and Persians do so without harming their children. Then he was asked about *`azl* — coital interruption — and answered:

> ***"That is the minor infanticide, and it is the female infant buried alive (Maw'udah) that will be questioned."***

Recorded also by Muslim, Ibn Majah, Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i.

### The atonement of Qays bin `Asim

Reported from `Umar bin Al-Khattab (via An-Nu`man bin Bashir and Simak bin Harb): Qays bin `Asim came to the Prophet ﷺ and said, *"O Messenger of Allah, I buried some daughters of mine alive in the period of ignorance."*

The Prophet ﷺ said: ***"Free a slave for each one of them."***

Qays said he was an owner of camels, not slaves. The Prophet ﷺ said: ***"Then sacrifice a camel for each one of them."***

The door does not close. A man who has done the worst thing in the surah is given a road back, calibrated to what he actually owns.

## 4. The Consequence (81:14)

> *Every person will know what he has brought.*

Ibn Kathir: **this is the conclusive response** of everything preceding. At the moment those things occur, every soul will know what it has done, and it will be brought forth for it. He anchors it with two ayat:

> *On the Day when every person will be confronted with all the good he has done, and all the evil he has done, he will wish that there were a great distance between him and his evil.* (3:30)

> *On that Day man will be informed of what he sent forward, and what he left behind.* (75:13)

Twelve cosmic catastrophes, and the verb at the end of them is **know**.

## 5. The Four Oaths (81:15–18)

Muslim and An-Nasa'i recorded from `Amr bin Hurayth that he prayed the Morning prayer behind the Prophet ﷺ and heard him reciting precisely these verses.

**Al-Khunnas, Al-Jawar Al-Kunnas.** Ibn Jarir recorded from Khalid bin Ar`arah that `Ali was asked about this and said: **"These are the stars that withdraw (disappear) during the day and sweep across the sky (appear) at night."** Things that hide and then run — an oath by reliable return.

**By the night when it `As`as.** Ibn Kathir gives this unusual treatment because the word points both ways:

- *Advancing:* Mujahid — its darkening; Sa`id bin Jubayr — when it begins; Al-Hasan Al-Basri and `Atiyah — when it covers the people.
- *Departing:* Ibn Abbas (via both Ali bin Abi Talhah and Al-`Awfi) — **when it goes away**; likewise Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Zayd bin Aslam and his son.

Ibn Kathir states his own view in the first person: *"I believe that the intent… is when it **approaches**, even though it is correct to use this word for departing also. However, approachment is a more suitable usage here."* His reason is symmetry — the pairing of night's darkness arriving with morning's light breaking, as in *By the night as it envelops, by the day as it appears in brightness* (92:1–2), *By the forenoon, by the night when it darkens* (93:1–2), and *Cleaver of the daybreak; He has appointed night for resting* (6:96).

He then concedes the point gracefully: many scholars of language hold that *`As`as* genuinely carries both meanings at once, **"therefore it is correct that the intent could be both of them, and Allah knows best."**

**By the day when it Tanaffas.** Ad-Dahhak: when it rises. Qatadah: when it brightens and advances. (The root is *breath* — the day exhaling.)

## 6. The Chain of Custody (81:19–25)

The oaths answer a single question: where did this Qur'an come from? The surah replies by naming and vouching for every link.

**The carrier — Jibril (81:19–21).** *Verily, this is the Word of a most honourable messenger.* Ibn Abbas, Ash-Sha`bi, Maymun bin Mihran, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ar-Rabi` bin Anas and Ad-Dahhak all identify the messenger here as **Jibril**, not Muhammad ﷺ — the Qur'an is *being conveyed by* him. Four credentials follow:

- **Dhi Quwwah** — mighty in creation, mighty in strength, mighty in actions (cf. 53:5–6).
- **Makin with the Lord of the Throne** — high status and lofty rank with Allah.
- **Obeyed there** — Qatadah: *"in the heavens. He is not one of the lower ranking angels. Rather he is from the high ranking, prestigious angels. He is respected and has been chosen for the delivery of this magnificent Message."*
- **Amin, trustworthy** — and Ibn Kathir pauses at the enormity of this: the Almighty Lord has personally commended His angelic messenger, exactly as He commends His human one in the very next verse.

**The recipient — Muhammad ﷺ (81:22).** *And your companion is not a madman.* Ash-Sha`bi, Maymun bin Mihran and Abu Salih: this is Muhammad ﷺ. The word **"your companion"** does the work — the man they are calling insane is someone they have lived beside their entire lives.

**The sighting (81:23).** *And indeed he saw him in the clear horizon.* Muhammad ﷺ saw Jibril **in the true form Allah created him in, with six hundred wings**.

Ibn Kathir places this precisely: it is **the first sighting**, at Al-Batha' in Makkah, described in 53:5–10. From this he draws a dating inference — *"it seems apparent, and Allah knows best, that this Surah was revealed **before the Night Journey (Al-Isra')**, because nothing has been mentioned in it except this sighting."* The second sighting, at Sidrat al-Muntaha (53:13–16), appears only in An-Najm, revealed after Al-Isra'.

**He withholds nothing (81:24).** Two established recitations, two meanings, both accepted:

| Recitation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Zanin** (with *ẓā'*) | He is not following false conjecture about what Allah revealed — not a liar, not a wicked man |
| **Danin** (with *ḍād*) | He is not **stingy** with it — he conveys it to everyone |

Sufyan bin `Uyaynah treats them as one point. Qatadah's gloss on *Danin* is the memorable one: **"The Qur'an was unseen, and Allah revealed it to Muhammad — and he did not withhold it from the people. Rather he announced it, conveyed it, and offered it to everyone who wanted it."** Ibn Jarir preferred *Danin*; Ibn Kathir declines to choose: *"both of the recitations have been confirmed by numerous routes of transmission, and its meaning is correct either way."*

**Not from a devil (81:25).** *And it is not the word of the outcast Shaytan* — he is neither **able** to produce it nor is it **befitting** of him to do so, as in *And it is not the Shayatin who have brought it down. Neither would it suit them nor can they. Verily, they have been removed far from hearing it* (26:210–212).

## 7. Then Where Are You Going? (81:26)

Ibn Kathir reads this not as *where are you headed* but as **"where has your reason gone?"** — in rejecting a Qur'an this manifest, clear and evident.

He illustrates it with a scene from after the Prophet's ﷺ death. The delegation of **Banu Hanifah** came to Abu Bakr As-Siddiq as Muslims, and he asked them to recite something from the Qur'an. What they recited was from the so-called Qur'an of **Musaylimah the Liar** — total gibberish, terribly poor in style. Abu Bakr said:

> ***"Woe unto you! Where have your senses gone? By Allah, this speech did not come from a god."***

The test Abu Bakr applied was not scholarly. He simply **listened**. Qatadah adds the other half of the meaning: going away from the Book of Allah and from His obedience.

## 8. The Reminder, and Whose Will It Is (81:27–29)

> *Verily, this is no less than a Reminder to the creatures — to whomsoever among you who wills to walk straight.*

A reminder for all mankind. And whoever seeks guidance **must adhere to this Qur'an, for it is his salvation and his guidance — there is no guidance in anything other than it.**

Then the final correction:

> *And you cannot will unless Allah wills — the Lord of all that exists.*

Ibn Kathir: the will is **not left to you**, such that whoever wishes to be guided is guided and whoever wishes to stray strays. All of it proceeds according to the will of Allah, the Lord of all that exists.

Reported from Sulayman bin Musa: when 81:28 was revealed — *to whomsoever among you who wills to walk straight* — **Abu Jahl** seized on it and said, *"The matter is up to us. If we wish, we will stand straight; and if we do not wish, we will not stand straight."*

So Allah revealed 81:29.

The surah's last verse exists because a man tried to read the second-to-last verse as a licence.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**At-Takwir is twelve sentences that never finish, so that one sentence can.** The sun folded like a turban, the stars poured out, the mountains gone, the seas on fire, the most valuable animal on earth standing unmilked — and every one of those clauses is grammatically incomplete until *every person will know what he has brought*. The universe is dismantled in order to produce a moment of self-knowledge.

**The `Ishar and the buried girl are the same argument from opposite ends.** One is what people valued most and will abandon in an instant. The other is what they valued least and will be made to answer for forever. The Day inverts the entire scale of worth — and the surah's proof that this is a moral universe is that the smallest, most disposable victim in Arabia is the one given a voice at the tribunal.

**And it is not a threat delivered anonymously.** Half the surah is the report; half is the credentials of everyone who touched it — a trustworthy angel of high rank, a companion they know is not mad, an eyewitness sighting on a clear horizon, and a messenger who was stingy with none of it. *Then where are you going?*

**The last word is not yours.** You may will to walk straight — and even that willing is Allah's to give.
