*Revealed in Makkah · 89 ayat · named for the "gold adornments" of 43:35 — the luxury Allah says He would have handed to disbelievers en masse, precisely because it is worth nothing*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Az-Zukhruf is a surah about **the argument from status**. The Quraysh had one objection to Muhammad ﷺ that they kept returning to, and it was not really theological: *why him?* Why not a great man from Makkah or At-Ta'if — someone with wealth, followers, standing? Prophethood, in their reckoning, should have gone to whoever the market had already valued highest.

The surah dismantles that reasoning from every direction at once. It points out that **they already admit Allah made the heavens and the earth**, and then worship things they know did not. It shows them **assigning daughters to Allah** — the very children whose birth darkens their own faces. It shows them with **no argument at all except that their fathers did it**. And it answers the "why him" question with the line the whole surah turns on: ***Is it they who would portion out the mercy of your Lord?***

Then it goes further, and this is the surah's boldest move. If wealth were a sign of divine favour, Allah says, He would have given the disbelievers **silver roofs, silver staircases, silver doors, silver couches, and gold** — and He withholds that only because people are stupid enough to read it as love. Fir'awn is brought in as the case study: a man who argued for his own prophethood on the grounds that he owned the rivers of Egypt and Musa had a stammer.

It closes with Isa — worshipped without ever asking to be, *a servant on whom We bestowed favour* — and with the Prophet ﷺ told to stop arguing and simply say: **peace**.

---

## 1. The Book in the Mother of the Book (43:1–4)

> *Ha Mim. By the manifest Book.*

**Manifest** means plain and clear in both wording and meaning, because it came in the language of the Arabs — which Ibn Kathir calls the most eloquent language for communication among people. Hence *We have made it a Qur'an in Arabic that you may be able to understand*: not merely that you may read it, but that you may **ponder its meanings** (cf. 26:195).

Then the verse that establishes its rank before it establishes anything else:

> *And verily, it is in the Mother of the Book with Us, indeed exalted, full of wisdom.*

| Phrase | Ibn Kathir's gloss | Source |
|---|---|---|
| *the Mother of the Book* | Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz, the Preserved Tablet | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid |
| *with Us* | in Our presence | Qatadah |
| *exalted* | occupying a position of honour and virtue | Qatadah |
| *full of wisdom* | clear, with no confusion or deviation | — |

The point of telling the people of earth about its status among the angels is so **they will respect it, venerate it and obey it** (cf. 56:77–80, 80:11–16).

---

## 2. The Mercy of Not Withdrawing It (43:5)

> *Shall We then take away the Reminder from you, because you are a people excessive?*

The primary reading (Ibn Abbas, Abu Salih, Mujahid, As-Suddi, favoured by Ibn Jarir): *do you imagine We will overlook you and not punish you, when you do not do as you were commanded?*

But **Qatadah's** reading is the one Ibn Kathir calls *very good* and dwells on:

> *"By Allah, if this Qur'an had been taken away when the first generations of this Ummah rejected it, they would have been doomed — but Allah by His mercy persisted in sending it and calling them to it for twenty years, or for as long as He willed."*

Ibn Kathir draws the implication out: Allah **did not stop calling them** even while they were heedless and turning away — so that whoever was decreed to be guided would find the guidance still there when they turned.

---

## 3. They Have Always Mocked (43:6–8)

> *And how many a Prophet have We sent amongst the men of old. And never came there a Prophet to them but they used to mock at him.*

This is consolation, and it is stated as a general law rather than an exception. Then the answer to it: *We destroyed men stronger in power than these* — i.e. `O Muhammad, those who rejected the earlier Messengers were mightier than the ones rejecting you' (cf. 40:82).

*And the example of the ancients has passed away* — three readings preserved:

- **Mujahid:** the way of their life
- **Qatadah:** their punishment
- **Others:** their lesson — *We made them a lesson for the disbelievers who came after them*, as at the surah's own end: *We made them a precedent, and an example to later generations* (43:56)

---

## 4. They Already Know Who Made It (43:9–12)

> *And if you ask them: "Who created the heavens and the earth?" They will surely say: "The All-Mighty, the All-Knower created them."*

The entire idolatry of Makkah rests on a contradiction they state out loud: **they concede that the Creator of all things is Allah alone, with no partner — and go on worshipping others alongside Him.**

Then four exhibits, each a mercy they use daily:

- **The earth like a bed** — smooth, stable, firm enough to travel on, stand on, sleep on; created above water, and steadied with mountains lest it shake
- **Roads within it** — the paths between the mountains and the valleys, *that you may find your way* from city to city, land to land
- **Water in due measure** — exactly what suffices for crops, fruit, and drink for you and your cattle; and the barren land stirs and swells and puts forth every lovely kind. Ibn Kathir: the revival of dead earth is placed here **as the argument for the revival of dead bodies** — *and even so you will be brought forth*
- **Pairs, ships and cattle** — every kind of plant and animal, and beasts subjugated so that you may eat their meat, drink their milk, and ride their backs

## 5. The Traveller's Supplication (43:13–14)

> *In order that you may mount on their backs, and then may remember the favour of your Lord when you mount thereon, and say: "Glory to Him Who has subjected this to us, and we could have never had it. And verily, to Our Lord we indeed are to return!"*

**Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, As-Suddi and Ibn Zayd** all gloss the middle clause identically: *we could not have done this ourselves.* Were it not that Allah subdued these things, no human strength would have managed it.

The last line is the hinge. Ibn Kathir notes the Qur'an's habit of **using the small journey to point at the large one**:

| Earthly thing mentioned | What it is made to point toward |
|---|---|
| The journey (43:13–14) | *to Our Lord we indeed are to return* |
| Provision for the road (2:197) | *the best provision is Taqwa* |
| Garments and adornment (7:26) | *the raiment of Taqwa, that is better* |

---

## 6. Assigning Daughters to God (43:15–20)

The idolaters split their cattle between Allah and their false gods, and rigged the split (6:136). They did the same with children — keeping sons, assigning daughters to Allah (cf. 53:21–22: *Is it for you the males and for Him the females? That indeed is a division most unfair!*).

Ibn Kathir lets the next verse do the demolition on its own:

> *And if one of them is informed of the news of that which he sets forth as a parable to the Most Gracious, his face becomes dark, and he is filled with grief!*

A man is told a daughter has been born to him — the same category of being he has just attributed to God — and **he hates the news, it depresses and overwhelms him, and he keeps away from people out of shame.** *So how can they dislike that so much and yet attribute it to Allah?*

The follow-up (43:18) reflects the reasoning of the time about what women were held to lack — adornment supplying what was thought missing, and an inability to argue one's own case in a dispute — and the whole point of raising it is that **this is the category they assigned upward**.

Then, on *"If the Most Gracious had willed, we should not have worshipped them"* — the excuse of divine decree — Ibn Kathir enumerates **four distinct errors combined in a single sentence**:

1. They attributed offspring to Allah
2. They claimed He chose *daughters* over sons, and made the angels — servants of the Most Gracious — female
3. They worshipped them with **no proof, evidence or permission**: on opinion, whim, and imitation of forefathers
4. They then used Allah's decree as an alibi — reasoning which "betrayed their ignorance," since every Messenger from the first was sent with *Worship Allah, and avoid all false deities* (16:36)

**Mujahid** on *they have no knowledge whatsoever of that*: **"They do not appreciate the power of Allah."**

## 7. "We Found Our Fathers" (43:21–25)

> *Or have We given them any Book before this to which they are holding fast?*

No — no scripture licensed their idolatry (cf. 30:35). What they actually have is one sentence:

> *We found our fathers following a certain way, and we guide ourselves by their footsteps.*

Ibn Kathir notes that *Ummah* here means **way or religion**, as at 23:52. And then he makes the historical point that gives the passage its weight: **this is not a Makkan sentence.** Every luxurious elite of every warned town in history said the identical thing (43:23), just as every nation before said *a sorcerer or a madman* (51:52–53). *Their hearts and their words are similar.*

The Prophet's counter-offer is met with the flattest possible refusal:

> *"Even if I bring you better guidance than that which you found your fathers following?" They said: "Verily, we disbelieve in that with which you have been sent."*

Ibn Kathir: even convinced of its truth, **they would not have followed it** — because of their evil intentions and their arrogance towards the truth and its people.

---

## 8. Ibrahim's Word, Left Behind Him (43:26–28)

> *"Verily, I am innocent of what you worship, except Him Who created me; and verily, He will guide me." And he made it a word lasting among his offspring, that they may turn back.*

The Quraysh claimed descent from Ibrahim and claimed to have taken their religion from him. So the surah quotes him **disowning exactly what they do**.

The "word" he left lasting is identified unanimously — **Ikrimah, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, As-Suddi, and Ibn Abbas**: *La ilaha illallah*. And they add a detail that lands: *"there are still those among his offspring who say it."* **Ibn Zayd**: "the word of Islam" — the same thing.

*That they may turn back* — that they may **come back to this word**.

---

## 9. "Why Not a Great Man of the Two Towns?" (43:29–32)

> *And they say: "Why is not this Qur'an sent down to some great man of the two towns?"*

The two towns are **Makkah and At-Ta'if** (Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd). Several mufassirun name the two men the Quraysh had in mind: **Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah** and **Urwah bin Mas'ud Ath-Thaqafi** — though Ibn Kathir thinks the phrasing is generic: *a* great man from either town.

The reply is three-part:

> *Is it they who would portion out the mercy of your Lord?*

**The matter has nothing to do with them.** Allah knows best where to place His message, and reveals it only to the one **purest in heart and soul and noblest in descent**.

> *It is We Who portion out between them their livelihood in this world, and We raised some of them above others in ranks, so that some may employ others in their work.*

If they cannot even allocate *wealth* among themselves — if that is manifestly assigned from above, and assigned unequally on purpose, so that people need one another (**As-Suddi**) — on what basis do they expect to allocate prophethood?

> *But the mercy of your Lord is better than what they amass.*

## 10. Silver Roofs: Wealth Is Not a Sign of Divine Pleasure (43:33–35)

This is the passage the surah is named for, and one of the most startling in the Qur'an.

> *And were it not that mankind would have become of one community, We would have provided for those who disbelieve in the Most Gracious, silver roofs for their houses, and elevators whereby they ascend, and for their houses, doors, and thrones on which they could recline, and adornments of gold.*

**Ibn Abbas, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and As-Suddi** on the opening clause: were it not that many ignorant people would read wealth as a sign that **We love the person We gave it to** — and so agree upon disbelief for the sake of getting rich.

The catalogue is deliberately obscene: staircases and ladders of silver, locking doors of silver, couches of silver, and gold on top (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd). Allah's own verdict on the pile:

> *Yet all this would have been nothing but an enjoyment of this world. And the Hereafter with your Lord is for those who have Taqwa.*

Ibn Kathir: their reward for whatever good they did is **hastened** into worldly luxury, so that they arrive in the Hereafter with **no merit left outstanding**. Three narrations seal it:

- *"If this world were worth a gnat's wing before Allah, He would never give a disbeliever a drink of water."* (At-Tirmidhi — **Hasan Sahih** — and Ibn Majah, via Abu Hazim from Sahl bin Sa'd)
- **Umar bin Al-Khattab**, seeing the Prophet ﷺ on a rough mat that had marked his side, weeping: *"O Messenger of Allah, look at Chosroes and Caesar with all that they have, and you are the best of Allah's creation."* He sat up: **"Are you in doubt, O son of Al-Khattab? Those are people for whom the enjoyments are hastened in this world."** In another report: **"Does it not please you that this world is for them and the Hereafter is for us?"**
- From the Two Sahihs: *do not drink from vessels of gold and silver nor eat from such plates* — **"for these things are for them in this world and for us in the Hereafter."**

*And the Hereafter with your Lord is for those who have Taqwa* — Ibn Kathir: **exclusively** theirs; no one else will share it with them.

---

## 11. The Companion You Get for Looking Away (43:36–39)

> *And whosoever Ya'shu from the remembrance of the Most Gracious, We appoint for him Shaytan to be a Qarin to him.*

**'Asha** literally denotes weakness of vision; here it means **weakness of insight** — willfully ignoring and turning away. The penalty is not immediate punishment but **assignment**: a devil attached to you, who shows you the road to Hell (cf. 4:115, 61:5, 41:25).

> *And verily, they hinder them from the path, but they think that they are guided aright!*

Then the meeting, at the worst possible moment:

> *Till, when he comes to Us, he says: "Would that between me and you were the distance of the two easts — a worst companion indeed!"*

(Some recited it as *when they both come to Us* — the devil and the man he escorted.) And the reply that closes the door:

> *It will profit you not this Day, as you did wrong, that you will be sharers in the punishment.*

Your being together in the Fire will not help you in the slightest.

---

## 12. What Is and Is Not the Messenger's Job (43:40–45)

> *Can you make the deaf to hear, or can you guide the blind or him who is in manifest error?*

Ibn Kathir: **that is not up to you.** All you have to do is convey; guidance is not yours to give.

> *And even if We take you away, We shall indeed take vengeance on them. Or We show you that wherewith We threaten them — then verily, We have perfect command over them.*

`We are able to do both' — but **As-Suddi**, and Ibn Jarir with him, held that Allah would not take His Messenger in death until He had given him the joy of seeing his enemies brought low, with power over them and their wealth.

> *So hold you fast to that which is revealed to you. Verily, you are on the straight path.*

And then a verse the Arabs of Makkah are addressed by name:

> *And verily, this is indeed a Dhikr for you and your people, and you will be questioned.*

Two readings, both preserved:

- **An honour** for you and your people (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd) — an honour *in that it was revealed in their language*, so they understand it best of all mankind and are therefore **the most obliged to be steadfast in its commands**. This, Ibn Kathir notes, is exactly how the best of them were — the first emigrants and those who followed them.
- **A reminder** to you and your people — with the qualification that singling them out **does not exclude anyone else** (cf. 21:10, 26:214).

*And you will be questioned* — about this Qur'an: how you acted on it and what your response to it was.

## 13. Fir'awn: The Man Who Argued From His Rivers (43:46–56)

Musa is sent to Fir'awn and his chiefs with mighty signs — his hand, his staff, the flood, the locusts, the Qummal, the frogs, the blood, the loss of crops and lives — *and not an Ayah We showed them but it was greater than its fellow preceding it.* Their response was to laugh.

Each time a sign struck, they came to Musa with a bargain:

> *"O you sorcerer! Invoke your Lord for us according to what He has made a pact with you. Verily, we shall guide ourselves."*

Ibn Kathir stops to correct a reading almost everyone gets wrong. **"O sorcerer" was not an insult.** Ibn Jarir: it meant *expert, knowledgeable one.* The scholars of that era **were** the magicians, magic was not held reprehensible then, and — decisively — *they were in a state of need, so it would not have been appropriate for them to insult him.* **They meant it as an honorific.**

And each time the torment lifted, they broke the promise (cf. 7:133–135).

Then Fir'awn's speech, the surah's portrait of the argument from status:

> *"O my people! Is not mine the dominion of Egypt, and these rivers flowing underneath me? See you not then? Am I not better than this one who is despicable and can scarcely express himself clearly?"*

**Qatadah**: they had gardens and rivers of flowing water. The unstated contrast is that Musa and his followers were **poor and weak** (cf. 79:23–25).

On *mahin* — "despicable" — the glosses stack:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sufyan** | insignificant |
| **Qatadah, As-Suddi** | weak |
| **Ibn Jarir** | he had no power, authority or wealth |

Ibn Kathir's verdict: the description is a lie, and **inverted** — *it is he who was despicable and insignificant, lacking in physical, moral and religious terms, and Musa who was noble, truthful, righteous and upright.*

On the speech defect he is careful and generous. Something had happened to Musa's tongue in childhood, burnt by a coal; he asked for the knot to be loosened *so that they may understand my speech*, and was answered: *You are granted your request, O Musa* (20:36). **Al-Hasan Al-Basri** suggested some residue may have remained that Musa never asked to be relieved of — because he had only ever asked for what stood in the way of **conveying the message**. And then the principle: **a person cannot be blamed for physical matters over which he has no control.** Fir'awn had the intelligence to know that. He said it anyway, *to confuse and mislead his people, who were ignorant and stupid.*

> *"Why then are not golden bracelets bestowed on him, or angels sent along with him?"*

He asked for jewellery and an entourage. Ibn Kathir: **he looked only at outward appearances**, and missed inner realities far clearer than the ones he fixed on.

> *Thus he fooled his people, and they obeyed him. Verily, they were ever a people who were rebellious.*

### The delay before the drowning

> *So when they angered Us, We punished them, and drowned them all.*

Ibn Abbas: *they provoked Our wrath*; Ad-Dahhak: *they made Us angry.* And then the hadith Ibn Abi Hatim records from **Uqbah bin Amir** — the surah's sharpest warning, and the counterpart to the silver-roof passage:

> *When you see that Allah gives a person what he wants even though he is persisting in sin, that means that Allah is enticing him into destruction* (**istidraj**).

— and the Prophet ﷺ then recited *So when they angered Us, We punished them, and drowned them all.*

Two more of the Salaf read the same verse the same way:

- **Ibn Mas'ud**, when sudden death was mentioned: *"It is a relief for the believer and a source of regret for the disbeliever"* — then recited it (via Tariq bin Shihab)
- **Umar bin Abdul-Aziz**: *"I found that punishment comes with negligence"* — then recited it

## 14. The Argument About the Son of Maryam (43:57–65)

> *And when the son of Maryam is quoted as an example, behold, your people cry aloud thereat.*

**Ibn Ishaq preserves the incident**, and it is worth having in full because it explains an otherwise baffling passage.

The Prophet ﷺ was sitting in the Masjid with **Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah** when **An-Nadr bin Al-Harith** joined them and was defeated in argument. The Prophet ﷺ then recited: *Certainly you and that which you worship besides Allah are fuel for Hell; you will enter it* (21:98) — and left, going to sit elsewhere.

**Abdullah bin Az-Zab'ari** arrived, was told what had happened, and produced what everyone present thought was a devastating counter:

> *"Ask Muhammad whether everyone that is worshipped instead of Allah will be in Hell with those who worshipped him — for we worship the angels, and the Jews worship Uzayr, and the Christians worship the Messiah, Isa bin Maryam."*

Al-Walid and the assembly were delighted. The Prophet's reply:

> **"Everyone who likes to worship something other than Allah will be with the one whom he worshipped — for indeed they are worshipping the Shaytan and whoever told them to worship that person."**

And the Qur'an answered each strand separately:

| Their move | The response |
|---|---|
| *What about Isa and Uzayr?* | *Those for whom the good has preceded from Us — they will be removed far therefrom* (21:101) |
| *We worship the angels as daughters of Allah* | *Glory to Him! They are but honoured servants* (21:26) |
| Their delight at the whole manoeuvre | *And when the son of Maryam is quoted as an example, behold, your people cry aloud thereat* |

*Cry aloud* is glossed variously: **they laughed / were astounded** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, As-Suddi, Ad-Dahhak), **they were repelled and laughed** (Qatadah), **they turned away** (Ibrahim An-Nakha'i).

And the verdict on the whole exercise:

> *They quoted not the above example except for argument. Nay! But they are a quarrelsome people.*

Ibn Kathir explains **why it was never a real argument**: 21:98 addresses *the Quraysh*, who worshipped idols and false gods. **They did not worship the Messiah.** He was never inside the verse's scope, and they knew it — *what they said was only for the sake of argument; they did not really believe in it.*

Then the hadith of **Abu Umamah** (Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi — *Hasan Sahih* — Ibn Majah, Ibn Jarir), which generalises the diagnosis:

> **"No people go astray after having been guided, but they resort to argument"** — and he recited this very verse.

### And what Isa actually was

> *He was not more than a servant. We granted Our favour to him, and We made him an example for the Children of Israel.*

A servant blessed with prophethood and messengership — and a **sign, proof and evidence** of Allah's power to do whatever He wills.

> *And he shall be a known sign for the Hour.*

Ibn Kathir gives the correct reading as **the descent of Isa before the Day of Resurrection**, supported by 4:159 (*there is none of the People of the Scripture but must believe in him before his death* — i.e. before Isa's death) and by an alternate recitation reading the word as *sign* rather than *knowledge*. **Mujahid**: one of the signs of the Hour will be his appearance. Narrated similarly from **Abu Hurayrah, Ibn Abbas, Abu Al-'Aliyah, Abu Malik, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak** — and Ibn Kathir notes the reports are **Mutawatir** that he will descend as a just ruler and fair judge.

Isa's own summary of his mission closes the section: *I have come to you with Al-Hikmah* — **prophethood** — *and to make clear to you some of that in which you differ* — **religious matters, not worldly ones** (Ibn Jarir; Ibn Kathir: "what he said is good") — *Verily, Allah! He is my Lord and your Lord. So worship Him. This is the straight path.*

> *But the sects from among themselves differed.*

Some said he was the servant and Messenger of Allah — **which is true** — while others claimed he was the son of Allah, or Allah Himself. *So woe to those who do wrong from the torment of a painful Day!*

## 15. The Hour, and the Day Friendship Turns (43:66–67)

> *Do they only wait for the Hour that it shall come upon them suddenly while they perceive not?*

It is real and will inevitably come; the negligent are unprepared; **it will catch them unawares**, and the regret they feel that Day will neither benefit nor protect them.

> *Friends on that Day will be foes one to another, except those who have Taqwa.*

Ibn Kathir states it as a rule with no exceptions: **every friendship formed for any purpose other than Allah turns to enmity on the Day of Resurrection** — and only what was for His sake lasts. He cites Ibrahim to his people: *The love between you is only in the life of this world, but on the Day of Resurrection you shall disown each other and curse each other* (29:25).

---

## 16. The Call That Raises Everyone's Hope, and the Line That Takes It Back (43:68–73)

> *My servants! No fear shall be on you this Day, nor shall you grieve — you who believed in Our Ayat and were Muslims.*

**Al-Mu'tamir bin Sulayman** from his father, and it is the most vivid image in the surah:

> When the Day of Resurrection comes and the people are resurrected, there will be no one left who is not filled with terror. Then a caller will cry out: ***My servants! No fear shall be on you this Day, nor shall you grieve*** — and **all the people will be filled with hope**. Then it will be followed by: ***you who believed in Our Ayat and were Muslims*** — and **all of mankind will be filled with despair apart from the believers.**

Belief here means both halves: **their hearts believed, and they submitted inwardly and outwardly** to the Laws of Allah.

Then the entry: *Enter Paradise, you and your wives* — your counterparts — *Tuhbarun*, in delight and joy.

- **Trays and cups of gold** — fine vessels containing food and drink, **without spouts or handles**
- *All that their souls desire, and all that eyes could delight in* — good food, delightful fragrances, beautiful scenes
- *And you will abide therein forever* — Ibn Kathir: **you will never leave it or want to exchange it**

And then the reminder attached to it:

> *This is the Paradise which you have been made to inherit because of your deeds that you used to do.*

Ibn Kathir handles the apparent tension precisely: the deeds were the **cause of your being included in Allah's mercy** — for **no one is admitted to Paradise by deeds alone**; that is by mercy and grace. But **the ranks and degrees within it are attained according to deeds.**

## 17. Malik, and the Request That Is Refused (43:74–80)

> *Verily, the criminals will be in the torment of Hell to abide therein forever. It will not be lightened for them* — Ibn Kathir: **not even for one moment** — *and they will be plunged into destruction with deep regrets, sorrows and in despair therein* — they will despair of any goodness.

> *We wronged them not, but they were the wrongdoers.*

Proof had been established and Messengers sent; they rejected and rebelled; they are punished accordingly. *And your Lord is not at all unjust to His servants.*

Then the last request the damned make:

> *And they will cry: "O Malik! Let your Lord make an end of us." He will say: "Verily, you shall abide forever."*

**Al-Bukhari** records via Safwan bin Ya'la from his father that he **heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ reciting this on the Minbar**. What they are asking for is **annihilation** — *let Him destroy our souls and give us respite from our predicament*. It is denied, as it is elsewhere:

- *Neither will it have a complete killing effect on them so that they die, nor shall its torment be lightened for them* (35:36)
- *There he will neither die nor live* (87:11–13)

Ibn Kathir: *you will have no way out from it and no refuge.*

Then the reason, stated as their own doing:

> *Indeed We have brought the truth to you, but most of you have a hatred for the truth.*

`We explained it clearly and in detail — but your nature could not accept it and did not seek it; you followed and venerated falsehood, stood in the way of truth, refused it, and despised its followers.'

And their strategy exposed: *Or have they plotted some plan? Then We too are planning* — **Mujahid**: "they wanted to plot some evil, but We are also planning" (cf. 27:50). The idolaters were **trying their utmost to refute truth with falsehood**, and Allah arranged the consequences to fall back on them. *Or do they think We hear not their secrets and their private counsel? Yes — and Our messengers are by them, to record*: the angels writing their deeds, **major and minor**.

## 18. "If He Had a Son, I Would Be the First to Worship" (43:81–85)

> *Say: "If the Most Gracious had a son, then I am the first of the worshippers."*

Ibn Kathir is careful with the logic here, because the sentence is easily misread. It is a **conditional that does not imply its condition is possible**. The meaning is: *were it so, I would worship Him on that basis, because I am one of His servants — I obey whatever He commands and am not too proud to worship Him.* Compare 39:4: *Had Allah willed to take a son, He could have chosen whom He willed out of what He created. But glory be to Him! He is Allah, the One, the Irresistible.*

Then the disavowal, and the instruction to stop arguing:

> *Glorified is the Lord of the heavens and the earth, the Lord of the Throne! Exalted be He from all that they ascribe. So leave them to speak nonsense and play until they meet the Day of theirs which they have been promised.*

And the statement of His uniqueness across both realms:

> *It is He Who is the only God in the heaven and the only God on the earth* — the God of whoever is in the heavens and whoever is on earth; **all of them worship Him and are humbled before Him** (cf. 6:3).

> *And Blessed be He to Whom belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth, and all that is between them, and with Whom is the knowledge of the Hour, and to Whom you all will be returned.*

Creator and Sovereign, controlling them **with none to resist or oppose**; the timing of the Hour known to no one but Him; and every person requited for what he did.

---

## 19. Who May Intercede (43:86–87)

> *And those whom they invoke instead of Him have no power of intercession — except for those who bear witness to the truth knowingly, and they know.*

The idols and false gods can do nothing for them. The exception is precise: **the one who bears witness to the truth with knowledge and insight** — his intercession with Allah will avail, **by Allah's leave**.

And the contradiction restated one final time:

> *And if you ask them who created them, they will surely say: "Allah." How then are they turned away?*

They will admit He alone is the Creator, with no partner — **and still worship, alongside Him, things that own nothing and can do nothing.** Ibn Kathir's verdict: *this is the utmost foolishness and stupidity.*

---

## 20. The Prophet's Complaint, and the Word He Is Given Instead (43:88–89)

> *And his saying: "O my Lord! Verily, these are a people who believe not!"*

This is **Muhammad ﷺ complaining to his Lord about his own people** — so Ibn Mas'ud, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Jarir. Qatadah: *"These are the words of your Prophet, when he complained about his people to his Lord."* The parallel is 25:30: *And the Messenger will say: "O my Lord! Verily, my people deserted this Qur'an."*

And the answer is not a counter-argument. It is a dismissal and a greeting:

> *So turn away from them, and say: Salam — peace! But they will come to know.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss on *Salam* is the instruction the whole surah has been building toward: **do not respond to them in the same evil manner in which they address you — but try to soften their hearts, and forgive them in word and deed.**

*But they will come to know* is the warning behind the courtesy. Ibn Kathir closes: **His punishment, which cannot be warded off, struck them; His religion and His word was supreme.** Then Jihad and striving were prescribed, until the people entered the religion of Allah in crowds and Islam spread throughout the east and the west. *And Allah knows best.*

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Az-Zukhruf is the Qur'an's answer to anyone who reads worldly standing as divine endorsement.**

The Quraysh wanted revelation given to a great man of the two towns. Fir'awn argued for his own supremacy from his rivers and Musa's stammer. Both were making the same mistake, and the surah refuses it twice — once in a rhetorical question (***Is it they who would portion out the mercy of your Lord?***), and once in the most extravagant hypothetical in the Book: **Allah would hand disbelievers silver houses and gold, and withholds it only because people are foolish enough to mistake the gift for love.** If the whole world were worth a gnat's wing, a disbeliever would not get a drink of water from it. The Prophet ﷺ slept on a mat that marked his side, and told the man who wept over it that Chosroes and Caesar were simply being **paid early**.

Against that stands what the surah says actually carries weight. **Ibrahim's word** — *La ilaha illallah* — left behind him as an inheritance more durable than any estate, and still being said by his offspring. **Isa** — *not more than a servant on whom We bestowed favour* — worshipped by millions who never asked him, and coming back at the end to say so himself. And **the caller on the Day of Resurrection** whose first line lifts all of humanity into hope and whose second takes it back from everyone but those who believed and submitted.

Everything else in the surah is the wrong version of this: gods you concede did not create anything; daughters assigned upward by men whose faces darken at their birth; a religion whose only evidence is *our fathers did it*; a devil assigned to you as a companion because you looked away; a friendship that becomes enmity the moment it is weighed; a plea to Malik to be destroyed, refused.

And at the very end, after eighty-nine verses of argument, the Prophet ﷺ is told to stop arguing. *Turn away from them, and say: peace.*

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Az-Zukhruf. All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and in Him is all strength and protection.*
