*Revealed in Makkah · 34 ayat · named for a wise man who was never a prophet*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Luqman is a Makkan surah about **what a person chooses to listen to**. It opens by dividing humanity in two: those whose hearts soften at the Book, and those who buy *lahw al-hadith* — idle talk — and turn a deaf ear. Everything after that is Allah producing evidence: the pillarless heavens, the mountains, the rain, the ships, the alternation of night and day — and the honest admission of the idolaters themselves that **Allah made all of it**, which convicts them out of their own mouths.

Set in the middle of this, like a jewel in a setting, is the advice of a man who was not a prophet, not an Arab, not free, and not admired for his appearance — and to whom Allah nonetheless gave *al-Hikmah*. His counsel to his son is the surah's practical heart, and it is the only extended piece of parental advice in the Qur'an.

The surah ends where all certainty ends: five things known to Allah alone, one of which is the ground you will die on.

---

## 1. The Muhsinin, and What Makes Them So (31:1–5)

> *A guide and a mercy for the Muhsinin — those who perform the Salah and give Zakah, and have faith in the Hereafter with certainty.*

The Qur'an is guidance, healing and mercy — but Ibn Kathir notes the qualification: **for the Muhsinin**, those who do good deeds *in accordance with the Shariah*. He unpacks the description into a portrait:

- They establish the obligatory prayers **properly and at their correct times**, and follow them with optional and supererogatory prayers.
- They pay Zakah to those who deserve it.
- They uphold the ties of kinship.
- They are certain of reward and punishment in the Hereafter, and seek their reward **with Allah** — not showing off, and not fishing for thanks from people.

Whoever does this is described as being *on guidance from their Lord*, meaning they follow His guidance **with clear understanding**, and *successful* — in this world and the next.

---

## 2. Idle Talk: The Purchase That Costs Everything (31:6–7)

> *And of mankind is he who purchases idle talk (lahw al-hadith) to mislead from the path of Allah without knowledge, and takes it by way of mockery.*

Ibn Kathir places this deliberately. Allah had just described the blessed in another surah — *the skins of those who fear their Lord shiver from it, then their skin and their heart soften to the remembrance of Allah* (39:23). Here He describes the opposite type: those who **turn away from the Qur'an and turn instead to flutes, singing and musical instruments**.

The classical comments:

- **Ibn Mas'ud:** *"This — by Allah — refers to singing."*
- **Qatadah:** he may not spend a penny on it, but *purchasing* it means he loves it — **and the more misguided he is, the more he loves it**, preferring falsehood to truth and the harmful to the beneficial.
- Another view: it means buying **singing servant girls**.
- **Ibn Jarir**, broadest of all: *all speech that hinders people from seeing the signs of Allah and following His path*.

*To mislead from the path of Allah* means he does this **to oppose Islam and its followers**; **Mujahid** on *takes it by way of mockery*: mocking Allah's path and making fun of it.

And the response of such a man to revelation (31:7): he turns away **in pride, as if he never heard it, as if there were deafness in his ear** — because it annoys him, since he gains nothing from it and has no interest in it. The promised *painful torment* on the Day of Resurrection will hurt him **exactly as much as hearing the Book of Allah hurt him**. As they showed the signs of Allah no respect, they will be shown none.

---

## 3. The Other Destiny (31:8–9)

For those who believe and do righteous deeds: *Gardens of Delight, to abide therein.* Ibn Kathir lists what "delight" holds — food, drink, clothing, dwellings, means of transportation, spouses, beauty and delightful sounds — **which have never crossed the mind of any human being**. They stay forever, never leaving, and **never desiring change**.

*It is a promise of Allah in truth* — it will undoubtedly come to pass, because Allah never breaks His promise. *All-Mighty* — He has subjugated all things. *All-Wise* — in what He says and does, and in making this Qur'an a guidance.

---

## 4. Look Up, Look Down, Look Around (31:10–11)

> *He has created the heavens without any pillars that you see, and has set on the earth firm mountains lest it should shake with you.*

**Al-Hasan and Qatadah:** *"It has no pillars, visible or invisible."*

The mountains stabilise the earth and lend it weight, lest it shake with its water. The scattered creatures are *all kinds of animals, the total number of whose kinds and colours is known to no one except the One Who created them*.

Then the pivot Ibn Kathir highlights: having told us He is **the Creator**, Allah immediately reminds us He is **the Provider** — sending down water and raising every goodly kind of plant in pairs, *beautiful to look at*.

**Ash-Sha'bi** adds a striking line: *"People are also produce of the earth — so whoever enters Paradise is goodly, and whoever enters Hell is vile."*

The challenge lands: *So show Me what those besides Him have created.* The idols you call upon have created nothing. *Nay, the wrongdoers are in plain error* — **ignorant and blind**, in an error that is clear, obvious, and not at all hidden.

---

## 5. Who Was Luqman? (31:12)

The Salaf differed: prophet, or righteous servant? **The majority held the latter** — wisdom without prophethood.

What the reports preserve about him is worth sitting with:

| Narrator | Description |
|---|---|
| **Ibn Abbas** (via Sufyan Ath-Thawri ← Al-Ash'ath ← Ikrimah) | An **Ethiopian slave** who was a **carpenter** |
| **Jabir bin Abdullah** (to Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr) | Short, with a flat nose, from **Nubia** |
| **Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib** (via Yahya bin Sa'id Al-Ansari) | From the black peoples of southern Egypt, with thick lips — *"Allah gave him wisdom but withheld Prophethood from him"* |
| **Mujahid** (via Shu'bah ← Al-Hakam) | *"A righteous servant, but not a Prophet"* |

And the passage Ibn Kathir preserves from **Al-Awza'i ← Abdur-Rahman bin Harmalah** — a black man came to Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib with a question, and Sa'id said to him:

> *"Do not be upset because you are black, for among the best of people were three who were black: Bilal, Mahja' the freed slave of Umar bin Al-Khattab, and Luqman the Wise."*

**The tongue and the heart** (Ibn Jarir ← Khalid Ar-Raba'i): Luqman's master told him to slaughter a sheep and bring **the best two pieces**; he brought the tongue and the heart. Later, told to bring **the worst two**, he brought the tongue and the heart. Challenged, he answered:

> *"There is nothing better than these if they are good, and there is nothing worse than these if they are bad."*

*Al-Hikmah* here means **understanding, knowledge and eloquence**. The command attached to it — *give thanks to Allah* — is for the blessings Allah gave **him alone among his people and contemporaries**. And whoever gives thanks, the benefit returns to himself; whoever is ungrateful harms only himself, for Allah is *Al-Ghani* — **even if every person on earth disbelieved, He would need nothing and no one besides Himself**.

## 6. Luqman's Advice — I: Tawhid and Parents (31:13–15)

His full name, per a report from **As-Suhayli**: Luqman bin 'Anqa' bin Sadun; his son, **Tharan**. Ibn Kathir notes the logic of the scene — Luqman gave his best knowledge to *the closest and most beloved of all people to him*. And he began with the one thing that must come first:

> *"O my son! Join not in worship others with Allah. Verily, joining others in worship with Allah is a great Zulm indeed."*

**Al-Bukhari ← Abdullah bin Mas'ud:** when *those who believe and confuse not their belief with Zulm* (6:82) was revealed, the Companions were distressed — *"Who among us does not wrong himself?"* The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"That is not what it means. Have you not heard what Luqman said: 'O my son! Join not in worship others with Allah. Verily, joining others in worship with Allah is a great Zulm indeed'?"* (also in Muslim)

Then **parents** — the pairing that recurs throughout the Qur'an (cf. 17:23).

*His mother bore him in weakness and hardship upon weakness and hardship* — **Mujahid**: the hardship of bearing the child. **Qatadah**: exhaustion upon exhaustion. **Ata' Al-Khurasani**: weakness upon weakness. Allah mentions the mother's exhaustion and the nights lost to the child **specifically to remind the son of her earlier kindness to him**.

A famous derivation sits here: since weaning is *in two years* (31:14) and *the bearing of him and the weaning of him is thirty months* (46:15), **Ibn Abbas and other Imams concluded the shortest possible pregnancy is six months.**

And then the limit (31:15). If parents *strive* to make you commit shirk, **do not obey them** — but the disobedience is narrow and the kindness is not withdrawn: *behave with them in the world kindly*, treating them with respect, while following **the path of him who turns to Me** — i.e. the believers.

**At-Tabarani (in Al-Ishrah) ← Sa'd bin Malik**, who said this ayah was revealed about him: he honoured his mother, and when he became Muslim she refused food and drink, telling him people would say he had killed his own mother. After two days and nights of her starving herself, he said:

> *"O my mother, by Allah, even if you had one hundred souls and they were to depart one by one, I would not give up this religion of mine for anything. So if you want to, eat; and if you want to, do not eat."*

**So she ate.**

## 7. Luqman's Advice — II: The Mustard Seed and the Manner of a Man (31:16–19)

**The weight of a mustard seed (31:16).** Even a sin the size of a mustard seed, hidden **inside a rock**, or anywhere in the heavens or the earth, Allah will bring it forth on the Day of Resurrection to be placed in the Scales (cf. 21:47; 99:7–8). *Verily, Allah is Latif, Khabir* — His knowledge is **subtle**, so that nothing escapes Him however small; and He is Well-Aware **even of the footsteps of an ant in the darkest night**.

**Prayer, enjoining good, and patience (31:17).** Offer the prayer properly at its appointed times. Enjoin good and forbid evil **to the best of your ability and strength**. And bear with patience whatever befalls you — because **Luqman knew that whoever commands good and forbids evil will inevitably meet harm and annoyance from people**. *These are some of the important commandments* — patience under the harm people cause is among the weightiest of them.

**Do not turn your face away (31:18).** Do not turn your face from people when you speak with them or they speak with you, looking down on them arrogantly. *Rather be gentle towards them and greet them with a cheerful face*, as in the hadith:

> *"...even if it is only by greeting your brother with a cheerful countenance. And beware of letting your lower garment trail below your ankles, for this is a kind of boasting, and Allah does not like boasting."*

Nor walk in insolence — do not be boastful, arrogant, proud and stubborn *for Allah will hate you* (cf. 17:37: *you can neither rend the earth nor reach the mountains in height*). The one Allah does not love is **the man who shows off and admires himself, feeling he is better than others**.

**Walk and speak in the middle (31:19).** Walk moderately — **neither slow and lazy, nor excessively fast**, but somewhere between the two extremes. And lower your voice: do not exaggerate in speech or raise your voice unnecessarily. **Mujahid and others**: the ugliest of voices is the donkey's; a man raising his voice produces a noise like it in loudness, and this is hateful to Allah. Ibn Kathir notes the force of the comparison — likening a loud voice to a donkey's implies it is **forbidden and extremely blameworthy**, since the Prophet ﷺ said *"It is not befitting us to be an evil parable"* (in the hadith of the man who takes back his gift, like a dog returning to its vomit).

## 8. Three More Sayings of Luqman

Ibn Kathir appends a few of the many proverbs narrated from him:

- **Imam Ahmad ← Ibn Umar**, that the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Luqman the Wise used to say: when something is entrusted to the care of Allah, He protects it."*
- **As-Sari bin Yahya:** *"Wisdom puts the poor in the company of kings."*
- **Awn bin Abdullah:** *"O my son! When you come to a gathering of people, greet them with Salam, then sit at the edge of the group, and do not speak until you see that they have finished speaking. Then if they remember Allah, join them; but if they speak of anything else, leave them and go to another group."*

---

## 9. Blessings Counted, Ancestors Followed (31:20–21)

Allah lists what He has subjected: **in the heavens** — the stars that light the night, the clouds, rain, snow and hail, and the sky made a canopy that covers and protects. **On earth** — stability, rivers, trees, crops and fruits. And He has *completed and perfected His graces, apparent and hidden*, **by sending Messengers, revealing Books, and removing doubts and excuses**.

Yet still some dispute about Allah — about His Tawhid and His sending of Messengers — *without knowledge or guidance or a Book giving light*, i.e. with **no sound evidence and no valid inherited scripture**.

And when told to follow what Allah revealed, their entire argument is: *we shall follow what we found our fathers following*. Ibn Kathir's rebuttal is the Qur'an's own (2:170): **and what if their fathers understood nothing and were not guided?** The verse closes with the terrible question — *even if Shaytan invites them to the torment of the Fire?*

---

## 10. The Most Trustworthy Handhold (31:22–24)

> *And whosoever submits his face to Allah, while he is a Muhsin, then he has grasped the most trustworthy handhold.*

To submit the face is to **do deeds sincerely for His sake**, submitting to His commandments and following His Laws; to be a *Muhsin* is to **do what the Lord commanded and abstain from what He forbade**. Such a person *has received a firm promise from Allah that He will not punish him*.

And to the Prophet ﷺ: *let not the disbelief of the disbeliever grieve you* — do not grieve over them, for their return is to Allah, and He is *All-Knower of what is in the breasts*, from Whom **nothing whatsoever is hidden**. They are let to enjoy **a little while** in this world, and then compelled to a torment that is *terrifying and difficult to bear* (cf. 10:69–70).

---

## 11. Convicted by Their Own Answer (31:25–26)

Ask them who created the heavens and the earth, and **they will say: "Allah."** They admitted He alone was the Creator — and still worshipped things they themselves recognised as **created by Him and subjugated to Him**. *By their admitting that, proof is established against them.* Say: **All praise be to Allah** — and most of them know not.

*Allah is Al-Ghani, Al-Hamid* — He needs no one and nothing besides Himself, while **everything else is in need of Him**; and He is worthy of praise for all He has created and decreed, in every affair.

---

## 12. Trees as Pens, Oceans as Ink (31:27–28)

> *And if all the trees on the earth were pens, and the sea, with seven seas behind it to add to it, yet the Words of Allah would not be exhausted.*

Ibn Kathir opens with the Prophet's ﷺ own confession of inadequacy: *"I cannot praise You enough; You are as You have praised Yourself."*

Every tree made into a pen, every sea into ink, topped up seven times over — **the pens would break and the ink would run dry**, even if more were brought. Two cautions he adds:

- **The seven is not literal.** The number indicates a vast amount; it is *not* to be understood as the seven oceans of the world — an idea taken from Israelite stories, *which we neither believe nor reject*.
- **"Like it" is not "one more."** In 18:109, *even if We brought like it for its aid* does not mean a single further sea, but another and another and another — **because there is no limit to the signs and Words of Allah**.

Then (31:28): creating and resurrecting **all** of mankind is, in relation to His power, **like creating and resurrecting a single soul**. *His command, when He intends a thing, is only that He says to it "Be!" — and it is* (36:82); *Our commandment is but one, as the twinkling of an eye* (54:50). He need not repeat or confirm it. And *All-Hearer, All-Seer*: He hears all they say and sees all they do **as if hearing and seeing a single soul**.

## 13. Night, Day, Sun and Moon (31:29–30)

He merges night into day and day into night — **taking from one and adding to the other**, so days lengthen into summer and shorten into winter.

*Each running its course for a term appointed* — either each runs **within its set limits**, or **until the Day of Resurrection**; Ibn Kathir accepts both as correct. In support of the first, the hadith of **Abu Dharr** in the Two Sahihs:

> *"O Abu Dharr! Do you know where this sun goes?" — "Allah and His Messenger know best." — "It goes and prostrates beneath the Throne, then it seeks permission from its Lord, and soon it will be said: 'Go back from whence you came.'"*

**Ibn Abi Hatim ← Ibn Abbas** (chain Sahih): the sun is like flowing water, running its course through the sky by day, and when it sets it travels beneath the earth until it rises in the east — *and the same is true of the moon*.

*That is because Allah, He is the Truth* — He truly exists and is truly divine, and **everything else invoked besides Him is falsehood**. He needs nothing; everything depends on Him; nothing created could move **even an atom's weight** without His permission. **If all the people of heaven and earth gathered to create a single fly, they could not do it.** He is *Al-'Ali* — none higher; *Al-Kabir* — greater than everything, before Whom all else is insignificant.

---

## 14. Ships, Waves, and the Convenient Believer (31:31–32)

> *See you not that the ships sail through the sea by Allah's grace?*

Ibn Kathir's observation is quietly beautiful: **if He had not given the water the strength to carry the ships, they would not sail**. And the sign is read only by *every patient, grateful person* — meaning **one who bears difficulty with patience and gives thanks at times of ease**.

Then the test (31:32). When waves cover them *like shades* — **like mountains or clouds** — they call on Allah **with total sincerity, worship purely His** (cf. 17:67). But once He brings them safe to land, *there are among them those that stop in between*. **Mujahid** takes the "middling" one here as the denier, comparing 29:65: *when He brings them safely to land, behold, they give a share of their worship to others.*

The two words at the end are worth keeping:

- **Khattar** — one who **betrays**, who stabs in the back; a man who breaks every promise he makes. Ibn Kathir calls it *the worst form of treachery* (so said Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, and Malik ← Zayd bin Aslam).
- **Kafur** — one who **denies blessings and gives no thanks**, but forgets them and does not remember them.

---

## 15. No One Can Stand In For You (31:33)

> *Fear a Day when no father can avail aught for his son, nor a son avail aught for his father.*

Even if the father wanted to **offer himself as a sacrifice in his son's place, it would not be accepted from him** — and the same in reverse.

Two deceptions are then named. *Let not this present life deceive you* — do not let contentment with this world make you forget the Hereafter. *Nor let al-Gharur deceive you about Allah* — **the Shaytan** (so Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak and Qatadah), who *makes promises and arouses false desires, and Shaytan's promises are nothing but deceptions* (4:120).

Ibn Kathir closes with the account **Ibn Abi Hatim** recorded from **Wahb bin Munabbih**, of 'Uzayr asking an angel whether the righteous could intercede for the wrongdoers, or fathers for sons. The answer:

> On the Day of Resurrection all matters will be settled and Allah's dominion made manifest, and **no exceptions will be made**. No one will speak except by the permission of the Most Merciful. **No father will answer for his son, no son for his father, no man for his brother, no servant for his master.** No one will care about anybody except himself, or feel grief or compassion for anyone except himself. Everyone will be worried only about himself, weeping for himself and carrying his own burden — **and no one will carry the burden of another.**

## 16. The Five Keys of the Unseen (31:34)

> *Verily, Allah — with Him is the knowledge of the Hour; He sends down the rain; and He knows what is in the wombs. No person knows what he will earn tomorrow, and no person knows in what land he will die.*

These are **the keys of the Unseen**, whose knowledge Allah has kept for Himself. The timing of the Hour is *not known to any Prophet who was sent nor any angel who is close* (cf. 7:187). No one knows when rain will fall — though once He issues the command, **the angels entrusted with it know**, as do those of His creation He wills. No one knows what is in the wombs — though once He decrees male or female, blessed or doomed, **the entrusted angels know**. No one knows what tomorrow will earn him, in this world or the next. And no one knows the land of his death.

Ibn Kathir stacks the evidence:

- **Imam Ahmad ← Buraydah**, the Prophet ﷺ: *"There are five things which no one knows except Allah..."* then he recited the ayah.
- **Imam Ahmad / Al-Bukhari ← Ibn Umar**: *"The Keys of the Unseen are five, which no one knows except Allah"* — recorded by Al-Bukhari in the Book of the Rain Prayer and again in his Tafsir.
- **Al-Bukhari ← Abu Hurayrah** — the famous **hadith of Jibril**, which Ibn Kathir gives in full here because it ends on this very verse:

> **Iman** — to believe in Allah, His Angels, His Books, His Messengers, the meeting with Him, and the Resurrection.
> **Islam** — to worship Allah alone and associate nothing with Him, to establish the prayer, to pay the obligatory Zakah, and to fast Ramadan.
> **Ihsan** — *"to worship Allah as if you see Him; and if you do not see Him, then He sees you."*
> **The Hour** — *"The one who is asked knows no more than the one asking"* — but among its signs: **when the servant woman gives birth to her mistress**, and **when the barefoot and naked become leaders of the people**. Its timing is one of the five.

The man left; the Prophet ﷺ said *"Bring him back to me"* — and he could not be found. *"That was Jibril, who came to teach the people their religion."*

**Qatadah** on the last of the five: no one knows what is in the wombs — male or female, red or black, or what it is; no one knows whether tomorrow brings good or bad. *You do not know, O son of Adam, when you will die. You might die tomorrow; you might be stricken by calamity tomorrow.*

And on *no person knows in what land he will die* — no one knows **where his resting place will be**: on land or in the sea, on a plain or in the mountains. Two hadiths close the surah:

> *"If Allah wants to take a person's soul in a particular land, He will give him a reason to go there."*

> **At-Tabarani, Al-Mu'jam Al-Kabir ← Usamah bin Zayd:** *"Allah does not will that a person should die in a certain land but He gives him a reason to go there."*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

1. **You are what you choose to listen to.** The surah's opening division is not between the clever and the ignorant, but between the man whose heart softens at revelation and the man who *buys* distraction and then turns a deaf ear. Qatadah's warning is the sharpest line in the tafsir: **the more misguided he is, the more he loves it.**

2. **Wisdom is not status.** Allah gave *al-Hikmah* to a black Nubian slave and carpenter, and gave prophethood to others. Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib's answer to the man who came to him — *do not be upset because you are black* — is preserved in the Qur'an's own commentary for a reason.

3. **The order of Luqman's advice is the syllabus.** Tawhid first. Then parents — honoured always, obeyed in everything but shirk. Then the awareness that a mustard seed is enough to be weighed. Then prayer, enjoining good, and the patience you will certainly need for it. Then, last and not least: **your face, your walk, your voice.** Character is not an appendix to creed.

4. **Everyone concedes the Creator; the argument is only ever about the consequences.** Asked directly, even the idolaters said *"Allah."* Their real defence was never evidence — it was *we found our fathers doing it*.

5. **Nobody stands in for you.** Not a father for his son, not a son for his father. And you will die on ground you cannot name, on a day you cannot know — which is exactly why the surah spends its middle telling you how to live.
