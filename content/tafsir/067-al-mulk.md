*Revealed in Makkah · 30 ayat · "The Dominion" — named for its opening word · the surah of thirty verses that intercedes for its reciter*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Mulk is a **surah of surveying the kingdom** — and of discovering, at every point of inspection, that there is only one King.

It opens by placing all dominion in one Hand, and then spends thirty verses walking the reader around the estate to check the claim. Look **up**: seven heavens, no flaw. Look **again**: your eye gives out before the creation does. Look **outward**: birds hanging in empty air. Look **down**: the earth you walk on, the springs you drink from. And interleaved with each survey is the same rhetorical question, asked five different ways:

> *Who else? Who is an army for you? Who provides, if He withholds? Who holds the birds up? Who brings the water back?*

The answer is never stated, because it does not need to be.

The second thread is **the intellect**. This is the surah where the people of the Fire give the most devastating confession in the Qur'an — *had we but listened or used our reason* — and where Allah asks, **"Should not He Who created know?"** and then points out that He gave you hearing, sight, and a mind, and you barely used them. The surah's opening test — *which of you is best in deed* — is finally answered by the two groups it describes at the end: the man walking face-down and blind, and the man walking upright on a straight road.

---

## 1. Its Virtues

Two hadiths, and both make the same striking claim — that the surah itself acts on behalf of the person who recites it:

- **Abu Hurayrah** (Imam Ahmad; also At-Tirmidhi and the four Sunan compilers, who graded it *Hasan*): there is a chapter of thirty verses that will **intercede for its reciter until he is forgiven** — *Blessed be He in Whose Hand is the dominion.*
- **Anas** (At-Tabarani and Al-Hafiz Ad-Diya' Al-Maqdisi): a chapter that will **argue on behalf of its reciter until it causes him to enter Paradise.**

Note the verbs: it *intercedes*, and it *disputes on your behalf*. A recited surah becomes an advocate.

---

## 2. The Hand That Holds Everything (67:1–2)

> *Blessed be He in Whose Hand is the dominion; and He is Able to do all things.*

Ibn Kathir: Allah deals with all His creatures however He wishes, **and there is none who can reverse His decree**. He is not questioned about what He does — because of His force, His wisdom, and His justice. All three are named together; power without wisdom and justice would be a different claim entirely.

**"Who created death and life."** Two things Ibn Kathir draws out here:

1. Those who hold that **death is an existing created thing** cite this verse — it is something Allah *created*, not merely an absence.
2. The parallel at 2:28 — *how can you disbelieve, seeing that you were dead and He gave you life* — shows that "death" here names the first stage, **non-existence**, and "life" the beginning of existence.

**"That He may test you which of you is best in deed."** The most quietly demanding sentence in the surah. Ibn Kathir, citing **Muhammad bin 'Ajlan**, points to what Allah *did not* say:

> He did not say *which of you does the most deeds*.

The measure is quality, not volume.

**"And He is Al-'Aziz, Al-Ghafur."** The Almighty — the Most Great, Most Powerful, Most Honourable — and, alongside that, the One who forgives whoever turns back to Him after disobeying and opposing His command. Ibn Kathir keeps the two together deliberately: *even though Allah is Almighty, He also forgives, shows mercy, pardons and excuses.*

---

## 3. Look Again. And Again. (67:3–4)

**The seven heavens, layer above layer.** Ibn Kathir raises a genuine question here: are they stacked directly on one another, or separated by space? Two views exist; he holds the **second to be more correct**, as established in the hadith of the Isra' and other narrations.

**"You can see no fault in the creation of the Most Gracious."** Done flawlessly — no disunion, no conflict, no inconsistency, no deficiency, flaw, or defect.

The word *futur* (rifts) is glossed by the early authorities:

| Authority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Ath-Thawri | cracks |
| As-Suddi | tears |
| Qatadah | *"Do you see any defects, O son of Adam?"* |

Then the command is repeated: **look again, and yet again** — Qatadah: *it means twice.* And the result:

- **Khasi'** — *humiliated* (Ibn 'Abbas); *despised* (Mujahid, Qatadah)
- **Hasir** — *exhausted* (Ibn 'Abbas); *broken-down fatigue from weakness* (Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi)

The point is precise and rather beautiful: **the eye wears out before the creation does.** You do not stop looking because you found a flaw; you stop because you are tired, having found none.

---

## 4. Lamps That Are Also Missiles (67:5)

The stars adorn the lowest heaven — some moving, some fixed — and serve as projectiles against the devils.

Here Ibn Kathir makes a careful astronomical clarification: **the stars themselves are not what is thrown.** Just as the stars are called "lamps" figuratively, so "We made them missiles" is that same manner of speech. What is actually hurled is **the meteors beneath them**, taken from the stars. *And Allah knows best.*

He supports the picture with As-Saffat 37:6–10, and then quotes **Qatadah**, whose statement (recorded by Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim) is the surah's sharpest warning against speculation:

> The stars were created for three purposes: **adornment of the sky, missiles against the devils, and signs for navigation.** Whoever seeks any other meaning in them *has spoken with his own opinion, has lost his portion, and has burdened himself with what he has no knowledge of.*

An astrology verdict, delivered in one sentence.

---

## 5. The Fire, and the Question Its Keepers Ask (67:6–11)

Hell is described from the inside:

- **Shahiq** — Ibn Jarir: the sound of shouting.
- **"While it is simmering"** — Ath-Thawri: *it will boil them the way a few seeds are boiled in a great deal of water.*
- **"It almost bursts up with fury"** — parts of it nearly break apart from other parts, from the severity of its rage against them.

Then the interrogation. Every time a group is thrown in, the keepers ask: **"Did no warner come to you?"** And the answer is always yes.

Ibn Kathir reads this as Allah **reminding us of His own justice**: He punishes no one until the proof has been established and a Messenger sent — *And We never punish until We have sent a Messenger* (17:15); compare the identical scene at 39:71.

Which produces the confession:

> *Had we but listened, or used our intelligence, we would not have been among the dwellers of the blazing Fire.*

Ibn Kathir separates the two failures precisely: **we did not have the understanding to comprehend what the Messengers brought, and we did not have the intelligence to guide us to follow them.** Revelation heard, and reason applied — they missed both.

And a hadith (Imam Ahmad, from Abu Al-Bakhtari At-Ta'i, from a Companion) that lands hard on the phrase "then they will confess their sin":

> *The people will not be destroyed until they themselves confess their guilt.*

---

## 6. Fearing Him Unseen (67:12–14)

> *Verily, those who fear their Lord unseen — theirs will be forgiveness and a great reward.*

Ibn Kathir defines it as fearing to stand before your Lord, being anxious about what lies between you and Allah **when no other people are present** — refraining from disobedience, and performing obedience when no one sees but Him.

He brings the hadith of the **seven whom Allah will shade on the Day when there is no shade but His**, and selects the two from that list that match this description exactly:

- The man tempted by a **woman of beauty and high standing**, who says: *Verily, I fear Allah.*
- The man who gives charity **so secretly that his left hand does not know what his right hand spent.**

Both are acts performed where only Allah is watching. Then, immediately: *whether you keep your talk secret or disclose it, He is the All-Knower of what is in the breasts* — meaning the ideas and thoughts occurring in hearts.

And the verse the whole section turns on:

> **"Should not He Who has created know?"** — *doesn't the Creator know?*

He is **Al-Latif** (Most Kind and Courteous) and **Al-Khabir** (All-Aware).

---

## 7. Walk the Earth — and Still Rely on Him (67:15)

Allah lists what He did to make the earth habitable: made it a stable abode, set mountains in it, caused springs to gush, fashioned pathways, placed useful things and fertile ground in it.

**"So walk in its manakib"** — Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, As-Suddi and Qatadah: its **outermost borders, its roads, its regions**. Travel where you wish, in trade and in seeking earnings — *and know that your efforts will benefit you nothing unless Allah makes matters easy for you.*

Then Ibn Kathir states the principle plainly:

> **Striving by using the means does not negate Tawakkul.**

And proves it with the bird hadith ('Umar bin Al-Khattab; Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi — *Hasan Sahih* — An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah):

> *If you would trust in Allah as He truly should be trusted in, He would provide for you as He provides for the birds: they leave in the morning with empty stomachs and return in the evening with full ones.*

Ibn Kathir's own gloss is the key to it: **the bird searches, morning and evening, while depending on Allah.** It is not fed in the nest. Reliance and effort are the same act.

---

## 8. How Secure Do You Feel? (67:16–18)

Two threats, framed as questions:

- That the earth **sink with you and quake** — move back and forth, disrupted.
- That a **Hasib** be sent — a wind carrying gravel that strikes you (compare 17:68).

The reason the threat has not yet fallen is stated as mercy, not indifference. Ibn Kathir cites 35:45: *if Allah were to punish men for what they earned, He would not leave a moving creature on the surface of the earth — but He gives them respite to an appointed term.* He is forbearing, He pardons, He does not hasten.

And previous nations already ran this experiment: *how terrible was My reproach* — **great, severe, and painful**.

---

## 9. The Birds, and the Two Walkers (67:19–22)

> *Do they not see the birds above them, spreading out their wings and folding them in? None upholds them except the Most Gracious.*

Ibn Kathir: sometimes they spread their wings in the wind (gliding), sometimes they gather and beat them (flapping) — and **Allah holds them up in the air by subjecting the air to them**, out of His mercy and gentleness. Compare 24:79.

Then the challenges: **who is an army for you besides Ar-Rahman?** *There is no protector or helper for you besides Him.* And **who provides for you if He withholds His provision?** No one gives, withholds, creates, provides or helps but Allah alone. Ibn Kathir's sting: **they know this, and worship others anyway** — persisting in obstinance, arrogance, and flight from a truth they neither listen to nor follow.

Which brings the surah's central image (67:22):

> *Is he who walks prone on his face more rightly guided, or he who walks upright on a straight path?*

The disbeliever is a man walking bent over, head down, **not knowing where he is going or how** — lost, astray, confused. The believer stands erect: *straight within himself, while his path is straight as well.*

And Ibn Kathir insists this is **not only a metaphor**. It is their likeness in this world and their literal state in the next: the believer gathered walking upright toward a Paradise opening before him, the disbeliever gathered **dragged on his face** toward the Fire, with his "companions" — meaning those like him (37:22–23).

When Anas asked how people could be gathered on their faces, the Prophet ﷺ replied (Two Sahihs):

> *Is not He who made them walk on their legs able to make them walk on their faces?*

---

## 10. The Faculties You Were Given (67:23–27)

> *It is He Who created you, and endowed you with hearing and seeing, and hearts.*

*Hearts* here means **intellects and powers of reasoning** — the very tools the people of the Fire admitted they never used. *Little thanks you give*: how little you use these abilities to obey Him, act on His commands, and avoid His prohibitions.

*He created you on the earth* — spread and distributed across its regions, **with your differing languages, colours, shapes and appearances** — and He will gather you after this separation just as He originally divided you.

They ask *when?* The answer is a boundary marker: **the knowledge is with Allah alone**; the Prophet's ﷺ commission is only to inform them that it is real and will occur. *I am only a plain warner.*

And when it finally comes into view, faces darken — because *everything that is to occur will occur, even if it takes a long time*. What they mocked will encircle them, and there will appear to them from Allah **what they had never reckoned on** (39:47–48).

---

## 11. Two Closing Challenges (67:28–30)

**"If Allah destroys me and those with me, or has mercy on us — who will save the disbelievers from a painful torment?"**

Ibn Kathir turns the taunt around: *save yourselves.* Nothing rescues you from Allah except **repentance, seeking forgiveness, and returning to His religion.** Your hopes that punishment will strike us will not benefit you — whether He punishes us or shows us mercy, there is no escape from what awaits you.

And the counter-position, stated once: *He is the Most Gracious; in Him we believe, and upon Him we rely* — belief and Tawakkul in every affair (compare 11:123, *so worship Him and rely upon Him*).

Then the final question of the surah, and perhaps the most physical:

> **"If your water were to sink away, who could bring you flowing water?"**

*Ghawr* — sinking — is the exact opposite of *ma'in*, springing forth. Ibn Kathir: if it went down into the lowest depths of the earth, **it would not be reachable with iron axes nor strong arms.** No one can do this but Allah. It is purely out of His favour that water springs up for you and runs to the various regions of the earth **in the amount suitable for the needs of His servants, be it little or abundant.**

The surah that began with dominion in His Hand ends with a glass of water you cannot replace.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Mulk is a test administered through the eyes.** *He created death and life to test which of you is best in deed* — and then hands you the instruments: look at the sky, look again, look at the birds, look at the ground, look at the water. Each inspection returns the same two findings: **nothing is defective, and nothing has another owner.**

The people who fail are not people who lacked evidence. They are people who had **hearing, sight and hearts** and did not use them, and who say so themselves in the Fire: *had we but listened, or used our reason.* Their walk gives them away — face-down, not knowing where they are going.

The people who pass are described in one line: they **fear their Lord unseen**, when nobody is watching. And they go out to work anyway, like the bird that leaves the nest hungry and comes back full — because trusting Allah was never the opposite of moving.

*Blessed be He in Whose Hand is the dominion.*
