*Revealed in Makkah · 83 ayat · named for the two disjointed letters that open it*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ya Sin is an argument for the Resurrection, built almost entirely out of things the listener can already see. It opens by swearing on the Qur'an that Muhammad ﷺ is genuinely sent, then spends the rest of its length answering one objection: *the dead do not come back.*

The answer never appeals to abstraction. It points at dead ground that greens after rain, at a sun that has never once overtaken the moon, at a ship that floats because the sea has been made to carry it, at a camel a child can lead, at fire pulled out of a living green branch. Each is offered as evidence of the same claim — the One who did that once can do it again.

Between the signs sit two narratives: **a town that killed the man who believed** (36:13–29), and **the Day the mouths are sealed and the limbs speak** (36:65). Both make the same point from opposite ends of time. The surah closes on a bone crumbled in a mocker's hand, and the reply that has become the surah's signature: *when He intends a thing, He says to it "Be" — and it is.*

---

## 1. The Virtues of the Surah

Ibn Kathir opens with two narrations, and is characteristically careful about their strength:

- **Abu Ya'la, from Abu Hurayrah** ؓ — whoever recites Ya Sin at night wakes forgiven; and whoever recites the *Ha Mim* in which Ad-Dukhan is mentioned wakes forgiven. *Its chain is good (jayyid).*
- **Ibn Hibban, in his Sahih, from Jundub bin Abdullah** ؓ — whoever recites Ya Sin at night **seeking the Face of Allah** will be forgiven.

The second narration carries the condition that governs the first: the recitation is not a mechanism. It is an act done for Allah's Face.

---

## 2. Ya Sin, and the Oath (36:1–5)

> *Ya Sin. By the Qur'an, full of wisdom, truly you are one of the Messengers, on a straight path, sent down by the Almighty, the Most Merciful.*

On the disjointed letters, Ibn Kathir simply refers back to what he wrote at the opening of Al-Baqarah and moves on.

- **Full of wisdom** — *Al-Muhkam*: perfected, such that falsehood cannot reach it from before it or behind it.
- **On a straight path** — an upright methodology, an upright religion, an upright Law.
- **Sent down by the Almighty, the Most Merciful** — the path you have brought is revelation from the Lord of might, who is merciful to His believing servants.

He reads the whole oath as one sentence: *this book is perfect, therefore the man carrying it is genuine.*

---

## 3. Warning a People Whose Fathers Were Not Warned (36:6–7)

The Arabs are meant here — no warner had come to them before him. But Ibn Kathir immediately blocks the narrow reading: **naming one group does not exclude the others.** He points back to the mass-transmitted (*mutawatir*) evidence for the universality of the mission, gathered under *"Say: O mankind, I am the Messenger of Allah to you all"* (7:158).

> *Indeed the Word has proved true against most of them, so they will not believe.*

Ibn Jarir: punishment has become inevitable for most of them, because Allah decreed in the Mother of the Book — Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz — that they would not believe.

---

## 4. The Collar, the Barrier, and the Covering (36:8–10)

> *We have put on their necks iron collars reaching to the chins, so their heads are raised up. And a barrier before them, and a barrier behind them, and We have covered them up, so they cannot see.*

This is a picture, not a physical restraint in this world. Ibn Kathir draws it out precisely: **the man decreed to be among the doomed is, in the matter of guidance, like a man chained at the neck with his hands bound beneath his chin so his head is forced upward.** The hands are not mentioned because mentioning the collar is enough — they are implied.

- **Ibn Abbas** ؓ links it to *"let not your hand be tied to your neck"* (17:29): their hands are bound to their necks, so they cannot stretch them out to do any good.
- **Mujahid**: heads raised, hands over their mouths, restrained from any good.
- **On the barriers** — Mujahid: between them and the truth, from both directions, so they are lost between them. Qatadah: they move from one misguidance to the next.
- **On the covering** — their eyes have been blinded to the truth. Ibn Abbas is reported to have recited it *fa-a'shaynahum*, from *al-'asha*, weakness of sight.
- **Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam**: Allah placed this barrier between them and Islam and faith, so they will never reach it — then recited 10:96–97, *they will not believe even if every sign comes to them, until they see the painful torment*. And he said: **whoever Allah has prevented will never be able.**

**Ikrimah's narration** gives it a scene. Abu Jahl said, *If I see Muhammad I will do such and such.* Then these verses came down — and he would stand there saying *Where is he? Where is he?* while the people said *Here is Muhammad,* and he could not see him.

---

## 5. Who the Warning Actually Reaches (36:11)

> *You can only warn him who follows the Reminder and fears the Most Gracious unseen. Give him glad tidings of forgiveness and a generous reward.*

The Reminder is the Qur'an. **Fearing Him unseen** — Ibn Kathir defines it as fearing Him when no one sees you but Allah, knowing that He is watching and sees what you do. Compare 67:12.

The reward is *karim* — vast, great, beautiful.

---

## 6. The Deeds, and the Traces They Leave (36:12)

> *Verily, We give life to the dead, and We record what they sent before them, and their traces, and all things We have recorded in a Clear Book.*

Ibn Kathir first notes the double meaning of *We give life to the dead* — on the Day of Resurrection, and also **in this world, to hearts that had died in misguidance** (compare 57:17, where reviving dead earth is offered right after a passage on hardness of the heart).

Then he opens up *athar* — traces — into the richest passage in the first half of the surah. **What is recorded is not only what you did, but what you left running after you.**

- **The hadith of the two precedents** (Muslim, from Jarir bin Abdullah Al-Bajali ؓ — narrated in the incident of the destitute men of Mudar in woolen rags): whoever sets a good precedent in Islam has its reward *and* the reward of everyone who acts on it after him, without diminishing theirs in the slightest; and whoever sets an evil precedent bears its burden and the burden of everyone who acts on it after him, without lightening theirs in the slightest. **The Prophet ﷺ then recited this very verse.**
- **The three that continue** (Muslim, from Abu Hurayrah ؓ): when the son of Adam dies his deeds end except three — knowledge others benefit from, a righteous child who prays for him, ongoing charity he left behind.
- **The literal reading: footsteps.** Mujahid, Al-Hasan and Qatadah all take *athar* as footprints. Qatadah's line is the one to keep: if Allah were going to overlook anything of yours, son of Adam, He would have overlooked what the wind erases of these footprints — *but He counts them, and whether each step was toward obedience or away from it.* So let whoever can have his footsteps written for the sake of Allah do that.
- **Banu Salamah** (Ahmad, and Muslim, from Jabir ؓ): they wanted to move their houses nearer the Masjid. The Prophet ﷺ told them — *stay where you are, and your footsteps will be recorded; stay where you are, and your footsteps will be recorded.*
- **The man who died away from home** (Ahmad, from Abdullah bin 'Amr ؓ; also An-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah): the Prophet ﷺ prayed over a man who died in Al-Madinah and said, *would that he had died elsewhere than his birthplace* — because when a man dies away from where he was born, **the distance between his birthplace and where his footsteps ended is measured out for him in Paradise.**
- **Zayd bin Thabit to Anas** ؓ, who was walking quickly: *do you not feel that your footsteps are being written down?*

**Imam Mubin** here is the source-record itself, Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz (Mujahid, Qatadah, Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd). Ibn Kathir gathers 17:71, 39:69 and 18:49 — *what Book is this, that leaves out nothing small or large but has recorded it.*

---

## 7. The Town, the Three Messengers, and the Man Who Ran (36:13–29)

The parable Muhammad ﷺ is told to put to his people. Two messengers were sent and denied; a third reinforced them.

**The town's objections** are the standing objections of every rejecting nation, and Ibn Kathir lines them up as such:

- *You are only human beings like us* — echoed at 64:6, 14:10, 23:34, 17:94. Their real demand was angels.
- *The Most Gracious has revealed nothing; you are only lying.*
- *We see an evil omen in you.* Qatadah: they meant *anything bad that happens to us is because of you.* Mujahid: *people like you never enter a town without its people being punished.* Then the threat — stoning, and a painful torment.

**The Messengers' three answers** are worth separating, because they are a complete model of how to respond:

1. *Our Lord knows we have been sent to you* — i.e. if we were lying He would take the utmost vengeance on us; instead He will make us prevail, and you will learn whose end is good (compare 29:52).
2. *Our duty is only to convey plainly* — deliver, and the consequence is yours.
3. *Your evil omen is with you* — thrown back. Ibn Kathir stacks the parallels: Fir'awn's people blaming Musa (7:131), Salih's people (27:47), and the verdict of 4:78, **say: all things are from Allah.** Their "omen" was nothing but being reminded to worship Allah alone.

**Then the man from the farthest part of the town, running.** The reports Ibn Ishaq relays (from Ibn Abbas, Ka'b Al-Ahbar, Wahb bin Munabbih) name him **Habib An-Najjar**: a rope-maker, sick with leprosy, who gave half of everything he earned in charity, and whose natural disposition was sound. He arrives as the town resolves to kill its messengers.

His speech is a compressed sermon:

> *Follow those who ask no wage of you, and who are rightly guided… And why should I not worship Him who created me and to whom you will be returned?… Shall I take besides Him gods whose intercession cannot help me and who cannot save me?… I have believed in your Lord, so listen to me.*

On that last line, Ibn Kathir weighs two readings — that he addressed his people, or the Messengers, asking them to *bear witness before Him* — and prefers the second as more apparent.

**They killed him on the spot.** Ibn Mas'ud's report (via Ibn Ishaq) says they stamped on him until his intestines came out. Qatadah's report has him stoned while saying *O Allah, guide my people, for they do not know* — praying for them until he died. *May Allah have mercy on him.*

Then, immediately:

> *It was said: Enter Paradise. He said: Would that my people knew — that my Lord has forgiven me and made me of the honoured ones.*

- **Mujahid**: it was said to Habib, *Enter Paradise* — and it was his by right, for he had been killed. He entered it with all its provision, and Allah took from him the sickness, grief and exhaustion of this world.
- **Qatadah**: you will never find a believer who is not sincere. Seeing with his own eyes how Allah had honoured him, he wished his people could see it too.
- **Ibn Abbas** ؓ (via Ibn Abi Hatim) puts the two halves of his life side by side: **sincere towards his people while alive** — *O my people, follow the Messengers* — **and sincere towards them after death** — *would that my people knew.*
- **Abu Mijlaz** (via Sufyan Ath-Thawri): *made me of the honoured ones* — because of my faith in my Lord and my belief in the Messengers. He wanted them to see the reward, so that seeing it they would follow.

**The punishment came without an army.** *We sent not against his people after him an army from heaven, nor was it needful for Us to send.* Ibn Mas'ud's reading: the matter was simpler than that; there was no need to outnumber them. One *Sayhah* — a single shout — and they were still. The scholars of tafsir report that Jibril seized the pillars of their city gate and loosed one cry, and not a soul was left in any body.

*(A second reading, from Mujahid and Qatadah, takes "an army from heaven" to mean another Message after him. Ibn Jarir prefers the first, on the grounds that a Message does not need an army to carry it.)*

---

## 8. Ibn Kathir's Objection: Was It Really Antioch? (a note on method)

Most of the Salaf identified the town as **Antioch** and the three as envoys of 'Isa عليه السلام. Ibn Kathir reports that faithfully — and then, unusually, argues against it in three numbered points. It is one of the clearest windows in the surah onto how he works.

1. **The text says they were sent by Allah**, not by the Messiah. *When We sent to them two Messengers… so We reinforced them with a third.* Had they been Disciples, something would have indicated it. And had they been sent by the Messiah, why would the people have said *you are only human beings like ourselves* — an objection that only makes sense against a claim of divine sending?
2. **The people of Antioch believed.** Antioch was the first city to accept the Messiah — one of the four patriarchal cities, along with Jerusalem (the Messiah's city), Alexandria (where the hierarchy was settled) and Rome (Constantine's city, from which the Patriarch later moved to Constantinople). But this surah says the town rejected its messengers and was destroyed to the last man.
3. **Chronology.** The Antioch episode falls after the Tawrah was revealed — and Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri ؓ and others among the Salaf held that **after the Tawrah, Allah did not destroy an entire nation by sending a punishment**; instead He commanded the believers to fight the idolaters (citing 28:43).

His conclusion is careful rather than triumphant: this must be a different town — or, if the name is to be kept, a different Antioch, since the famous one is not known to have been destroyed either before or during Christian times. *And Allah knows best.*

## 9. Alas for Mankind (36:30–32)

> *Alas for mankind! There never came a Messenger to them but they mocked him.*

Ibn Abbas ؓ: **woe to mankind.** Qatadah: woe to mankind, who neglected the command of Allah. Ibn Kathir reads it as regret felt on the Day of Resurrection, when they see the punishment with their own eyes and grasp what their mockery was.

Then a short, sharp refutation. *Do they not see how many generations We destroyed before them? They will not return to them* — **this is a rejection of reincarnation.** Against those who said *there is nothing but our life of this world, we die and we live* (23:37), believing they would cycle back into the world as they were, the verse states flatly that no destroyed generation returns.

But everyone returns *to Allah*: **and surely, all of them will be brought before Us** — every past nation and every one yet to come, gathered and requited (compare 11:111).

---

## 10. The Dead Land (36:33–36)

The first sign, and the template for all the rest:

> *And a sign for them is the dead land. We give it life, and bring forth grain from it, so they eat.*

Dead, arid, no vegetation — then water comes, and it stirs and swells and puts forth every lovely kind. Gardens of date palms and grapes, and springs made to gush and to run **to the places where they are needed**.

> *…and their hands made it not. Will they not then be grateful?*

Ibn Abbas and Qatadah: all of it comes by Allah's mercy, not by human effort, labour or strength. *(Ibn Jarir preferred reading the particle as "what their hands made" — i.e. they eat both from Allah's bounty and from what they planted and tended — which fits Ibn Mas'ud's recitation. Ibn Kathir reports the disagreement without forcing it.)*

Then the widest statement of the passage: **glory to Him who created all the pairs** — in what the earth grows, in themselves (male and female), *and of that which they do not know*. Kinds of creation about which they know nothing at all (compare 51:49).

---

## 11. The Night, the Sun, and the Moon (36:37–40)

> *And a sign for them is the night: We withdraw the day from it, and they are in darkness.*

The day is stripped away and night comes — which is the literal sense, and matches the hadith on breaking the fast: *when night comes from here, and day departs from here, and the sun has set, the fasting person breaks his fast.*

**The sun's fixed course** (*mustaqarr*). Ibn Kathir gives two views:

1. **A place.** Its resting place is beneath the Throne — and since the Throne is the roof of creation, the sun is beneath it wherever it goes. At noon it is nearest; at midnight, at the opposite point of its orbit, furthest. There it prostrates and asks permission to rise. **Al-Bukhari, from Abu Dharr** ؓ: at sunset the Prophet ﷺ asked him, *do you know where the sun goes?* — then said it goes and prostrates beneath the Throne, and recited this verse. (Ibn Kathir takes the occasion to say the Throne is not a sphere as the astronomers claim, but a dome on pillars borne by the angels.)
2. **A term.** Its appointed time runs out on the Day of Resurrection, when it halts and is rolled up. Qatadah: it has a fixed term and will not exceed it. Abdullah bin 'Amr ؓ: it keeps to its summer orbit for a set time and its winter orbit for a set time, neither exceeded.

Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas ؓ recited it as *la mustaqarra laha* — **with no fixed course** — meaning it never settles anywhere, never slows and never stops, night or day, until the Resurrection (compare 14:33).

*That is the decree of the Almighty* — which none can oppose. *The All-Knowing* — who knows every movement and every stillness, and set the pattern in which there is no variation or inversion (compare 6:96).

**The moon's mansions.** Its orbit tells the months as the sun tells the days (2:189, 10:5, 17:12). Ibn Kathir describes the cycle with real attention: the sun has its own light and rules the day; the moon's light is reflected, small and low at the month's start, climbing and brightening each night until it is full on the fourteenth, then waning to nothing — *till it returns like the old dried curved date stalk*, which Ibn Abbas ؓ explains as the stem that carries the bunch of dates.

He then preserves the Arab names for each three nights of the month: **Ghurar, Nufal, Tusa', 'Ushar, Al-Bid** (white, for the moonlight), **Dura'** (from *dar'a'*, the black-headed sheep, because the night begins dark before the moon rises late), **Zulam, Hanadis, Da'adi, Mihaq** — the last for the moon's disappearance.

**And the discipline of it:** the sun does not overtake the moon, nor does night outstrip day.

- **Mujahid**: each has a limit it neither transgresses nor falls short of; when one's time comes, the other departs.
- **Ikrimah**: each has its own time of authority — it is not fitting for the sun to rise at night.
- **Ad-Dahhak**: night does not leave from here until day comes from there.
- **Mujahid** again, on *nor does the night outstrip the day*: **they pursue one another rapidly** — with no gap and no interval between them.

*They all float, each in an orbit* — Ibn Abbas ؓ and the Salaf: *in an orbit like the arc of a spinning wheel.*

---

## 12. The Laden Ship (36:41–44)

> *And a sign for them is that We carried their offspring in the laden ship.*

Their *offspring* means their forefathers — carried in the Ark, filled with provisions and animals, two of every kind. Ibn Abbas ؓ: **laden means filled.** Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah and Ibn Zayd: this was the ship of Nuh عليه السلام, and apart from those aboard, none of the descendants of Adam remained on earth.

*And We have created for them the like of it, on which they ride.* Two readings, both preserved:

- **Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas** ؓ: **the camel — the ship of the land**, on which they carry goods and ride.
- **Ibn Jarir from Ibn Abbas** ؓ, who asked his students directly and answered himself: the ships built after Nuh's ship, on its pattern. (Also Abu Malik, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, Abu Salih, As-Suddi.)

And underneath the whole image, the point: *if We willed, We could drown them, and there would be no answer to their cry, nor would they be saved — except as a mercy from Us, and an enjoyment for a while.* **The sea does not hold you up. He does.**

---

## 13. The Two Refusals (36:45–47)

> *And when it is said to them: Beware of what is before you and what is behind you, that you may receive mercy* — they turn away.

Mujahid takes both as sins — past and future. Ibn Kathir notes the wording implies no answer will come: they simply ignore it. And *never came a sign from their Lord but they turned from it* — signs of *tawhid* and of the truth of the Messengers, neither accepted nor benefited from.

Then the second refusal, and it is the uglier one:

> *When it is said to them: Spend of what Allah has provided you — those who disbelieve say to those who believe: Shall we feed those whom, if Allah willed, He would have fed? You are only in plain error.*

Told to spend on the poor and needy among the Muslims, they turn the decree into an excuse: *if Allah wanted them fed, He would feed them; we are simply in accord with His will.* **Predestination used as a reason to withhold** — and Ibn Kathir lets the verse's own verdict stand: the error they attribute to the believers is theirs.

---

## 14. A Single Shout, in the Marketplaces (36:48–50)

> *They await only a single Sayhah, which will seize them while they are disputing.*

Their question — *when will this promise come, if you are truthful?* — is the question of those who do not believe it at all (42:18).

Ibn Kathir sets the scene with unusual concreteness: the blast of terror will come **while people are in their marketplaces and places of work, arguing and haggling as they always do**. Israfil will sound a long note, and there will be no one left on earth who does not tilt his head to listen to the sound coming from heaven.

> *Then they will not be able to make a bequest, nor return to their family.*

No time to settle their affairs — *because the matter is more serious than that* — and no time to get home.

---

## 15. Who Raised Us From Our Sleeping Place? (36:51–54)

The third blast: the Trumpet of the Resurrection.

> *And behold, from the graves they will come out quickly to their Lord.* — *Yansilun*: walking fast (compare 70:43, racing to a goal).

> *They will say: Woe to us! Who has raised us from our place of sleep?*

Their **graves** — from which, in the world, they were certain they would never rise. Ibn Kathir handles the obvious difficulty head-on: this does not contradict the punishment of the grave. **Compared to what comes after it, that will seem like a nap.**

Ubayy bin Ka'b ؓ, Mujahid, Al-Hasan and Qatadah: they will sleep before the Resurrection — Qatadah specifying that this is between the two blasts.

And when they say it, they are answered: *This is what the Most Gracious promised, and the Messengers spoke the truth.* The believers reply, says more than one of the Salaf; the angels reply, says Al-Hasan. Ibn Kathir sees no conflict — both are possible.

*It will be but a single Sayhah, and they will all be brought before Us* (compare 79:13–14, 16:77, 17:52). One command, and all of them gathered.

> *This Day none will be wronged in anything, nor requited except for what you used to do.*

---

## 16. The People of Paradise, Busy (36:55–58)

> *Verily, the dwellers of Paradise that Day will be busy with joyful things.*

Al-Hasan Al-Basri and Isma'il bin Abi Khalid: **too busy to think about the torment the people of Hell are suffering.** Mujahid and Qatadah: busy with the delights they are enjoying. Ibn Abbas ؓ: rejoicing.

They and their spouses in pleasant shade — Mujahid: **the shade of trees** — reclining on thrones, which Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others gloss as **beds beneath canopies**. Fruits of every kind, and whatever they ask for, they find.

And then the last thing given, which is not a thing:

> *Salam — a word from the Lord, Most Merciful.*

Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abbas ؓ: **Allah Himself, who is As-Salam, will greet the people of Paradise with peace** (compare 33:44).

---

## 17. Get You Apart (36:59–62)

> *And O you criminals, get you apart this Day.*

The separation — ordered to stand clear of the believers (compare 10:28, 30:14, 30:43, 37:22–23). And then the rebuke, which is framed as a covenant they broke:

> *Did I not command you, O Children of Adam, that you should not worship Shaytan — he is a plain enemy to you — and that you should worship Me? That is the straight path.*

They obeyed an open enemy and disobeyed Ar-Rahman, who created them and provided for them. *And indeed he led astray a great multitude of you* — a large number, say Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi and Sufyan bin 'Uyaynah. *Did you not then understand?*

Ibn Kathir's paraphrase of the charge is worth keeping: **you were shown the straight path and you took a different one, on the instruction of your enemy.**

---

## 18. The Mouths Sealed, the Limbs Speaking (36:63–67)

Hell is shown to them — *this is the Hell you were promised; burn in it today for your disbelief* (compare 52:13–15). Then:

> *This Day We shall seal up their mouths, and their hands will speak to Us, and their legs will bear witness to what they used to earn.*

This is the moment of denial collapsing. Ibn Kathir gives it two of the most vivid narrations in the surah.

**Anas bin Malik** ؓ (Ibn Abi Hatim; also Muslim and An-Nasa'i): the Prophet ﷺ smiled until his molars showed, and when asked why, described a servant arguing with his Lord on that Day —

- *My Lord, will You not protect me from injustice?* — **Of course.**
- *Then I will accept no witness against me but myself.* — **Today you are witness enough against yourself, and the honourable scribes will testify against you.**
- His mouth is sealed. His limbs are told: **Speak.** And they speak of what he did.
- Then he is allowed to speak again, and says to them: *May you be doomed — it was for you that I was fighting.*

**Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari** ؓ (Ibn Jarir) sets the believer and the denier side by side. The believer is shown his deeds privately, admits them — *yes, my Lord, I did that* — and Allah forgives and conceals them, so that no creature sees his sins while his good deeds are displayed, and he wishes everyone could see. The hypocrite is shown his and denies them: *by Your glory, this angel wrote down things I never did.* The angel names the day and the place. He swears again — and **at that, his mouth is sealed.** Abu Musa added: I think the first part of him to speak will be his right thigh.

Then two statements of what Allah *could* have done and did not:

- *If We willed, We could have wiped out their eyes, and they would race for the path — how then would they see?* Ibn Abbas ؓ: We could have misguided them all away from guidance; on another occasion, We could have blinded them. Al-Hasan: covered their eyes so they stumbled about. Mujahid, Abu Salih, Qatadah, As-Suddi: the path here is the right way. Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas: **they would not see the truth.**
- *If We willed, We could have transformed them where they stood* — destroyed them (Ibn Abbas), changed their form (As-Suddi), turned them to stone (Abu Salih), fixed them sitting on their feet (Al-Hasan, Qatadah) — **unable to go forward or back.**

The restraint is the point. He has not done it. They still have their eyes and their legs.

---

## 19. Long Life, Reversed (36:68)

> *And he to whom We grant long life, We reverse him in creation. Will they not then understand?*

The longer a man lives, the further back he travels: strong, then weak; capable and active, then not. Ibn Kathir reads it alongside 30:54 — *created you weak, then gave strength after weakness, then weakness and grey hair after strength* — and 22:5, *brought back to the most abject age, so that he knows nothing after having known.*

The conclusion he draws is not about ageing but about design: **this world is transient and was built to end.** So *will they not understand* — that they were made for another world that does not pass away, and from which there is no exit.

---

## 20. He Was Not Taught Poetry (36:69–70)

> *We have not taught him poetry, nor is it suitable for him.*

Ibn Kathir means this literally. The Prophet ﷺ did not know how to compose verse, had no liking for it and no natural inclination toward it — it is narrated that he never held a line of poetry in correct metre, but would transpose its words or recall it incompletely.

**Al-Bayhaqi's report in Ad-Dala'il** is the illustration. To Al-'Abbas bin Mirdas ؓ, the Prophet ﷺ quoted back his own line — *do you distribute my booty and the booty of the servants between Al-Aqra' and 'Uyainah?* — and Al-'Abbas corrected him: *it is 'Uyainah and Al-Aqra'.* He ﷺ said: **it is all the same.** The metre had gone; the meaning had not.

What he *was* taught is the Qur'an — *falsehood cannot reach it from before it or behind it* (41:42). Not poetry, as the ignorant among Quraysh claimed; not sorcery, fabrication or a spell, as others said. Ibn Kathir adds that he was both disinclined to verse by nature and forbidden it by Law.

> *That it may warn whoever is living, and that the Word may be justified against the disbelievers.*

**Whoever is living** — Qatadah: alive in heart and alive in insight. Ad-Dahhak: wise. The warning goes out to *whomsoever it reaches* (6:19), and those who reject it have their meeting place appointed (11:17). The same Book is **mercy to the believers and evidence against the disbelievers.**

---

## 21. The Cattle (36:71–73)

> *Do they not see that We created for them, of what Our Hands have fashioned, the cattle — and they are their owners?*

Qatadah: their masters. And Ibn Kathir dwells on *subdued* with genuine wonder: they are made submissive and do not resist. **A young child can come to a camel and make it kneel, or stand, and drive it — and a train of a hundred camels or more can be led by one small boy.**

From them: riding and the carrying of burdens to every region; meat when they choose to slaughter; wool, fur and hair for furnishings and comfort; and milk to drink — and their urine as medicine for those who need it.

*Will they not then be grateful?* — Ibn Kathir's gloss: will they not worship the One who created and subdued all this, instead of showing gratitude to others.

---

## 22. Gods Who Will Be Brought as a Troop (36:74–76)

> *They have taken gods besides Allah, hoping to be helped. They cannot help them — but they will be brought forward as a troop.*

The idols are too weak, too insignificant, too powerless. They cannot help their own worshippers, cannot help themselves, cannot take revenge on anyone who damages them — **they are inanimate; they neither hear nor understand.**

*Brought forward as a troop* — Mujahid: at the Reckoning, the idols gathered and made present while their worshippers are judged, **which adds to their grief and makes the proof against them more complete.** Qatadah and Al-Hasan read it the other way round, and Ibn Jarir preferred this: **the idolaters were the troop** — they got angry for the sake of their gods in this world, and could neither benefit them nor protect them, *for they were only idols.*

Then, to the Prophet ﷺ: *let not their speech grieve you.* Their denial and disbelief. *We know what they conceal and what they reveal* — and Ibn Kathir's expansion is the consolation: **nothing they did will be overlooked, great or small, on the Day when every deed of every life is laid open.**

---

## 23. The Crumbled Bone (36:77–79)

The occasion of revelation, reported two ways and both accepted:

- **Mujahid, Ikrimah, 'Urwah bin Az-Zubayr, As-Suddi, Qatadah**: Ubayy bin Khalaf came with a dry bone, crumbling it and scattering it in the air — *Muhammad, are you claiming Allah will resurrect this?* The reply: **yes — Allah will cause you to die, then resurrect you, then gather you into the Fire.**
- **Ibn Abi Hatim, from Ibn Abbas** ؓ (and Ibn Jarir from Sa'id bin Jubayr): the same with Al-'As bin Wa'il, who took a bone from a valley bed and crumbled it.

Ibn Kathir declines to adjudicate, and makes the more useful point: **the definite article in *Al-Insan* is generic.** Whoever it was revealed about, the verse addresses every person who denies resurrection.

> *Does man not see that We created him from a Nutfah? Yet he stands forth as an open opponent. And he puts forth a parable for Us and forgets his own creation, saying: who will give life to bones after they have rotted?*

The argument is turned back on him in one move. He was made from a despised drop (77:20–22), from a mingled fluid (76:2) — *something insignificant, weak and despised* — and **he forgets that.** He looks at dry bones and calls the greater thing impossible, while the greater thing has already happened to him.

> *Say: He will give them life who created them the first time, and He is the All-Knower of every creation.*

Ibn Kathir: He knows where every bone went — in every region of the earth, wherever it disintegrated and dispersed.

**And the story that settles it** (Ahmad, from Hudhayfah ؓ via Rib'i; many versions in the two Sahihs): a man near death told his family to burn his body until the flesh was gone and the bones were brittle, then grind them and scatter them — in one version, half on land and half at sea on a windy day. They did it. Allah commanded the sea to give back what it held, and the land likewise, and said **Be** — and he was a man, standing.

*Why did you do that?* — **Because I feared You.** And He forgave him.

---

## 24. Fire From the Green Tree (36:80)

> *He who produces fire for you out of the green tree, and behold, you kindle from it.*

Ibn Kathir's reading: the One who began this tree from water, brought it to green and fruiting, then dried it to wood that lights a fire — **He changes a thing into its opposite, and nothing prevents Him.** Qatadah: the One who brought fire out of this tree is able to resurrect him.

It is also a specific and literal sign. The reference is to the **Markh** and **'Afar** trees of the Hijaz (reported from Ibn Abbas ؓ): a traveller with no kindling takes two green branches, rubs them together, and **fire comes out of the living wood.** Life out of what looks dead; fire out of what is green and wet.

---

## 25. Be — And It Is (36:81–83)

> *Is not He who created the heavens and the earth able to create the like of them? Yes indeed — He is the All-Knowing Supreme Creator.*

The final argument is one of scale: seven heavens with their stars, seven earths with all that is in them. **The creation of the heavens and the earth is greater than the creation of mankind** (40:57). Ibn Jarir pairs it with 46:33 — *He was not wearied by their creation.*

> *His command, when He intends a thing, is only that He says to it: Be — and it is.*

Once. Not repeated, not confirmed, not reinforced.

**Ahmad, from Abu Dharr** ؓ, in the divine saying that puts the whole surah in a sentence:

> *O My servants, all of you are sinners except those I protect — so seek My forgiveness and I will forgive you. All of you are in need except those I make independent. I am Most Generous, Majestic, and I do whatever I will. My giving is a word and My punishment is a word. When I want a thing, I say to it "Be" — and it is.*

> *So glorified be He in whose Hand is the dominion of all things, and to Him you will be returned.*

*Malakut* and *Mulk* mean the same thing, Ibn Kathir concludes, against those who split them into physical and spiritual realms — and this is the view of the majority of the scholars of tafsir (compare 23:88, 67:1).

He ends the surah with two reports on the night prayer, both containing the same phrase:

- **Hudhayfah bin Al-Yaman** ؓ (Ahmad): the Prophet ﷺ prayed the seven long surahs in seven rak'ahs; his bowing was as long as his standing and his prostration as long as his bowing — *Glory be to the Owner of Malakut, might, pride and greatness* — **and by the end my legs were nearly broken.**
- **'Awf bin Malik Al-Ashja'i** ؓ (Abu Dawud; also At-Tirmidhi in Ash-Shama'il, and An-Nasa'i): he recited Al-Baqarah, and **passed no verse of mercy without stopping to ask for it, and no verse of punishment without stopping to seek refuge from it** — then bowed as long as he had stood, prostrated as long as he had bowed, then stood and recited Al 'Imran, and then one surah after another.

---

## The Thread

Ya Sin makes its case entirely from what is already in front of you.

A field that was dead last season. A sun that has kept an appointment for as long as anyone has looked up. A hundred camels led by a child. A ship that does not sink. Two wet green branches that give fire. **Every one of these is something impossible-sounding that happens anyway**, and the surah puts them in a row so that when the man crumbles the dry bone and asks *who could possibly bring this back*, the answer is already standing behind him.

The two stories are the same argument in human scale. Habib An-Najjar ran toward the messengers when his town was preparing to kill them, and the last thing he said, while being stoned, was a prayer for the people stoning him — *would that my people knew.* The man in the hadith of the sealed mouths spent his whole life defending himself and then heard his own thigh testify against him. One man had nothing to hide and wanted everyone to see; the other had everything to hide and was shown that hiding was never available.

And underneath both sits the verse Ibn Kathir spends the most space on, which is not about the Hereafter at all: **your traces are being written.** Not only the deeds, but the precedent you set, the knowledge you leave, the child who prays for you, the charity still running after you — and, quite literally, the footprints. *If Allah were going to overlook anything, He would have overlooked what the wind erases.*

*Stay where you are, and your footsteps will be recorded.*

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Ya Sin. All praise and thanks are due to Allah.*
