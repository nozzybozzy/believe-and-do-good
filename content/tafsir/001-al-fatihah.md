## 1. Its Names, and What Each One Teaches

A surah with many names is a surah of many dimensions. Ibn Kathir opens by listing them, because each name reveals a function:

| Name | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Al-Fatihah** | The Opener — of the Book, and of every prayer |
| **Umm al-Kitab / Umm al-Qur'an** | The Mother of the Book: the Qur'an begins with it, and it holds the meanings of the whole Qur'an in miniature. (Ibn Jarir: Arabs call anything comprehensive an *umm* — the membrane around the brain, the banner that gathers an army) |
| **As-Sab' al-Mathani** | The seven oft-repeated verses (Qur'an 15:87) |
| **Al-Hamd** | The Praise |
| **As-Salah** | The Prayer — because reciting it is a condition for the prayer's validity |
| **Ash-Shifa' / Ar-Ruqyah** | The Cure / the Remedy — from the incident where a Companion healed a poisoned tribal chief with it, and the Prophet ﷺ asked, *"How did you know it is a ruqyah?"* |

## 2. Its Virtues

- **The greatest surah in the Qur'an.** The Prophet ﷺ took Abu Sa'id bin al-Mu'alla by the hand and promised to teach him the greatest surah before they left the mosque — then taught him Al-Fatihah.
- **Unmatched in any prior revelation.** To Ubayy bin Ka'b: nothing like it was sent down in the Tawrah, Injil, Zabur, or the Furqan.
- **A light given to no prophet before.** Jibril heard a door open in heaven that had never opened; an angel descended with glad tidings of two lights — the Opening of the Book, and the closing verses of Al-Baqarah — every letter of which brings benefit.
- **Indispensable to prayer.** *"There is no prayer for whoever does not recite the Opening of the Book."* Required of the imam and of those behind him, in every rak'ah.

## 3. The Heart of It: A Conversation with Allah

The hadith Ibn Kathir returns to again and again — Allah says:

> *"I have divided the prayer between Myself and My servant into two halves, and My servant shall have what he asks for."*

And then, line by line:

- Servant: *All praise be to Allah, Lord of all that exists* → **"My servant has praised Me."**
- Servant: *The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful* → **"My servant has glorified Me."**
- Servant: *Owner of the Day of Recompense* → **"My servant has related all matters to Me."**
- Servant: *You alone we worship, You alone we ask for help* → **"This is between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he sought."**
- Servant: *Guide us to the straight path…* → **"This is for My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked."**

This is the structural key to the surah: **three and a half verses of praise, three and a half of petition** — and Ibn Kathir notes the order is deliberate. You praise the One you are about to ask, *before* you ask. It is the most fitting and effective way to seek help.

## 4. Before Reciting: Isti'adhah and Basmalah

**Seeking refuge** (*A'udhu billahi min ash-shaytan ir-rajim*) is commanded before recitation (16:98). Only three passages in the whole Qur'an carry this theme (7:199–200, 23:96–98, 41:34–36), and in each, the pattern is the same: **be gentle with the human enemy** — kindness may soften him into an ally — but **seek refuge from the satanic enemy**, because he takes no bribe and returns no kindness. He is pure enmity, and only the One who created him can repel him.

- *Shaytan* — from *shatana*, "to be far": far from every good. (Or from *shata*, "burned," since he was made of fire.)
- *Rajim* — the outcast, driven away from all righteousness; or the one who pelts, since he hurls doubts into hearts.
- **Ruling:** recommended, not obligatory, per the majority; 'Ata' bin Abi Rabah held it required.
- **Also said when angry** — the Prophet ﷺ told a man red-faced with rage that these words would dissolve what he felt.

**Bismillah.** The scholars differ sharply on whether it is an ayah of Al-Fatihah (Shafi'i: yes, recited aloud; Malik and Abu Hanifah: not an ayah) — and Ibn Kathir's verdict is generous: *all these imams agree the prayer is valid either way.* What is agreed is the *barakah*: say "Cursed Shaytan" and he swells to the size of a house; say "Bismillah" and he shrinks to a fly. Hence it is recommended before every deed — ablution, eating, a sermon, intimacy, entering a place of need.

## 5. Verse by Verse

**1:1 — In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.**
*Allah* — the Name belonging to Him alone, described by all His other attributes. *Ar-Rahman* and *Ar-Rahim* both derive from *rahmah*, but Ar-Rahman is broader and exclusive to Allah — mercy encompassing all creation in both lives; Ar-Rahim is particular, and He has described others by it (the Prophet ﷺ is called *ra'uf, rahim* in 9:128). Allah said: *"I am Ar-Rahman. I created the womb (raham) and named it from My Name: whoever keeps it, I keep ties with him; whoever severs it, I sever ties with him."*

**1:2 — All praise and thanks be to Allah, Lord of all that exists.**
The *alif-lam* on *al-Hamd* gathers **every** kind of praise to Allah alone. Ibn Kathir draws a fine distinction: **hamd** is praise for what someone *is* as well as what he does; **shukr** is thanks only for what was done. *Rabb* = owner, master, the one with authority to lead — used absolutely, it belongs only to Allah. *'Alamin* = every category of created thing besides Allah, each generation a world of its own; from *'alamah*, a **sign** — because all creation is a sign pointing to its Creator and His Oneness.

**1:3 — The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.**
Repeated here, after *Rabb*, for a reason: **"Rabb" carries a warning; "Ar-Rahman Ar-Rahim" carries encouragement.** Fear and hope, held in balance — as in *"I am the Oft-Forgiving… and My torment is the most painful torment"* (15:49–50).

**1:4 — The Owner of the Day of Recompense.**
His ownership is total, but *that* Day is singled out because on it no one else will claim ownership of anything, and none will speak without His permission. *Din* = the reckoning and requital. 'Umar's counsel follows: *hold yourself accountable before you are held accountable; weigh yourself before you are weighed.*

**1:5 — You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help.**
The pivot of the surah — and the grammar carries the theology. The speech shifts from the third person to **direct address**: having praised Him, the servant now stands before Him. Placing the object *Iyyaka* first means **"You and no other."** *'Ibadah* linguistically means to be made humble and level (a paved road is *mu'abbadah*); religiously it is the union of **utmost love, humility, and fear.** The first half disowns *shirk*; the second disowns any claim to power or strength of one's own. Worship is named before help because worship is the goal and help is the means. Some of the Salaf said: **Al-Fatihah is the secret of the Qur'an, and this verse is the secret of Al-Fatihah.**

**1:6 — Guide us to the straight path.**
*Why ask for guidance if you already have it?* Because the servant needs Allah every hour to remain firm, to be made **more** firm, and to be kept from drifting — he has no power to benefit or harm himself. This is why believers are also told *"Believe in Allah"* though they already believe (4:136), and why they pray *"Our Lord, let not our hearts deviate after You have guided us"* (3:8). The Prophet's ﷺ parable: the path runs between two walls (Allah's limits) lined with curtained doors (the forbidden); a caller at the gate is **the Book of Allah**, and a caller above is **the admonisher in every Muslim's heart.**

**1:7 — The path of those You have blessed — not of those who earned anger, nor of those who went astray.**
The blessed are named in 4:69: **the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous.** The two failures are the two ways guidance is lost: **knowledge without action** (which earns anger) and **zeal without knowledge** (which ends in wandering). Ibn Kathir, following the hadith of 'Adi bin Hatim, identifies these respectively with the Jews and the Christians — while noting both blame is shared and it is the *dominant* trait of each that is meant. The believer's path is to **know the truth and act on it.**

Note the adab of the wording: the blessing is attributed openly to Allah — *"You have bestowed"* — while the anger is left without a named subject, and going astray is related to those who went astray, though nothing occurs outside His decree. Ibn Kathir affirms firmly that guidance and misguidance are Allah's alone (18:17, 7:186), against the Qadariyyah.

## 6. Amin

Not part of the surah, but strongly recommended after it — meaning **"O Allah, answer our invocation."** The Prophet ﷺ said it aloud, drawing out the word, until the mosque shook with the voices behind him. *"When the imam says Amin, say Amin — for whoever's Amin coincides with the angels', his past sins are forgiven."*

---

## 7. In One Breath

Al-Fatihah praises Allah by His most beautiful Names, affirms the Hereafter, teaches the servant to direct all worship and all reliance to Him alone, asks for the one thing everything else depends on — guidance, and firmness upon it — and asks to be kept from the two roads that lose it. It ends by placing the one who prays in the company he hopes to be raised with: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous.
