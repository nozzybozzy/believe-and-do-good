*Revealed in Makkah · 20 ayat · "The City" — Makkah, sworn by in the opening verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Balad makes one claim about the human condition and then gives one instruction, and the two are connected more tightly than they first appear.

The claim: **you were created in *kabad*** — hardship, strain, toil. Not that life contains hardship, but that man was made inside it. Ibn Abbas points at the sheer physical labour of simply becoming a person: the birth, the teeth pushing through. Mujahid runs it from the drop of fluid onward and quotes the verse about a mother bearing a child with hardship and delivering him with hardship (46:15) — then adds that she nurses him with hardship, and that his living is hardship too. **So he endures all of this.**

Then the surah watches what a man does with that. He accumulates. He boasts about what he has burned through — *I have wasted wealth in abundance!* — and underneath the boast are two assumptions the surah names and demolishes: **that nobody can overpower him**, and **that nobody is watching**. Against these it sets four gifts he did not give himself: two eyes, a tongue, two lips, and the knowledge of the two roads.

And then the instruction, which arrives as an accusation of something *not* done: **he has not attempted the steep path.** *Al-'Aqabah* — the hard uphill climb. The surah then tells you exactly what that climb consists of, and this is where it turns: **freeing a slave, and feeding the hungry — an orphan who is your relative, or a destitute man in the dirt.**

Read together, the logic is unmistakable. Man is created inside hardship; the only road out of it goes uphill; and the uphill road is **relieving somebody else's hardship**. That is the whole surah.

---

## 1. The Oath by a City, Sworn to Its Own Resident (90:1–2)

> *Nay! I swear by this city; And you are free in this city.*

Ibn Kathir: this is Makkah, **the Mother of the Towns** — and the oath is addressed to the one living in it. Khusayf reports from Mujahid that the opening *"La"* (Nay) is a refutation aimed at Quraish before the oath begins. Shabib bin Bishr, from Ikrimah, from Ibn Abbas: the city is Makkah.

The second verse is the striking one. Ibn Abbas reads it as spoken to the Prophet ﷺ: **"O Muhammad! It is permissible for you to fight in it."** The same is reported from Sa'id bin Jubayr, Abu Salih, 'Atiyah, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, As-Suddi and Ibn Zayd. Al-Hasan Al-Basri puts a boundary on it: Allah made it lawful for him **for one hour of a day**.

The agreed-upon hadith spells out both the permission and its expiry:

> *Verily, Allah made this city sacred on the Day that He created the heavens and the earth. Therefore, it is sacred by the sanctity of Allah until the Day of Judgement. Its trees should not be uprooted, and its bushes and grasses should not be removed. And it was only made lawful for me for one hour of a day. Today its sanctity has been restored just as it was sacred yesterday. So let the one who is present inform those who are absent.*

And in another wording, closing the loophole in advance:

> *So, if anyone tries to use the fighting of the Messenger of Allah as an excuse, then tell him that Allah permitted it for His Messenger and He has not permitted it for you.*

**A permission granted once, for one hour, to one man — and pre-emptively denied to everyone who would later cite it.**

## 2. By the Begetter and What He Begot (90:3)

> *And by the begetter and that which he begot.*

Mujahid, Abu Salih, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Sufyan Ath-Thawri, Sa'id bin Jubayr, As-Suddi, Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Khusayf, Shurahbil bin Sa'd and others: **Adam, and his children.**

Ibn Kathir calls this view *good and strong*, and gives the reason from the structure of the oath itself: Allah swears by the Mother of the Towns — **the dwelling** — and then immediately swears by **the dwellers in it**, the father of mankind and his offspring.

Two other readings are preserved: Abu 'Imran Al-Jawni took it as Ibrahim and his progeny (recorded by Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim), while **Ibn Jarir preferred a general reading** — every father and every child. Ibn Kathir accepts that as acceptable too.

---

## 3. Created in Kabad (90:4)

> *Verily, We have created man in Kabad.*

The commentators approach the word from different angles and arrive in the same place:

- **Ibn Abbas** (via 'Ata): he was created while in hardship — *"Don't you see him?"* — then mentions his birth and the sprouting of his teeth.
- **Mujahid:** a drop, then a clot, then a lump of flesh, **enduring in his creation**; and the mother bears him with hardship, delivers him with hardship, nurses him with hardship, and his livelihood is hardship.
- **Sa'id bin Jubayr:** in hardship and seeking a livelihood.
- **Ikrimah:** in hardship and long-suffering.
- **Qatadah:** in difficulty.
- **Al-Hasan:** enduring the hardships of this world by living, **and the severity of the Hereafter.**

Al-Hasan's is the widest: the strain does not end at death for the one who has not prepared.

---

## 4. The Two Assumptions Underneath the Boast (90:5–7)

> *Does he think that none can overcome him? He says: "I have wasted wealth in abundance!" Does he think that none sees him?*

**"None can overcome him."** Al-Hasan Al-Basri: he thinks **no one is able to take his wealth from him**. Qatadah sharpens it to the accounting: the son of Adam supposes he will **not be asked about this wealth — how he earned it and how he spent it**.

**The boast.** Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi: he is saying *I spent an abundance of wealth* — and the Qur'an's own verb for it is **wasted**. The bragging and the squandering are the same act.

**"None sees him."** Mujahid: does he imagine that **Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, does not see him**? Others among the Salaf said the same.

Wealth misread as invulnerability, and privacy misread as unobservedness. The next three verses answer both by listing what he was given.

---

## 5. Two Eyes, a Tongue, Two Lips, Two Roads (90:8–10)

> *Have We not made for him two eyes, and a tongue and two lips, and shown him the two ways?*

- **Two eyes** — to see with.
- **A tongue** — to speak with, and **so that he can express what is inside him**.
- **Two lips** — Ibn Kathir's gloss is unusually tender and practical: to help him speak, to help him eat, and **to beautify his face and his mouth**.
- **The two ways** — the two paths. Sufyan Ath-Thawri ← 'Asim ← Zirr ← **Abdullah bin Mas'ud**: **"The good and the evil."** Reported likewise from 'Ali, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Abu Wa'il, Abu Salih, Muhammad bin Ka'b, Ad-Dahhak and 'Ata' Al-Khurasani.

Ibn Kathir sets it beside 76:2–3 — *We created man from a mixed drop, in order to try him: so We made him hearer and seer. Verily, We showed him the way, whether he be grateful or ungrateful.* **The faculties and the fork in the road are given together.** Knowing which way is which is itself a blessing, and it is also what removes the excuse.

---

## 6. The Steep Path He Did Not Climb (90:11–13)

> *But he has not attempted to pass on the path that is steep. And what will make you know the path that is steep? Freeing a neck...*

Ibn Zayd: this means — **will he not traverse the path which contains salvation and good?** And then, characteristically, the Qur'an does not leave the metaphor to the reader. It asks *what will make you know?* and answers itself.

**The first step of the climb is freeing a human being.**

**Imam Ahmad** ← Sa'id bin Marjanah ← **Abu Hurayrah**, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> *Whoever frees a believing slave, Allah will free for every limb of the slave one of his limbs from the Fire — to such an extent that He will free a hand for a hand, a leg for a leg, and a private part for a private part.*

'Ali bin Al-Husayn asked Sa'id whether he had heard this from Abu Hurayrah himself. Sa'id said yes. **'Ali bin Al-Husayn then called for the swiftest servant he owned and said: "Go — for you are free for the Face of Allah."** Recorded by Al-Bukhari, Muslim, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i.

**Ahmad** ← 'Amr bin 'Abasah, from the Prophet ﷺ:

> *Whoever builds a Masjid so that Allah may be remembered in it, Allah will build a house for him in Paradise; whoever frees a Muslim person, it will be his ransom from Hell; and whoever grows grey in Islam, it will be a light for him on the Day of Judgement.*

And a fuller version through Abu Umamah, from 'Amr bin 'Abasah — asked for a hadith with no deficiency or mistakes in it:

- Whoever has **three children born to him in Islam who die before puberty**, Allah will enter him into Paradise by virtue of His mercy to them.
- Whoever **grows grey in the way of Allah**, it will be a light for him on the Day of Judgement.
- Whoever **shoots an arrow in the way of Allah that reaches the enemy** — hit or miss — receives the reward of freeing a slave.
- Whoever **frees a believing slave**, Allah frees each of his limbs from the Fire for every limb of the slave.
- Whoever **equips two riding animals in the way of Allah** — Paradise has eight gates, and Allah will let him enter by whichever he chooses.

Ibn Kathir notes these come by **different routes of transmission that are good and strong.**

---

## 7. Feeding on a Day of Hunger (90:14–16)

> *Or giving food in a day full of Masghabah, to an orphan near of kin, or to a Miskin cleaving to dust.*

**Masghabah** — Ibn Abbas: *of hunger*. 'Ikrimah, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah and others said the same; the root *saghb* means hunger. **Not surplus food on an ordinary day — food given on a day when food is scarce.**

**"An orphan near of kin"** — one who is related to him (Ibn Abbas, 'Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi). Ibn Kathir brings the hadith of **Salman bin 'Amir**, recorded by Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i with an authentic chain:

> *Charity given to a poor person is one charity; given to a relative it counts as two — **charity, and connecting the ties of kinship**.*

**"A Miskin cleaving to dust"** (*Dha Matrabah*) — poor, miserable, clinging to the dirt; those in a state of destitution. Ibn Abbas gives the picture exactly: **one who is dejected in the street and has no house or anything else to protect him from the dust.**

---

## 8. "Then He Became One of Those Who Believed" (90:17)

> *Then he became one of those who believed, and recommended one another to patience, and recommended one another to compassion.*

The word order matters, and Ibn Kathir addresses it directly. The freeing and the feeding are named first, and only then the faith — the sense being that **along with these beautiful and pure characteristics, he was a believer in his heart, seeking the reward from Allah.** The deeds are not a substitute for belief; they are what belief looks like when it is real, and they are only worth anything with it.

He supports this with two verses:

> *And whoever desires the Hereafter and strives for it with the necessary effort due for it **while he is a believer** — such are the ones whose striving shall be appreciated.* (17:19)

> *Whoever works righteousness, male or female, **while being a true believer**...* (16:97)

**Patience and compassion**, in Ibn Kathir's reading, are social and specific: to be **patient with the harm people cause**, and to be **merciful with them**. The hadiths:

> *The merciful are shown mercy by the Most Merciful. Be merciful to those on the earth, and He Who is above the heavens will be merciful to you.*

> *Allah will not be merciful to whoever is not merciful to the people.*

And Abu Dawud, from Abdullah bin 'Amr:

> *Whoever does not show mercy to our young, nor recognise the right of our elders, is not of us.*

---

## 9. Right Hand, Left Hand, and a Sealed Door (90:18–20)

> *They are those on the Right. But those who disbelieved in Our Ayat, they are those on the Left. Upon them Fire will be Mu'sadah.*

Those who have these characteristics are **the Companions of the Right Hand**. Those who rejected the signs are the Companions of the Left.

**Mu'sadah** — Abu Hurayrah, Ibn Abbas, 'Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Mujahid, Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi, 'Atiyah Al-'Awfi, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and As-Suddi all give the same one-word gloss: **"shut."**

- **Ibn Abbas:** its doors will be closed.
- **Ad-Dahhak:** it will be sealed over them, and it will have no door.
- **Qatadah:** it will be shut — **no light in it, no crevice, and no way out of it, ever.**

Ibn Kathir's own summary: sealed over them, with no way to avoid it and no way out.

The surah that opened with a man wondering whether anyone could overpower him ends with a door that has no handle on the inside.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Everyone is born into hardship. The question is only whose hardship you spend your life on.** *Kabad* is the given condition — the birth, the teeth, the earning, the enduring. The surah does not offer an escape from it. It offers a climb.

**The steep path is defined entirely in terms of other people.** Not fasting, not night prayer, not withdrawal. **A neck freed. A hungry man fed on a hungry day — preferably an orphan who is your own relative, or someone with nothing between him and the dirt.** Ibn Kathir keeps the definition as concrete as the Qur'an left it.

**Three things worth carrying:**

- **The two things a wealthy man is most likely to get wrong are named in consecutive verses:** that nobody can overpower him, and that nobody is watching. Qatadah's version of the first is the practical one — he assumes **he will never be asked how he earned it and how he spent it.**
- **Faith and mercy are stated as one package.** The believer of 90:17 is not merely a believer; he counsels patience with people's harm and mercy toward them. And the warning attached is total: *Allah will not be merciful to whoever is not merciful to the people.*
- **Giving to a relative in need pays twice** — as charity, and as *silat ar-rahim*. The Qur'an's own example of the ideal recipient is family.

And the detail that quietly reframes the whole surah: **two lips are listed among the blessings** — for speech, for eating, and for the beauty of a person's face. The God who is about to demand the steep climb of you first took the trouble to make your mouth look right.
