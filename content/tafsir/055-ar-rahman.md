*Revealed in Makkah · 78 ayat · named for the divine attribute that is its first word — the only surah in the Qur'an to open with a name of Allah standing alone*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ar-Rahman is an **inventory of favours, addressed to two species at once**. The refrain that runs through it thirty-one times — *Then which of the blessings of your Lord will you both deny?* — is in the dual, and Ibn Kathir is emphatic about who the two are: **mankind and the Jinn**. Nearly every interpretive decision in his commentary flows from that fact.

The surah's method is simply to walk through creation and name what has been given: the Qur'an, speech, the sun and moon on their calculated courses, the balance, the earth laid flat, fruit and grain, the two seas and the pearls in them, the ships. Then it names what is coming: the sky splitting, the criminals dragged by their forelocks, and — at far greater length — four gardens, described in matched pairs, one pair ranked above the other.

What holds it together is a single argument Ibn Kathir states plainly at 55:13: these are favours **you are standing inside of**. You cannot deny them because you are surrounded by them. The only honest answer to the refrain is the one the Jinn gave and the Quraysh did not.

And note the two poles the surah is strung between: it opens with **Ar-Rahman** — mercy — and closes with **Dhul-Jalal wal-Ikram** — majesty and honour. The same name closes the middle of the surah too, at 55:27, when everything else has perished.

---

## 1. The Night the Jinn Answered Better (Introduction)

Ibn Kathir opens not with the verses but with an embarrassment.

Tirmidhi records from **Jabir**: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ went out to his Companions and recited Ar-Rahman to them from beginning to end. *They said nothing.* He then told them:

> *I recited it to the Jinns, on the night of the Jinns, and their response was better than yours! Whenever I recited "Then which of the blessings of your Lord will you both deny", they said: "None of Your favours do we deny, our Lord! All praise is due to You."*

Tirmidhi grades this *Gharib*; Al-Bazzar also collected it. Ibn Jarir preserves a second version from **Abdullah bin Umar**, where the Prophet ﷺ asks: *Why do I hear the Jinn giving a better response to their Lord than you?*

This sets the terms for everything that follows. **The refrain is a question, and it expects an answer.** The surah is not designed to be read silently.

Ibn Kathir also notes the placement dispute: **Ibn Mas'ud considered Ar-Rahman the beginning of the Mufassal** section — the point at issue in the report about a man who claimed to recite the whole Mufassal in a single *rak'ah*, and was rebuked for reciting the Qur'an in haste, *as if it is poetry*.

---

## 2. Mercy, Then the Qur'an, Then Man (55:1–4)

> *Ar-Rahman! He has taught the Qur'an. He created man. He taught him Al-Bayan.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as Allah informing us of His favour and mercy for His creatures: He revealed the Qur'an to His servants and **made memorising and understanding it easy** for those on whom He has bestowed mercy.

**Al-Bayan**, per Al-Hasan, is *eloquent speech*. Ibn Kathir's gloss is unusually physical: this refers to Allah teaching the servants how to recite by making it easy for them to speak and to **pronounce letters with the various parts of the mouth — the alveolar bridge, the tongue and the lips**.

Worth pausing on the order of the four lines. Mercy is named first. The teaching of the Qur'an is mentioned *before* the creation of man. Then man. Then the faculty that lets him receive it.

---

## 3. Everything Running on Calculation (55:5–6)

> *The sun and the moon on fixed courses.*

**Husban** — precise calculation. They move in their orbits in perfect succession, *never delayed nor disturbed*. Ibn Kathir brings 36:40 (*It is not for the sun to overtake the moon, nor does the night outstrip the day*) and 6:96 (*the sun and the moon for calculating*).

> *And the Najm and the trees prostrating.*

Here the commentators split, and Ibn Kathir sides against Ibn Jarir:

| Reading | Held by |
|---|---|
| **An-Najm = plants that lie on the ground** (stemless vegetation), against trees that stand on trunks | Ibn Abbas (via Ali bin Abi Talhah), Sa'id bin Jubayr, As-Suddi, Sufyan Ath-Thawri — **Ibn Jarir's preference** |
| **An-Najm = the star in the sky** | Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah |

Ibn Kathir: *"This is the saying that is the most obvious, and Allah knows best"* — the star — and his evidence is 22:18, which lists together the sun, the moon, **the stars**, the mountains and the trees among those prostrating to Allah.

---

## 4. The Balance, Three Times Over (55:7–9)

This is the surah's most compact piece of moral architecture. The word *mizan* appears three times in three verses, and Ibn Kathir reads it at descending scale:

1. **Cosmic** — *the heaven He raised, and He set up the balance*: meaning **justice**, as in 57:25, where the Messengers were sent with the Book and the balance *that mankind may keep up equity*.
2. **Existential** — *in order that you may not transgress the balance*: He created the heavens and earth in justice and truth, so that everything is founded on and observing justice and truth.
3. **Commercial** — *observe the weight with equity and do not make the balance deficient*: **do not cheat in weights and measures** (26:182).

The move from the architecture of the sky to a shopkeeper's scales happens in the space of two lines, and the same word carries both.

---

## 5. The Earth Laid Out (55:10–13)

The earth *put down for Al-An'am* — which **Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah and Ibn Zayd** all take to mean **the creatures** generally, not just livestock. Allah raised the heaven and laid the earth, **balanced with firm mountains** so it would be stable for its residents: creatures *different in species, shape, colour and language*.

Then the produce:

- **Fruits** of various colours, tastes and scents.
- **Date palms with Akmam** — Ibn Jurayj from Ibn Abbas: *sheathed fruit stalks*, the sheaths in which the seeds grow into a cluster of dates. Ibn Kathir notes the date tree is singled out **because of its benefit both fresh and dry**.
- **Corn with 'Asf and Rayhan** — '*Asf* is straw (Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Abu Malik); Al-Awfi's version from Ibn Abbas has it as green leaves cut from the stem, called '*Asf* once dried. *Rayhan* is leaves (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid), or sweet-scented plants (Al-Hasan). Ibn Kathir's synthesis: **the crops that produce straw — wheat and barley — and the leaves that grow on their stems.**

Then the first refrain, and Ibn Kathir's decisive gloss:

> *"O mankind and Jinn, which of the favours that Allah has given to you do you deny?"*

His reasoning is that these are **favours obvious to you while you are surrounded by them** — you cannot deny or reject them. Hence the only right answer is the Jinn's: *"O Allah! None of Your favours do we deny. All praise is due to You."* Ibn Abbas used to say simply: **"Nay, our Lord!"**

---

## 6. Clay and Smokeless Fire (55:14–16)

> *He created man from sounding clay like the clay of pottery. And the Jinn He created from a smokeless flame of fire.*

Man from clay of the kind used in pottery; the Jinn from **the tip of the flame of a smokeless fire** (Ad-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas; also Ikrimah, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Ibn Zayd). Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas: *from the best part of the fire, from its smokeless flame.*

The hadith of **A'ishah** (Ahmad, and Muslim) settles the three-part taxonomy:

> *The angels were created from light, the Jinn from a smokeless flame of fire, and Adam from what was described to you.*

---

## 7. Two Easts, Two Wests (55:17–18)

The **two easts and two wests** are the sunrise and sunset of *summer and winter* — the seasonal drift of the sun's rising and setting points. Ibn Kathir cross-reads this with the plural of 70:40 (*Lord of all the points of sunrise and sunset*) and the singular of 73:9 (*The Lord of the east and the west*), and the point of the variation is the **benefits it brings to the created — mankind and Jinn** alike.

---

## 8. The Two Seas, the Pearls, the Ships (55:19–25)

> *He has Maraja the two seas meeting together. Between them is a barrier which none of them can transgress.*

**Maraja** — *let them loose*, per Ibn Abbas. The two are **fresh water and salt water**, the former coming from running rivers; the barrier is a partition of land placed between them **so that neither spoils the characteristics the other was created with**. Ibn Zayd reads *meeting together* as itself constrained: He prevents them from meeting by the dividing barrier. The parallel is 25:53.

> *Out of them both come out pearls and Al-Marjan.*

*Marjan* is either **small pearls** (Mujahid, Qatadah, Abu Ruzayn, Ad-Dahhak, and reported from Ali) or **large precious pearls** (Ibn Jarir, from some of the Salaf).

Ibn Kathir preserves a report from **Ibn Abbas** whose chain he calls *Sahih*:

> *"When it rains, the oysters in the sea open their mouths. What falls in them, the drops, turns into pearls."*

> *And His are Al-Jawar Al-Munsha'at, in the seas like A'lam.*

**Ships.** Mujahid draws the line at the sail: *whatever ship hoists a sail is from the Munsha'at; if it does not, it is not.* Qatadah reads *Munsha'at* as **created**; others read *Munshi'at* as **launched**. *A'lam* means they are **like mountains** in size — and Ibn Kathir immediately turns from the image to the economics: trade and commercial services, cargo moved from province to province, goods people need.

---

## 9. Everything Perishes; One Face Remains (55:26–30)

> *Whatsoever is on it will perish. And the Face of your Lord, Dhul-Jalal wal-Ikram, will remain forever.*

All the residents of the earth will die; the residents of the heavens will die, except whom Allah wills. **Only Allah's Honourable Face will remain**, because our Lord is the Ever Living Who never dies. Qatadah's observation is characteristically blunt: *"First, Allah mentioned His creatures and then He said that all of this will perish."*

**Ash-Sha'bi gives a rule for reciters** that is really a rule for the heart:

> *"When you have recited 'Whatsoever is on it will perish' — do not stop. Continue reading: 'And the Face of your Lord Dhul-Jalal wal-Ikram will remain forever.'"*

Compare 28:88 (*Everything will perish save His Face*).

On **Dhul-Jalal wal-Ikram**, Ibn Kathir's definition is a pair of obligations: He is *Worthy of being revered, and thus never defied; and obeyed, and thus never disobeyed.* Ibn Abbas: **Owner of greatness and pride.** The related verses are those about seeking His Face — 18:28 (*keep yourself patiently with those who call on their Lord morning and afternoon, seeking His Face*) and 76:9 (*We feed you seeking Allah's Face only*).

Ibn Kathir also records the supplication in this context, whose last line is the sharpest:

> *"O You the Ever Living Who sustains all that exists… We seek refuge with Your mercy. Grant us success in all of our matters. **Please, do not abandon us to rely on ourselves even for an instant**, nor on any of Your creation."*

> *Whosoever is in the heavens and on the earth begs of Him. Every day He is in some affair.*

Allah is **Rich, free of all want**; every creature stands in need of Him in all conditions, seeking His help *willingly or unwillingly*. And what are these daily affairs? Mujahid, from 'Ubayd bin 'Umayr:

> *"Of His affairs is that He answers the supplicant, or gives to the one requesting, or removes adversity, or cures the one seeking to be cured."*

---

## 10. Nowhere to Go (55:31–36)

> *We shall attend to you, O Thaqalan!*

Ibn Jurayj: *we shall judge you*. Al-Bukhari: *we shall recompense you* — adding that **nothing occupies Allah from attending to anything else**; the phrase is an Arabic idiom, as when a person says *"I will attend to you"* though he is busy with nothing at all. **Ath-Thaqalan** are the humans and the Jinn, confirmed by the hadith that everyone hears the *adhan* **except the Thaqalayn**, explained in another narration as *except mankind and the Jinn*.

> *O assembly of Jinn and men! If you are able to pass beyond the zones of the heavens and the earth, then pass beyond them! But you will never be able to pass them, except with authority.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as a challenge that cannot be met: **you will never escape Allah's orders and decrees, because they encompass you.** You are surrounded by His rule wherever you may be. He also applies it to the Gathering itself, where the angels — **seven lines deep in every direction** — will surround the creatures, and none will escape. *Except with authority* means: except with a command from Allah. The supporting verses are 75:10–12 (*Where is the refuge to flee? No! There is no refuge!*) and 10:27.

> *There will be sent against you both, Shuwaz of fire and Nuhas, and you will not be able to defend yourselves.*

- **Shuwaz** — the flame of fire (Ibn Abbas); *the flame above the fire and below the smoke* (Abu Salih); *a flood of fire* (Ad-Dahhak).
- **Nuhas** — the smoke of the fire (Ibn Abbas, Abu Salih, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Abu Sinan); or **molten brass poured over their heads** (Mujahid, Qatadah); *liquid copper* (Ad-Dahhak). Ibn Jarir notes the Arabs called smoke *Nuhas* and *Nihas*, but the reciters read *Nuhas* here.

The picture Ibn Kathir assembles: those who try to flee on the Day of Resurrection are **driven back by the angels** with smoke, fire and molten brass.

---

## 11. The Sky Turns Red (55:37–45)

> *Then when the heaven is rent asunder, and it becomes Wardah like Dihan.*

They will **melt as sediment and silver melt when heated**, and be **coloured as dyes stain** — sometimes red, sometimes yellow, blue or green. As-Suddi: *rosy in colour, and like filthy oil.* Mujahid on *Dihan*: *like the colours of dyes.* Ibn Kathir's point is the scale of the horror. Parallels: 69:16, 25:25, 84:1–2.

> *On that Day he will not be questioned about his sin, neither human nor Jinn.*

Ibn Kathir resolves the apparent contradiction with 15:92–93 (*by your Lord, We shall certainly call all of them to account*) by **staging** it: this describes a particular moment (compare 77:35–36, *a Day when they shall not speak*), after which all creatures **will** be questioned. Qatadah's version: *they will be questioned, then their mouths will be sealed, and their hands and feet will reveal what they used to do.*

> *The criminals will be known by their marks, and they will be seized by their foreheads and feet.*

Al-Hasan and Qatadah: **known by their dark faces and their blue eyes**. Ibn Kathir then adds his own contrast — *"I say"* — that this is the mirror image of the believers' mark: **the light that will appear on the limbs they used to wash in wudu.**

The seizure is brutal: the angels of punishment bend their heads down to their feet and throw them so into the Fire. Al-A'mash from Ibn Abbas: *"He will be taken by his forehead and his feet and be broken just as a stick is broken to be thrown into an oven."*

> *This is the Hell which the criminals denied. They will go between it and Hamim An!*

Alternating punishments: fire, then **Hamim** — a drink like molten copper that tears the intestines and internal organs (40:71–72). On *An*: **at the ultimate temperature, boiling fiercely** (Ibn Abbas, and after him Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak, Al-Hasan, Ath-Thawri, As-Suddi). Qatadah: *"Its boiling started when Allah created the heavens and the earth."* Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi describes the servant stirred in that water until the flesh melts and only bones and eyes remain — though he also transmits a second reading, *prepared*, which Ibn Kathir shows is compatible (compare 88:5 and 33:53).

**And then the passage's most important sentence.** After all of this, Ibn Kathir explains why a description of torment is followed by *Then which of the blessings of your Lord will you both deny?* —

> Punishing the disobedient, as well as favouring those who had Taqwa, is **from Allah's grace, mercy, justice, compassion and kindness** for His creatures. His warnings should encourage all creatures to abandon the Shirk and disobedience they engage in — *and this is why Allah reminded them of this favour.*

**The warning is itself counted among the blessings.**

## 12. The First Two Gardens (55:46–61)

> *But for him who fears the standing before his Lord, there will be two Gardens.*

The one who **fears standing before Allah** on the Day of Resurrection and *restrained himself from the desires* (79:40) — who does not indulge or prefer this worldly life, who knows the Hereafter is better and more lasting.

Al-Bukhari records from **Abdullah bin Qays** the hadith that governs the whole second half of the surah:

> *There are two gardens made of silver — their vessels and all that they contain. And there are two gardens made of gold — their vessels and all that they contain. And nothing stands between the people in the 'Adn Garden and looking at their Lord but **the covering of pride before His Face**.*

Ibn Kathir then makes the point he will repeat: **this verse is general and applies to humans and Jinn alike** — proof that believing, God-fearing Jinn enter Paradise. The whole surah is addressed to the Thaqalayn, so its promises are too.

The description, item by item:

- **Afnan (55:48)** — trees with beautiful young branches producing every kind of ripened fruit; Ata' Al-Khurasani: **spreading branches that reach the branches of other trees**.
- **Two springs flowing (55:50)** — Al-Hasan Al-Basri names them **Tasnim** and **As-Salsabil**. Atiyah: one is flowing water, the other wine that delights those who drink it.
- **Every fruit in pairs (55:52)** — every type they knew before, and better, and types they did not know. *Delights that no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no heart has ever imagined.* Ibn Abbas: **there is no fruit in this life, sweet or bitter, that does not exist in Paradise — even the colocynth.** And his sharper formulation: *"There is nothing in the world that is in the Hereafter except in name"* — the shared word conceals an enormous difference in enjoyment and value.
- **Couches lined with Istabraq (55:54)** — thick silk brocade (Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah); Abu Imran Al-Jawni: *thick silk embroidered with gold*. The rhetorical logic is inference from the lining outward, and Ibn Mas'ud states it: **"This is their interior — so what about it if you saw their exterior?"**
- **Fruits near at hand** — descending from their branches to whoever wants them, reclining or otherwise (69:23, 76:14).
- **Qasirat At-Tarf (55:56)** — chaste wives **restraining their glances**, desiring none but their husbands, seeing them as the most beautiful men in Paradise (Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Ata' Al-Khurasani, Ibn Zayd). One will say to her husband: *"By Allah! I neither see anything in Paradise more handsome than you nor more beloved to me than you. So praise be to Allah Who made you for me and made me for you."*
- **Untouched before by human or Jinn** — Ibn Kathir takes this as a **second proof that believing Jinn enter Paradise**. Artat bin Al-Mundhir reports that Damrah bin Habib, asked whether the Jinn enter Paradise, said: *"Yes, and they will get married. The Jinn will have Jinn women and the humans will have female humans."*
- **Like Yaqut and Marjan (55:58)** — *pure as rubies and white as Marjan* (Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Ibn Zayd).

Two hadiths are placed here. **Muslim**, via Muhammad bin Sirin, on a dispute over whether Paradise holds more men or women, answered by Abu Hurayrah:

> *The first group to enter Paradise will look like the moon when it is full, and the next batch as radiant as the radiant star in the sky. Each one of them will marry two wives. The marrow of the bones of their shins will be seen through the flesh. **None will be unmarried in Paradise.***

And **Ahmad**, from Anas:

> *A morning or an evening journey in Allah's cause is better than the world and whatever is on its surface. A place in Paradise as small as that occupied by the whip of one of you is better than the world and whatever is on its surface. If one of the women of the people of Paradise looked directly at the earth, she would fill what is between Paradise and earth with a good scent… **the veil over her head is better than this life and all that is on its surface.***

> *Is there any reward for good other than good?*

The Hereafter's good is the only fitting recompense for good done here (10:26). But Ibn Kathir closes the section by removing any claim of entitlement: **these blessings cannot be earned merely by good deeds — they come by Allah's favour and bounty.**

## 13. The Second Two Gardens, and Why They Are Lower (55:62–77)

> *And below these two, there are two other Gardens.*

Ibn Kathir treats the ranking as demonstrable, and — unusually — he proves it **word by word**. Following the gold/silver hadith and **Abu Musa**'s comment, the **two gold gardens are for the Muqarribin** (those brought near) and the **two silver gardens for Ashab Al-Yamin** (the people of the right).

| Feature | First pair (higher) | Second pair (lower) |
|---|---|---|
| Foliage | **Afnan** — spreading, intermingling branches, or varied delights | **Mudhammatan** — dark green from intense irrigation (Ibn Abbas); *"full of greenery"* (Muhammad bin Ka'b) |
| Water | **Two springs flowing free** (*tajriyan*) | **Two springs gushing** (*naddakhatan*) — Ibn Abbas: *"free flowing is stronger than gushing"*; Ad-Dahhak: full of water and constantly gushing |
| Fruit | **Every kind of fruit in pairs** | **Fruits, date palms and pomegranates** — narrower, less varied |
| Women | **Qasirat At-Tarf** — restraining their own glances | **Hur guarded in pavilions** — Ibn Kathir: those who restrain *themselves* are better than those who are guarded, though both are secluded |

The first sign, he notes, is the ordering itself: the first pair is mentioned first, and then the second is introduced as *below these two* — *"an obvious form of honouring the first two gardens over the latter."*

Other details of the lower pair:

- **Khayrat Hisan (55:70)** — either various good and delightful things (Qatadah), or, per **the majority**, the plural of *Khayrah*: a **righteous, well-mannered, beautiful woman** — also reported from Umm Salamah as a hadith from the Prophet ﷺ. Another hadith has Al-Hur Al-'Ayn singing: *"We are Al-Khayrat Al-Hisan, we were created for honourable husbands."*
- **The pavilions (55:72)** — Al-Bukhari, from Abdullah bin Qays: *in Paradise the believer will have **a tent from a hollow pearl, sixty miles wide**. In each corner there are wives who do not see the other wives, and the believer will visit them all.* Another narration gives thirty miles; Muslim's version gives sixty miles in length.
- **Rafraf and 'Abqariy (55:76)** — *Rafraf* is **cushions** (Ibn Abbas, and Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak); Al-'Ala' bin Badr: *arrayed hanging over the couches*. *'Abqariy* is **rich carpets** (Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi).

## 14. The Closing Name (55:78)

> *Blessed be the Name of your Lord, Dhil-Jalali wal-Ikram.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is a chain of four obligations: Allah is **worthy of being honoured and always obeyed, revered and thus worshipped, appreciated and never unappreciated, remembered and never forgotten.** Ibn Abbas, again: *Owner of greatness and pride.*

Then three practical consequences, which is how the surah ends — not in contemplation, but in habit:

1. **Honouring what belongs to Him.** The hadith: *Among the acts of venerating Allah are honouring the elderly Muslim, the one in authority, and the carrier (memoriser) of the Qur'an who avoids extremism and laziness with it.*
2. **Persisting in the name itself.** Ahmad, from Rabi'ah bin 'Amir, who heard the Prophet ﷺ say: ***Persist in invoking Allah with "Ya Dhal-Jalal wal-Ikram"*** (O Owner of greatness and honour). An-Nasa'i also collected it.
3. **Saying it after every prayer.** Muslim and the Four Sunan, from A'ishah: after the Salam the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would sit only as long as it took him to say —

> *Allahumma Antas-Salam, wa minkas-salam. Tabarakta ya Dhal-Jalali wal-Ikram.*
>
> **O Allah! You are As-Salam, and peace comes from You. Blessed be You, Ya Dhal-Jalal wal-Ikram.**

Ibn Kathir ends: *This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Ar-Rahman; all praise is due to Allah and all favours come from Him.*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The refrain is addressed to you and to something else.** Every one of the thirty-one repetitions is in the dual — mankind and the Jinn — which is why Ibn Kathir keeps stopping to note the consequences: believing Jinn enter Paradise, marry, and are counted among those who feared the standing before their Lord. You are not the only species being spoken to.

**The favours you cannot deny are the ones you are standing inside.** Ibn Kathir's reason the question is unanswerable is not that the blessings are impressive but that they are *ambient*: light, calculation, balance, food, water, speech. Denial would require stepping outside them.

**The warning counts as a favour.** The refrain follows the description of Hell as surely as it follows the description of gardens. Ibn Kathir says this explicitly — the threat of punishment is grace, because it is issued in time to be acted on.

**Justice scales down without changing.** The *mizan* Allah hung in the sky is the same *mizan* on a market stall. Cosmic order and honest weights are one word and one command.

**Do not stop on the first half of the verse.** Ash-Sha'bi's instruction is the surah in miniature: *whatsoever is on it will perish* is not a place to rest. Read on to the Face that remains.

**And the whole surah is a movement between two names** — from **Ar-Rahman**, which opens it before anything is created, to **Dhul-Jalal wal-Ikram**, which closes it after everything is gone. Mercy first; majesty last; and the recommended response to both, per the Prophet ﷺ, is to keep calling on Him by the second.
