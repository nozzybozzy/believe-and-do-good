*Revealed in Makkah · 8 ayat · "The Fig" — named for the oath in its first verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

At-Tin swears by three places and then makes one statement about you.

The oaths climb: a land of figs and olives, a mountain, a city. The claim they support is that **you were made in the best form there is** — and the very next verse says you can be brought down to the lowest of the low. Between the height and the fall stands a single exception: *those who believe and do righteous deeds.*

Then the surah turns and asks the reader directly: **so what is it, after all this, that makes you deny the Day of Recompense?** You have seen the beginning. You know what the One who began it can do. And the final verse closes the argument not with a threat but with a name — *Is not Allah the best of judges?* Ibn Kathir reads this as being about justice specifically: the One who will not wrong anyone, and who will therefore not leave the wronged unpaid.

Eight verses: a coronation, a warning, a way out, and a question you are meant to sit with.

---

## 1. Its Place in Prayer

Ibn Kathir opens with a hadith about how the surah was heard rather than what it means.

**Al-Bara' bin `Azib** (via Adi bin Thabit, narrated by both Malik and Shu`bah) said that the Prophet ﷺ used to recite Surah At-Tin in one of the rak`ahs while traveling — and added:

> *"I have never heard anyone with a nicer voice or recitation than him."*

**The Group** recorded it in their books. It is a small detail with a large implication: this is a surah of the road, chosen for the shortened prayer of a journey.

---

## 2. The Three Oaths (95:1–3)

> *By At-Tin and Az-Zaytun. By Tur Sinin. By this city of security.*

Two readings run through the Salaf, and Ibn Kathir preserves both without forcing a choice.

**The literal reading**
- **Mujahid** on the fig: *"It is this fig that you have."*
- **Mujahid and `Ikrimah** on the olive: *"It is this olive which you press"* — for the oil.

**The reading as places**
- **Al-Awfi from Ibn `Abbas:** At-Tin is the Masjid of Nuh, built on **Mount Al-Judi**.
- **Ka`b Al-Ahbar, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd and others** on Az-Zaytun: *"It is the Masjid of Jerusalem"* — Bayt Al-Maqdis.
- **Tur Sinin** — Ka`b Al-Ahbar and several others: the mountain **upon which Allah spoke to Musa**.
- **This city of security** — Makkah. Ibn `Abbas, Mujahid, `Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Ibrahim An-Nakha`i, Ibn Zayd, Ka`b Al-Ahbar. Ibn Kathir notes plainly: **there is no difference of opinion about this one.**

---

## 3. Why These Three, in This Order

Here is the interpretation Ibn Kathir gives most room to, reported from *some of the Imams*: the three oaths are **three places, each the site of a Messenger of the highest rank, each bringing a great code of law.**

| Oath | Place | Messenger |
|---|---|---|
| The fig and the olive | Jerusalem | `Isa bin Maryam |
| Mount Sinin | Mount Sinai | Musa bin `Imran |
| The city of security | Makkah — where whoever enters is safe | Muhammad ﷺ |

They add that these same three are named at the end of the Tawrah, in a passage describing Allah as coming from **Mount Sinai** (where He spoke to Musa), shining from **Sa`ir** (the mountain of Jerusalem, from which `Isa was sent), and appearing from the mountains of **Faran** (the mountains of Makkah, from which Muhammad ﷺ was sent).

Ibn Kathir's own observation about the sequence is the point worth keeping: they are listed **in their order of existence in time** — and so the surah swears by a noble place, then a nobler one, and then one nobler than both.

## 4. The Best Form — and the Fall (95:4–6)

> *Verily, We created man in the best form.*

This, Ibn Kathir says, is **the subject being sworn about** — everything before it was preparation for this line. Man was created in the best image and form: **standing upright, with straight limbs that Allah beautified.**

> *Then We reduced him to the lowest of the low.*

**The view Ibn Kathir adopts:** this means **the Hellfire** — held by Mujahid, Abu Al-`Aliyah, Al-Hasan, Ibn Zayd and others. After all that attractiveness and beauty, the destination is the Fire *if they disobey Allah and belie the Messengers.*

**The other view:** it means **decrepit old age** — reported from Ibn `Abbas and `Ikrimah, and the reading **Ibn Jarir preferred**. `Ikrimah adds a striking line: *whoever gathers the Qur'an* — memorises it all — *will not be returned to decrepit old age.*

**Why Ibn Kathir does not follow it:** the next verse makes an exception for *those who believe and do righteous deeds*. If the fall were senility, that exception would not hold — **believers grow senile too.** The Fire reading makes the exception coherent, and it matches the structure of Al-`Asr:

> *By Al-`Asr. Verily man is in loss, except those who believe and perform righteous deeds.* (103:1–3)

And their reward is *ghayru mamnun* — **without end**, a reward that will not be cut off.

---

## 5. The Question, and the Judge (95:7–8)

> *Then what causes you to deny after this the Recompense?*

Addressed, Ibn Kathir says, to **the Son of Adam**. The argument compressed into it: *you know the beginning* — you have seen yourself made — *and you know that the One able to begin the creation is able to repeat it, which is easier.* So what is left to deny?

> *Is not Allah the best of judges?*

Meaning: is He not the best of judges, **Who does not oppress or do any injustice to anyone?** And Ibn Kathir ends on the consequence of that justice rather than on fear of it —

> **From His justice is that He will establish the Judgement, and give retribution to the person who was wronged in this life against whoever wronged him.**

The Day of Recompense, in the surah's last word on itself, is not primarily a threat. It is the guarantee that nothing done to you was ever unrecorded.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **The surah's whole claim rests on verse 4.** The three oaths — the land of Jesus, the mountain of Moses, the city of Muhammad ﷺ — are the buildup; *We created man in the best form* is what they were sworn upon. You are the thing being testified to.
- **The height is not the safety.** Being made in the best form is where you *start*; *the lowest of the low* is where it ends without belief and righteous action. The exception clause is the surah's entire mercy.
- **Ibn Kathir chooses "the Hellfire" over "old age"** for one clean reason: believers also grow old, so only the Fire reading lets the exception in verse 6 mean anything.
- **The oaths ascend in time and in honour** — Jerusalem, then Sinai, then Makkah — noble, nobler, noblest.
- **The closing question answers itself.** You have already witnessed the harder act: being made from nothing. Repetition is easier. Denial has no evidence left to stand on.
- **The last verse is about being wronged.** His being *the best of judges* is why the oppressed will be repaid — the Judgement is justice arriving, not merely punishment.
