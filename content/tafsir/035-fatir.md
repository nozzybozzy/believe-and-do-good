*Revealed in Makkah · 45 ayat · named from its opening word — Allah as* Fatir, *the Originator of the heavens and the earth*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Fatir is a Makkan surah of **evidence**. It never argues abstractly; it points. At wind and cloud and dead ground turning green. At fresh water and salt water lying side by side. At mountains streaked white and red and pitch-black. At the colours of human skin. Each sign carries the same two conclusions: *the One who did this is the only one worth worshipping*, and *the One who did this can raise you again.*

Threaded through the evidence is a second concern — **who actually benefits from evidence**. The surah keeps sorting people: the blind and the seeing, the living and the dead, the three kinds of inheritors of the Book, the one who fears Allah and the one whose evil deeds look beautiful to him. Its answer is unusually direct: *It is only those among His servants who have knowledge that fear Allah.*

And running underneath both is a steady word to the Prophet ﷺ: they denied the messengers before you, so do not destroy yourself in sorrow over them.

---

## 1. What *Fatir* Means (35:1)

> *All praise is due to Allah, Fatir of the heavens and the earth.*

Ibn Abbas confesses he did not know the meaning of the word until he overheard two bedouins arguing over a well. One said to the other, ***"Ana fatartuha"*** — *I started it.*

So *Fatir* is **the Originator** — the one who began a thing that was not there before. Ad-Dahhak adds that wherever the phrase appears in the Qur'an, it means the Creator of the heavens and the earth.

It is a small story, and it carries the whole surah: the God being described is not a manager of an existing world. He is the one who opened it.

---

## 2. The Angels and Their Wings (35:1)

> *Who made the angels messengers with wings, two or three or four. He increases in creation what He wills.*

- **Messengers** — meaning between Him and His Prophets.
- **With wings** — with which they fly, to convey quickly what they have been commanded to convey.
- **Two, three, four** — and more. On the Night of Isra the Messenger ﷺ saw Jibril with **six hundred wings**, and between each pair of wings was a distance like that between the east and the west.
- **He increases in creation what He wills** — As-Suddi: *"He increases their wings and creates them as He wills."*

The verse begins as praise and ends as a rule: creation is not a closed inventory. He adds to it whenever He wishes.

---

## 3. Nothing Withheld, Nothing Given, Except by Him (35:2)

> *Whatever of mercy Allah may grant to mankind, none can withhold it; and whatever He may withhold, none can grant it thereafter.*

What He wills happens; what He does not will does not happen. None can give what He withholds, and none can withhold what He gives.

Ibn Kathir anchors this in two prayers the Prophet ﷺ actually said.

**After the prayer** (from Warrad, freed slave of Al-Mughirah bin Shu'bah, who wrote it out for Mu'awiyah):

> *There is no god except Allah alone, with no partner. To Him be praise and dominion, and He is able to do all things. O Allah, there is none who can withhold what You give, and none can give what You withhold, and good fortune and richness in anything cannot benefit one against Your will.*

The same narration records what the Prophet ﷺ **forbade** in that sitting: gossiping, asking too many questions, wasting money, burying girls alive, disobeying one's mother, and withholding from others while asking from them.

**On rising from bowing** (Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, in Sahih Muslim), the same words appear again, preceded by *praise filling the heavens and the earth, and filling whatever You wish besides* — and the striking phrase: *the truest words that any servant says — and all of us are Your servants —* are these.

Parallel: *And if Allah touches you with harm, there is none who can remove it but He* (10:107).

---

## 4. Is There Any Creator Other Than Allah? (35:3)

> *O mankind! Remember the grace of Allah upon you! Is there any creator other than Allah who provides for you from the sky and the earth?*

The logic is one step long. **He is alone in creating and alone in providing; therefore He should be worshipped alone.** Idols and false gods do neither.

*La ilaha illa Huwa — how then are you turning away?* Ibn Kathir reads the rebuke as: how can you turn away *after this has been made clear*, and still worship idols?

---

## 5. Three Warnings Before the Argument Resumes (35:4–6)

**To the Prophet ﷺ:** *if they deny you, so were Messengers denied before you.* Earlier messengers brought the same message of Tawhid to their peoples and were denied and opposed. You have their example. *And to Allah return all matters* — meaning, He will requite them in full.

**To mankind, twice:**

1. *Let not this present life deceive you.* This life is nothing compared to the great good Allah has promised — so do not let transient attractions distract you from what lasts.
2. *And let not the chief deceiver deceive you about Allah.* Ibn Abbas: this is **Shaytan**. Do not let him divert you from following the Messengers and believing what they say, for he is the arch-liar. (Same pairing closes Surah Luqman, 31:33.)

**On the enemy:** *Surely Shaytan is an enemy to you, so take him as an enemy.* He has openly declared his enmity — so be *more* hostile to him, oppose him, and do not believe what he tempts you with. *He only invites his followers that they may become the dwellers of the blazing Fire* — he wants you to enter it with him.

Ibn Kathir closes the section with a du'a of his own: that Allah make us enemies of Shaytan and followers of His Book and His Messengers — *for He is able to do whatever He wills and He will respond to all supplication.* And he cites 18:50: *Will you then take him and his offspring as protectors rather than Me, while they are enemies to you?*

---

## 6. The Man Whose Evil Deeds Look Beautiful (35:7–8)

> *Is he, then, to whom the evil of his deeds is made fair-seeming, so that he considers it as good?*

Believers get **forgiveness** (for whatever sins they did) and **a great reward** (for the good deeds they did). Disbelievers get severe torment — because they obeyed Shaytan and disobeyed Ar-Rahman.

Then the harder case: the disbeliever or wrongdoer who does evil *believing he is doing something good*. Ibn Kathir's verdict is blunt — such a person has been left astray by Allah, **so what can you do for him? You cannot help him at all.**

> *So destroy not yourself in sorrow for them.*

Do not grieve over it. Allah is Wise in His decree; He leaves astray whom He leaves astray and guides whom He guides, and He does so with perfect knowledge and wisdom.

---

## 7. Dead Ground, and the Coccyx (35:9)

> *And it is Allah Who sends the winds, so that they raise up the clouds, and We drive them to a dead land, and revive therewith the earth after its death. As such will be the Resurrection!*

Ibn Kathir notes how often the Qur'an argues for the Resurrection this way — the earth is dead and lifeless, nothing growing, then water comes down and *it is stirred to life, and it swells, and puts forth every lovely kind of growth* (22:5).

The mechanism is described literally: when Allah wishes to resurrect the bodies, **He will send rain from beneath the Throne**, covering the whole earth, and the bodies will grow in their graves like seeds grow in the ground.

And there is a seed. From the Sahih:

> *Every part of the son of Adam will disintegrate apart from the coccyx — from which he was created, and from it he will be made anew.*

The Hadith of Abu Razin puts the same argument as a question:

> *O Abu Razin, do you not pass through the valley of your people and see it arid and barren, then pass through it again and see it stirred to life and green?*
> — *Yes.*
> — ***Thus will Allah bring the dead back to life.***

---

## 8. Where Honour Actually Lives (35:10)

> *Whosoever desires Al-'Izzah — then to Allah belongs all Al-'Izzah.*

*Al-'Izzah* is honour, power, glory. If you want it in this world or the next, **be obedient to Allah** — because He is the Sovereign of both, and all of it belongs to Him.

Mujahid reads the verse as a direct rebuttal of idol-worship: whoever seeks honour *by worshipping idols* has misread where honour is kept.

Three parallels Ibn Kathir stacks up:

- *Do they seek Al-'Izzah with them? Verily, then to Allah belongs all honour* (4:139)
- *Let not their speech grieve you, for all Al-'Izzah belongs to Allah* (10:65)
- *But Al-'Izzah belongs to Allah, and to His Messenger, and to the believers — but the hypocrites know not* (63:8)

---

## 9. Words That Ascend, and Deeds That Carry Them (35:10)

> *To Him ascend the good words, and the righteous deeds exalt it.*

**The good words** are remembrance, recitation of the Qur'an, and supplication — the view of more than one of the Salaf.

**Ibn Mas'ud** told his companions: if we narrate a hadith to you, we will bring you proof of it from the Book of Allah. When the Muslim servant says *Subhanallah wal-hamdulillah wa la ilaha illallah wallahu akbar wa tabarakallah*, **an angel takes these words and puts them under his wing**, then ascends with them — and he does not pass any group of angels but they seek forgiveness for the one who said them, until he brings them before Allah. Then Ibn Mas'ud recited this verse.

**An-Nu'man bin Bashir** narrates the Prophet ﷺ saying that these words of remembrance

> *go around the Throne buzzing like bees, mentioning those who said them. Would one of you not like to have something with Allah mentioning him?*

**And the righteous deeds exalt it** — Ibn Abbas, in the report of Ali bin Abi Talhah, gives the mechanism:

> The good word is the remembrance of Allah; the righteous deed is **the performance of obligatory duties**. Whoever remembers Allah *while doing an obligatory duty*, his deed carries his remembrance up to Allah. **Whoever remembers Allah but does not perform the obligatory duties — his words will be rejected, as will his deed.**

Dhikr without duty has nothing to ride on.

---

## 10. The Plotters — Which Is to Say, the Show-Offs (35:10)

> *But those who plot evils, theirs will be a severe torment. And the plotting of such will perish.*

Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr and Shahr bin Hawshab all read this as **those who show off in their actions** — who deceive people into thinking they are obeying Allah, while Allah in fact hates them for the display.

Ibn Kathir's comment on why it always fails is one of the finest passages in the surah:

> No one conceals a secret but Allah will cause it to become known — **on his face, or by a slip of the tongue, or He will cause the person to wear it like a cloak.** If it is good, the consequences will be good; if bad, bad.

The show-off can go on deceiving only the fool. Believers with insight discover him soon enough, from up close. And *nothing at all can be hidden from the Knower of the Unseen*.

---

## 11. Dust, Then a Drop, Then a Recorded Lifespan (35:11)

> *And Allah did create you from dust, then from Nutfah, then He made you pairs.*

He began the creation of your father Adam from dust, then created his offspring from a drop of worthless water. **Then He made you pairs** — male and female, as a kindness and a mercy, that you may find repose in them.

> *And no female conceives or gives birth but with His knowledge.*

Nothing is hidden from Him at all — with 6:59 (*not a leaf falls but He knows it*) and 13:8–9 (*Allah knows what every female bears, and by how much the wombs fall short or exceed*) placed alongside.

> *And no aged man is granted a length of life, nor is a part cut off from his life, but is in a Book.*

Two readings are preserved, and Ibn Kathir gives both:

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ibn Abbas** (via Al-Awfi), Ad-Dahhak | Every appointed lifespan — long or short — is decreed and written. No one surpasses his term or falls short of it. The "cutting off" is not a shortening of what was written. |
| **Abu Malik, As-Suddi, Ata al-Khurasani** | The "cutting off" is the *ongoing decrease* — his time is constantly diminishing, and all of it is known to Allah year by year, month by month, hour by hour, all written in His Book. |

And in the tafsir of this very ayah, An-Nasa'i records from Anas bin Malik:

> ***Whoever would like to have ample provision and a long life, let him uphold the ties of kinship.***

*Surely, that is easy for Allah* — His knowledge encompasses all things, and nothing at all is hidden from Him.

---

## 12. Two Seas (35:12)

> *And the two seas are not alike: this is palatable, sweet and pleasant to drink, and that is salty and bitter.*

The sweet water is **the rivers flowing among people** — great and small, distributed according to people's needs across regions and lands. The salty is **the ocean, on which the great ships sail** — undrinkable.

From both alike you take **fresh tender meat** (fish) and **ornaments that you wear** — as in *Out of them both come out pearl and coral. Then which of the blessings of your Lord will you both deny?* (55:22–23).

*And you see the ships cleaving* — travelling through it, plowing the water with their beak-shaped bows. Mujahid notes that **the wind drives the ships, and the wind cannot drive any ships except the big ones.**

*That you may seek of His bounty* — through journeys of trade from one land to another. *And that you may give thanks* — for the subjugation of this mighty creation to you, so that you go where you wish and nothing stops you.

---

## 13. A Qitmir (35:13–14)

> *He merges the night into the day, and He merges the day into the night. And He has subjected the sun and the moon, each runs its course for a term appointed.*

He takes from the length of one and adds it to the shortness of the other until they are equal, then reverses it — so they take from one another in summer and in winter. The sun, moon, stars and planets all run in their appointed courses until the Day of Resurrection.

> *And those whom you invoke instead of Him own not even a Qitmir.*

Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ata, Atiyah al-Awfi, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and others agree: a **Qitmir** is *the thin thread attached to the pit of a date*. The false gods do not possess anything in the heavens or on earth — not even that.

Three failures, in sequence:

1. **They do not hear you** — they are inanimate, with no soul in them.
2. **Even if they heard, they could not answer** — they have no power to do any of what you ask.
3. **On the Day of Resurrection they will disown you** — as in 46:5–6 (*when mankind are gathered, they will become their enemies and will deny their worshipping*) and 19:81–82 (*they took gods to give them honour; nay, they will deny their worship of them and become opponents to them*).

*And none can inform you like Him Who is the All-Knower* — Qatadah: **He is referring to Himself, for He undoubtedly tells the truth.**

---

## 14. You Are the Poor Ones (35:15–18)

> *O mankind! It is you who stand in need of Allah. But Allah is the Rich, Worthy of all praise.*

All creation needs Him in everything it does; He has no need of any of it. He is unique in being free of all need, and Worthy of praise in all that He does, says, decrees and legislates.

> *If He willed, He could destroy you and bring about a new creation. And that is not hard for Allah.*

Then the verse that lands hardest:

> *And no bearer of burdens shall bear another's burden; and if one heavily laden calls another to bear his load, nothing of it will be lifted even though he be near of kin.*

Even if the one called is his father or his son. **Each person will be preoccupied with his own self and his own situation.**

> *You can warn only those who fear their Lord unseen and perform the Salah.*

The only ones who draw a lesson from what you brought are those possessed of insight, who fear their Lord and do as He commands. *And he who purifies himself, purifies only for the benefit of himself* — righteous deeds come back to the one who did them. *And to Allah is the Return.*

---

## 15. Four Pairs That Are Not Equal (35:19–26)

> *Not alike are the blind and the seeing. Nor are darkness and light. Nor are the shade and the sun's heat. Nor are the living and the dead.*

The last pair is the parable: **the believers are the living, the disbelievers the dead.** Compare 6:122 (*Is he who was dead and We gave him life and set for him a light whereby he walks among men — like him who is in darkness from which he can never come out?*) and 11:24 (*the blind and the deaf and the seer and the hearer — are they equal when compared?*).

Ibn Kathir draws the two trajectories out to their ends:

- **The believer** sees and hears, and walks in the light on a straight path in this world and the next, until he settles in Gardens with shade and springs.
- **The disbeliever** is blind and deaf, walking in darkness he cannot escape, lost in this world and the next, until he ends in fierce hot wind and boiling water and the shadow of black smoke, neither cool nor good.

> *Verily, Allah makes whom He wills to hear, but you cannot make hear those who are in graves.*

Just as the dead cannot benefit from guidance once they have died as disbelievers, so you cannot guide those decreed to be doomed. *You are only a warner* — your task is to convey.

> *And there never was a nation but a warner had passed among them.*

No nation of the sons of Adam was left without warners, and none was left with an excuse — *You are only a warner, and to every people there is a guide* (13:7); *We have sent among every Ummah a Messenger* (16:36).

*And if they deny you, those before them also denied* — with clear signs, with the Scriptures, and with the Book giving light. *Then I took hold of those who disbelieved — and how terrible was My denial!* Meaning: **how great and intense and terrible do you think My punishment was?**

---

## 16. Fruits, Mountains, and Skin (35:27–28)

> *See you not that Allah sends down water from the sky, and We produce therewith fruits of various colours…*

One thing — water from the sky — becomes an immense variety of colours, tastes and scents. Compare 13:4: neighbouring tracts of the same earth, *watered with the same water, yet some of them We make more excellent than others to eat.*

> *…and among the mountains are Judad, white and red, of varying colours, and others Gharabib black.*

- **Judad** — Ibn Abbas, Abu Malik, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and As-Suddi: **pathways**, streaks running through the rock.
- **Gharabib** — Ikrimah, Abu Malik, Ata al-Khurasani, Qatadah: mountains high and intensely black. Ibn Jarir notes that when the Arabs describe something as very black they say *ghirbib*.

> *And likewise, men and moving creatures and cattle are of various colours.*

Ibn Kathir names them: among mankind there are Berbers, Ethiopians and non-Arabs who are very black; Slavs and Romans who are very white; Arabs in between; and Indians. Animals vary the same way, even within a single species, and a single animal may carry patches of different colours. Compare 30:22: *the difference of your languages and colours — verily, in that are signs for men of sound knowledge.*

His comment on the whole spectacle: **Blessed be Allah, the Best of creators.**

---

## 17. The Verse on Knowledge (35:28)

> *It is only those among His servants who have knowledge that fear Allah. Verily, Allah is Almighty, Oft-Forgiving.*

The reason is causal, not decorative: **the more one knows of the Almighty, All-Powerful, All-Knowing — with His perfect attributes and most beautiful Names — the more one will fear Him.**

Four voices on what that fear is:

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah): they are *those who know that Allah is able to do all things.*
- **Ibn Abbas**, again, in fuller form: the one who truly knows Ar-Rahman is the one who **associates nothing with Him in worship, accepts as lawful what He permitted and as unlawful what He prohibited, obeys His commands, and is certain that he will meet Him and be brought to account.**
- **Sa'id bin Jubayr**: *"Fear is what stands between you and disobeying Allah."*
- **Al-Hasan al-Basri**: the knowledgeable person is the one who **fears Ar-Rahman with regard to the Unseen, likes what Allah wants him to like, and shuns what angers Allah.** Then he recited this very ayah.

And a taxonomy Sufyan ath-Thawri narrates — the knowledgeable are three:

| | Knows Allah | Knows the command of Allah | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | ✓ | ✓ | Fears Allah, and knows the *hudud* and the *fara'id* |
| **Second** | ✓ | ✗ | Fears Allah, but does not know the limits or obligations |
| **Third** | ✗ | ✓ | Knows the limits and obligations, but does not fear Allah |

---

## 18. The Trade That Never Perishes (35:29–30)

> *Verily, those who recite the Book of Allah, and perform the Salah, and spend out of what We have provided for them, secretly and openly — they hope for a trade-gain that will never perish.*

Three acts: **recite and believe**, **establish prayer at its prescribed times, night and day**, and **spend from what Allah gave, in secret and in the open**. What they hope for is a reward from Allah that *will inevitably be theirs*.

> *That He may pay them their wages in full, and give them even more, out of His grace.*

Not just payment — multiplication, *such as has never occurred to them.*

- ***Ghafur*** — He forgives their sins.
- ***Shakur*** — **He appreciates even a little of their good deeds.**

---

## 19. The Book Confirming the Books (35:31)

> *And what We have revealed to you of the Book, it is the very truth, confirming that which was revealed before it.*

The Qur'an confirms the previous scriptures **just as they bore witness to its coming** and to its being sent down from the Lord of the worlds.

*Verily, Allah is All-Aware and All-Seer of His servants* — He knows who deserves to receive the blessing He gives to one and not another. The Prophets are favoured above the rest of mankind; some Prophets were given more than others; and **the position given to Muhammad ﷺ is higher than that of all the rest.**

---

## 20. Three Kinds of Inheritor (35:32)

> *Then We gave the Book as inheritance to such of Our servants whom We chose. Then of them are some who wrong themselves, and of them are some who follow a middle course, and of them are some who are, by Allah's leave, foremost in good deeds. That — that is indeed a great grace.*

They are this Ummah, and they are three:

| | Who they are | Ibn Abbas: their outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Those who wrong themselves** | Careless about some obligatory actions; commit some forbidden ones | Will be forgiven |
| **Those on the middle course** | Fulfil obligations and avoid the forbidden, but neglect some good deeds and do some disliked ones | An easy accounting |
| **The foremost** | Do the obligatory and the encouraged, avoid the unlawful and disliked, and avoid even some things permitted | Enter Paradise without being brought to account |

At-Tabarani records from Ibn Abbas that the Messenger ﷺ said one day:

> ***My intercession will be for those among my Ummah who commit major sins.***

And Ibn Abbas maps the three onto three doors into Paradise: the foremost, **without account**; the middle course, **by the mercy of Allah**; and those who wrong themselves, along with the People of Al-A'raf, **by the intercession of Muhammad ﷺ.**

There was disagreement — some said those who wrong themselves are not part of this Ummah nor among the chosen. **Ibn Kathir's verdict: the correct view is that they are.** Imperfect, falling short of the straight path, and still inside the choosing.

---

## 21. The Heirs of the Prophets (35:32)

Ibn Kathir pauses here to say that of all who receive this blessing, **the scholars are the most fortunate and the most entitled to this mercy** — and tells the story of the man who travelled from Madinah to Damascus to find Abu Ad-Darda'.

*What brought you here, my brother?* — *A Hadith I heard you narrate from the Messenger of Allah.*
*Have you come for trade?* — *No.*
*Have you come for any other reason?* — *No.*
*Have you come only to seek this Hadith?* — ***Yes.***

Then Abu Ad-Darda' gave it to him:

> *Whoever follows a path to seek knowledge, Allah will show him the way to Paradise. The angels lower their wings, being pleased with the one who seeks knowledge. Whoever is in the heavens and on earth — even the fish in the sea — ask forgiveness for the scholar. The superiority of the scholar over the worshipper is like that of the moon over all other heavenly bodies. **The scholars are the heirs of the Prophets, for the Prophets did not leave behind Dinars and Dirhams — they left behind knowledge**; and whoever receives it receives an abundance of good fortune.*

---

## 22. Gardens of 'Adn (35:33–35)

> *'Adn Gardens will they enter, therein will they be adorned with bracelets of gold and pearls, and their garments therein will be of silk.*

On the ornaments — from the Sahih, Abu Hurayrah: ***The ornaments of the believer will reach as far as his Wudu' reached.***

On the silk — forbidden to men in this world precisely so that it may be permitted in the next. ***Whoever wears silk in this world will not wear it in the Hereafter***, and of the disbelievers: *It is for them in this world, and for you in the Hereafter.*

> *And they will say: "All praise be to Allah, Who has removed from us all grief."*

Grief here means **fear of everything that is feared** — lifted, and with it all the anxieties of the world and the Hereafter they used to carry.

*Verily, our Lord is Oft-Forgiving, Most Ready to appreciate* — Ibn Abbas and others: **He forgives them their major sins, and appreciates even the smallest of their good deeds.**

> *Who, out of His grace, has lodged us in a home that will last forever.*

Out of His grace — because *our good deeds are not equivalent to this*. From the Sahih:

> ***None of you will enter Paradise by virtue of his good deeds.***
> — *Not even you, O Messenger of Allah?*
> — ***Not even me, unless Allah encompasses me with His mercy and grace.***

> *Where toil will touch us not, nor weariness.*

Two words that both mean exhaustion. Ibn Kathir suggests the doubling negates both kinds: **neither their bodies nor their souls will tire.** They exhausted themselves in worship in this world; in Paradise their obligations end, and what remains is eternal rest.

---

## 23. The Fire, and the Request to Go Back (35:36–37)

> *Neither will it have a complete killing effect on them so that they die, nor shall its torment be lightened for them.*

*Wherein he will neither die nor live* (20:74). From Sahih Muslim: *As for the people of Hell who will dwell therein, they will neither live nor die there.* And 43:77 — *O Malik! Let your Lord make an end of us.* — *Verily, you shall abide forever.*

Ibn Kathir's note: in that state they will think that if only they could die it would be a rest for them — and that can never happen.

Not lightened, either: *That will not be lightened for them, and they will be plunged into destruction with deep regrets* (43:74–75); *whenever it abates, We shall increase for them the fierceness of the Fire* (17:97); *No increase shall We give you, except in torment* (78:30).

> *Therein they will cry: "Our Lord! Bring us out, we shall do righteous good deeds, not that we used to do."*

They ask to return and live differently. **But Allah knows that if He sent them back they would return to exactly what they had been forbidden** — He knows they are lying, and He will not respond. Compare 40:11–12: *when Allah alone was invoked you disbelieved, but when partners were joined to Him you believed.*

---

## 24. Sixty Years, and No Excuse Left (35:37)

> *Did We not give you lives long enough, so that whosoever would receive admonition could receive it? And the warner came to you.*

Meaning: you lived long enough that if you were the kind of person who would benefit from the truth, **you would have benefited from it during your lifetime.**

Ibn Kathir gathers the hadiths on the age at which the excuse runs out:

- *Allah has left no excuse for the person who lives to be sixty or seventy years old* — said three times (Abu Hurayrah, recorded by Ahmad).
- In Al-Bukhari's *Book of Riqaq*: ***Allah has left no excuse for a man who reaches the age of sixty.***
- And why sixty is the marker: ***The usual life span in my Ummah is between sixty and seventy years, and only a few pass this age.***

**And the warner came to you** — two readings:

- **Grey hair** (Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Abu Ja'far al-Baqir, Qatadah, Sufyan bin Uyaynah) — the body's own warning.
- **The Messenger of Allah ﷺ** (As-Suddi, Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam), with 53:56 recited in support: *This is a warner of the warners of old.*

Ibn Jarir favours the second, and Qatadah's synthesis is the cleanest: *proof will be established against them by the fact that **they lived long enough and that Messengers came to them.*** Compare 43:77–78, 17:15 (*We never punish until We have sent a Messenger*), and 67:8–9 — *Did no warner come to you?* — *Yes, indeed a warner did come to us, but we denied him.*

---

## 25. Successors on the Earth (35:38–39)

He knows the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and *He is the All-Knower of what is in the breasts* — all that is hidden, and the secrets of the hearts.

> *He it is Who has made you successors, generations after generations in the earth.*

People succeed one another, generation after generation (compare 27:62).

> *So whosoever disbelieves, on him will be his disbelief… And the disbelief of the disbelievers adds nothing but hatred of their Lord… adds nothing but loss.*

The longer they persist, the more Allah hates them, and the more they and their families lose on the Day of Resurrection.

Ibn Kathir sets the mirror image beside it: **for the believers, the longer they live and do good, the higher their rank in Paradise, the greater their reward, and the more beloved they become to their Creator.** The same passing years run in opposite directions depending on what fills them.

---

## 26. What Holds the Heavens Up (35:40–41)

> *Say: "Have you considered your partners whom you call upon besides Allah? Show Me what they have created of the earth. Or have they any share in the heavens? Or have We given them a Book, so that they act on clear proof therefrom?"*

Three questions, three empty answers — **they do not possess even the membrane covering the stone of a date**, and no revealed Book grounds their Shirk. *Nay, the wrongdoers promise one another nothing but delusions*: whims, opinions and personal desires, which are no more than misguidance.

> *Verily, Allah grasps the heavens and the earth lest they should move away from their places, and if they were to move away, there is not one that could grasp them after Him.*

*He withholds the heaven from falling on the earth except by His leave* (22:65); *among His signs is that the heaven and the earth stand by His command* (30:25).

> *Truly, He is Ever Most Forbearing, Oft-Forgiving.*

Ibn Kathir explains why *these two names* close a verse about cosmic force: **He sees His servants disbelieving in Him and disobeying Him — and He is patient, gives them time, does not hasten the punishment, conceals their faults and forgives them.** The hand that holds the heavens in place is the same hand that withholds the blow.

---

## 27. The Oath They Broke (35:42–43)

> *And they swore by Allah their most binding oath that if a warner came to them, they would be more guided than any of the nations.*

Quraysh and the Arabs swore it before the Messenger ﷺ came — that they would outdo every nation that had received a Messenger. Compare 6:156–157 (*if only the Book had been sent down to us, we would surely have been better guided than they*) and 37:167–170 (*if we had a reminder as had the men of old, we would have been the chosen servants of Allah — but they disbelieve therein, so they will come to know*).

> *Yet when a warner came to them, it increased in them nothing but flight.*

Ibn Kathir names the two forms the flight took:

1. **Arrogance in the land** — they were too proud to follow the signs of Allah.
2. **Plotting of evil** — they schemed to keep people off the path of Allah.

> *But the evil plot encompasses only him who makes it.*

The consequences come back on the plotters, not on others.

> *Then, can they expect anything but the Sunnah of the peoples of old? So no change will you find in Allah's Sunnah, and no turning off.*

The Sunnah is the punishment of Allah for denying His Messengers, and it does not alter — *when Allah wills a people's punishment, there can be no turning back of it* (13:11). No one can remove it or ward it off.

---

## 28. Travel and Look (35:44–45)

> *Have they not travelled in the land, and seen what was the end of those before them — though they were superior to them in power?*

Go and see: how Allah destroyed those who denied the Messengers, how their homes were emptied of them, how they lost everything after living in luxury — numerous, well-equipped, wealthy, with many children. **None of it protected them in the slightest when the command of the Lord came.** Nothing is impossible for Him, in the heavens or on the earth.

> *And if Allah were to punish men for that which they earned, He would not leave a moving creature on the surface of the earth.*

If He punished them for all their sins, He would destroy all the people of the earth and all their livestock and crops. Sa'id bin Jubayr and As-Suddi read it concretely: **He would have stopped sending rain, and every animal would have died as a result.**

> *But He gives them respite to an appointed term, and when their term comes, then verily, Allah is Ever All-Seer of His servants.*

He is delaying — until the Day of Resurrection, when each will be brought to account and rewarded or punished according to his deeds.

## The Thread

Fatir opens with a word Ibn Abbas learned from two bedouins arguing over a well — *I started it* — and everything after that is an argument from beginnings. The One who started the heavens sends the wind that raises the cloud that revives the dead valley, and that is your resurrection. The One who split fresh water from salt, and streaked the mountains white and red and black, and coloured human skin from one Ethiopia to one Rome, is not plausibly served by a stone that does not own the thread on a date pit.

But the surah's real subject is **who these signs land on**. Its answer is the verse the scholars have quoted for a thousand years: *It is only those among His servants who have knowledge that fear Allah.* Sa'id bin Jubayr's gloss is the whole of it in eight words — *fear is what stands between you and disobeying Allah.*

And it is a generous surah, for all its warnings. The Book is inherited by three kinds of people and **all three are among the chosen**, wrongdoers included. Words of dhikr are carried up under an angel's wing — but only if a fulfilled obligation carries them. Gold, pearls and silk wait at the end, and *none of you will enter Paradise by his deeds — not even me.* Allah holds the heavens from falling and, with the same forbearance, holds back the punishment your years have earned.

Which is why the surah keeps returning to time. Sixty years is the age at which the excuse runs out. Grey hair is itself a warner. The disbeliever's added years add only loss; the believer's add only rank. *He gives them respite to an appointed term* — and the whole of Fatir is an argument for what to do with the respite.

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Fatir. All praise and gratitude is due to Allah.*
