*Revealed in Makkah · 19 ayat · "The Cleaving" — named for its opening verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Infitar takes the universe apart in four verses and then asks one question.

The sky splits, the stars scatter, the seas burst into one another, the graves are turned over — and the moment the scaffolding of the world is gone, what stands exposed is a single soul with a ledger: *what it sent forward and what it left behind*. Only then does the surah turn and speak directly to you, and the question it asks is the sharpest in the Qur'an:

> **What has deceived you concerning your Lord, the Most Generous?**

Ibn Kathir is emphatic that this is **not an invitation to explain yourself**. It is a rebuke. And the choice of divine name is the whole point — you were not defrauded by a tyrant. You presumed on generosity.

What follows is the surah's answer to its own question, in three moves. First, a reminder of what that Generous Lord actually did for you: made you, shaped you, balanced you. Second, the real reason you presumed: *you deny the Day of Recompense.* Third, the fact that undoes the presumption entirely: two noble angels have been writing the whole time.

The surah closes on the loneliest verse in it — a Day when **no person has power for another** — and the Prophet ﷺ demonstrates it by naming his own family and telling them he cannot help them.

## 1. Its Virtues

**An-Nasa'i, from Jabir:** Mu'adh led the people in the night prayer and made his recitation long. The Prophet ﷺ said, *"Are you putting the people to trial, O Mu'adh?"* — and then named the surahs he should have used instead: **Al-A'la (87)**, **Ad-Duha (93)**, and **Al-Infitar (82)**.

The core of this hadith is in the Two Sahihs; Ibn Kathir notes precisely that **the mention of Al-Infitar comes only through An-Nasa'i**.

**From Abdullah bin Umar**, the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *Whoever would be pleased to look at the Day of Resurrection with his own eyes, let him recite:*

- **At-Takwir (81)** — *When the sun is Kuwwirat*
- **Al-Infitar (82)** — *When the heaven is cleft asunder*
- **Al-Inshiqaq (84)** — *When the heaven is split asunder*

Three surahs, read together, as a way of *seeing* rather than merely believing.

---

## 2. The World Comes Apart (82:1–5)

Four verbs, each glossed briefly by Ibn Kathir:

| Ayah | | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | The heaven is *infatarat* | It **splits** — as in 73:18, *whereon the heaven will be cleft asunder (Munfatir)* |
| **2** | The stars *intatharat* | They **fall**, scattered |
| **3** | The seas *fujjirat* | They **burst forth** into one another |
| **4** | The graves *bu'thirat* | They are **overturned** |

On the seas, three of the early authorities:

- **Ibn Abbas:** Allah will cause some of it to burst forth over other parts of it.
- **Al-Hasan:** the same — and **its water will go away**.
- **Qatadah:** its fresh water will mix with its salt water.

On the graves, **Ibn Abbas** gives one word: *"searched."* **As-Suddi**: they are moved, and those inside them come out.

Then the verse that all four exist to set up:

> **A person will know what he has sent forward and left behind.** *(82:5)*

Ibn Kathir's comment is almost blunt: *when this happens, then this will occur.* The cosmic demolition is not the subject. It is the lighting.

---

## 3. The Question (82:6)

> *O man! What has made you careless about your Lord, the Most Generous?*

Ibn Kathir corrects a common misreading before he explains anything else. **This is a threat, not a request for a reply.** Some people take it as though the Most Generous were genuinely asking, so that a man might answer, *"Your generosity is what deceived me"* — as if the divine kindness could be offered in his defence.

The actual meaning:

> *O son of Adam! What deceived you away from your Lord, the Most Generous — meaning the Most Great — so that you went forth disobeying Him, and met Him with that which was unbefitting?*

The same question is put again, in the hadith, at the only moment it cannot be dodged:

> **"O son of Adam! What deceived you concerning Me? O son of Adam! What was your response to the Messengers?"**

**On the occasion of revelation:** Al-Baghawi transmits from Al-Kalbi and Muqatil that the verse came down about **Al-Aswad bin Shariq**, who struck the Prophet ﷺ and was not punished in retaliation for it — and it was then that Allah revealed, *What has made you careless about your Lord, the Most Generous?*

## 4. What He Did For You (82:7–8)

> *Who created you, fashioned you perfectly, and gave you due proportion. In whatever form He willed, He put you together.*

The answer to *what deceived you concerning the Most Generous* is placed immediately beneath the question, as its rebuke: **He made you complete, straight, perfectly balanced and proportioned in stature — He fashioned you in the best of forms and shapes.**

### The spit in the palm

Imam Ahmad, from **Busr bin Jahhash Al-Qurashi**: one day the Messenger of Allah ﷺ spat into his own palm, placed his finger on it, and said, reporting from Allah the Mighty and Sublime:

> *"O son of Adam! How can you escape Me, when I created you from something similar to this? Then I fashioned you and balanced your creation, so that you walked between two garments — and the earth has a burial place for you.*
>
> *So you gathered wealth and withheld it, until your soul reached your collarbone. Then, at that moment, you say: 'I will give charity now.' But how will there be time for charity?"*

Also recorded by Ibn Majah. The arc is the surah in miniature: a drop, a shape, a shroud, a grave — and one lifetime of grip that only loosens when it is worthless.

### "In whatever form He willed"

**Mujahid** reads this as resemblance: *the father, the mother, the paternal uncle, or the maternal uncle.*

Ibn Kathir brings in the hadith from **Abu Hurayrah** (Two Sahihs). A man came saying, *"My wife has given birth to a black boy"* — the accusation left unspoken.

The Prophet ﷺ asked: **"Do you have any camels?"** — *Yes.* **"What colour?"** — *Red.* **"Do any of them have patches of grey?"** — *Yes.* **"How did this happen to them?"**

The man answered himself: *"It is probably an inherited strain."* The Prophet ﷺ said:

> **"Likewise, this is probably an inherited strain."**

A man's suspicion dismantled with his own camels — and it is placed here, under *in whatever form He willed*, because the forming was never the man's business in the first place.

## 5. The Real Reason, and the Two Who Are Watching (82:9–12)

> *Nay! But you deny the Day of Recompense.*

Ibn Kathir names the mechanism plainly: what compels a person to oppose the Most Generous and meet Him with disobedience is **the rejection, in the heart, of the Hereafter, the recompense and the reckoning**. Presumption is denial wearing better clothes.

> *But verily, over you are watchers — **Kiraman Katibin** — they know all that you do.*

**Noble** guardians, and **recording** ones. Ibn Kathir's instruction is characteristically practical:

> *There are noble guardian angels over you — so **do not meet them with evil deeds**, because they write down all that you do.*

---

## 6. Two Ends (82:13–16)

> *Verily, the Abrar will be in Delight; and verily, the Fujjar will be in the blazing Fire.*

The **Abrar** are defined in a single line: *those who obeyed Allah and did not meet Him with disobedience.* The **Fujjar** are their inverse, and their end is eternal.

On *they will not be absent therefrom*, Ibn Kathir refuses every possible loophole at once:

- Not absent from the torment **for even one hour**
- The torment will **not be lightened**
- They will **not be granted the death they will be requesting**
- Nor any rest — **not even for a single day**

---

## 7. The Day Nobody Can Help You (82:17–19)

> *And what will make you know what the Day of Recompense is? Again, what will make you know what the Day of Recompense is?*

The question is asked twice, and the doubling is the meaning: **a magnification of the affair**, then an affirmation of it.

> *(It will be) the Day when no person shall have power for another.* *(82:19)*

No one will be able to benefit anyone else, or pull him out of what he is in — **unless Allah gives permission** to whomever He wishes and is pleased with.

Ibn Kathir proves it with the hardest available example. The Prophet ﷺ, addressing his own clan:

> **"O children of Hashim! Save yourselves from the Fire, for I have no power to cause you any benefit from Allah."**

(Given at length at the end of the tafsir of Ash-Shu'ara', 26:214.)

> *And the Decision, that Day, will be with Allah.*

Closing on the transmitted comment:

> *"By Allah, the Decision is for Allah today as well — but on that Day, no one will try to dispute with Him about it."*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**You were not tricked. You were trusted, and you presumed.**

Al-Infitar's question — *what deceived you concerning your Lord, the Most Generous?* — is answered by its own next verse: nothing did. He made you, shaped you, balanced you, and let the consequences of your choices run late enough that you mistook the delay for indifference. Ibn Kathir's whole reading turns on that name: it is **generosity** you took advantage of, which is why the question is a rebuke and not an inquiry.

Two things follow. First, the record is already being kept — *Kiraman Katibin, they know all that you do* — so the reckoning is not a future event but a present one you cannot see. Second, when it is read out, **the room will be empty of advocates**: the Prophet ﷺ told his own family he could do nothing for them.

Which leaves the man in the hadith, soul at his collarbone, finally saying *I will give charity now.* The surah exists so that you do not have to be him.
