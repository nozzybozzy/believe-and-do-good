*Revealed in Makkah · 50 ayat · "Those Sent Forth" — named for the oath in its first verse · revealed in a cave at Mina*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Mursalat is a short, hard-driving Makkan surah that argues a single point: **what you have been promised is going to happen.** Everything else in it is either evidence for that claim or a picture of what it will look like.

It opens with five rapid oaths — winds, then angels — and delivers the answer to them in the seventh verse, before the reader has time to settle: *Surely, what you are promised will occur.* From there the surah dismantles the visible world (stars extinguished, sky split, mountains erased), assembles the Messengers for the Day of Sorting Out, and then does something unusual: it turns backward, to ruined nations, to a drop of despised water, to the earth that swallows your dead and carries your living — as if to say, *the God who did all this is not going to be stopped by your bones.*

Its most distinctive feature is a refrain. **Ten times** the surah stops and says:

> *Woe that Day to the deniers!*

The repetition is doing what repetition does in Al-Qamar and Ar-Rahman — it stamps each piece of evidence with the same verdict, so that the reader cannot take in a proof without also taking in its consequence.

The surah ends on a question rather than a scene: **if not this, then what?**

## 1. How It Was Revealed, and When It Was Last Recited

Two reports frame the surah, one from its beginning and one from the end of the Prophet's ﷺ life.

**Al-Bukhari from Ibn Mas'ud:** they were with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in a cave at Mina when Al-Mursalat was revealed. He was reciting it and Ibn Mas'ud was learning it directly from his mouth — *his mouth was still moist with it* — when a snake leapt out at them. The Prophet ﷺ said, **"Kill it!"** They went after it and it escaped. He then said:

> **"It was saved from your harm just as you all were saved from its harm."**

Muslim recorded it too, by way of Al-A'mash.

**Imam Ahmad, and both Sahihs by way of Malik, from Ibn Abbas:** his mother **Umm Al-Fadl** heard him reciting *By the Mursalat 'Urfa* and said:

> *"O my son! You reminded me with your recitation of this Surah. Verily, it is the last thing I heard from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He recited it in the Maghrib prayer"* — that is, shortly before he died.

So the surah is bracketed by a cave at the start and a final Maghrib at the end.

## 2. The Five Oaths: Winds and Angels (77:1–7)

> *By the Mursalat 'Urfa. The 'Asifat 'Asfa. And the Nashirat Nashra. The Fariqat that separate. The Mulqiyat that remind — excusing or warning.*

Ibn Kathir lays out a genuine difference among the Salaf on the first three, and no difference at all on the last three.

**The angels reading.** Abu Hurayrah on *Al-Mursalat*: "The angels." Similar statements from Masruq, Abu Ad-Duha, Mujahid (in one narration), As-Suddi and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas. Abu Salih said of Al-'Asifat, An-Nashirat, Al-Fariqat and Al-Mulqiyat that they *all* refer to angels — though in another narration he said Al-Mursalat means the Messengers, and An-Nashirat means rain.

**The wind reading.** Ath-Thawri's chain reaches Abu Al-'Ubaydayn, who asked **Ibn Mas'ud** about Al-Mursalat 'Urfa. He answered: **"The wind."** And the same for Al-'Asifat 'Asfa and An-Nashirat Nashra. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Qatadah agreed.

**Ibn Kathir's own judgement:** the wind reading is "the most obvious meaning" for the first three, and he supports it from the Qur'an itself —

- *And We send the winds fertilizing* (15:22)
- *And it is He Who sends the winds as heralds of glad tidings, going before His mercy* (7:57)

He then gives the linguistic sense of each: winds are called **'Asifat** when they roar with their blowing; **An-Nashirat** are the winds that scatter into clouds across the horizons of the sky at the will of the Lord. (Ibn Jarir was firm that Al-'Asifat is the wind, but declined to settle whether An-Nashirat is wind or angels.)

**On the last three there is no dispute** — Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Masruq, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ar-Rabi', As-Suddi and Ath-Thawri all say: **the angels.** And the reason is built into the words:

They descend with Allah's command to the Messengers, **separating** truth from falsehood, guidance from misguidance, lawful from forbidden — and they **deliver the revelation**, which carries both an absolvement for creation and a warning of Allah's torment for those who oppose His command. That is exactly what *excusing or warning* means.

**Then the answer to the oaths (v.7):** *Surely, what you are promised will occur.* Ibn Kathir spells out the content of the promise — the establishment of the Hour, the blowing of the horn, the resurrection of bodies, the gathering of the first and the last on one common ground, and the repayment of every doer according to his deed: good for good, evil for evil. **All of it will come to pass, and there is no avoiding it.**

## 3. The World Taken Apart (77:8–15)

Four collapses, then a question, then a name.

| Verse | Ibn Kathir's gloss |
|---|---|
| *When the stars lose their lights* | Their light departs — cf. *when the stars fall* (81:2), *when the stars have fallen and scattered* (82:2) |
| *When Furijat the heaven* | It is cleft asunder and split; its sides fall in, its edges go weak |
| *When the mountains are blown away* | Removed entirely — no sight or trace of them remains (cf. 20:105, 18:47) |
| *When the Messengers are Uqqitat* | Three readings, below |

On **Uqqitat**, Ibn Kathir preserves the disagreement rather than resolving it:

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Al-'Awfi): *gathered*. **Ibn Zayd** supports this with *On the Day when Allah will gather the Messengers together* (5:109).
- **Mujahid**: *postponed*.
- **Ibrahim** (via Mansur ← Ath-Thawri): *promised* — apparently reading it against *the Prophets and the witnesses will be brought forward, and it will be judged between them with truth* (39:69).

> *For what Day are these signs postponed? For the Day of Sorting Out.*

Allah is asking, in Ibn Kathir's paraphrase: *for which Day are the Messengers held back and their matter awaited, so that the Hour may be established?* The answer is **Yawm al-Fasl** — the Day of Sorting Out, when the people of Paradise are separated from the people of Hell. He cites 14:47–48: *think not that Allah will fail to keep His promise to His Messengers... on the Day when the earth will be changed to another earth.*

Then the magnification — *And what will explain to you what is the Day of Sorting Out?* — followed by the refrain's first appearance: **woe to them from Allah's torment that is coming.**

## 4. Four Arguments from Things You Already Know (77:16–28)

Having described the end, the surah turns to evidence available now.

**a) History (16–19).** *Did We not destroy the ancients?* — those who rejected the Messengers. *So shall We make later generations follow them* — meaning those who resemble them. **Thus do We deal with the criminals.** The precedent is not an anecdote; it is a policy.

**b) Your own origin (20–24).** *Did We not create you from a despised water?* — despised and weak *in comparison to the power of the Creator*. Ibn Kathir brings the hadith of Busr bin Jahhash, in which Allah says:

> **"O son of Adam! How can you think that I am unable, when I created you from something like this?"**

*Then We placed it in a place of safety* — the womb, which was prepared precisely to protect what is deposited in it. *For a known period* — a fixed term, from six months to nine. And then: *So We did measure; and We are the best to measure.* The one who measured your gestation to the month is not going to be defeated by your reassembly.

**c) The earth beneath you (25–26).** *Have We not made the earth Kifat — for the living and the dead?*

- **Ibn Abbas**: Kifat means a place of shelter.
- **Mujahid**: it holds the dead so that nothing of them is seen.
- **Ash-Sha'bi** (also Mujahid and Qatadah): **"Its interior is for your dead and its surface is for your living."**

**d) What holds it steady and what you drink (27).** *Fixed towers* — the mountains, by which the earth is held so it does not shake and sway. *Furat water* — cold and sweet, from the clouds or from the springs He causes to gush from the earth.

And the refrain lands with its sharpest gloss yet: **woe to whoever reflects on these created things, which demonstrate the greatness of their Creator — and then goes on rejecting Him anyway.**

## 5. The Shadow With Three Columns (77:29–40)

The address turns direct. On the Day of Judgement it will be said to them:

> *Depart to that which you used to deny! Depart to a shadow in three columns.*

Ibn Kathir: this is the flame of the Fire as it rises and ascends with smoke — and because of its severity and strength it will have **three columns**. Then the mockery in the next verse:

> *Neither shady nor of any use against the fierce flame.*

It is the shade of smoke thrown off by flame — a shade that is not a shade, and that gives no protection from the heat at all.

**The sparks (32–33).** *It throws sparks as Al-Qasr* — sparks shooting from the flame like huge castles.

- **Ibn Mas'ud**: like forts.
- **Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Malik ← Zayd bin Aslam**: the trunks of trees. And Al-Bukhari records **Ibn Abbas** explaining the usage from ordinary life: timber of three cubits or more, cut for building — *we used to call it Al-Qasr.*

*As if they were Sufr camels* — **black camels**, per Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak, the view Ibn Jarir favoured. But Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Sa'id bin Jubayr read *Jimalat Sufr* as **the ropes of ships**, bundled until they resemble coiled intestines.

**Silence and no excuse (35–37).** *That will be a Day when they shall not speak, and they will not be permitted to put forth any excuse.* They will neither be able to speak nor be granted permission to. The proof will already stand against them; they will be summoned to account for the wrong they did and will have nothing to say. Ibn Kathir adds an important interpretive key here: **the courts of that Day occur in stages** — sometimes Allah reports one stage, sometimes another — which is why different verses describe both speech and silence. The variety is meant to convey the terrors of the Day, not to contradict.

**The challenge (38–39).** *That will be a Day of Decision! We have brought you and the men of old together! So if you have a plot, use it against Me!*

He will gather all of them by His power on one plane, make them hear the caller and make them see. And then the challenge — which Ibn Kathir calls **a serious threat and a harsh warning**: *if you can save yourselves from being seized by Me, do so. But you certainly cannot.* He supports it with:

- *O assembly of Jinn and men! If you have power to pass beyond the zones of the heavens and the earth, then pass beyond! But you will never be able to, except with authority* (55:33)
- *And you will not harm Him in the least* (11:57)
- And the hadith qudsi: **"O My servants! You can never attain My benefit and thereby benefit Me, and you can never attain My harm and thereby harm Me."**

## 6. The Other Shade (77:41–45)

The contrast is deliberate and exact.

> *Verily, those who had Taqwa shall be amidst shades and springs, and fruits, such as they desire.*

Ibn Kathir points at the pairing directly: the wretched are under the shade of **Al-Yahmum** — putrid black smoke — and the people of Taqwa are under shade and beside springs. Same word, opposite realities.

Who are they? **His servants who have Taqwa and worship Him by performing the obligations and abandoning the forbidden things.** And *fruits, such as they desire* means: whatever they ask for, of every kind.

> *"Eat and drink comfortably for that which you used to do."*

This is said to them **out of kindness** — and it is followed by the principle underneath it: *Verily, thus We reward the Muhsinin* — this is Our reward for whoever does good deeds.

---

## 7. Eat a Little, and the Final Question (77:46–50)

> *Eat and enjoy yourselves for a little while. Verily, you are criminals.*

A command issued as threat and intimidation, addressed to those who deny the Day of Judgement. *A little while* means **short, small, brief** — and then you will be driven to the Fire already described. Ibn Kathir sets it beside:

- *We let them enjoy for a little while, then in the end We shall oblige them to a great torment* (31:24)
- *A brief enjoyment in this world! And then unto Us will be their return* (10:69–70)

> *And when it is said to them: "Bow down!" — they bow not down.*

Ibn Kathir reads this concretely: when these ignorant disbelievers are ordered to **pray with the congregation**, they refuse and turn away arrogantly. The refusal of the Hereafter shows up as a refusal to bend the body now.

And then the surah's last verse, which is a question left open:

> *Then in what statement after this will they believe?*

Meaning: **if they do not believe in this Qur'an, what talk will they believe in?** Compare *Then in which speech after Allah and His Ayat will they believe?* (45:6).

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The oaths and the answer.** Winds that are sent, roar, and scatter; angels that separate truth from falsehood and deliver revelation as either absolvement or warning — and all of it swearing to one sentence: *what you are promised will occur.*

**The evidence is not exotic.** Ibn Kathir's four proofs are a ruined nation, a drop of fluid, the ground under your feet, and a glass of sweet water. The God who measured a pregnancy to the month and made the earth a container with your dead inside it and your living on top of it is not going to struggle with resurrection.

**Ten times: "Woe that Day to the deniers."** The refrain is the surah's structure. Every proof is immediately priced. And Ibn Kathir's gloss on it at verse 28 is the one to carry: woe to the person who *reflects* on the creation, sees the greatness of the Creator in it — and still refuses.

**Two shades.** The disbelievers get a shadow in three columns that gives no shade and no protection; the people of Taqwa get shade, springs, and fruit on request. The vocabulary is identical; the outcome is not.

**The plot that will not work.** *If you have a plot, use it against Me.* You will never attain His harm and thereby harm Him.

**The closing question is the one to sit with.** *Then in what statement after this will they believe?* If this does not move a person, nothing said after it will.
