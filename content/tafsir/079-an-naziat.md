*Revealed in Makkah · 46 ayat · "Those Who Pull Out" — named for the angels sworn by in the opening verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

An-Nazi'at begins at the **moment of death** and ends with people standing on a levelled earth saying that the whole of life felt like one afternoon. Everything in between is the argument that connects those two points.

The surah opens with five oaths — and on Ibn Kathir's reading, every one of them is an oath **by angels**: angels tearing souls out of the dying, angels drawing them out gently, angels swimming and racing through the heavens, angels administering the affairs of creation by their Lord's command. The thing sworn to is not stated. It does not need to be: the resurrection arrives in the next breath, in two blasts of the Trumpet.

Then comes the objection, quoted in the words of the Quraysh themselves — *shall we really be brought back out of the graves, after we are rotted bones?* The surah answers it three ways, and the structure of the whole surah is really just these three answers:

1. **A precedent** — Musa and Fir'awn: a man who *knew* the truth, rejected it anyway, and was made an example of in both worlds.
2. **An argument from creation** — you think rebuilding you is hard? Look up. The sky is harder, and it is already built.
3. **A verdict** — two men, two dispositions, two destinations, in four verses.

It closes on the one question it refuses to answer: *when?* The Prophet ﷺ himself is told he has no share in that knowledge — only in the warning.

---

## 1. The Five Oaths: All of Them Angels (79:1–5)

> *By those who pull out, drowning. By those who free briskly. And by the swimmers, swimming. And by the racers, racing. And by those who arrange affairs.*

Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Masruq, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Abu Salih, Abu Ad-Duha and As-Suddi all read the first oath the same way: **these are the angels who remove the souls from the Children of Adam.**

And the first two oaths, taken together, describe **two different deaths**:

- **An-Nazi'at gharqa** — some souls are drawn out with difficulty, as though the person were being *drowned* in the extraction.
- **An-Nashitat nashta** — others the angels remove with ease, as if simply *unravelling* the soul from the body, briskly. (Ibn Abbas.)

The same event, the same angels, two entirely different experiences of it. The surah says nothing about who gets which; that silence is the point.

The remaining three oaths are read as angels by an almost unbroken roll of authorities — **the swimmers** (Ibn Mas'ud, Ali, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Abu Salih), **the racers** (Ali, Masruq, Mujahid, Abu Salih, Al-Hasan Al-Basri), and **those who arrange affairs** (Ali, Mujahid, Ata, Abu Salih, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas, As-Suddi).

Al-Hasan adds the line that makes the fifth oath land: **they administer the affairs from the heaven to the earth — by the command of their Lord, the Mighty and Majestic.** The universe is not running itself. It is being run, continuously, by beings under orders.

---

## 2. The Two Blasts (79:6–9)

> *On the Day the Rajifah shakes, followed by the Radifah.*

**Ibn Abbas: these are the two blasts of the Trumpet — the first and the second.** Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak say the same.

Mujahid pairs each with its parallel elsewhere:

- **Ar-Rajifah** — *On the Day the earth and the mountains shake* (73:14).
- **Ar-Radifah** — *And the earth and mountains shall be removed from their places, and crushed with a single crushing* (69:14).

Then the human response, in two short strokes:

- **Hearts that Day will tremble** — *afraid*, say Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Qatadah.
- **Their vision humiliated** — the eyes of the people, **lowly and disgraced from what they will witness of terrors**.

---

## 3. The Objection, in Their Own Words (79:10–12)

> *They say: "Shall we indeed be brought back from Al-Hafirah? Even after we are bones Nakhirah?"*

**Al-Hafirah is the graves** (Mujahid). The speakers are the idolaters of the Quraysh — and, Ibn Kathir adds, **whoever rejects the Hereafter as they did**. Their difficulty is physical: bodies destroyed, bones disintegrated and decayed.

**Nakhirah** = decayed. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Qatadah agree, and Ibn Abbas gives the image precisely: **it is the bone once it has decayed and air has entered into it.** Hollow. Porous. Finished.

Then the sarcasm — *it would in that case be a return with loss.* Muhammad bin Ka'b reports what the Quraysh actually said: **"If Allah brings us back to life after we die, then surely we will be losers."** They were not conceding the resurrection. They were mocking it.

---

## 4. One Shout (79:13–14)

> *But it will be only a single Zajrah. When behold, they are at As-Sahirah.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is stark: this is **a matter from Allah that will not occur twice, nor will there be any opportunity to affirm it or verify it.** No process, no interval, no second look. Allah commands **Israfil** to blow into the Sur, and *the first people and the last people will all be standing before their Lord, looking.*

Three cross-references drive the speed of it home:

> *On the Day when He will call you, and you will answer with His praise and obedience, and you will think that you have stayed but a little while!* (17:52)
> *And Our commandment is but one, as the twinkling of an eye.* (54:50)
> *And the matter of the Hour is not but as a twinkling of the eye, or even nearer.* (16:77)

**As-Sahirah**, where they find themselves, is glossed three ways that converge:

- **The entire earth** — Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah, Abu Salih.
- **The face of the earth** — Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Ad-Dahhak, Ibn Zayd.
- **A level place** — Mujahid, who adds: they will be in the earth's lowest part, and will be brought out to its highest.

Ar-Rabi' bin Anas then assembles the picture of what that ground actually *is*, from three passages — the earth changed for another earth (14:48), the mountains blasted and scattered as dust until the earth is *a level smooth plain, with nothing crooked or curved* (20:105–107), and the earth seen as a levelled plain (18:47). His conclusion is one of the most quietly beautiful lines in the surah:

> **It will be an earth that no sin will be performed on it, nor will any blood be shed upon it.**

## 5. Musa and Fir'awn: The Precedent (79:15–26)

Allah tells His Messenger ﷺ about His Messenger Musa — sent to Fir'awn, aided with miracles, and met with disbelief and transgression until Allah seized the man with *a mighty and powerful punishment*. Ibn Kathir states the purpose plainly: **this is the punishment of whoever opposes you, Muhammad, and rejects what you have been sent with.** Hence the closing verse of the passage: *In this is a lesson for whoever fears.*

**The commission.** Allah called Musa in **the holy valley** — meaning purified — of **Tuwa**, which (correctly, per Ibn Kathir) is the name of a valley, as established in Surah Ta Ha.

**The invitation, and how gentle it is.** *"Would you purify yourself?"* — meaning, *will you respond to the path and way that will purify you?* Ibn Kathir renders it directly: **'will you submit and be obedient?'** And *"that I guide you to your Lord"* means *'I will guide you to the worship of your Lord'* — *so that you fear*, i.e. **so that your heart will become humble, obedient and submissive to Him, after it was hard, evil, and far away from goodness.**

Note who is being invited: the man who has just been described as having transgressed all bounds. The offer is still made.

**The refusal, and the most important sentence in the surah.** *But he denied and disobeyed.* Fir'awn's heart disbelieved, and Musa's call *could not internally or externally affect it*. And then Ibn Kathir says something that reframes the entire question of belief:

> Along with this, **his knowledge that what Musa had come with was the truth did not necessitate his being a believer in it.** This is because **recognition is the knowledge of the heart, and faith is its action** — and it is to comply with the truth and submit to it.

Fir'awn was not ignorant. He was unsubmitting. Knowing is not believing; belief is what you *do* with what you know.

**The counter-campaign.** *Then he turned back, striving* — meaning **responding to the truth with falsehood**, by gathering the magicians to confront Musa's miracles. Then he assembled his people and announced: *"I am your lord, most high."*

Ibn Abbas and Mujahid supply the chilling context: he said this **after** having already said, *"I have not known of any other god for you all other than me"* — for the past forty years.

**The seizure.** Allah *avenged Himself against him with a severe vengeance, and made an example and admonition of him for those rebellious people in the world who are like him* — a punishment in both the Hereafter and this first life. Two verses complete the picture:

> *And on the Day of Resurrection, evil indeed is the gift gifted* — the curse in this world pursued by another curse in the Hereafter (11:99).
> *And We made them leaders inviting to the Fire; and on the Day of Resurrection, they will not be helped* (28:41).

## 6. Look Up: The Argument From Creation (79:27–33)

> *Are you more difficult to create, or is the heaven that He constructed?*

This is placed here deliberately — a refutation of the objection at 79:10–11, that renewing a creation after its original state is too much. Ibn Kathir's paraphrase: **'rather, the heaven is more difficult to create than you.'** Supported by:

> *The creation of the heavens and the earth is greater than the creation of mankind* (40:57).
> *Is not the One Who created the heavens and the earth capable of creating the like of them? Yes, indeed! He is the Supreme Creator, the All-Knowing* (36:81).

Then the surah simply itemises the work already done — and each item is explained by the phrase that follows it:

- **He raised its height and perfected it** — a lofty structure, vast in its space, with equal sides, **adorned with stars** in the night and the darkness.
- **Its night He covers, and He brings out its forenoon** — its night made dark and extremely black, its day bright, luminous, shining and clear. (*Aghtasha* = made it dark — Ibn Abbas, and likewise Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr and a large group.)
- **And after that He spread the earth** — *dahaha*, explained by the very next verse: **He brought forth from it its water and its pasture.**
- **And the mountains He has fixed firmly** — He settled them, made them firm, established them in their places.

**A chronology worth noting.** Ibn Kathir refers back to Surah Ha Mim As-Sajdah: **the earth was created before the heaven, but it was only spread out after the heaven's creation.** *Dahaha* is not the earth's origination but its being drawn out — bringing forth what was already in it with a forceful action. This was Ibn Abbas's position and the one Ibn Jarir preferred.

**And the point of all of it:** *as provision and benefit for you and your cattle.* Springs made to gush, rivers made to flow, vegetation and trees and fruit made to grow, mountains set firm so the earth would be **calmly settled with its dwellers** — a means of beneficial enjoyment granted **for the period that they need them, in this worldly abode, until the end of time and the expiration of this life.**

Even the mercy comes with an expiry date attached.

---

## 7. The Greatest Catastrophe, and the Two Men (79:34–41)

> *But when there comes At-Tammah al-Kubra — the Greatest Catastrophe.*

The Day of Judgement (Ibn Abbas), **called this because it will overcome every matter** — frightful and horrifying. As Allah says: *And the Hour will be more grievous and more bitter* (54:46).

Three things happen at once:

- **Man remembers what he strove for** — the Son of Adam will reflect on **all** of his deeds, the good and the evil alike. Paired with *On the Day will man remember, but how will that remembrance avail him?* (89:23).
- **Hell is made apparent for whoever sees** — it becomes visible to the onlookers, **so the people will see it with their own eyes.**
- **And then the sorting**, in two matched pairs:

| | |
|---|---|
| **He transgressed** — rebelled and behaved arrogantly | **He feared the standing before his Lord** — feared Allah's judgement of him |
| **And preferred the life of this world** — gave it precedence over the matters of his religion and his Hereafter | **And forbade himself from desire** — prevented his soul from following its desires, and compelled it to obey its Master |
| **→ Hell is his abode** — his food from the tree of **Zaqqum**, his drink from **Hamim** | **→ Paradise is his abode** — his final destination and place of return, the spacious Paradise |

Notice what the two men are distinguished by. Not knowledge — Fir'awn had that. **Preference.** One preferred this life; the other feared a standing and said no to himself.

---

## 8. The Question That Gets No Answer (79:42–46)

> *They ask you about the Hour — when will be its appointed time?*

The answer is a refusal, and a total one: **its knowledge is not with you, nor with any creature.** *To your Lord it is limited* — He alone knows the exact time of its occurrence.

> *Heavy is its burden through the heavens and the earth. It shall not come upon you except all of a sudden. They ask you as if you have a good knowledge of it. Say: "The knowledge thereof is with Allah."* (7:187)

And when **Jibril** himself asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about the Hour, he answered:

> **"The one questioned about it knows no more than the questioner."**

What the Prophet ﷺ *does* have is stated immediately: *You are only a warner for those who fear it.* Ibn Kathir puts it in the first person: **'I sent you to warn mankind and caution them to beware of the torment and punishment of Allah. So whoever fears Allah, fears standing before Him and His threat, then he will follow you, and thus be successful and victorious. However, whoever denies you and opposes you, then he will only suffer loss and failure.'**

**The final verse, and the surah's last word on time:**

> *The Day they see it, it will be as if they had not tarried except an afternoon or its morning.*

When they stand up from their graves to go to the place of Gathering, **they will feel that the period of the worldly life was short — it will seem to them that it was only the afternoon of one day.**

Juwaybir, from Ad-Dahhak, from Ibn Abbas: **'Ashiyyah** is the time between noon and the setting of the sun; **Duha** is what lies between sunrise and midday. Qatadah: this refers to **the time period of the worldly life in the eyes of the people, when they see the Hereafter.**

Not a small life. A *half-day* life — and only realised as such once the comparison is finally available.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**An-Nazi'at is a surah about the gap between knowing and submitting.**

It opens with angels pulling souls out — some roughly, some gently — and closes with those same souls standing on a scoured earth, discovering that everything they preferred this world for lasted about as long as an afternoon.

In between, it puts up the perfect test case. **Fir'awn knew.** Ibn Kathir is explicit: his knowledge that Musa had come with the truth *did not necessitate his being a believer in it*, because **recognition is the knowledge of the heart, and faith is its action.** He was offered purification, gently, and preferred to be lord instead.

So the surah does not ask you to be convinced. It assumes you already are — the sky is over your head, the water and pasture came out of the ground, the mountains are holding still, and all of it is on loan *until the end of time and the expiration of this life*. What it asks is the only thing that separates the two men at 79:37–41:

**One preferred this world. One feared the standing and refused himself something.**

And when you ask *how long do I have* — the honest answer, given by the Prophet ﷺ to Jibril, is that nobody knows. Which is precisely why the surah spends no time on the timetable and all of its time on the disposition.
