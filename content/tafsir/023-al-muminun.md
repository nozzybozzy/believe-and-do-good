*Revealed in Makkah · 118 ayat · "The Believers" — named for the very first word of promise in the surah*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Mu'minun is built on a single word carried from one end of it to the other: **falah** — success, victory, deliverance.

It opens with **"Successful indeed are the believers"** (23:1) and closes with **"Surely, the disbelievers will not be successful"** (23:117). Everything between those two verses is the working-out of that contrast. The surah first draws the portrait of the successful person in seven brief strokes, then spends the rest of its length asking, over and over, in different registers: *what exactly did the other side think they were succeeding at?*

Its method is threefold — **look at how you were made** (the embryo, the seven heavens, the rain, the olive, the cattle); **look at what happened to those who said this before** (Nuh, 'Ad/Thamud, Musa, the nameless generations); and **look at what you will say at the end** (*send me back* — refused; *how long did you stay?* — a day, or part of a day).

---

## 1. The Seven Qualities of the Successful (23:1–11)

> *Successful indeed are the believers.*

Ibn Kathir: they have attained victory and are blessed — they have *succeeded*. Then the characteristics:

| Quality | Ibn Kathir's gloss |
|---|---|
| **Khushu' in prayer** | Ibn Abbas: fear and tranquility. Ali and Ibrahim An-Nakha'i: the khushu' of *the heart*. Al-Hasan Al-Basri: "Their khushu' was in their hearts" — so they lowered their gaze and were humble toward others |
| **Turning from al-Laghw** | Falsehood — which includes shirk, sin, and any word or deed of no benefit |
| **Paying Zakah** | Most commentators: charity on wealth. But since this is Makkan and Zakah was legislated in Madinah (2 H), it may also mean **purification of the soul from shirk and filth**. Ibn Kathir: *possibly both are intended*, since purifying wealth is part of purifying the self |
| **Guarding chastity** | Within the lawful there is no blame; beyond it, one is an aggressor |
| **Keeping trusts and covenants** | The mirror image of the hadith on the hypocrite: lies when he speaks, breaks what he promises, betrays what he is entrusted with |
| **Strictly guarding the prayers** | Persistently offering them *at their appointed times*. Qatadah: "at the fixed times, with the proper bowing and prostration" |

**Note the frame.** The list *begins* with Salah and *ends* with Salah. Ibn Kathir reads this as deliberate — a statement of its rank, echoing the hadith: *"Know that the best of your deeds is Salah. None will preserve his wudu' except the believer."*

On khushu' he adds one of the most quotable lines in the whole tafsir: it is only attained by the one who **empties his heart totally**, pays no attention to anything besides the prayer, and prefers it above all else. At that point it becomes what the Prophet ﷺ called it — *"Salah was made the joy of my eye."*

### The inheritance

> *These are indeed the heirs. Who shall inherit Firdaws.*

*"If you ask Allah for Paradise, ask Him for Al-Firdaws — the highest part of Paradise, in its middle, from which spring the rivers of Paradise, and above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful."*

Why "inherit"? Because of the hadith of Abu Hurayrah: **every single person has two homes — one in Paradise and one in Hell.** Whoever enters Hell forfeits his home in Paradise, and the people of Paradise inherit it. Mujahid explains the logic: all were created to worship Allah alone; those who did what they were created for receive their own portion *and* the portion of those who refused.

## 2. Made from an Extract of Clay (23:12–16)

The embryology passage — one of the most famous in the Qur'an.

Adam from **tin** (clay), which itself came from dust gathered from across the earth. Ibn Kathir cites the hadith of Abu Musa: *Allah created Adam from a handful He picked up from throughout the earth, so the sons of Adam came forth accordingly — red and white and black and in between, evil and good and in between.*

Then his offspring, stage by stage: **nutfah → 'alaqah (clot) → mudghah (little lump) → bones → clothed with flesh → "another creation."** On that last phrase Ibn Kathir says: *then We breathed the soul into it, and it moved and became a new creature — one that could hear, see, understand and move.* Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas extends the stages past birth: infant, child, adolescent, youth, mature man, old man, senile man.

The safe lodging is the womb, "prepared and readily equipped for that."

And then the great hadith of Ibn Mas'ud, from *the Truthful One*: forty days as a nutfah, forty as a clot, forty as a lump; then the angel breathes in the soul and four things are decreed — **provision, lifespan, deeds, and whether wretched or blessed.** A man may do the deeds of Paradise until a forearm's length remains between him and it, and the decree overtakes him; and a man may do the deeds of Hell until a forearm's length remains, and the decree overtakes him the other way.

The sequence ends where it must: *So Blessed is Allah, the Best of creators* → *After that, surely you will die* → *Then surely you will be resurrected.* Creation, death, return — stated without a gap between them.

## 3. Seven Heavens, and Water Sent Down in Measure (23:17–22)

Allah pairs the creation of man with the creation of the heavens, as He so often does. *Seven Tara'iq* — Mujahid: the seven heavens. And the closing clause: **"and We are never unaware of the creation."** Ibn Kathir unfolds this into a meditation: no heaven conceals another from Him, no earth conceals another; there is no mountain but He knows its features, no sea but He knows its depths; He knows the count of what is in the hills, the sands, the seas and the trees. *Not a leaf falls but He knows it.*

Then rain — **"water in measure."** Not so much that it destroys lands and buildings, not so little that crops fail: exactly what is needed. Ibn Kathir illustrates with Egypt, a land he calls essentially barren sand — so Allah sends it the Nile, which carries down red soil from the Ethiopian rains to fertilise it. His comment: *Glory be to the Subtle One, the All-Knowing, the Most Merciful, the Forgiving.*

And **"verily, We are able to take it away"** becomes a list of everything that could have been otherwise: He could withhold the rain; divert it to the wilderness; make it salt; leave it standing on the surface unabsorbed; or sink it so deep underground you could never reach it. *But by His grace He causes sweet fresh water to fall, and it settles in the earth and becomes springs and rivers* — you irrigate with it, drink it, water your livestock, and purify yourselves with it.

Then the gardens of dates and grapes — and Ibn Kathir pauses to make a universal point: these were simply the fruits the people of the Hijaz knew. **The people of every region have their own fruits, which are Allah's blessing on them, and for which they can never properly give thanks.**

The **olive tree of Tur Sinai** (*tur* = a mountain — some say a mountain is only called *tur* if it has trees on it): oil and a condiment in one. *"Eat olive oil as a condiment and apply it as oil, for it comes from a blessed tree."*

And the **cattle**: milk that comes out from between dung and blood; meat; wool and hair for clothing; backs to ride on and to carry loads to lands you could not otherwise reach.

## 4. The Same Objection, Four Times Over (23:23–49)

Four prophetic missions in rapid succession, and Ibn Kathir shows that the disbelieving reply barely changes.

**Nuh** — *"He is no more than a human being like you, seeking to make himself superior. Had Allah willed, He would have sent angels."* And then the dismissal that recurs across the Qur'an: *he is only a man in whom is madness — so wait for him a while*, i.e. **wait until he dies; put up with him until you are rid of him.**

Nuh's answer is a single line: *"O my Lord! Help me because they deny me."* The ark is built "under Our Eyes." He is told to take a pair of each kind, and his family — *except those against whom the Word has already gone forth*. Ibn Kathir's gloss on the instruction not to plead for the wrongdoers is stark: **when you see the heavy rain begin to fall, do not let compassion overcome you and do not ask for more time for them.**

Two du'as are taught in the story: one on boarding (*All praise to Allah, Who has saved us from the wrongdoing people*) and one on landing (*My Lord, cause me to land at a blessed landing place*). Ibn Kathir notes: **Nuh mentioned Allah at the beginning of the journey and at the end** — and connects the first to the du'a we are taught for mounting any vehicle.

**The generation after** — 'Ad or Thamud (Ibn Kathir leaves it open; the mention of the *Sayhah*, the Cry, points toward Thamud). Same objection, now with an added sneer: *he eats what you eat and drinks what you drink.* And the flat denial of resurrection — *there is nothing but our life of this world.* The same prayer is offered — *help me because they deny me* — and the answer comes: **"In a little while, they are sure to be regretful."** Then the Cry, and *We made them as rubbish of dead plants* — the scum a flood leaves behind, useless to anyone.

**The nameless generations** — *no nation can advance its term nor delay it.* Messengers sent in succession; each denied by the majority; each people destroyed and turned into **Ahadith — stories, tales, lessons for those who came after.**

**Musa and Harun** to Fir'awn: *"Shall we believe in two men like ourselves, whose people are our servants?"* Ibn Kathir marks a turning point here that is easy to miss: **after the Tawrah was revealed, Allah no longer destroyed a nation with an overwhelming calamity** — instead He commanded the believers to fight.

## 5. 'Isa and Maryam, and the Question of the Rabwah (23:50)

Mother and son together are *a sign*. Ibn Kathir's framing is elegant: Adam was created without father or mother; Hawwa' from a male without a female; **'Isa from a female without a male**; and the rest of mankind from both — a complete set of possibilities, demonstrating that He does what He wills.

On the *rabwah* — the high ground with a settled place and flowing water — he catalogues the opinions honestly: Damascus (Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib, Abdullah bin Salam, Al-Hasan, Zayd bin Aslam, Khalid bin Ma'dan), Ramlah in Palestine, and Jerusalem. He settles on **Jerusalem**, and states his reasoning as a rule of method:

> Parts of the Qur'an explain other parts — so it is more appropriate to interpret a verse by another verse, then by the sound hadiths, then by other reports.

## 6. Lawful Food and Righteous Deeds (23:51–52)

> *O Messengers! Eat of the Tayyibat and do righteous deeds.*

Ibn Kathir draws the causal link the verse implies: **eating what is lawful helps a person to do righteous deeds.** He notes the prophets' own working lives — every prophet herded sheep (the Prophet ﷺ: *"Yes, I used to tend the sheep of the people of Makkah for a few qirats"*); Dawud ate from the earnings of his own hand.

Then the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, which pairs this verse with 2:172 and ends in one of the most sobering images in the Sunnah: a man on a long journey, dusty and dishevelled, stretching his hands to the sky calling *"O Lord, O Lord!"* — **while his food, drink and clothing are unlawful, and he has nourished himself with the unlawful.** How, then, can he be answered?

And then: *this your religion is one religion* — the call of every prophet is the same call — **but they broke it into sects, each group rejoicing in what it has.** Ibn Kathir: they rejoice in their misguidance *because they think they are rightly guided*.

## 7. Wealth and Children Are Not the Scoreboard (23:55–56)

> *Do they think that in wealth and children with which We prolong them, We hasten unto them with good things? Nay, but they perceive not.*

The correction of a whole way of reading the world. Do the deceived imagine that what We give them of wealth and children means they are **honoured and precious in Our sight?** No — *We only give those things to them in order to let them go further and to give them more time.*

Ibn Kathir stacks the parallel verses: *let not their wealth nor their children amaze you* (9:55); *We postpone the punishment only so that they may increase in sinfulness* (3:178); *it is not your wealth nor your children that bring you nearer to Us, but only he who believes and does righteous deeds* (34:37).

Then the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud, which states the principle exactly:

> **Allah gives the things of this world to those whom He loves and those whom He does not love, but He gives religious commitment only to those whom He loves. Whoever is given religious commitment by Allah is loved by Him.**

The same hadith continues into two further lines worth keeping: *no servant truly submits until his heart and his tongue submit, and he does not truly believe until his neighbour is safe from his harm*; and — on unlawful wealth — *if he gives it in charity it is not accepted, and if he leaves it behind it becomes his provision in the Fire. Allah does not wash away an evil deed with another; impurity cannot be washed away with impurity.*

## 8. Those Who Give While Their Hearts Tremble (23:57–61)

A second portrait of the successful, deeper than the first. They live in awe of their Lord *even though* they have reached ihsan — Al-Hasan Al-Basri's formulation is the key to the passage:

> **"The believer combines good deeds with awe, while the disbeliever combines evil deeds with a sense of security."**

Then the verse A'ishah misread, and the correction that has been quoted ever since. She asked: *"And those who give what they give with their hearts full of fear"* — are these the ones who **steal and commit zina and drink alcohol** while fearing Allah?

> *"No, O daughter of As-Siddiq. They are the ones who pray and fast and give in charity — while fearing that it will not be accepted from them."*

The fear is not the fear of the sinner. It is the fear of the obedient that their obedience may not have met its conditions. *These are the ones who hasten to good deeds, and they are foremost in them.*

## 9. The Justice, and Then the Reckoning (23:62–83)

**No soul is burdened beyond its capacity**, and the record *speaks the truth*, and nothing of a person's good deeds is omitted — while for the believers, many evil deeds are forgiven and overlooked.

Against that justice, the surah sets the behaviour of the deniers:

- **Their hearts are covered** from the Qur'an, through negligence and misguidance.
- When the punishment finally seizes **"those of them who lead a luxurious life,"** they scream for help — and are told, *Invoke not loudly this day; you will not be helped by Us.* No escape, whether you scream or stay silent.
- Their greatest sin: *My Ayat used to be recited to you, but you used to turn back on your heels.*
- **"In pride, talking evil about it by night."** Ibn Abbas explains the strange specificity: the Quraysh boasted of guardianship of the Ka'bah and would stay up around it at night talking — *"we are its people, we who stay up talking at night."* Ibn Kathir's verdict: **they did not use it for its proper purpose, so in effect they had abandoned it.**

Then the four rhetorical questions of 23:68–70 — *Have they not pondered the Word? Has something come to them that never came to their forefathers? Do they not recognise their Messenger? Or do they say there is madness in him?* — each dismantled:

- **On pondering.** Qatadah: *"By Allah, if the people had pondered its meaning and understood it properly, they would have found in the Qur'an a deterrent to disobeying Allah. But they only paid attention to the verses that are not entirely clear, and they were destroyed because of that."*
- **On recognising him.** They knew his honesty and trustworthiness perfectly well. Ibn Kathir gathers the witnesses: **Ja'far bin Abi Talib** to the Negus — *"Allah has sent us a Messenger whose lineage, honesty and trustworthiness are known to us"*; **Al-Mughirah bin Shu'bah** saying the same to the envoy of Kisra; and most tellingly **Abu Sufyan** before Heraclius, *still a disbeliever at the time*, unable to say anything but the truth about him.

**If the Truth had followed their desires, the heavens and earth would be corrupted.** Mujahid, Abu Salih and As-Suddi read *Al-Haqq* here as Allah Himself. The point: had Allah legislated according to what people wanted, the universe would collapse under the incoherence of their conflicting desires. Ibn Kathir supports this with 43:31–32 (*why wasn't this Qur'an sent to a great man of the two towns?* — *is it they who apportion the mercy of your Lord?*) and 17:100 (*if you possessed the treasures of my Lord's mercy, you would hold back for fear of spending*).

**He asks no wage.** Al-Hasan: a reward. Qatadah: a payment. He asks for none of it.

**And even if the distress were lifted** — *they would obstinately persist in their transgression, wandering blindly.* Ibn Kathir attaches Ibn Abbas's grammatical observation here: everything introduced with *"if" (lawu)* in this construction is something **that will never happen** — but Allah knows how it would be if it did.

The historical anchor comes with 23:76: *We seized them with punishment, but they humbled not themselves.* Ibn Abbas reports **Abu Sufyan coming to the Prophet ﷺ**, pleading by Allah and by kinship: *"we have been reduced to eating camel hair and blood"* — the famine that followed the Prophet's ﷺ supplication, *"O Allah, help me against them with seven years like the seven of Yusuf."* Even that did not humble them.

Then the blessings again, tightly: **hearing, eyes, hearts** — *little thanks you give*. He created you and scatters you across the earth, and gathers you back; He gives life and death; night and day alternate by His command, never departing from the pattern. *Will you not then understand?*

## 10. The Three Questions They Could Not Answer Wrongly (23:84–92)

The centrepiece of the surah's argument, and structurally beautiful:

| The question | Their answer |
|---|---|
| *Whose is the earth and whoever is in it?* | **"It is Allah's."** → *Will you not then remember?* |
| *Who is Lord of the seven heavens and Lord of the Great Throne?* | **"Allah."** → *Will you not then have taqwa?* |
| *In Whose Hand is the sovereignty of everything — who protects while none can protect against Him?* | **"To Allah."** → *How then are you deceived?* |

Ibn Kathir names the principle exactly: **the idolaters already believed in Tawhid ar-Rububiyyah — Allah as sole Creator, Owner and Controller — and that belief obligates Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah, that He alone be worshipped.** They conceded that what they worshipped could create nothing, owned nothing and controlled nothing; they claimed only that these things *brought them nearer* to Allah. The surah simply holds their own admission up to them.

On *"He protects, while against Whom there is no protector"* Ibn Kathir uses an Arab custom to make it felt: when a chief extended his protection to someone, no one could act against him — **yet no one could offer protection against the chief himself.** So it is, absolutely, with Allah. *He cannot be questioned about what He does, while they will be questioned* (21:23).

Then the argument against plurality of gods (23:91), which Ibn Kathir presents both scripturally and as the theologians' **argument from mutual resistance (tamanu')**: were there two creators, one would will a body to move and the other to be still. If neither prevailed, both are incapable — and the One whose existence is necessary cannot be incapable. If one prevails, *he* is God and the other is merely a possible being. Either way, plurality collapses. And the empirical half of the same point: if each god took away what he created, there would be no order in the universe — *but what we see is a universe ordered and cohesive, its upper and lower realms connected in the most perfect fashion.* **"You can see no fault in the creation of the Most Gracious"** (67:3).

## 11. How to Live in the Meantime (23:93–98)

Four instructions to the Prophet ﷺ, and through him to everyone:

1. **A du'a for when calamity comes**: *My Lord, if You show me what they are threatened with, then do not put me among the wrongdoing people.* Compare the hadith Ibn Kathir cites: *"If You want to test people, then take me to You without having to undergo the test."*
2. **Repel evil with what is better.** Ibn Kathir: this is the best way of behaving among people — **treat well the one who treats you badly, so as to soften his heart and turn his enmity into friendship, and his hatred into love.** With 41:34–35 attached: *none is granted this except those who are patient, and none is granted it except the owner of a great portion.*
3. **Seek refuge from the whisperings of the Shayatin** — because, unlike human enemies, **no trick helps against them and you cannot protect yourself by being kind to them.** Kindness works on people; only refuge works on devils.
4. **And seek refuge lest they come near you** — *in any matter of my life*. Hence the command to mention Allah at the start of every undertaking: eating, intimacy, slaughtering, and the rest.

## 12. "My Lord, Send Me Back" (23:99–100)

The most quoted verse of the surah, and Ibn Kathir gives it no comfort.

> *Until, when death comes to one of them, he says: "My Lord! Send me back, so that I may do good in what I left behind." **No!** It is but a word that he speaks.*

**Kalla** is a word of rebuke: *We will not respond to what he asks and We will not accept it from him.* And the request itself is dismissed as **"just talk on his part, unaccompanied by any action"** — if he were sent back, he would not do righteous deeds; *if they were returned, they would return to what they were forbidden* (6:28).

But Qatadah's comment turns the verse back on the living, and it is the single most useful line in the surah:

> **"By Allah, he will not wish to go back to his family and tribe, or to accumulate more of this world or satisfy his desires — he will wish only that he could go back to acts of obedience to Allah. May Allah have mercy on a man who does what the disbeliever will wish he had done when he sees the punishment of Hell."**

**Barzakh** — Mujahid: a barrier between this world and the Hereafter. Muhammad bin Ka'b: those in it are *neither of this world, eating and drinking, nor with the people of the Hereafter, being rewarded or punished.* Abu Sakhr: it refers to the graves. Ibn Kathir reads *"in front of them is Barzakh"* as a **threat of punishment in the Barzakh itself**, continuing until the Resurrection.

## 13. The Day Itself (23:101–116)

**The Trumpet, and the end of kinship.** *No kinship among them that Day, nor will they ask of one another.* Lineage will avail nothing; a father will not ask after his son. Ibn Mas'ud's image is worse than mere indifference: a call will go out — *whoever is owed something by another, let him come and take it* — and **a man will rejoice to find that he is owed something by his own father, child or wife, however little.**

**The Scales.** Ibn Abbas: whoever's good outweighs his bad, *even by one*, has succeeded. *They are the ones who attained what they wanted and were saved from an evil from which there is no escape.*

**The plea, and the closing of the door.** *Our Lord, our wretchedness overcame us... bring us out of this; if we return, we shall indeed be wrongdoers.* The reply: **"Remain you in it with ignominy! And speak you not to Me!"** Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas: *these are the words of Ar-Rahman when silencing them.* Abdullah bin 'Amr's report fills in the timeline — they call on Malik for forty years with no answer; then they call on their Lord, and the reply is withheld for twice the duration of this world; and after that reply **they will not utter a single word again.**

**And the reason given is mockery.** Before the file is closed, Allah reminds them of what they laughed at:

> *There was a party of My servants who used to say, "Our Lord! We believe, so forgive us and have mercy on us, for You are the Best of those who show mercy!" But you took them for a laughing stock, so much so that they made you forget My remembrance, while you used to laugh at them. **Verily, I have rewarded them this Day for their patience; they are indeed the ones who are successful.***

Ibn Kathir: *your hatred for them made you forget what I would do to you.* Note that the mocked believers' only recorded act here is **a du'a** — and it is the same du'a the surah will end on.

**"How long did you stay on earth?"** *A day, or part of a day — ask those who keep account.* The whole life, weighed from the other side, does not register. Ibn Kathir: *you would not have preferred the transient to the eternal, had you known.*

**"Did you think We created you in play?"** Either: with no purpose, nothing required of you and no wisdom on Our part — or: to amuse yourselves like the animals, who face no reward or punishment. **But you were created to worship Allah and carry out His commands.**

---

## 14. The Ending (23:117–118)

> *And whoever invokes besides Allah any other god, **of whom he has no proof** — his reckoning is only with his Lord. Surely, the disbelievers will not be successful.*

Ibn Kathir highlights the clause *of whom he has no proof*: the idolaters had **no evidence whatsoever** for what they did. It was inherited — *"We found our fathers following a certain way, and we will follow their footsteps"* (43:23). That absence of proof is what makes it lying and misguidance rather than error.

And then the last verse of the surah is a supplication placed in our mouths:

> *And say: "My Lord! Forgive and have mercy, for You are the Best of those who show mercy!"*

Ibn Kathir's definitions of the two words are worth memorising: **forgiveness** is the wiping away of sins and their concealment from people; **mercy** is being guided and helped to say and do good things. One clears the past; the other supplies the future.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Success is defined at the start and denied at the end.** *Qad aflaha al-mu'minun* (23:1) and *la yuflih al-kafirun* (23:117) are the two posts the whole surah is strung between. Everything else is evidence.
- **The list of the successful opens and closes with Salah**, and khushu' is located in the heart, not the limbs.
- **Wealth and children are not a verdict.** Allah gives dunya to those He loves and those He does not; He gives *din* only to those He loves.
- **The believer fears while obeying**; the disbeliever feels safe while sinning. A'ishah's question settles what the trembling in 23:60 actually is — the fear that one's prayer, fast and charity might not be accepted.
- **They knew.** They admitted the earth is Allah's, the seven heavens are His, the sovereignty of everything is in His Hand. The surah's argument is not that they lacked information but that they refused the conclusion.
- **Repel evil with what is better** — with people. **Seek refuge** — with devils. Different enemies, different weapons.
- **"Send me back" is refused**, and Qatadah tells you what to do with that information now, while the request would still be honoured.
- **A whole lifetime reads, from the far side, as "a day or part of a day."** And it was never play.
