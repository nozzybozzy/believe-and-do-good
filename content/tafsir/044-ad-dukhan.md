*Revealed in Makkah · 59 ayat · named for the smoke that will cover mankind, 44:10*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ad-Dukhan is a short Makkan surah that moves fast between four things: **a night, a smoke, a drowned tyrant, and two tables** — one set with Zaqqum and boiling water, the other with fruit and fine silk.

The logic connecting them is warning. The surah opens by establishing that the Qur'an came down on a blessed night as an act of mercy and as a warning — *Verily, We are ever warning* — and then asks what a people do who are warned and still play. It answers with a threat they can see coming (the smoke), a case study of a people who ignored a noble Messenger and were swallowed by the sea (Fir'awn), a mocking dismissal of the argument that the dead never come back (Tubba'), and finally the two destinations laid side by side.

That last pairing is why Ibn Kathir pauses here to note that the Qur'an is called **Al-Mathani** — the oft-repeated: when it describes the state of the doomed, it follows it with the state of the blessed.

The surah is also famous for a hadith at its head: the Prophet ﷺ once concealed this very surah in his mind and tested Ibn Sayyad on it. Ibn Sayyad, catching only a fragment through the Jinn, said *"It is Ad-Dukh."* The Prophet ﷺ told him: **Be off with you! You cannot get further than your rank.**

## 1. The Blessed Night (44:1–8)

> *By the manifest Book. We sent it down on a blessed night.*

The blessed night is **Laylatul-Qadr**, cross-referenced to *We sent it down in the Night of Al-Qadr* (97:1), and located in Ramadan by *The month of Ramadan in which was revealed the Qur'an* (2:185).

**"Therein is decreed every matter, Hakim."** Ibn Kathir gives the Salaf's reading — Ibn Umar, Mujahid, Abu Malik, Ad-Dahhak and others: on that night the decrees are transferred **from Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz to the angelic scribes**, who write down the coming year's affairs — life spans, provision, and everything that will happen until the year ends. *Hakim* means **decided and confirmed: it cannot be changed or altered.**

Three qualifications follow, and each is a claim about God's motive:

- **"As a command from Us"** — everything decreed and every revelation sent down happens by His command, His leave, His knowledge.
- **"Verily, We are ever sending"** — Messengers who recite Allah's clear signs to mankind. Ibn Kathir adds simply: *the need for this was urgent.*
- **"A mercy from your Lord"** — the warning is not hostility. It tells people what benefits and what harms them, *so that the proof of Allah may be established against His servants.*

The passage closes on the identity of the One who sent it: Lord of the heavens and the earth and all between, *La ilaha illa Huwa*, who gives life and causes death — your Lord **and the Lord of your forefathers** (cf. 7:158).

## 2. The Smoke: The Great Disagreement (44:9–16)

> *Nay! They play about in doubt. Then wait you for the Day when the sky will bring forth a visible smoke, covering mankind. This is a painful torment.*

The certain truth has reached them, and they doubt it and play. Then comes the threat — and with it, the surah's most debated passage. Ibn Kathir lays out **two full positions and then rules.**

### Position One — it already happened (Ibn Mas'ud)

Masruq's account: a man in the masjid of Kufah told his companions the smoke was a future sign that would blind and deafen the hypocrites but affect believers like a head cold. They took it to **Ibn Mas'ud**, who was lying down and *sat up with a start*. He quoted 38:86 — *Say: "No wage do I ask of you for this, nor am I one of the pretenders"* — and added the principle that frames the whole dispute:

> *It is part of knowledge that when a man does not know something, he should say, "Allah knows best."*

His explanation: when Quraysh refused Islam and grew stubborn, the Prophet ﷺ invoked against them **years like the years of Yusuf**. Famine reduced them to eating bones and carrion; a man would look up and see nothing between himself and the sky but a smoky haze, from sheer exhaustion. When rain was finally granted at a man's plea for Mudar, 44:15 came down — *We shall remove the torment for a while; verily, you will revert* — and revert they did. The **Batshah**, on this reading, was **Badr**.

Ibn Mas'ud's famous list: *Five things have come to pass — the smoke, the Romans, the moon, the Batshah, and the torment.* Narrated in the Two Sahihs, and followed by Mujahid, Abu Al-'Aliyah, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Ad-Dahhak, 'Atiyah Al-'Awfi, and Ibn Jarir.

### Position Two — it is still coming

The hadith of Hudhayfah bin Asid Al-Ghifari, recorded by Muslim: the Prophet ﷺ looked out on Companions discussing the Hour and listed **ten signs** before it —

> the sun rising from the west · **the smoke** · the beast · Ya'juj and Ma'juj · the appearance of 'Isa bin Maryam · the Dajjal · three collapses of the earth (east, west, and the Arabian Peninsula) · and a fire emerging from the bottom of Aden that drives the people before it, stopping when they stop.

And the Ibn Sayyad incident in the Two Sahihs points the same way: the surah the Prophet ﷺ concealed was *this* verse about a smoke yet to appear.

### The ruling

Ibn Kathir sides with the second: there are **numerous hadiths, Marfu' and Mawquf, Sahih and Hasan**, placing the smoke among the awaited signs, *and this is also the apparent meaning of the Ayat*. His decisive textual argument is one word — **"covering mankind."** A hunger-induced illusion seen by some idolaters in Makkah would not be described that way. And *"a visible smoke"* means clearly visible: **such that all people will see it.**

The rest of the passage tracks the same pattern of doomed pleading:

- **"Our Lord! Remove the torment from us, really we shall become believers!"** — the same bargain offered in 6:27 and 14:44, and answered by *Had you not sworn aforetime that you would not leave the world?*
- **"How can there be for them an admonition"** — a clear Messenger already came, and they turned away calling him *one taught, a madman*. Remembrance offered too late is worth nothing (89:23, 34:51–52).
- **"You will revert"** — because release from pressure does not produce faith (23:75; 6:28: *if they were returned, they would revert to what they were forbidden, and indeed they are liars*).

**On the Batshah:** Ibn Mas'ud said Badr; **Ibn Abbas said the Day of Resurrection** — a report Ibn Kathir grades *Sahih* to him, and the position of Al-Hasan Al-Basri and Ikrimah. Ibn Kathir's own verdict: Badr is possible and was indeed a day of vengeance, **but the apparent meaning is the Day of Resurrection.**

## 3. Musa and Fir'awn: A People Warned by a Noble Messenger (44:17–33)

The historical proof, offered because *We tried before them Fir`awn's people* — the same test, run once already.

Musa's demand is compressed into four lines, and Ibn Kathir reads each as an ethic:

| Musa says | Ibn Kathir's gloss |
|---|---|
| *Deliver to me the servants of Allah* | Let the Children of Israel go, and torment them not (20:47) |
| *I am to you a Messenger worthy of all trust* | What I convey to you is trustworthy |
| *Exalt not yourselves against Allah* | Do not be too arrogant to follow His signs — the arrogance of 40:60 |
| *If you believe me not, then keep away from me* | Let us leave one another alone and live in peace **until Allah judges between us** |

**"Lest you should stone me"** — Ibn Abbas and Abu Salih read this as **verbal assault: insults.** Qatadah reads it literally: refuge from any harmful word *or action* reaching him.

Only after long years, with the proof established and their stubbornness increasing, does Musa pray against them — the prayer of 10:88–89, which Allah answers: *Verily, the invocation of you both is accepted.*

**"Leave the sea as it is."** Musa wanted to strike the parted water so it would close behind him as a barrier. He was told not to. Ibn Abbas: *leave it as it is and keep moving.* Mujahid: leave the dry path — *do not command it to go back; leave it until the last of them have entered it.* The trap is meant to be walked into: *they are a host to be drowned.*

**The inheritance they left (44:25–29):** gardens, springs, crops, "goodly places" — fine dwellings, and per Mujahid and Sa'id bin Jubayr, **elevated ones** — and comforts they took delight in. Eating what they wanted, wearing what they liked, wealth and glory and power in the land. *Then all of that was taken away in a single morning.*

Then the line that stops the reader: **"And the heavens and the earth wept not for them."** A man asked Ibn Abbas whether the heavens and earth weep for anyone at all. His answer:

> Every person has a gate in the heavens through which his provision descends and his good deeds ascend. When a believer dies, **that gate is closed; it misses him and weeps for him** — and the place on earth where he prayed and remembered Allah weeps for him too. But the people of Fir'awn left no trace of righteousness on the earth and had no deeds ascending to Allah. So nothing wept.

Fir'awn is summed up as *arrogant and of the excessive* — proud, stubborn, and holding a foolish opinion of himself (28:4, 23:46). The Children of Israel were then **chosen above the nations**, which Mujahid and Qatadah both restrict to **the people of their own time**, on the pattern of *I have chosen you above men* to Musa (7:144) and *chosen you above the women of the nations* to Maryam (3:42) — Ibn Kathir noting that Khadijah is higher than Maryam or equal to her, as is Asiyah the wife of Fir'awn, and that **A'ishah's superiority over other women is like the superiority of Tharid over all other dishes.**

## 4. "Bring Back Our Forefathers" (44:34–37)

The idolaters' argument: *There is nothing but our first death, and we shall not be resurrected. Then bring back our forefathers, if you speak the truth.*

Ibn Kathir calls this **false evidence and a specious argument** — a demand for the wrong event at the wrong time. The resurrection belongs to the Day of Judgement, *not to this world*; it happens when this world has ended and ceased to be, when Allah brings all creatures back created anew.

Then the threat by comparison: **the people of Tubba'** — the kings of Himyar, i.e. Saba', destroyed and scattered through their land (as told in Surah Saba'). *Tubba'* was a title, like Chosroes for Persia, Caesar for Rome, Fir'awn for disbelieving Egypt, Negus for Ethiopia. They were Arabs descended from Qahtan as the Quraysh are Arabs descended from 'Adnan — a pointed likeness.

Ibn Kathir then tells the story of the one Tubba' who left Yemen conquering as far as Samarqand and founded Al-Hirah. Passing Madinah in the Jahiliyyah, he fought its people — **who fought him by day and sent him food by night**, until he felt ashamed and left them alone. Two Jewish rabbis with him warned that he would never prevail over that city, because a Prophet would migrate to it at the end of time. Passing Makkah, he meant to destroy the Ka'bah; they told him it was built by Ibrahim Al-Khalil and would matter greatly through that same coming Prophet. **So he performed Tawaf around it and covered it with a fine cloth**, then returned to Yemen and called its people to the religion of guidance — the religion of Musa, which was the guided religion of that age.

Hence the Prophet's ﷺ own reserve, reported by Abu Hurayrah: *I do not know whether Tubba' was a Prophet or not.* And 'Ata' bin Abi Rabah: *Do not revile Tubba', for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade reviling him.*

## 5. Not for Play (44:38–42)

> *We created not the heavens and the earth, and all that is between them, for mere play.*

Allah is far above play, folly and falsehood (38:27; 23:115–116: *Did you think that We had created you in play, and that you would not be brought back to Us?*).

The Day of Judgement is **"the time appointed for all of them"** — He will gather the first and the last. And there, **no relative will avail a relative**: no kinship among them that Day, nor will they ask of one another (23:101). A man will not even ask his brother how he is, *though he can see him with his own eyes* (70:10–11).

No help from within the family, none from outside. **Except him on whom Allah has mercy.** Nothing will be of any avail that Day except that — *He is the All-Mighty, the Most Merciful*: the Almighty, with immense mercy.

## 6. The Two Tables (44:43–57)

**Zaqqum.** The food of the sinners — those who sinned in word and deed, i.e. the disbelievers. Several commentators applied it to Abu Jahl; Ibn Kathir accepts he is included **but the verse is not specifically about him.**

A small teaching scene: a man reciting to Abu Ad-Darda' said *"the food of the orphan"* (*al-yatim*) instead of *al-athim*. Abu Ad-Darda' corrected him: **say, the food of the evildoer** — meaning he will have no other food but that. Mujahid: if a single drop of it fell on the earth, **it would corrupt the living of all the people of earth.**

It boils in the bellies like the dregs of oil, from its heat and rancidity. Then *Seize him* — and it was reported that when Allah says this to the keepers of Hell, **seventy thousand of them rush to seize him.** He is dragged and pushed on his back into the midst of the blaze; boiling water is poured over his head (22:19–20), an iron hooked rod splits it open, and the water goes down melting stomach and intestines until it passes through his heels. *May Allah protect us from that.* And the mockery: *Taste! Verily, you were the mighty, the generous* — Ibn Abbas via Ad-Dahhak: **meaning, you are neither mighty nor generous.**

**Then the reversal.** *Verily, those who have Taqwa will be in a place of security* — those who feared Allah and were dutiful in this world, now safe in the Hereafter from death, from the fear of ever leaving, from worry, grief, terror, exhaustion, from the Shaytan and his wiles, from all trouble and disaster. Gardens and springs — **in direct contrast to the Zaqqum and the boiling water.** Sundus, the finest silk; Istabraq, silk woven with shiny threads worn over it. Facing each other, seated so that **no one has his back to anyone else.** Hur with wide lovely eyes — a gift in addition to the wives given them (55:56, 55:58, 55:60). Every fruit they call for, brought whenever they want, with **the security of knowing the supply will never end.**

**"They will never taste death therein except the first death."** Ibn Kathir: an exception that *reinforces* the negation — the meaning is that they will never taste death there at all. Hence the hadith of the Two Sahihs: death brought forth in the image of a fine ram, made to stand between Paradise and Hell, **and slaughtered** — then it is announced, *O people of Paradise, it is eternal, no more death; O people of Hell, it is eternal, no more death.*

And the promise recorded by Muslim:

> *You will be healthy and never fall ill; you will live and never die; you will enjoy luxury and never be miserable; you will be youthful and never grow old.*

**"As a bounty from your Lord."** Ibn Kathir stresses the word: it is from His bounty and kindness, not a wage earned — and closes with the hadith of the Two Sahihs, *Work and strive hard, and know that no one will enter Paradise by virtue of his deeds.* They said: *Not even you, O Messenger of Allah?* He said: **Not even me, unless Allah showers me with His mercy and grace.**

## 7. Made Easy, and Then: Wait (44:58–59)

> *Certainly, We have made this easy in your tongue, in order that they may remember.*

The Qur'an was made plain and clear in **the most eloquent and beautiful of languages**, so that people would understand and know. And still, Ibn Kathir observes, despite all that clarity there are people who disbelieve and stubbornly go against it.

So the surah ends where it began — with waiting. *Wait then; verily, they too are waiting.* Consolation and promise in one line: **they will come to know who is victorious and whose word prevails**, in this world and the next. *Allah has decreed: "Verily, it is I and My Messengers who shall be the victorious"* (58:21); *We will indeed make victorious Our Messengers and those who believe* (40:51–52).

## If You Remember Nothing Else

1. **The Qur'an came down on Laylatul-Qadr, and it came as mercy before it came as warning.** *A mercy from your Lord* is the reason given for the whole revelation — the warning exists so that people know what helps and harms them.
2. **The smoke is still coming.** Ibn Kathir weighs Ibn Mas'ud's reading (a past famine over Makkah) against the ten-signs hadith and rules for the future sign — because *"covering mankind"* is too large a phrase for a local illusion.
3. **Relief is not repentance.** *We shall remove the torment for a while; verily, you will revert.* Faith produced only by pressure disappears when the pressure lifts.
4. **Every believer leaves a mark that grieves for him** — a gate in heaven that closes and weeps, a patch of earth where he prayed. Fir'awn's people left neither, and so nothing wept for them.
5. **Nothing you own transfers.** Gardens, springs, crops, delight — taken in a single morning; and on the Day, no relative can avail a relative in anything. **Except him on whom Allah has mercy.**
6. **Even the Prophet ﷺ enters by mercy, not by deeds.** *Not even me, unless Allah showers me with His mercy and grace.* Which is why the Garden is called *a bounty from your Lord* — and why the instruction is to work hard anyway.
