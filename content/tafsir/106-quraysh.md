*Revealed in Makkah · 4 ayat · named for the tribe it addresses · the shortest argument in the Qur'an for why a people should worship*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Quraysh is four verses long and contains a complete case. It names two gifts a tribe has been living inside without noticing, and draws the only conclusion that follows from them: **then worship the One who gave them.**

The gifts are the most basic two a human being can receive — **food and safety**. The surah does not argue for God's existence, does not describe the Hereafter, does not threaten. It simply points at the caravans that leave Makkah every winter and every summer and come back unrobbed, at the full stomachs of a tribe living in a barren valley, and says: *someone is doing this. Worship Him.*

Its other distinctive feature is that it is **half of an argument that begins in the previous surah**. Al-Fil describes what Allah did *to* the Elephant. Quraysh describes what He did it *for*.

---

## 1. The Seam Between Al-Fil and Quraysh

Ibn Kathir opens with a textual observation. In the **primary Mushaf — the original copy of 'Uthman** — this surah was separated from the one before it, and the Companions wrote *"In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful"* on the line between them.

And yet the two are directly related in meaning. **Muhammad bin Ishaq** and **'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam** both clarified that the sense runs continuously across the break:

> *"We have prevented the Elephant from entering Makkah and We have destroyed its people **in order to gather (Ilaf) the Quraysh** — to unite them and bring them together safely in their city."*

So the destruction of Abrahah's army was not an isolated act of vengeance on behalf of a building. It was **infrastructure**. The safety Quraysh enjoyed, the reputation that let their caravans pass unharmed, the trade that fed them — all of it rests on what happened in the valley the year the Elephant came.

**Ibn Jarir** holds a different reading of the grammar (below), and adds that **the consensus of the Muslims is that these are two separate and independent Surahs**. Ibn Kathir preserves both positions without forcing a choice: separate surahs, continuous meaning.

---

## 2. What *Ilaf* Means (106:1–2)

> *For the Ilaf of the Quraysh. Their Ilaf caravans, in winter and in summer.*

Two explanations are given:

**(a) Ilaf as uniting.** Allah gathered them, joined them, and settled them together safely in their city.

**(b) Ilaf as the habitual journey.** What they would gather on their two annual trade journeys —

| Season | Destination |
|---|---|
| **Winter** | **Yemen** |
| **Summer** | **Ash-Sham** (Syria) |

— travelling for trade and returning to their city **in safety**. And Ibn Kathir gives the mechanism precisely: they were safe **because of the respect people had for them as the residents of Allah's sanctuary**. Whoever knew them honoured them. And the protection was contagious — *even those who came to them and travelled with them would be safe because of them*.

That covers their life on the road. For their life at home, Ibn Kathir cites:

> *Have they not seen that We have made it a secure sanctuary, while men are being snatched away from all around them.* **(29:67)**

Everywhere else, people are seized. Here, they are not. **They live in the eye of a storm and have not looked up.**

### The repetition, and the Lam of amazement

Why say *Ilaf* twice? Ibn Kathir explains it as a subject **transferred from the first sentence in order to give it more explanation** — the second mention unpacks the first.

**Ibn Jarir** offers the reading he considers correct: the opening **Lam is a prefix of amazement**. The verse is not primarily *"because of"* — it is:

> *"**You should be amazed** at the uniting of the Quraysh, and at My favour upon them in that."*

The surah, on this reading, opens by asking the reader to be astonished at something ordinary.

---

## 3. The Command (106:3)

> *So, let them worship the Lord of this House.*

Meaning: **let them single Him out for worship**, exactly as He singled them out — giving them a safe sanctuary and a Sacred House.

Note what the verse does *not* say. It does not say *worship the House*. Quraysh already revered the Ka'bah; they had filled it with idols and built their identity on custodianship of it. The verse steps past the building to **its Lord**. The gift is evidence; the Giver is the point.

Ibn Kathir sets alongside it the Prophet's ﷺ own commission in the same terms:

> *I have been commanded only to worship the Lord of this city, Who has sanctified it and to Whom belongs everything. And I am commanded to be from among the Muslims.* **(27:91)**

---

## 4. The Two Favours (106:4)

> *Who has fed them against hunger, and has made them safe from fear.*

**"Fed them against hunger"** — He is the Lord of the House, **and** He is the One who feeds them. The same source for both; the sanctuary and the sustenance are not separate accounts.

**"Made them safe from fear"** — He favours them with **safety and gentleness**. Therefore they should single Him out alone, without partner: **no idol, no rival, no statue besides Him.**

And Ibn Kathir extends the promise past Quraysh, to anyone reading:

> **Whoever accepts this command, Allah will give him safety in both this life and the Hereafter. Whoever disobeys Him, He will remove both of them from him.**

---

## 5. The Warning Underneath (16:112–113)

The surah never threatens. But Ibn Kathir closes by placing beside it the verse that shows what happens when a town in exactly this position refuses:

> *And Allah puts forward the example of a township, that dwelt secure and well-content: its provision coming to it in abundance from every place, but it denied the favours of Allah. So, Allah made it taste **extreme of hunger and fear**, because of that which they used to do.*
>
> *And verily, there had come unto them a Messenger from among themselves, but they denied him, so the torment overtook them while they were wrongdoers.* **(16:112–113)**

Read the two passages together and the structure is exact:

| Surah Quraysh gives | Surah An-Nahl removes |
|---|---|
| **Fed against hunger** | **Made to taste extreme hunger** |
| **Safe from fear** | **Made to taste fear** |

Same two gifts, given and withdrawn. And the trigger named in 16:113 is not obscure — **a Messenger came from among themselves, and they denied him.** Which is precisely what Quraysh was doing when this surah was revealed to them.

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Quraysh, and all praise and thanks are due to Allah.*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **The Elephant was destroyed so that the caravans could run.** What looks like a rescue was a foundation being laid — Al-Fil and Quraysh are one argument split across a Bismillah.
- **The two things worth being amazed at are the two you never notice**: that you ate today, and that nothing came for you in the night. Ibn Jarir's *Lam* of amazement is aimed at exactly that blindness.
- **The verse points past the House to the Lord of the House.** Custodianship of sacred things is not worship of the One who made them sacred.
- **Safety is conditional, not owned.** *Whoever accepts, Allah gives him safety in this life and the Hereafter; whoever disobeys, He removes both.* The township of 16:112 had everything Makkah had — until it denied the favour, and tasted the exact opposites: hunger, and fear.
