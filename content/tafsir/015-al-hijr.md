*Revealed in Makkah · 99 ayat · named for the rocky valley of Thamud, mentioned once, at 15:80*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Hijr is a Makkan surah addressed to a Prophet ﷺ under sustained mockery, and almost everything in it serves one purpose: **to steady him**. It does this in four movements that recur throughout — *they have always done this to messengers*; *look at what I have made and what I sustain*; *look at what happened to the last four towns that did this*; *and now: proclaim it openly, and I will handle the mockers Myself*.

The surah opens with disbelievers wishing they had been Muslims, and closes with an instruction to worship until death. Between those two poles it places the guarantee that gives the whole book its permanence — **"We revealed the Dhikr, and We are its Guardian"** — which is why a surah of consolation is also the surah Muslims cite for the preservation of the Qur'an.

---

## 1. The Regret That Comes Too Late (15:2)

> *How much would those who disbelieved wish that they had been Muslims.*

Ibn Kathir takes this as a report about the Hereafter, not this world: they will regret having lived in disbelief and will wish they had submitted.

Two explanations are preserved, and the second is one of the most striking passages in the surah:

- **Ibn Mas'ud** (via Sufyan Ath-Thawri ← Salamah bin Kuhayl ← Abi Az-Za'ra'): this concerns the **Jahannamiyyun** — sinners among the believers who stay in Hell for a time — at the moment the disbelievers *see them being brought out*.
- **Ibn Abbas and Anas bin Malik** (via Ibn Jarir): it refers to the day Allah detains sinful Muslims in Hell alongside the idolaters. The idolaters taunt them: *what you used to worship on earth has not helped you.* Then, by virtue of His mercy, **Allah becomes angry on their behalf** and removes them — and it is at that sight that the wish is uttered.

The wish, in other words, is not abstract remorse. It is provoked by watching mercy operate on someone else.

---

## 2. "Leave Them to Eat and Enjoy" (15:3)

Not permission — a threat. Ibn Kathir reads it alongside its two parallels: *Enjoy your brief life! But certainly, your destination is the Fire* (14:30), and *Eat and enjoy yourselves for a little while. Verily, you are the guilty* (77:46).

And the phrase **"let them be preoccupied with false hope"** he glosses precisely: hope of the kind that *distracts from repentance and from turning to Allah*. The danger named here is not despair. It is comfortable postponement.

---

## 3. No Town Goes Early, No Town Goes Late (15:4–5)

Allah never destroys a township until the evidence has been established against it and its allotted term has ended — and when that term arrives, **it is neither advanced nor delayed**.

Ibn Kathir reads this as directed squarely at Makkah: a warning to abandon their Shirk, stubbornness and disbelief *while the clock is still running*.

---

## 4. "You Are a Madman — Bring Us Angels" (15:6–8)

The two standing accusations, and the standing answer.

The demand for angels is not curiosity; Ibn Kathir places it in a lineage of the same demand made in bad faith:

| Speaker | The demand |
|---|---|
| **Quraysh** (15:7) | Bring angels, if you are truthful |
| **Pharaoh** (43:53) | Why no golden bracelets, or angels sent with him? |
| **Those who deny the Meeting** (25:21–22) | Why are angels not sent to us, why do we not see our Lord? |

And 25:22 supplies the answer this surah gives in one line: *On the Day they do see the angels, there will be no good news for the guilty.* Hence **"We do not send the angels down except with the truth — and then they would have no respite."** Mujahid: *with the Message, and with the punishment.* The thing they are demanding is the thing that ends the possibility of their repentance.

---

## 5. The Guarantee (15:9)

> *Verily, We, it is We Who revealed the Dhikr, and surely We will guard it.*

Ibn Kathir's comment is brief and total: Allah revealed the Qur'an, and **He is protecting it from being changed or altered**. Placed here — immediately after the accusation of madness — it functions as the counterweight to everything the mockers can do. They can insult the man; they cannot touch the message.

---

## 6. Mockery Is Not New, and Hearts Can Be Closed (15:10–13)

Every nation before mocked its messenger. Ibn Kathir names the purpose explicitly: **consolation** for the rejection by Quraysh.

Then the harder half: *Thus We allow it to enter the hearts of the guilty* — Allah lets disbelief enter the hearts of those too stubborn and too arrogant to follow guidance. **Anas and Al-Hasan Al-Basri** said "it" here refers to **Shirk**.

And the closing phrase, *the sunnah of the ancients has gone forth*, means what Ibn Kathir says is by now well known: the destruction of those who rejected messengers, and the rescue of the prophets and their followers — in this world and the next.

---

## 7. A Door Opened in Heaven Would Not Be Enough (15:14–15)

The strongest statement in the surah about the limits of evidence: even if a gate to heaven were opened and they were taken up through it all day long, they would say *our eyes have been dazzled; we are a people bewitched.*

The Salaf gloss the word four ways, and the range is instructive:

| Authority | *"Our eyes have been dazzled"* means |
|---|---|
| Mujahid, Ibn Kathir (the reciter), Ad-Dahhak | Our vision has been blocked |
| Qatadah ← Ibn Abbas | Our eyesight has been taken away |
| Al-Awfi ← Ibn Abbas | We were confused and put under a spell |
| Ibn Zayd | The dazzled man is simply *one who cannot reason* |

The point is not that they lack proof. It is that a mind committed to a conclusion will reinterpret anything.

---

## 8. The Sky: Constellations, and a Guarded Border (15:16–18)

*We have put the Buruj in the heaven and beautified it for the beholders* — **Mujahid and Qatadah**: the heavenly bodies (Ibn Kathir supports this with 25:61). **Atiyah Al-Awfi**: sentinel fortresses.

Then the guarding of that sky from every outcast devil, and the "shooting star" that pursues the one who steals a hearing. Ibn Kathir cites **Al-Bukhari ← Abu Hurayrah** in full, and it is one of the most vivid hadiths in the tafsir:

> When Allah decrees a matter in heaven, the angels beat their wings in submission to His Word, with a sound like **a chain dragged across a smooth rock**. When the fright leaves their hearts they ask one another, *"What did your Lord say?"* They answer: *"The truth. And He is the Most High, the Most Great."*

The eavesdroppers are standing stacked one above another — Sufyan, narrating, demonstrated it by spreading the fingers of his right hand and holding them one above the other. Sometimes the flame catches the listener before he can pass the word down; sometimes he passes it on first. It descends until it reaches **the mouth of a sorcerer or fortune-teller, who then tells a hundred lies and gets one thing right** — and people say, *did he not tell us this would happen on such a day?*

That last line is the whole explanation of soothsaying in Ibn Kathir's system: one stolen truth, laundered through ninety-nine lies.

---

## 9. The Earth: Everything in Due Measure (15:19–20)

The earth spread out, firm mountains set in it, and *all kinds of things caused to grow in it in due proportion*.

On **"mawzun"** — in due proportion — **Ibn Abbas**: *with their predetermined proportions*. Ibn Kathir then lists the agreement, which is unusually broad: Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ikrimah, Abu Malik, Mujahid, Al-Hakim bin Utaybah, Al-Hasan bin Muhammad, Abu Salih and Qatadah.

And *We have provided means of living in it, for you and for those whom you provide not*:

- **Mujahid**: the riding animals and cattle.
- **Ibn Jarir**: slaves — men and women — as well as the animals and cattle.

The meaning Ibn Kathir draws out: you are being reminded of the livelihoods made easy for you, the animals subjugated for you to ride and eat, the servants you benefit from — **and that the provision of every one of them comes from Allah alone, not from you.**

---

## 10. The Treasuries, and the Rain That Is Never More (15:21)

> *There is not a thing but the supplies for it are with Us, and We do not send it down but in a known measure.*

Sent down as He wills — out of wisdom and mercy toward His servants, "in a way that He is under no obligation to do. **But He has decreed mercy for Himself.**"

Then the report Ibn Jarir preserves from **Abdullah bin Mas'ud**, which is the quietest and most beautiful thing in this section:

> *No year has more rain than another* — but Allah divides it between them as He wills. It rains here one year, and there another year.

The total is fixed. Only the distribution moves.

---

## 11. The Fertilizing Winds (15:22)

*We send the winds fertilizing* — fertilizing the clouds so they give rain, and the trees so they open their leaves and blossoms.

Ibn Kathir notes a grammatical point worth keeping: **the winds are plural here because they produce results**, unlike *ar-rih al-'aqim*, the barren wind of 51:41, which is singular and described as barren precisely because it produces nothing — since results require two or more things to meet.

- **Ibn Mas'ud**: the wind is sent bearing water from the sky, then fertilizes the clouds until rain falls generously — *just as the milk of a pregnant camel flows*. (So too Ibn Abbas, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Qatadah.)
- **Ad-Dahhak**: Allah sends it to the clouds; the cloud is fertilized and becomes full of water.
- **Ubayd bin Umayr Al-Laythi** distinguishes four winds in sequence: one that stirs up the earth, one that raises the clouds, one that forms them, and one that pollinates the trees.

Then the water itself: *and We give it to you to drink*. Ibn Kathir's paraphrase — We send it down fresh and sweet, and **if We had wished We could have made it salt** (56:68–70). *And it is not you who are the owners of its supply*: you are not the one maintaining the springs, wells and rivers so that you may drink all year, water your livestock and irrigate your crops.

---

## 12. The First of You and the Last of You (15:23–25)

*We give life and cause death, and We are the Inheritors* — He will inherit the earth and everyone on it.

On *We know the first generations of you and the present generations* (15:24), Ibn Kathir preserves a small, humane scholarly exchange. **Awn bin Abdullah** raised with **Muhammad bin Ka'b** the interpretation that the ayah refers to the rows in prayer — the front rows and the back. Muhammad bin Ka'b answered plainly: *this is not the case*; it refers to those who are dead or killed, and to those who have yet to be created.

Awn's reply is the part worth remembering: **"May Allah help you and reward you with good."** A correction received well.

**Ibn Abbas**: the first generations are all who have passed since Adam; the latter are those alive now and those still to come, until the Day of Resurrection.

---

## 13. What Man Is Made Of, and What the Jinn Are Made Of (15:26–27)

Man from *salsal* — dried, sounding clay — of *hama' masnun*, altered mud. The jinn, created **earlier**, from the smokeless flame of fire.

- **Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah**: *salsal* is dry mud.
- **Mujahid**, in another report: it means *putrid* — and here Ibn Kathir states a working principle he applies throughout the tafsir: **"it is more appropriate to interpret an Ayah with another Ayah"**, and 55:14–15 settles it toward "sounding clay like the potter's clay."
- **Ibn Abbas** on the smokeless flame: *it is the flame that kills*.
- **Ibn Mas'ud** (via Abu Ishaq ← 'Umar Al-Asamm): this smokeless flame is **one of seventy parts** of the fire from which the jinn were created.

And from the Sahih:

> The angels were created from light, the jinn were created from a smokeless flame of fire, and Adam was created from that which has been described to you.

Ibn Kathir's reading of why this is mentioned at all: the ayah is **intended to point out the noble nature, good essence and pure origin of Adam** — which is exactly what Iblis will refuse to see.

---

## 14. Iblis: Envy, Expulsion, and the Reprieve (15:28–44)

Allah announced Adam to the angels *before* creating him, and honoured him by commanding prostration. Iblis, standing among the angels, refused — Ibn Kathir names the causes as a cluster: **envy, disbelief, stubbornness, arrogance and false pride**.

His objection is a comparison of raw materials, and Ibn Kathir cross-references it to his other two recorded outbursts: *I am better than him; You created me from fire* (7:12), and *Do you see this one whom You have honoured above me?* (17:62).

**Sa'id bin Jubayr** (via Ibn Abi Hatim) preserves a strange and memorable detail: when Allah cursed Iblis, his image was changed from that of the angels, and he made a sound like a bell — **and every bell that rings on earth until the Day of Resurrection is part of that**.

Then the bargain. Iblis asks for respite; it is granted "until the Day of the time appointed." His threat: *because You misled me, I shall adorn the path of error for them on earth* — Ibn Kathir: I will make sin dear to them, and encourage, provoke and harass them into it. But the exception is written into his own oath: **"except Your chosen servants among them."**

And the answer: *This is the way which will lead straight to Me* — all of you return to Me and I will requite you. **You shall have no authority over My servants** — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, *you will have no way to reach those for whom I have decreed guidance.*

### The Prophet and Iblis at the place of prayer

Ibn Jarir records from Yazid bin Qusayt a narrative about an unnamed prophet whom Iblis confronted in his place of worship, seating himself between the man and the Qiblah. The prophet sought refuge with Allah three times; Iblis taunted him — *do you know who you are seeking refuge from? Here he is.* Then each demanded the other go first:

> **Iblis:** Tell me the thing by which you are saved from me.
> **The Prophet:** No — you tell me how you overpower the son of Adam.

The prophet finally recited *you shall have no authority over My servants*, and Iblis said, **"I heard this before you were even born."** Then the prophet recited *if an evil whisper comes to you from Shaytan, seek refuge with Allah* (7:200), adding: *by Allah, I never sense you are near but I seek refuge with Allah from you.*

Iblis conceded: **"You have spoken the truth. In this way you will be saved from me."** And then gave up his own answer:

> **"I seize him at times of anger, and times of desire."**

### Seven gates

Hell has seven gates, *for each of those gates a class assigned* — each portion of Iblis's followers entering by the gate their deeds decree, settling at the level their deeds decree. Ibn Abi Hatim records from **Samurah bin Jundub**, from the Prophet ﷺ:

> Among the people of Hell are those whom the Fire will swallow up to the ankles, and those up to the waist, and those up to the collarbone.

---

## 15. And on the Other Side: Gardens and Springs (15:45–50)

Deliberately placed against the seven gates. *Enter it in peace and security* — Ibn Kathir: **peace** means free of all problems, **security** means free of all fear and concern; **no fear of expulsion, and no fear that the condition will be disrupted or end.**

*We shall remove any deep feeling of bitterness from their breasts.* **Abu Umamah** (via Al-Qasim, whom Ibn Kathir openly grades as weak in his reports from him — then corroborates from the Sahih rather than leaning on it): the people of Paradise enter carrying whatever enmity is left in their hearts from this world; when they come together, Allah removes it.

The Sahih version, from **Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri**:

> The believers will be removed from the Fire and detained on **a bridge between Paradise and Hell**. Judgment will be passed between them concerning the wrongs they committed against one another in this world, until they are cleansed and purified. Then permission will be given to them to enter Paradise.

*No sense of fatigue shall touch them* — like the palace promised to Khadijah, *of jewels, in which there will be no toil and no fatigue*. And the announcement made to the dwellers of Paradise:

> You will be healthy and never fall sick; you will live and never die; you will be young and never grow old; you will stay here and never leave.

Then the two verses Ibn Kathir treats as a matched pair — *I am the Forgiving, the Most Merciful* / *My torment is the most painful torment* — and his conclusion: **we must always be in a state between hope for His mercy and fear of His punishment.**

---

## 16. Ibrahim's Guests (15:51–56)

They entered and said *Salam*; he said *we are frightened of you*. Ibn Kathir supplies the reason from the fuller account elsewhere: **they would not eat the roasted calf he offered them.**

Their news is of Ishaq. Ibrahim's response — *do you give me this good news while old age has overtaken me?* — Ibn Kathir reads not as doubt but as **wonder and astonishment, asking for confirmation**, since he and his wife were both old. And they confirm it, good news upon good news.

His closing line is the one people carry from this surah:

> *And who despairs of the mercy of his Lord except those who are astray?*

---

## 17. Lut, and an Oath Sworn by the Prophet's Life (15:57–77)

The angels come to Lut in the form of young men with handsome faces. The townspeople arrive *rejoicing* — Ibn Kathir: happy at the news of his guests. Lut pleads: *these are my guests, do not shame me; have Taqwa of Allah, do not disgrace me*, and offers them the lawful path of marriage instead. All of this happens, Ibn Kathir notes, **while they were still unaware of the calamity due the following morning**.

He also pauses on a point of reading: the events here are not in strict sequence with the earlier statement that the guests were messengers — *the conjunction "and" does not imply sequence*, especially where something indicates otherwise.

Then 15:72, which Ibn Kathir treats as the single greatest honour in the surah:

> *Verily, by your life, in their wild intoxication, they were wandering blindly.*

**Allah swore by the life of His Prophet ﷺ.** Amr bin Malik An-Nakari ← Abu Al-Jawza' ← **Ibn Abbas**: *Allah has never created or formed any soul dearer to Him than Muhammad — and I never heard that Allah swore by the life of anyone else.* Qatadah glosses the phrase itself: "in their misguided state… they were playing."

The destruction comes as the **Sayhah** at sunrise, the city overturned, stones of baked clay (*sijjil*) raining down.

*Surely in this are signs for the mutawassimin* — and the word gets four readings:

| Authority | *Mutawassimin* are |
|---|---|
| Mujahid | Those who have insight and discernment |
| Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak | Those who look |
| Qatadah | Those who learn lessons |
| Ibn Kathir's summation | **Those who ponder** |

And *they were right on the highroad*: the ruined city — now a foul-smelling lake — sits on a route still travelled to this day (37:137–138: *you pass by them in the morning, and at night; will you not reflect?*).

---

## 18. Two More Ruins on the Same Road (15:78–84)

**The Dwellers of Al-Aykah** — the people of Shu'ayb. Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah and others: *al-Aykah* means intertwined trees. Their crimes: **Shirk, banditry, and cheating in weights and measures**. Their punishment came in three forms — the Sayhah, the earthquake, and the torment of the Day of Shadow. They lived near Lut's people and at a later time, which is why Shu'ayb could say to them, *and the people of Lut are not far off from you* (11:89).

**The Dwellers of Al-Hijr** — Thamud, the people of Salih, and the surah's namesake. Ibn Kathir notes the plural in *they denied the Messengers*: **whoever denies even one Messenger has disbelieved in all of them.** Their sign was the she-camel brought out of solid rock, sharing the water on alternate days; when they killed her they were given three days (11:65).

They *hewed out dwellings from the mountains, feeling secure* — Ibn Kathir: they had **no real need** of those houses; it was extravagance and work without purpose. And the Messenger ﷺ passed those very dwellings on the way to Tabuk, covered his head, urged his camel faster, and said:

> Do not enter the dwellings of those who were punished unless you are weeping — and if you do not weep, then make yourself weep, out of fear that what struck them may strike you.

*And all that they used to earn availed them not*: the crops, the fruits, and the water they had refused to share — none of it protected them when the command of their Lord came.

---

## 19. The World Was Not Made in Play (15:85–86)

The heavens and earth created *with truth*, and the Hour is coming — with justice, *to requite those who do evil with what they have done* (53:31), against the alternative that Allah dismisses in 38:27 and 23:115–116: that any of this was purposeless, or play.

*So overlook their faults with gracious forgiveness* — **Mujahid, Qatadah and others: this was before fighting was prescribed**, and Ibn Kathir agrees, on chronological grounds: the surah is Makkan, and fighting was legislated after the Hijrah.

*Verily, your Lord is the Knowing Creator* — Ibn Kathir reads this as a proof of resurrection: **the Creator, for whom nothing is too much; the Knowing, who knows what has scattered from people's bodies across the regions of the earth** (36:81–83).

---

## 20. The Seven Mathani (15:87–88)

The one place in the Qur'an where the phrase occurs, and the scholars divide:

| Opinion | Held by |
|---|---|
| **The seven long Surahs** — Al-Baqarah, Al-'Imran, An-Nisa', Al-Ma'idah, Al-An'am, Al-A'raf, Yunus | Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak |
| **Al-Fatihah**, its seven ayat | Ali, Umar, Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas; also Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Ibn Abi Mulaykah, Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Mujahid |

Sa'id bin Jubayr on the first: *in them Allah explains the obligations, the Hudud, the stories and the rulings.* Ibn Abbas on the second: **the Bismillah completes the seven ayat**, which Allah gave exclusively to this Ummah.

Al-Bukhari records two hadiths for the second view — the Prophet ﷺ promising **Abu Sa'id bin Al-Mu'alla** the greatest surah in the Qur'an before leaving the Masjid, then teaching him Al-Fatihah; and **Abu Hurayrah**: *Umm Al-Qur'an is the seven Mathani and the Grand Qur'an.*

Ibn Kathir refuses to make these compete. Al-Fatihah is the seven Mathani **and this does not contradict** the seven long surahs, because both share the attributes — as does the whole Qur'an, described in 39:23 as *oft-recited, its parts resembling one another*.

Then the instruction that follows: **do not stretch your eyes toward what We have given them.** Al-Awfi ← Ibn Abbas: this *forbids a man to wish for what his companion has*. Mujahid, on who is meant: **the rich**. Be content with the Grand Qur'an you were given; do not grieve at their rejection — *and lower your wing to the believers*, i.e. be gentle with them, as in 9:128.

---

## 21. "I Am a Plain Warner" — and the Naked Warner (15:89)

From the two Sahihs, **Abu Musa**, the Prophet's ﷺ parable for his own position:

> My likeness and that with which Allah sent me is a man who came to his people and said: *O my people, I have seen the invading army with my own eyes, and I am a naked warner — so escape, escape!*
>
> Some obeyed and set out at nightfall, moving slowly, and escaped. Others disbelieved him and stayed where they were until morning, when the army overtook them and wiped them out.

The "naked warner" — the man who strips to make himself unmistakable — is the image the Prophet ﷺ chose for himself. Urgency is the whole message.

---

## 22. Al-Muqtasimin: The Men Who Agreed on a Story (15:90–93)

Those who *made the Qur'an into parts*. Two readings:

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Al-Bukhari): the People of the Book, who split their Book — believing in some of it and rejecting some.
- **Ata', Ad-Dahhak and others**: the Quraysh, and the "parts" are the contradictory accusations they made — sorcerer, madman, soothsayer.

Ibn Kathir also notes the root: *taqasamu* is to swear to one another (27:49, where Salih's people swear a night attack), and the deniers in this world are constantly swearing — 16:38, 14:44, 7:49 — as if they took an oath on every single thing they denied. Hence the name.

### Al-Walid's committee

Muhammad bin Ishaq ← Ibn Abbas preserves the meeting. With the pilgrimage season approaching and Arab delegations about to arrive, **Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah** gathered the Quraysh and told them to **agree on a single line** — no contradictions.

They proposed *soothsayer*; he rejected it. *Madman*; rejected. *Poet*; rejected. *Sorcerer*; rejected. They asked what, then, they should say. His answer is an accidental testimony:

> **"By Allah, what he says is as palatable as something sweet — so you cannot say anything against it without it being obviously false. Therefore the most appropriate thing you can say is that he is a sorcerer."**

They left having agreed on the least implausible lie — and Ibn Kathir says this ayah came down about them.

### The questioning

*By your Lord, We shall certainly question them all, for all that they used to do.* **Abu Al-Aliyah** (via Ar-Rabi'): all people will be asked two things on that Day — **what they used to worship, and what their response to the Messengers was.**

And on the apparent tension with *on that Day no question will be asked of man or jinn as to his sin* (55:39), **Ibn Abbas** resolves it neatly: they will not be asked *"did you do such and such?"* — Allah knows better than they do. They will be asked **"why did you do such and such?"**

---

## 23. Come Out Into the Open (15:94–96)

*Therefore openly proclaim what you have been commanded.* Ibn Abbas: **"Go ahead with it."** Mujahid: reciting the Qur'an aloud in prayer. And from Abu Ubaydah ← **Abdullah bin Mas'ud**, the historical marker:

> The Prophet ﷺ was still practising and preaching Islam secretly until this ayah was revealed — **then he and his Companions came out into the open.**

*And turn away from the idolaters* — do not pay attention to those who want to turn you from the signs of Allah; *they wish that you should compromise, so that they would compromise* (68:9).

*Truly, We will suffice you against the mockers* — paired with 5:67, *Allah will protect you from mankind.*

Muhammad bin Ishaq names the five great mockers and, via Yazid bin Ruman ← Urwah bin Az-Zubayr, records that Jibril stood with the Prophet ﷺ during Tawaf as each passed by:

| The mocker | The sign, and the end |
|---|---|
| **Al-Aswad bin Al-Muttalib** (Bani Asad) | Jibril threw a green leaf in his face; he went blind. The Prophet ﷺ had supplicated against him: *O Allah, make him blind and take his son.* |
| **Al-Aswad bin Abd Yaghuth** (Bani Zahrah) | Pointed at his stomach; it swelled and he died of dropsy |
| **Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah** (Bani Makhzum) | Pointed at an old, trivial scratch above his ankle — from an arrow that had caught in his trailing garment two years earlier; it reopened and killed him |
| **Al-'As bin Wa'il** (Bani Sahm) | Pointed at the instep of his foot; on the road to At-Ta'if a thorn pierced it and he died |
| **Al-Harith bin At-Talatilah** (Khuza'ah) | Pointed at his head; it filled with pus and killed him |

Ibn Kathir's closing note on 15:96 is blunt: *who make another god along with Allah; but they will come to know* is **a strong warning and a grave threat.**

---

## 24. When the Chest Tightens (15:97–98)

> *Indeed, We know that your breast becomes tight because of what they say.*

Ibn Kathir's paraphrase of the consolation is worth reading slowly: We know you are distressed by their insults — **but do not let that weaken your resolve or cause you to give up conveying the Message.** Put your trust in Him; He will suffice you and support you. And then the prescription: *keep yourself busy with remembering Allah, praising Him, glorifying Him, and worshipping Him* — meaning Salah.

The remedy for a tight chest, in this surah, is not withdrawal. It is prostration.

Imam Ahmad ← **Nu'aym bin Hammar**:

> O son of Adam! It is not too difficult for you to perform four Rak'ah at the beginning of the day — and if you do, **I will take care of you until the end of it.**

---

## 25. "Until the Certainty Comes to You" (15:99)

The last verse, and Ibn Kathir ends the surah by settling what *al-Yaqin* means — because a great deal rides on it.

**Al-Bukhari**: *Salim said, "It means death."* (Salim bin Abdullah bin Umar; Ibn Jarir records the same from him.)

From the Sahih, **Umm Al-'Ala'** of the Ansar, when the Prophet ﷺ entered upon Uthman bin Maz'un after his death and she said, *my testimony over you is that Allah has honoured you*:

> **"How do you know that Allah has honoured him?"**
> *"May my father and mother be sacrificed for you — if not him, then who?"*
> **"As far as he is concerned, the certainty has come to him, and I hope for good for him."**

So the ayah means: **acts of worship remain obligatory as long as a man's mind is sound** — and he prays according to his ability, as in Al-Bukhari from Imran bin Husayn: *pray standing; if you cannot, then sitting; if you cannot, then on your side.*

From this, Ibn Kathir delivers his sharpest rejection in the surah. Some of the Sufis interpret *yaqin* as **ma'rifah** — "spiritual knowing" — and hold that whoever attains that level is freed from these obligations. Ibn Kathir:

> **This is disbelief, misguidance and ignorance.**

His argument is not merely textual but observational: the Prophets and their companions were **the most knowledgeable of all people about Allah** — His rights, His attributes, the glorification He deserves — *and at the same time they were the people who worshipped Him the most, continuing in good deeds until the day they died.* Knowledge, in every case on record, increased worship rather than excusing it.

---

## The Thread

Al-Hijr is a surah about **who is actually in a position to be worried**.

Its addressee is a man being called mad in his own city, whose chest tightens at what he hears. And the surah's answer is not argument but reversal: *they* are the ones on a term that cannot be extended; *they* are the ones whose eyes would find a way to disbelieve an open door in heaven; *they* are the ones assembling a committee to decide which lie is least embarrassing. Meanwhile the sky is guarded, the rain is measured out by the year, the winds are sent to fertilize, and the message itself has a Guardian who is not the man carrying it.

Four ruined towns are laid out on the same road — Sodom, Al-Aykah, Al-Hijr, and by implication Makkah — and the traveller is told that the route is still open, that he passes them morning and evening, and that the only question is whether he *looks*.

And so the instruction at the end is not to win the argument. It is to stop hiding, to be gentle with the believers, to stop measuring yourself against the comforts of the rich, and to keep praying until the certainty arrives.

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Al-Hijr. Praise be to Allah, the Lord of all that exists.*
