*Revealed in Makkah · 20 ayat · "The Enwrapped One" — named for the opening address to the Prophet ﷺ, found in a blanket*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Muzzammil is **the surah in which the night prayer was made obligatory, and the surah in which it was lifted** — and the twelve months between those two events are contained inside the same twenty verses. This is unusual enough that A'ishah tells the whole story when asked about it: the beginning of the surah made standing at night a duty, the Companions stood until their feet swelled, Allah withheld the end of the surah for a year, and then the final verse came down and made it voluntary.

So the surah has two halves that face each other. The first (73:1–9) is a **training programme for a man about to carry something extremely heavy**: get up, pray most of the night, recite slowly, remember your Lord's name, cut yourself off for Him, rely on Him alone. The second (73:10–20) tells him **what he is being trained for** — mockery he must absorb patiently, a warning to the wealthy who reject him, a Day that turns children grey — and then, at the very end, relaxes the discipline for the community while keeping the principle.

The logic Ibn Kathir draws out is that the preparation precedes the mission and is proportionate to it. *A heavy word is coming; here is how you build the shoulders for it.*

---

## 1. "O You Wrapped Up" — the Call to Get Up (73:1–4)

The address is affectionate and slightly teasing. Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak and As-Suddi read it as **"O you who are asleep."** Qatadah: the one wrapped in his garments. Allah commands His Messenger ﷺ to stop being covered up and to stand in prayer — the same movement described elsewhere as:

> *Their sides forsake their beds, to invoke their Lord in fear and hope.* (32:16)

Then the measure is given, and it is deliberately loose: **half the night, or a little less, or a little more.** Ibn Kathir's paraphrase: *We have commanded you to stand for half the night, a little more or a little less — there is no hardship on you in that slight increase or decrease.* Even the strictest version of the obligation was built with give in it.

Ibn Kathir notes this duty was **obligatory upon the Prophet ﷺ alone**, citing:

> *And in some parts of the night offer the Salah with it, as an additional prayer for you. It may be that your Lord will raise you to Maqam Mahmud.* (17:79)

---

## 2. *Rattil the Qur'an Tartil* — How to Recite (73:4)

This is the section Ibn Kathir treats most lovingly, and it is the most practically useful part of the surah.

**Meaning:** recite it slowly, *for that will help in understanding the Qur'an and contemplating it.* Speed is not the point; comprehension is.

The evidence is a series of eyewitness descriptions of the Prophet's ﷺ own recitation:

- **A'ishah**: he recited a chapter so slowly that it took longer than chapters actually longer than it.
- **Anas** (Sahih Al-Bukhari), asked how the Messenger ﷺ recited: *"He used to elongate the letters."* Anas then demonstrated with the Basmalah — drawing out *Allah*, drawing out *Ar-Rahman*, drawing out *Ar-Raheem*.
- **Umm Salamah** (Ahmad, Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi): he paused verse by verse — stopping after each line of Al-Fatihah rather than running them together.

And the supporting hadiths on beautifying recitation:

> *Beautify the Qur'an with your voices.*

> *He is not of us who does not chant nicely with the recitation of the Qur'an.*

> *Verily, he has been given this windpipe from the windpipes of the family of Dawud* — said of Abu Musa, who replied that had he known the Prophet ﷺ was listening, he would have beautified it further.

**Ibn Mas'ud's instruction** is the passage worth memorising: do not scatter the Qur'an like scattered sand, and do not rush through it like the hasty recitation of poetry. *Stop at its amazing parts and make your heart move with it. None of you should let his concern be to reach the end of the chapter.*

When a man boasted to him of reciting the entire Mufassal (Qaf to An-Nas) in a single rak'ah, Ibn Mas'ud's verdict was: *this is rushing, like the haste of reciting poetry.* (Al-Bukhari)

## 3. "A Word Thaqil" — Why Revelation Was Heavy (73:5)

Two readings, and Ibn Jarir accepts both at once.

**Heavy in what it demands.** Al-Hasan and Qatadah: *the actions with it.* The obligations it carries are weighty.

**Heavy in the receiving.** Ibn Kathir assembles the physical testimony:

- **Zayd bin Thabit**: revelation came while the Prophet's ﷺ thigh rested on his — *and my thigh was almost crushed due to it.*
- **Abdullah bin Amr** (Ahmad) asked whether he felt anything when revelation came. The reply: *I hear a ringing and then I remain quiet when that occurs. There has not been a single time that revelation has come to me except that I thought my soul was about to be taken.*
- **Al-Harith bin Hisham** asked how it came (Al-Bukhari, at the opening of the Sahih): *Sometimes it comes to me like the ringing of a bell, and it is most severe upon me. Then this state passes away from me after I have grasped what is inspired. Sometimes the angel comes to me in the form of a man and talks to me and I grasp whatever he says.* A'ishah added that she saw sweat drop from his forehead **on a very cold day** as it ended.
- **A'ishah** (Ahmad): if it came while he was riding, the animal's *Jiran* — the bottom of its neck — would begin moving intensely.

Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam adds the third weight: *just as it is heavy in this world, it will also be heavy on the Day of Judgement in the Scales.*

---

## 4. Why Night — the *Nashi'ah* (73:6–7)

> *Verily, rising at night is better for understanding and more suitable for speech.*

**What Nashi'ah means:** Umar, Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zubayr all said **the entire night is Nashi'ah**; Mujahid agreed, and in one narration specified *after Isha*. The word is used when a person stands at night to pray. Ibn Kathir's conclusion: it refers to the **hours of the night** — every hour of it is a Nashi'ah.

**Why it works:** standing at night is *better for training the heart and the tongue, and more conducive to recitation* — more comprehensive for performing the recitation and better for understanding it than daytime recitation. The reason is plainly stated: **the daytime is when people disperse and move about, raise their voices and are busy.**

There is a lovely small exchange preserved here. Anas bin Malik recited the verse with the wording *ashaddu wat'an* and *aqwamu qeela*; a man objected that they recite it differently. Anas replied: *Aswab* (most correct), *Aqwam* (most suitable), *Ahya'* (best for preparation) — these words all mean the same thing.

**73:7 — *lengthy Sabh* in the day.** Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah and Ata' bin Abi Muslim: leisure time and sleep. Others (Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ath-Thawri): a long stretch of free time. Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam draws the conclusion: *this means for your needs — therefore leave the night open for your religious devotion.*

---

## 5. Remembrance, Devotion, Reliance (73:8–9)

> *And remember the Name of your Lord and devote yourself to Him with complete devotion.*

**Tabattul** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Abu Salih, Atiyah, Ad-Dahhak and As-Suddi: *make your worship solely for Him alone.* Al-Hasan: *strive and devote yourself to Him.*

The timing Ibn Kathir gives it is practical: make much Dhikr of Him and spend your time worshipping Him **when you have completed your occupations and your worldly needs** — exactly the pattern of:

> *So when you have finished, devote yourself for Allah's worship.* (94:7)

An important guardrail from Ibn Jarir: a devout worshipper is called *Mutabattil*, and the Prophet ﷺ **forbade At-Tabattul** where it means total devotion to worship while avoiding marriage. Devotion, not withdrawal from life.

**73:9 — *Lord of the east and the west; La ilaha illa Huwa, so take Him as a trustee.*** He owns and controls the affairs of every region. The argument is a symmetry: *just as you single Him out for worship, you should also single Him out for reliance.* Compare *So worship Him and rely upon Him* (11:123), and *You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help* (1:5).

---

## 6. Patience, and a Warning to the Comfortable (73:10–14)

> *And be patient with what they say, and keep away from them in a good way.*

Be patient with what the foolish among his people say, and keep away **in a way that is not blameworthy**. Then the tone changes, and Ibn Kathir frames it as a threat delivered by the One *Whose anger nothing can stand before*:

> *And leave Me alone to deal with the deniers, those who are in possession of good things of life.*

Meaning: *leave Me to deal with the rich rejectors who own great wealth.* Ibn Kathir's reasoning is pointed — **they are more able to obey than anyone else, and more is asked of them, because they have what others do not.**

*And give them respite for a little* — a little while only, as in *We let them enjoy for a little, then in the end We shall oblige them to enter a great torment* (31:24).

What waits:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ankal** | Fetters (agreed by a long chain of authorities including Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Tawus, Qatadah, Ath-Thawri) |
| **Jahim** | A blazing fire |
| **Food with *Ghussah*** | Ibn Abbas: *it will get stuck in the throat and will neither enter nor come out* |
| **The earth and mountains** | They will quake; the mountains become heaps of poured sand, then are destroyed utterly — until the earth is flat, with no valley and no hill |

## 7. "As We Sent a Messenger to Fir'awn" (73:15–18)

The address turns to the Quraysh and to everyone after them. A Messenger has been sent **as a witness over you** — witnessing your deeds — just as one was sent to Fir'awn. Fir'awn disobeyed, and *We seized him with a severe punishment*.

Ibn Kathir's application is a warning by comparison, reported from Ibn Abbas and Mujahid: **you are more deserving of destruction if you deny, because your Messenger is more noble and greater than Musa, the son of Imran.**

> *So Allah seized him with punishment for his last and first transgression.* (79:25)

**The Day that turns children grey (73:17).** Two possible senses, both good, though Ibn Kathir prefers the first: *how can you attain safety from a Day of such horror if you disbelieve?* — or *how can you attain piety while denying the Day of Judgement?*

Why children turn grey: the severity of its horrors, earthquakes and confusion — the Day Allah says to Adam, *"Send a group to the Fire."* Adam asks how many. The answer: **from every thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine to the Fire, and one to Paradise.**

*Whereon the heaven will be cleft asunder* — Al-Hasan and Qatadah: split **because of it**, from its severity and horror. *His promise is certainly to be accomplished*: it will occur, and there is no avoiding it.

## 8. The Verse That Lifted the Burden (73:19–20)

> *Verily, this is an admonition, therefore whosoever wills, let him take a path to His Lord.*

Ibn Kathir immediately places the qualifier beside it: *meaning, from those whom Allah wills that they be guided* — as in *But you cannot will, unless Allah wills* (76:30).

Then the long final verse, which Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and others of the Salaf all state **abrogated the obligatory night prayer**.

**How the abrogation is argued in the verse itself:**

- *Your Lord knows that you do stand a little less than two-thirds of the night, or half, or a third* — sometimes this, sometimes that, and *all of these are done unintentionally*; you are not able to be consistent, because it is difficult for you.
- *And Allah measures the night and the day* — sometimes they are equal, sometimes one is longer.
- *He knows that you are unable to pray the whole night, so He has turned to you.*
- *So recite of the Qur'an as much as may be easy* — **without specification of any set time**. Note that Allah uses *recitation* here to mean *prayer*, as in *And offer your Salah neither aloud, nor in low voice* (17:110), where "Salah" means the recitation within it.

**The excuses named:** the sick who cannot manage it; travellers *seeking of Allah's bounty* in business and trade; and *those fighting in Allah's cause*. Ibn Kathir flags the last as a proof of Prophethood — **this surah was revealed in Makkah, and fighting was not legislated until long after**, so the verse reports an unseen future.

**A'ishah's account (Ahmad, and similarly in Sahih Muslim), via Sa'id bin Hisham:**

> *Allah made standing at night obligatory at the beginning of this Surah. So the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Companions stood for an entire year during the night until their feet swelled. Allah held back the revelation of the end of this Surah for twelve months. Then Allah revealed the lightening of this burden at the end of this Surah. Then the standing for night prayer became voluntary after it used to be obligatory.*

In the same narration she is asked about his character and replies: *"Have you not read the Qur'an? Verily, the character of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was the Qur'an."* And she describes his Witr — **eight rak'ahs without sitting until the eighth, then a ninth, then two while seated: eleven.** When he grew older and heavier: **seven plus two seated: nine.** If sleep, pain or illness kept him from the night prayer, he prayed **twelve rak'ahs during the day**. She adds that she never knew him to recite the entire Qur'an in one night, nor fast a complete month other than Ramadan.

The confirming hadith from the Two Sahihs, to a man asking what was required of him: ***Five obligatory prayers during a day and a night.*** Was anything more obligatory? ***"No, except what you may do voluntarily."***

**The rest of 73:20 — the closing commands:**

- **Salah and Zakah.** Ibn Kathir treats this as evidence that **Zakah was made obligatory in Makkah**, with the Nisab thresholds and amounts clarified later in Al-Madinah.
- **"Lend to Allah a handsome loan"** — meaning charitable donations, rewarded most abundantly. *Who is he that will lend to Allah a goodly loan so that He may multiply it to him many times?* (2:245)
- **"Whatever good you send before you, you will find it with Allah, better and greater in reward."** Illustrated by the hadith recorded by Al-Bukhari: the Prophet ﷺ asked which of them loved his own wealth more than his heir's wealth. They all said they did. He told them to consider what they were saying, then said: ***The wealth of one of you is only that which he sends forth, and the wealth of his heir is that which he leaves behind.***
- **"And seek forgiveness of Allah"** — remember Him and seek forgiveness often, in all your matters. *Verily, He is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful to whoever seeks His forgiveness.*

Ali bin Abi Talhah's report from Ibn Abbas summarises the whole arc: the original command *became difficult on the believers, then Allah lightened the matter for them and had mercy on them* — **so Allah made the matter easy, and unto Him is due all praise, and He did not make matters difficult.**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The night is given to you so that the day can be survived.** The surah's own reason for choosing the night is not mystical: the day is when people scatter, voices rise, and business happens. The night is quiet, and quiet is where the heart and tongue get trained.

**Slow is the point.** *Rattil* is not an aesthetic preference. Ibn Mas'ud's warning — don't scatter it like sand, don't rush it like poetry, don't let your concern be reaching the end of the chapter — is the standard. Stop where it astonishes you.

**Weight is proportionate.** A heavy word required a year of heavy nights before it came. That the Prophet ﷺ nearly crushed a man's thigh, that sweat ran on a cold day, that the camel's neck shook — this is what "a Word Thaqil" cost the one who carried it.

**The obligation was lifted; the invitation was not.** Twelve months of swollen feet ended with *recite what is easy* — no set amount, no set time. What remains is five prayers and whatever you choose to add, and Ibn Kathir's closing note on that mercy is the right register for the whole surah: **He made the matter easy, and He did not make matters difficult.**

**And what you actually own is what you already sent ahead.** Everything else is your heir's.
