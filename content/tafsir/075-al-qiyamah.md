*Revealed in Makkah · 40 ayat · "The Resurrection" — the surah that answers the man who asks "When?" by describing what his own throat will feel like*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Qiyamah takes one sneering question — ***"When will be this Day of Resurrection?"*** — and refuses to answer it with a date. Instead it does four things in sequence, and they are more devastating than a date would have been.

- It shows **the body being reassembled**, down to the fingertips.
- It shows **the man being handed his own file** and discovering he cannot argue with it, because his own limbs are the witnesses.
- It exposes **why he asked the question in the first place** — not because the evidence was thin, but because *you love the present life and neglect the Hereafter*.
- And then it moves the scene from the Day of Judgement back to **the moment the soul reaches his collarbones** — the private, unavoidable appointment that happens long before the public one.

Between the terror and the threat, the surah drops in a passage of pure tenderness: an instruction to the Prophet ﷺ to **stop straining** to memorise the revelation, because Allah Himself will gather it in his chest. And it gives the believers the single greatest promise in the Qur'an: **faces that day, radiant, looking at their Lord.**

---

## 1. Two Oaths, One Argument (75:1–2)

> *Nay! I swear by the Day of Resurrection. And nay! I swear by An-Nafs Al-Lawwamah.*

Ibn Kathir opens with the grammar, because the grammar carries the meaning. When the thing being sworn about is a **negation**, it is permissible to place *"La"* before the oath to strengthen it. Here what is being affirmed is the reality of the final abode, and what is being negated is the ignorant claim that bodies will not be raised.

**Qatadah**: this simply means *I swear by both of these things* — also reported from Ibn Abbas and Sa'id bin Jubayr.

The first oath needs no explanation. The second is the surah's quiet masterpiece: Allah swears by **the self that blames itself**, and places it alongside the Day of Judgement as though the two were of a kind.

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri** (via Qurrah bin Khalid) gives the most human gloss in the surah:

> Every believer blames himself. He asks: *What did I intend by that statement? What did I intend by my eating? What did I intend by what I said to myself?* But the sinner proceeds ahead, and he does not blame himself.

- **Sa'id bin Jubayr** (via Ibn Jarir), and similarly **'Ikrimah**: he criticises himself over the good and the bad alike.
- **Mujahid**: he is sorry for the good he missed, and blames himself for it.

The self-critical conscience, in other words, is presented as *evidence* for the Resurrection. A soul that keeps auditing itself is a soul built for an audit.

## 2. The Fingertips (75:3–4)

> *Does man think that We shall not assemble his bones? Yes, We are able to put together in perfect order the tips of his fingers.*

The objection was: how could scattered bones be gathered from all their various places? The answer does not merely meet the challenge — it overshoots it.

Ibn Kathir: not only will the bones be gathered, but the **Banan** — the fingertips — will be reassembled precisely. And if Allah wished, He could resurrect a man with **more than he originally had**, or reshape his fingertips so they are all equal in length.

The reply to "can You even manage the bones?" is "I could redesign the fingerprints."

---

## 3. What the Question Really Is (75:5–6)

> *Nay! Man desires to break out ahead of himself. He asks: "When will be this Day of Resurrection?"*

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Sa'id): to proceed forward.
- **Mujahid**: he wants to press ahead following his own whims.
- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah), and **Ibn Zayd**: this is the disbeliever who denies the Day of Reckoning.

So the "When?" is not an enquiry. Ibn Kathir is explicit: **it is a question of denial**, rejecting the very existence of the Day, of the same kind Allah answers elsewhere — *Say: the appointment to you is for a Day which you cannot put back an hour nor put forward* (34:29–30).

The man is not short of information. He is short of intention.

---

## 4. When the Sight Is Dazzled (75:7–12)

Five images, arriving fast:

**The eyes (75:7).** Abu 'Amr bin Al-'Ala' recited *Bariqa* with a kasrah, meaning *diminished*; others read *Baraqa* with a fathah, close in sense. Either way: eyes darting this way and that in horror, unable to settle on anything, dazzled and humbled by what they are witnessing. Ibn Kathir links it to *their gaze returning not towards them* (14:43).

**The moon (75:8).** Its light simply goes.

**The sun and moon together (75:9).** **Mujahid**: they will be rolled up. **Ibn Zayd** recited *When the sun is wound round and its light is lost, and when the stars fall* (81:1–2) as the explanation. Ibn Mas'ud's recitation had the sun and moon *joined between each other*.

**The instinct to run (75:10–12).** *"Where is the refuge to flee?"* — and the answer: ***No! There is no refuge.***

**Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr** and others of the Salaf read this as: **there will be no salvation.** Compare *You will have no refuge that Day, nor any denying* (42:47).

Then the line that closes the escape route by redefining it: ***Unto your Lord will be the place of rest that Day*** — the *mustaqarr*, the place of return and the final destination. There is nowhere to run *to*, because the only destination is Him.

---

## 5. The File, and the Witness (75:13–15)

> *On that Day man will be informed of what he sent forward and what he left behind... Nay! Man will be well informed about himself, though he may put forth his excuses.*

Ibn Kathir: he will be informed of **all** his deeds — the old and the recent, the first and the last, the small and the large. *They will find all that they did placed before them, and your Lord treats no one with injustice* (18:49).

But the sharper point is the second ayah. He does not merely receive a verdict; **he is the witness against himself**. As Allah says elsewhere: *Read your book. You are sufficient as a reckoner against yourself this Day.*

- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah), naming the witnesses: his hearing, his sight, his two hands, his two legs, his limbs.
- **Qatadah**: he is a witness against himself. And in a second narration — the one that stings — *if you wish to see him, you will see him as a man who sees the shortcomings and sins of other people, yet is heedless of his own.*

Ibn Kathir then quotes a saying he attributes to what was written in the Injil: *O son of Adam, do you see the small splinters in your brother's eye and disregard the tree stump in your own, so that you do not see it?*

On the excuses:

- **Mujahid**: even as he argues in his own defence, he is a witness against himself.
- **Qatadah**: he will offer false excuses, and they will not be accepted.
- **As-Suddi**: this means his argument.
- **Al-'Awfi ← Ibn Abbas**: this is *apologising* — and he strings together the verses that show how worthless it will be: *The Day when their excuses will be of no profit to wrongdoers* (40:52); *they will offer submission to Allah on that Day* (16:87); *We used not to do any evil* (16:28); and the most extraordinary of all, the oath they will swear in Allah's own presence — *By Allah, our Lord, we were not those who joined others in worship* (6:23), of which Allah says: *they think they have something. Verily, they are liars* (58:18).

---

## 6. The Interruption: How Revelation Was Received (75:16–19)

> *Move not your tongue concerning it to make haste therewith. It is for Us to collect it and that it be recited. And when We have recited it to you, then follow its recitation. Then it is for Us to make it clear.*

The Prophet ﷺ used to strain to catch the revelation, reciting along with the angel while the angel was still reciting, terrified of losing a word of it. These four ayat release him from that anxiety — and they lay out **three guaranteed phases**:

1. **Gathering** it in his chest — *It is for Us to collect it.*
2. **Recitation** — Allah will make it easy for him to recite it exactly as it came down.
3. **Explanation** — *Then it is for Us to make it clear*: its meaning, interpretation and clarification, according to what Allah intended and legislated.

Compare *Be not in haste with the Qur'an before its revelation is completed to you, and say: My Lord, increase me in knowledge* (20:114).

**Imam Ahmad ← Ibn Abbas** preserves the scene with a wonderful chain of imitation: Ibn Abbas said *"I will move my lips as the Messenger of Allah ﷺ moved his,"* and his student Sa'id then said *"I will move my lips as I saw Ibn Abbas move his."* Recorded also by Al-Bukhari and Muslim. Bukhari's wording gives the resolution: **whenever Jibril came, he would be silent; and when Jibril left, he would recite it just as Allah had promised him.**

A surah of thunder pauses to tell a frightened man: *you will not lose it. Listen.*

---

## 7. The Real Reason for Denial (75:20–21)

> *But no! Rather you love the present life of this world, and neglect the Hereafter.*

Ibn Kathir's diagnosis is blunt and it explains everything that came before. The reason they reject the Day of Judgement and oppose the Qur'an is **not** an intellectual obstacle. It is that their entire concern is the present life. They are preoccupied with it, and distracted from what follows.

Denial is a symptom. Attachment is the disease.

---

## 8. Faces Looking at Their Lord (75:22–23)

> *Some faces that Day shall be Nadirah. Looking at their Lord.*

*Nadirah* comes from *Nadarah*: **splendid, radiant, glowing, delighted with goodness.**

And then the meaning Ibn Kathir defends at length: ***they will see Him with their very eyes.*** This is the surah's summit, and he treats it as a doctrinal matter that cannot be conceded.

- **Al-Bukhari** records: *Verily, you all will see your Lord with your own eyes.*
- **Abu Sa'id and Abu Hurayrah** (both Sahihs): asked whether they would see their Lord, the Prophet ﷺ replied — *Are you harmed by seeing the sun and the moon when there are no clouds beneath them?* They said no. *Then you will surely see your Lord like that.*
- **Jabir** (both Sahihs): looking at the full moon, he said — *You will see your Lord just as you see this moon. So if you are able to avoid missing a prayer before the rising of the sun (Fajr) or before its setting ('Asr), then do so.* The vision is promised, and in the same breath tied to the two prayers most easily lost.
- **Suhayb** (Muslim alone): when the people of Paradise have entered Paradise, Allah asks if they want anything more. They answer: *Have You not whitened our faces, entered us into Paradise, and saved us from the Fire?* **Then He removes the veil** — and nothing they have been given is more beloved to them than looking at their Lord. That is the *Ziyadah*, the "extra" of *For those who have done good is the best, and extra* (10:26).
- **Jabir** (Muslim alone): *Allah will appear before the believers while He is laughing* — on the open plains of the Resurrection.

Ibn Kathir notes that some narrations place this vision on the plains and some in the Gardens, and says he would present every chain and wording if not for the space it would take. His conclusion: the believers' seeing Allah in the Hereafter is confirmed through **numerous authentic routes**, agreed upon by the Companions, the Successors and the Salaf, and **it is not possible to deny it or refuse it**.

---

## 9. And the Other Faces (75:24–25)

> *And some faces that Day will be Basirah, thinking that some calamity is about to fall on them.*

- **Qatadah**: gloomy. **As-Suddi**: their colour will change.
- *Thinking* here means **being certain** — not suspicion, but knowledge.
- The calamity: **Mujahid** — a disaster. **Qatadah** — an evil. **As-Suddi** — they will be certain they are about to be destroyed. **Ibn Zayd** — they will know they are entering the Fire.

Ibn Kathir sets it beside the Qur'an's other paired portraits: *the Day when some faces become white and some become black* (3:106); *bright, laughing, rejoicing* against *dust-stained, covered in darkness* (80:38–42); *humiliated, labouring, weary* against *joyful, happy with their endeavour, in a lofty Paradise* (88:2–4, 8–10).

## 10. The Collarbones (75:26–30)

The surah now does something unexpected: it leaves the Day of Judgement and moves the camera to a bedroom.

> *Nay, when it reaches to the collarbones. And it will be said: "Who can cure him?" And he will think that it was the parting; and one shank will be joined with another shank. The drive will be on that Day to your Lord!*

On *"Kalla"*: if taken as a **negation**, the meaning is — *O son of Adam, at that moment you will not be able to deny what I informed you of; you will witness it with your own eyes.* If taken as **affirmation** (which Ibn Kathir considers the more obvious reading), it means: *it is certainly true* — your soul will be pulled from your body until it reaches your collarbones.

**"Who can cure him?"**
- **Ibn Abbas** (via 'Ikrimah): who is there to recite prayers of healing over him?
- **Abu Qilabah**: who is the doctor who can cure him? — and **Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Ibn Zayd** say similar.

Both readings describe the same scene: a room full of people trying everything, and nothing working.

**"One shank joined with another shank" (75:29)**
- **Ibn Abbas**: this is **the last day of the days of this world and the first day of the days of the Hereafter** — hardship meeting hardship, *except for the one Allah has mercy upon*.
- **'Ikrimah**: the great matter joined with the great matter. **Mujahid**: a test joined with a test.
- **Al-Hasan Al-Basri**: your two shins, when they are bound together. In another narration from him — *his two legs have died, and they will not carry him, though he used to walk about on them.*

Ibn Kathir places beside it the passage that describes the same helplessness from the other side of the bed: *Why do you not intervene when it reaches the throat, and you at that moment are looking on — but We are nearer to him than you, though you see not... bring back the soul, if you are truthful* (56:83–87).

**"The drive will be on that Day to your Lord" (75:30).** The soul ascends, and Allah says: *Return My servant to the earth, for I created them from it, I return them into it, and from it I will bring them out another time* — from the lengthy hadith of Al-Bara'. And: *He is the Irresistible over His servants, and He sends guardians over you, until when death approaches one of you, Our messengers take his soul... then they are returned to Allah, their True Master* (6:61–62).

## 11. He Neither Believed Nor Prayed (75:31–35)

> *So he neither believed nor prayed! But on the contrary, he denied and turned away! Then he walked in conceit to his family, admiring himself!*

Ibn Kathir: **no good in him, internally or externally.** Inwardly he denied the truth; outwardly he refused to act on it. The words he uses for the man: stubborn, cruel, obstinate, wanton, lazy — *having no concern and doing no deeds.*

On the strut home: **Ibn Abbas** (via Ad-Dahhak) — arrogantly. **Qatadah and Zayd bin Aslam** — strutting. Compare *when they returned to their own people, they would return jesting* (83:31), and *he was among his people in joy; he thought he would never come back* — *Yes! Verily, his Lord was ever beholding him* (84:13–15).

Then the fourfold threat:

> *Woe to you! And then woe to you! Again, woe to you! And then woe to you!*

**An-Nasa'i ← Sa'id bin Jubayr ← Ibn Abbas**: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said this **to Abu Jahl**, and then Allah revealed the ayah. **Ibn Abi Hatim ← Qatadah**: it is *a threat followed by a threat, just as you hear it* — the Prophet ﷺ took hold of Abu Jahl's clothing and said it to his face. Abu Jahl's reply is preserved verbatim, and it is the whole surah's target in one sentence:

> *"Are you threatening me, O Muhammad? By Allah, neither you nor your Lord are able to do anything — and I am the mightiest person walking between Makkah's two mountains."*

Ibn Kathir notes the rhetorical mode: this is the Qur'an's idiom of mockery and intimidation, the same register as *Taste it! Verily you were the mighty, the generous* (44:49), *Eat and enjoy yourselves a little; verily you are criminals* (77:46), *So worship what you like besides Him* (39:15), and *Do what you will* (41:40).

## 12. Left Neglected? (75:36–40)

> *Does man think that he will be left neglected?*

Two readings, and Ibn Kathir takes **both**:

- **As-Suddi**: neglected meaning *not resurrected*.
- **Mujahid, Ash-Shafi'i, 'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam**: neglected meaning *not commanded and prohibited* — left without law.

So: he is not abandoned **in this life** without obligation, and he is not abandoned **in his grave** without resurrection. He is commanded here, and gathered there.

Then the closing proof, which uses his own origin against his own doubt:

> *Was he not a Nutfah of semen emitted? Then he became an 'Alaqah; then shaped and fashioned in due proportion. And made of him two sexes, male and female. Is it not so then, that He would be able to give life to the dead?*

A weak drop from a despised fluid → a clot → a lump of flesh → a formed body → the soul breathed in → a complete creation with sound limbs, male or female, by the permission and decree of Allah.

Ibn Kathir raises a subtlety on the final question: does "able to repeat that" refer to the **origination** only, or to the origination *and* the process of perfecting it? Two views are held; he notes **the first is more popular**, and adds — *and Allah knows best.* Compare *He it is Who originates the creation, then repeats it; and that is easier for Him* (30:27).

---

## 13. The Supplication at the End

**Abu Dawud ← Musa bin Abi 'A'ishah**: a man used to pray on the roof of his house, and whenever he recited *Is it not so then, that He would be able to give life to the dead?* he would answer it aloud:

> ***"Glory to You — of course."***

Asked about it, he said he had heard it from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. Abu Dawud alone transmitted this, and did not name the Companion — **but, Ibn Kathir says, there is no harm in that.**

The surah opened with a man asking a question in bad faith. It ends with an anonymous man on a rooftop answering one in good faith.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Qiyamah's argument is that you already know.** It never produces new information for the denier — it produces *his own body* as the evidence. His fingertips prove the reassembly. His limbs will testify at the trial. His origin as a drop of fluid answers his doubt about the Resurrection. His collarbones set the date. And the conscience that blames him now, the *Nafs Lawwamah*, is sworn by as a sign of the very Day he claims not to believe in.

Which is why the surah's diagnosis lands where it does: not *you weren't convinced*, but ***you love the present life and neglect the Hereafter.*** Denial was never the problem. Preference was.

Between the two halves of that argument sits the mercy: **stop straining — We will gather it in your chest.** And beyond it, the reward that makes every hardship in the surah look small — *faces that Day, radiant, looking at their Lord*, the veil removed, given nothing more beloved to them than that.

The self that blames itself here is spared the blame there. **Audit yourself now, or be handed the audit then.**
