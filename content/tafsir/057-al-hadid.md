*Revealed in Madinah · 29 ayat · named for the iron of 57:25, mentioned once · the first of the Musabbihat*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Hadid is a surah about **money, and what it is actually for**.

It opens on the largest possible scale — everything in the heavens and the earth glorifying Allah, the four names that place Him before, after, above and beneath all things — and then narrows, without warning, onto a very small and very human question: *will you spend?* The move is deliberate. Ibn Kathir's reading is that the surah first establishes **who owns everything**, and only then asks you to part with some of it. Once you accept that the wealth in your hands was in someone else's hands before you and will be in someone else's hands after you, the command to give stops sounding like a loss.

Three arguments run through it, layered on top of each other:

- **You are a trustee, not an owner.** *Spend of that whereof He has made you trustees.*
- **What you keep is what you lose.** The Son of Adam says "my wealth, my wealth" — but the only wealth he ever really had was what he ate, wore out, or gave away.
- **What you give is a loan to Allah, and He repays at extraordinary rates.**

Between these sits the surah's most sobering passage — the believers walking by a light that matches their deeds, some mountain-sized, some no bigger than a flickering fingertip — and its most famous admonition: ***Has not the time yet come for the believers that their hearts should be humble?*** Ibn Mas'ud says it landed on them only four years into Islam.

The surah ends with iron: a metal that makes both the sword and the plough, sent down as the instrument by which justice is actually enforced.

## 1. The Ayah Better Than a Thousand Ayat (57:1–3)

Ibn Kathir opens with the Prophet ﷺ reciting the **Musabbihat** — the surahs beginning with glorification of Allah — before sleeping, and saying that among them is *an Ayah better than a thousand Ayat.* Ibn Kathir's judgement, "and Allah knows best," is that the ayah in question is **57:3**:

> *He is Al-Awwal and Al-Akhir, Az-Zahir and Al-Batin. And He is the All-Knower of everything.*

Before reaching it, the surah states that **everything that exists** — creatures and plants alike — praises Him, echoing 17:44: *there is not a thing but glorifies His praise, but you understand not their glorification.* He is *Al-'Aziz*, the One to whom all things submit; *Al-Hakim*, wise in creating, commanding and legislating; the absolute Owner who gives life and death and to whom nothing is impossible — **whatever He wills, is; whatever He does not will, will never be.**

### The remedy for doubt

Ibn Kathir records the incident from Abu Zamil that gives 57:3 its practical use:

> "I mentioned to Ibn Abbas that I felt something in my heart. He said, `Doubts' — and then laughed. Then he said, `No one can escape this.'"

Ibn Abbas cited 10:94 (*if you are in doubt, ask those who read the Book before you*), and then gave the prescription: **when you feel any of this in your heart, recite 57:3.**

Ibn Kathir notes that scholars of tafsir have offered *"about ten and some odd"* different explanations of this ayah. Al-Bukhari relays the shortest, from Yahya — identified by Ibn Kathir's own teacher Al-Hafiz Al-Mizzi as **Yahya ibn Ziyad Al-Farra'**, author of *Ma'ani Al-Qur'an*: *Az-Zahir: knowing all things. Al-Batin: knowing all things.*

But the clearest gloss is the Prophet's own bedtime supplication (Abu Hurayrah, in Muslim), which turns the four names into four directions in which nothing exists but Allah:

> **You are Al-Awwal — nothing is before You.**
> **You are Al-Akhir — nothing is after You.**
> **You are Az-Zahir — nothing is above You.**
> **You are Al-Batin — nothing is below You.**
>
> *Remove the burden of debt from us and free us from poverty.*

## 2. Nothing Escapes His Knowledge (57:4–6)

Creation in six days, then *Istawa* over the Throne — Ibn Kathir refers the reader back to Surat Al-A'raf rather than repeat himself.

Then the inventory of what He knows:

| Phrase | Ibn Kathir's reading |
|---|---|
| *What goes into the earth* | Every seed and every drop of water that enters the soil |
| *What comes forth from it* | Plants, vegetation, fruits |
| *What descends from the heaven* | Rain, snow, hail — and decrees and commandments brought down by the angels |
| *What ascends thereto* | The angels, and deeds |

On the last, the hadith: *To Him ascend the deeds of the night before the day falls, and the deeds of the day before the night falls.*

> *And He is with you wheresoever you may be.*

Meaning, Ibn Kathir says: watching and witnessing you — **on land or at sea, night or day, at home or in the open desert; all of it is the same before His knowledge**, all of it under His sight and hearing. He knows your secrets and your public statements alike (11:5; 13:10). This is the knowledge that grounds the Prophet's ﷺ definition of *Ihsan* to Jibril: **to worship Allah as if you see Him, for though you do not see Him, He surely sees you.**

*He merges night into day* — He lengthens one over the other, or equalises them, turns winter to spring to summer to autumn, all by wisdom and due measure. *And to Allah return all the matters* — He is the Most Just, who does not wrong by the weight of a speck; one good deed is multiplied tenfold, and on the Day of Resurrection even a mustard seed's weight is brought to the scales (21:47).

## 3. Believe, and Spend What Was Never Yours (57:7–9)

> *Believe in Allah and His Messenger, and spend of that whereof He has made you trustees.*

Ibn Kathir's commentary on the word **trustees** is the heart of the surah:

- The wealth you hold **was in the hands of those before you and was later transferred to you.**
- You will in turn **surrender it to someone else.** Those who inherit it may obey Allah with it — and gain more happiness than you from what was once yours. Or they may disobey Allah with it — in which case **you helped them commit evil.**

Either way, holding it is not neutral. If you ignore what Allah ordained regarding it, He will hold you accountable.

### "My wealth, my wealth"

Imam Ahmad records that Abdullah bin Ash-Shikhkhir came to the Prophet ﷺ as he recited *At-Takathur* and said:

> *The Son of Adam claims, "My wealth, my wealth." But is there anything belonging to you except that which you consumed, which you used, or which you wore until it became worn — or which you gave as charity and sent forward?*

Muslim's addition: *Other than that, you will go away from it and leave it behind for other people.*

### The believers with the most amazing faith

To the reproach *what is the matter with you that you believe not, while the Messenger invites you* — Ibn Kathir asks: what could possibly prevent you, with the Messenger among you and the proofs in front of you? He then brings the hadith from Sahih Al-Bukhari:

> "Who do you consider among the believers as having the most amazing faith?"
> — "The angels." — *And what prevents them, when they are with their Lord?*
> — "Then the Prophets." — *What prevents them, when revelation comes down to them?*
> — "Then us." — *What prevents you, when I am amongst you?*
>
> ***The believers with the most amazing faith are people who will come after you; they will find pages, and they will believe in them.***

On the **covenant** already taken from you: Ibn Kathir gives two views — the pledge of allegiance to the Prophet ﷺ (cf. 5:7, *when you said: we hear and we obey*), or, per Ibn Jarir and Mujahid, the primordial covenant taken from mankind while still in Adam's loin.

The Messenger brings you *out from darkness into light* — from the darkness of **ignorance, disbelief and contradictory statements** into the light of **guidance, certainty and faith**. Ibn Kathir's structural note: having commanded belief and spending, Allah now removes every barrier between people and faith — and then returns to the subject of spending.

## 4. Spend Without Fear (57:10)

> *And what is the matter with you that you spend not in the cause of Allah? And to Allah belongs the heritage of the heavens and the earth.*

Meaning, says Ibn Kathir: **spend, and do not fear poverty or scarcity.** The One in whose cause you spend owns the heavens and the earth and their treasuries; He is the Owner of the Throne. And He is the One who said:

> *Whatsoever you spend of anything, He will replace it. And He is the best of providers.* (34:39)
> *Whatever is with you will be exhausted, and whatever is with Allah will remain.* (16:96)

Those who trust Him spend, and *do not fear destitution coming to them from the Owner of the Throne.*

### Before the Conquest and after

> *Not equal among you are those who spent and fought before the conquering.*

Before Makkah fell, **only the righteous embraced Islam**, and things were hard. After it, people entered the religion of Allah in crowds. The two are not the same, though *to all Allah has promised the best.*

Most scholars take "the Conquest" as **Makkah**; Ash-Sha'bi and others say **Al-Hudaybiyyah**, and Ibn Kathir finds evidence for this in the dispute between **Khalid bin Al-Walid and Abdur-Rahman bin Awf** — Khalid, who accepted Islam between Hudaybiyyah and the Conquest, told Abdur-Rahman he boasted about battles fought before him. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *Do not bother my Companions. By He in whose Hand is my soul! If you spent an amount of gold equal to Uhud, you would not reach the level of their actions.*

And in the Sahih: *…it would not reach one Mudd of one of them, nor half of it.*

Ibn Kathir explains **why** the ranks differ: Allah knows the intention and the sincerity of the earlier group, *spending in times of hardship, poverty and dire straits.* Hence the maxim he quotes: **spending one Dirham can precede a hundred thousand.**

> There is no doubt among the people of faith that **Abu Bakr As-Siddiq** has the best share of this Ayah. He was the chief of those who implemented it among all the followers of all the Prophets. He spent all of his wealth seeking the Face of Allah — voluntarily, not to repay any debt or favour owed to any human being.

## 5. The Handsome Loan, and Abu Ad-Dahdah's Garden (57:11)

> *Who is he that will lend Allah a handsome loan? Then He will increase it manifold to his credit.*

Umar bin Al-Khattab said this refers to spending in Allah's cause; others said spending on one's children. Ibn Kathir's ruling: **it is more general than either** — anyone who spends in Allah's cause with good intention and a sincere heart falls under it. The multiplied repayment is *Paradise on the Day of Resurrection.*

Then the story, from Ibn Abi Hatim via Ibn Mas'ud. When the ayah was revealed, **Abu Ad-Dahdah Al-Ansari** asked: *"O Messenger of Allah, does Allah ask us for a loan?"*

> — *Yes, O Abu Ad-Dahdah.*
> — "Give me your hand." And the Prophet ﷺ placed his hand in his.
> — "Verily, I have given my garden as a loan to my Lord."

The garden held **six hundred date trees**, and his own wife and children were living in it. He went and called her: *"Umm Ad-Dahdah! Leave the garden — I have given it as a loan to my Lord."*

She said: ***"That is a successful trade, O Abu Ad-Dahdah!"*** — and moved the goods and the children out.

The Prophet ﷺ said: *How plentiful are the sweet date clusters that Abu Ad-Dahdah has in Paradise!* And in another narration: *How many a date tree, lowering down clusters full of pearls and gems, for Abu Ad-Dahdah in Paradise!*

## 6. The Light You Walk By (57:12–15)

> *On the Day you shall see the believing men and the believing women — their light running forward before them and in their right hands.*

The believers who spent in charity arrive at the Gathering with light preceding them — **according to the level of their deeds.** Ibn Mas'ud's description is the passage most worth memorising:

> "They will pass over the Sirat according to their deeds. Some will have a light as large as a mountain. Some as a date tree. Some as big as a man standing. **The least among them has a light as big as his index finger — lit at times, and extinguished at others.**"

Ad-Dahhak adds: *everyone* is given light on that Day. At the Sirat, the light of the hypocrites goes out — and seeing this, the believers panic that theirs will go out too. **That is the moment they cry: "O our Lord! Perfect our light for us."** (*And in their right hands* — Ad-Dahhak: their books of records, cf. 17:71.)

### The wall

> *"Wait for us! Let us get something from your light!"* — *"Go back to your rear! Then seek a light!"*

Ibn Abbas describes it: the people are gathered in darkness; Allah sends a light; the believers march toward it as their guide to Paradise; the hypocrites follow them — and then **the light is extinguished for the hypocrites**. They are told to go back to the darkness they came from and look for a light *there*.

Then a wall is put up between them, with a gate. **Inside is mercy — Paradise. Outside is torment — the Fire.** Al-Hasan and Qatadah say it stands between Paradise and Hellfire; Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd identifies it with the screen of 7:46, and Ibn Kathir considers this correct.

> *"Were we not with you?"* — *"Yes! But you led yourselves into temptations, you looked forward to our destruction; and you doubted, and you were deceived by false hopes, till the command of Allah came to pass."*

The hypocrites' claim is real: *were we not with you at Friday prayers, at congregational prayers, on Arafah, in battle?* And the believers concede it — **"Yes, you were with us"** — before naming what was missing. Ibn Kathir's summary of the reply:

> "You were with us in bodies which were heartless and devoid of intentions. You were cast in doubt and suspicion. You were showing off for people, and remembered Allah little."

Mujahid: the hypocrites lived among the believers, **married among them**, and betrayed them even while associating with them. *They were dead.* Both groups receive a light on that Day; **the hypocrites' light is extinguished exactly when they reach the wall — and that is where the two camps part.**

No ransom is accepted. *Your abode is the Fire. That is your protector* — the only shelter fitting for you, Ibn Kathir says, because of your disbelief and your doubt.

## 7. "Has Not the Time Yet Come?" (57:16–17)

> *Has not the time yet come for the believers that their hearts should be humble for the remembrance of Allah, and that which has been revealed of the truth?*

A question, not a statement. Ibn Kathir renders it: *has the time not come for the believers to feel humility in their hearts at the remembrance of Allah, at hearing subtle advice and the recitation of the Qur'an — so that they comprehend it, abide by it, and hear and obey?*

Then the detail that gives the ayah its force. Muslim records — near the very end of his book — that **Abdullah bin Mas'ud** said:

> "Only four years separated our acceptance of Islam and the revelation of this Ayah, in which Allah **subtly admonished us**."

Four years. If the first generation could be asked this after four years, the question is not addressed to the negligent — it is addressed to believers.

### The warning against hardened hearts

> *Lest they become as those who received the Scripture before, and the term was prolonged for them and so their hearts were hardened.*

What happened to them, in Ibn Kathir's account: as time passed they **changed the Book they had and sold it for a miserable price**; they threw it behind their backs; they became consumed by opinions and false creeds; **they took their rabbis and priests as gods beside Allah.** Consequently their hearts hardened, they would not accept advice, and neither Allah's promises nor His threats moved them (cf. 5:13).

This is why, Ibn Kathir says, **Allah forbade the believers from imitating them in any way — in basic matters and in detailed ones.**

### And then the answer, immediately:

> *Know that Allah gives life to the earth after its death!*

The placement is the whole point. Just as Allah revives dead, dry ground with abundant rain, **He softens hearts after they harden, guides the confused after they stray, and relieves hardship after it intensifies.** The light of faith finds its way back into hearts that had closed. Ibn Kathir ends this section as a doxology: *All praise is due to Him who guides whomever He wills after they were misguided… He is the All-Wise, the Most Just in all that He does, the Most Subtle, the Most Aware, the Most High.*

## 8. The Charitable, the Siddiqun, the Martyrs (57:18–19)

Those who give Sadaqat — **men and women** — and lend Allah a handsome loan: their reward is multiplied from tenfold up to seven hundredfold and beyond. The condition Ibn Kathir attaches: they give **with a good heart seeking Allah's pleasure**, not seeking worldly return or gratitude from the recipient.

> *And those who believe in Allah and His Messengers, they are the Siddiqun, and the martyrs with their Lord.*

There is a genuine grammatical dispute here, and Ibn Kathir preserves it: does the sentence run on ("they are the Siddiqun **and** the martyrs"), or stop and restart? Al-Awfi from Ibn Abbas, Abu Ad-Duha, Masruq, Ad-Dahhak and Muqatil bin Hayyan hold that it **stops** — believers are the Siddiqun; the martyrs are a separate group. Ibn Mas'ud: *"They are of three categories"* — those who spend in charity, the Siddiqun, and the martyrs.

Ibn Kathir's own argument settles it from 4:69, where **the Prophets, the Siddiqin, the martyrs and the righteous** are listed as distinct ranks — proving Siddiq and martyr are two categories, *"and there is no doubt that Siddiq is a better status than the martyr."*

Two hadiths anchor the section:

**On the lofty mansions** (Malik's *Muwatta'*, and both Sahihs) — the people of Paradise will look up at the dwellers of the lofty mansions as one looks at *a brilliant star far away on the horizon.* Asked whether these were for Prophets alone, the Prophet ﷺ said: ***No! By Him in whose Hand is my soul — these are for men who believed in Allah and believed in the Messengers.***

**On the souls of the martyrs** (in the Sahih) — they live in the bodies of green birds, flying where they wish in Paradise and returning to nests in chandeliers. Allah once looked upon them and asked what they wanted. They said: *"That You return us to the world, so we may fight in Your cause and be killed as we were killed the first time."* Allah said: **"I have decreed that they shall not be returned to it again."**

### The four ranks of martyrs

Imam Ahmad, from Umar bin Al-Khattab — the Prophet ﷺ raised his head as he described the first rank until *his cap fell off*:

1. A **true believer** who meets the enemy, fulfils his duty to Allah, and is killed — the people will look up to his level in Paradise *like this*.
2. A believer who meets the enemy and is killed by **a stray arrow**.
3. A believer who **mixed good deeds with evil**, who meets the enemy and is truthful to Allah until killed.
4. A believer who **committed sins excessively**, who meets the enemy and is truthful to Allah, and is killed.

Then, briefly, the counterweight: *those who disbelieve and deny Our Ayat — they shall be the dwellers of the blazing Fire.* Ibn Kathir notes the deliberate order — the destination of the happy, then the destination of the miserable.

## 9. What This Life Actually Is (57:20–21)

> *Know that the life of this world is only play and amusement, pomp and mutual boasting among you, and rivalry in respect of wealth and children.*

Ibn Kathir calls this Allah **degrading the significance of this life and belittling it** — this is what it amounts to *for its people* (cf. 3:14).

### The parable of the rain

Life is like *Ghayth* — and Ibn Kathir stops on the word: **the rain that comes after people have despaired** (42:28). The growth pleases the tiller; then it dries, yellows, and becomes straw.

He draws two parallels:

- **Of the world:** just as farmers admire the vegetation, **the disbelievers admire this life** — they are the most eager for it and it is dearest to them. Then the green fades and becomes scattered dust.
- **Of the person:** young and strong and handsome; then the mannerisms change and the strength weakens; then old and feeble, *"moving becomes difficult for them, while doing easy things becomes beyond their ability."* (cf. 30:54 — *created you in weakness, then strength after weakness, then weakness and grey hair.*)

So the Hereafter contains only two things: **severe torment, or forgiveness from Allah and His pleasure.** And this life *is only a deceiving enjoyment* — it deceives whoever inclines toward it into thinking there is no dwelling after it.

> *Paradise is nearer to any of you than the strap on his shoe — and so is the Fire.* (Imam Ahmad, from Abdullah; also Al-Bukhari)

Both are that close. Which is precisely why the next ayah says **race**:

> *Race with one another in hastening towards forgiveness from your Lord, and a Paradise the width whereof is as the width of the heaven and the earth.*

### And the race is winnable by the poor

The hadith of the poor emigrants, which Ibn Kathir places here for a reason. They complained: *"the wealthy people will get higher grades and permanent enjoyment — they pray as we pray and fast as we fast, but they give in charity and free slaves, and we cannot."*

> *Shall I tell you of a deed that, if you acted upon it, would let you catch up with those who have surpassed you, so that none would overtake you and be better than you except those who do the same? Say* **"Subhan Allah," "Allahu Akbar," and "Alhamdulillah" — thirty-three times each after every prayer.**

They came back later: *"Our wealthy brethren heard what we did and started doing the same."*

The Prophet ﷺ replied — and this is the ayah again — ***"That is the favour of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills."***

## 10. Written Before It Happened (57:22–24)

> *No calamity occurs in the earth nor in yourselves but it is inscribed in the Book of Decrees before We bring it into existence.*

Qatadah's gloss: *in the earth* = famine; *in yourselves* = suffering and disease. He adds: *"We were told that every person who suffers a prick of a thorn, a twisted ankle, or a bleeding vein, has it occur on account of his sins — and what Allah forgives is even more."*

Ibn Kathir calls this ayah **clear evidence against the Qadariyyah**, who deny that Allah preordained and foreknew all things. The hadith from Abdullah bin Amr bin Al-'As (Muslim):

> *Allah ordained the measures of everything **fifty thousand years** before He created the heavens and the earth* — and His Throne was over the water.

### Why you were told this

> *In order that you may not grieve at the things over that you fail to get, nor rejoice over that which has been given to you.*

The purpose of the doctrine is emotional, not theoretical. Ibn Kathir puts it in Allah's voice:

> "We informed you of Our encompassing knowledge… so that you may know that **what has met you would never have missed you, and what has missed you would never have met you.** Therefore, do not grieve for what you have missed — had it been destined for you, you would have achieved it."

And the other half: don't boast about what you were given, *because it is not you who earned it by your efforts.* Hence *Allah likes not prideful boasters.*

**Ikrimah's summary is the practical rule:**

> "Everyone of us feels happiness and grief. However, **make your joy with gratitude, and endure your grief with patience.**"

Then the censure of the miser — and note the doubling: those who are stingy **and who enjoin stinginess upon others**, spreading the vice as well as holding it. *And whosoever turns away* from obedience — *then Allah is Rich, Worthy of all praise* (cf. Musa in 14:8: *if you disbelieve, you and all on earth together, then verily Allah is Rich, Owner of all praise*).

## 11. The Book, the Balance, and the Iron (57:25)

> *Indeed We have sent Our Messengers with clear proofs, and revealed with them the Scripture and the Mizan, that mankind may keep up justice. And We brought forth iron, wherein is mighty power, as well as many benefits for mankind.*

Three things sent down together, and the sequence is the argument:

1. **Clear proofs** — the miracles and unequivocal evidences.
2. **The Scripture** — containing the true text.
3. **The Mizan** — which Mujahid, Qatadah and others define as **justice**: the truth attested to by sound, straight minds, against misguided opinion (cf. 55:7, *He raised the heaven high and set up the Mizan*).

And then **iron**, which Ibn Kathir reads as *"a deterrent for those who refuse the truth and oppose it after the proof has been established against them."*

He sets it in history deliberately: the Messenger ﷺ remained in Makkah **thirteen years** while revelation argued against the idolaters and explained Tawhid in detail. *When the evidence was established against those who defied him*, Allah decreed the Hijrah, and then permitted fighting. Iron comes **after** the Book, never instead of it.

> *I was sent with the sword just before the Hour, so that Allah be worshipped alone without partners. My provision was placed under the shadow of my spear, and those who defy my order were disgraced and humiliated — and **he who imitates a people is one of them**.* (Imam Ahmad, Abu Dawud, from Abdullah bin Umar)

But Ibn Kathir is careful to give iron both its faces, and he lists them:

- ***Mighty power*** — swords, spears, daggers, arrows, shields.
- ***Many benefits for mankind*** — coins, hammers, axes, saws, chisels, shovels; the tools people use for **tilling, sowing, cooking, making dough**, and manufacturing everything else a livelihood requires.

The same metal builds the world and defends it.

*That Allah may test who it is that will help Him and His Messengers in the unseen* — meaning, whose **intention** in carrying a weapon is the defence of Allah's religion. And the closing clarification: Allah is Powerful, Almighty; **He does not need mankind's help at all** — He ordered Jihad *to test people with each other.*

## 12. Nuh, Ibrahim, Isa, and the Monasticism Nobody Asked For (57:26–27)

Every Prophet sent after Nuh came **from his offspring**; every revealed Book and every Messenger after Ibrahim came **from Ibrahim's offspring**. And still: *among them are some who are guided, but many of them are rebellious.*

The last Prophet of the Children of Israel was **Isa**, who foretold Muhammad ﷺ. In the hearts of his followers Allah placed **compassion and mercy**.

> *But the monasticism which they invented for themselves, We did not prescribe for them, but (they sought it) only to please Allah therewith — yet they did not observe it with the right observance.*

Ibn Kathir gives **two possible senses** of *only to please Allah*: either they invented it intending to please Allah (Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah), or — *"We did not ordain that for them; We ordained only that they seek what pleases Allah."*

**The ayah criticises them twice over:**

1. They **invented things in their religion that Allah never legislated**.
2. They then **failed to fulfil even what they themselves had invented** and claimed was a means of drawing near to Allah.

### How it started

An-Nasa'i records from Ibn Abbas a remarkable account. After Isa, kings corrupted the Tawrah and the Injil, while believers still recited the originals. The kings were told: *"We were never confronted by more severe criticism than from these people"* — they recite *whosoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed, such are the disbelievers* (5:44), and they expose our failings. So the king gathered the believers and threatened them with death unless they recited only the corrupted version.

They refused, and split into three groups:

- **"Build us a narrow tower"** — let us ascend it, send up our food and drink, and you will be spared hearing us.
- **"Let us wander the land"** — eating and drinking like beasts; if you find us in your territory, kill us.
- **"Build us homes in the deserts"** — where we can dig wells and plant vegetables, and we will neither refute you nor pass by you.

That, says the narration, is the monasticism this ayah is about.

### And the alternative

> *Every Prophet has a Rahbaniyyah; the Rahbaniyyah of this Ummah is **Jihad in the cause of Allah**.* (Imam Ahmad, from Anas bin Malik; Abu Ya'la's wording: *every Ummah has a Rahbaniyyah…*)

Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri's advice to a man who asked him for counsel — the same counsel he had sought from the Messenger ﷺ:

> - Adhere to the **Taqwa of Allah**, because it is the chief of all matters.
> - Fulfil the obligation of **Jihad**, because it is the Rahbaniyyah of Islam.
> - Take care of the **remembrance of Allah and the recitation of the Qur'an**, because it is your closeness in the heavens and your good fame on earth.

## 13. Two Rewards, and Whose Grace It Is (57:28–29)

> *O you who believe! Have Taqwa of Allah, and believe in His Messenger. He will give you a double portion of His mercy, and He will give you a light by which you shall walk, and He will forgive you.*

Ibn Kathir, following Ibn Abbas (and with Ad-Dahhak, Utbah bin Abi Hakim and Ibn Jarir agreeing), reads this as addressed to the **People of the Scriptures who then accept Islam** — they earn their reward twice.

The supporting hadith (Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari, in both Sahihs) — **three who receive a double reward**:

1. A believer **from the People of the Scriptures** who was true to his own Prophet and then believed in Muhammad ﷺ.
2. A **slave** who fulfils both Allah's rights and his master's duties.
3. A man who **educates a slave-girl** properly and teaches her good manners without violence, then frees her and marries her.

Sa'id bin Abdul-'Aziz relates that **Umar bin Al-Khattab** asked a Jewish rabbi what the maximum multiplication of reward was for them. *"A Kifl — about three hundred and fifty good merits."* Umar said: **"Praise be to Allah, who gave us two Kifls."**

### The parable of the labourers

The Prophet ﷺ (in Al-Bukhari, via Abdullah bin Umar and again via Abu Musa) compared the three communities to workers hired for one day:

- The **Jews** worked from dawn to midday for one Qirat.
- The **Christians** worked from Zuhr to Asr for one Qirat.
- **You** worked from Asr to sunset — for **two Qirat each**.

The first two groups objected: *"We did more work but got less wages."* Allah asked: **"Have I been unjust to you regarding your reward?"** They said no. So He said:

> ***"Then it is My grace, which I bestow on whomever I will."***

In Abu Musa's longer version the earlier groups **quit before the day ended**, forfeiting what they had earned — and the last group completed the work and collected the wages of all three.

Which is exactly what the final ayah is for:

> *So that the People of the Scriptures may know that they have no power whatsoever over the grace of Allah, and that His grace is in His Hand, to bestow on whomsoever He wills.*

Meaning, Ibn Kathir says: **so that they become certain that they cannot prevent what Allah gives, nor give what Allah prevents.**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **You are a trustee, not an owner.** The wealth in your hands belonged to someone else before you and will belong to someone else after you. The only part of it that was ever truly yours is the part you ate, wore out, or gave away.
- **Give without calculating the loss.** *Whatsoever you spend, He will replace it.* Abu Ad-Dahdah handed over a garden of six hundred trees with his family still living in it, and his wife called it a successful trade.
- **Nothing is before Him, after Him, above Him or below Him.** When doubt enters the heart — and Ibn Abbas laughed and said no one escapes it — recite 57:3.
- **Every light on that Day is sized to its owner's deeds** — from a mountain to a flickering fingertip. The believers' prayer at the Sirat is *"O our Lord, perfect our light for us."*
- **The hypocrites were present for everything and absent from all of it.** *You were with us in bodies which were heartless and devoid of intentions.*
- **Has the time not come?** Ibn Mas'ud's generation was asked this **four years** into Islam. It is not a rebuke for the negligent; it is a question for believers.
- **Hard hearts are not a permanent condition.** *Know that Allah gives life to the earth after its death* — placed immediately after the warning, on purpose.
- **What missed you was never going to reach you.** Which is why the correct response to both halves of life is Ikrimah's: **joy with gratitude, grief with patience.**
- **The Book comes before the sword.** Thirteen years of proof in Makkah, then iron — the same metal that makes the plough, the coin and the shovel.
- **And none of it is owed to you.** *That is the grace of Allah, which He bestows on whom He wills.*
