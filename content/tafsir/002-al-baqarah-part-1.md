## 1. Its Virtues

Khalid bin Ma'dan called Al-Baqarah **the Fustat (the tent) of the Qur'an** — the canopy under which everything else shelters. Some scholars said it contains a thousand reports, a thousand commands, and a thousand prohibitions.

- **A house it is recited in is a house Shaytan leaves.** *"Do not turn your houses into graves. Verily, Shaytan does not enter the house where Surah Al-Baqarah is recited."* Ibn Mas'ud: *"Shaytan flees the house where Al-Baqarah is heard."*
- **The high peak of the Qur'an.** *"Everything has a hump, and Al-Baqarah is the high peak of the Qur'an."* Recited at night, Shaytan stays out of the house three nights; by day, three days.
- **The ten protecting ayat** (Ibn Mas'ud): the first four, **Ayat al-Kursi (255)**, the two that follow (256–257), and the last three. Recite them in a night and Shaytan will not come near you or your family — and recited over a senile person, they would rouse him.
- **A qualification for leadership.** The Prophet ﷺ appointed the *youngest* man in an expedition as its commander, because he had memorized Al-Baqarah. One of the chiefs said, "By Allah, I did not learn Al-Baqarah for fear I could not implement it."
- **Two lights with Al 'Imran.** *"Learn Al-Baqarah and Al 'Imran, for they are two lights that shade their people on the Day of Resurrection — like two clouds, two spaces of shade, or two lines of flying birds"* — and they will argue on behalf of their people.
- **A battle cry.** At Hunayn and at Al-Yamamah, when the ranks broke, the call went out: **"O Companions of Surah Al-Baqarah!"** — and they returned from every direction.
- **Angels descend for it.** Usayd bin Hudayr recited it at night; his horse startled, and he looked up to see a cloud radiating with lights like lamps. The Prophet ﷺ: *"They were the angels, come close to hear your voice. Had you kept reading, the people would have seen them by morning."*

## 2. Alif Lam Mim — The Disconnected Letters (2:1)

Ibn Kathir's position is a model of scholarly restraint. These letters are **among the things whose knowledge Allah kept for Himself** — reported from Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali and Ibn Mas'ud. Other views: they are names of surahs; or they are simply letters of the alphabet, cited in part to stand for the whole.

Removing repetitions, **fourteen** letters open the surahs: Alif, Lam, Mim, Sad, Ra, Kaf, Ha, Ya, 'Ayn, Ta, Sin, Ha, Qaf, Nun — exactly half the Arabic alphabet. *"So glorious is He Who made everything subtly reflect His wisdom."*

Against those who claim parts of the Qur'an are meaningless, Ibn Kathir is firm: **these letters carry meaning.** But where no authentic report from the Prophet ﷺ explains them, *"we stop where we were made to stop"* and say: **"We believe in it; all of it is from our Lord"** (3:7). Whoever finds one scholar's view convincing may follow it; otherwise, refrain from judgment.

## 3. The Four Categories of People (2:2–20)

Mujahid's structural key to the opening: **four ayat describe the believers, two the disbelievers, and thirteen the hypocrites** — the longest treatment for the most dangerous and least visible category.

### The Believers (2:2–5)

**"This is the Book, wherein there is no doubt, a guidance for the Muttaqin."**

The Book is the Qur'an; *Rayb* is doubt — and Ibn Abi Hatim notes he knows of **no disagreement** on this among the Salaf. Better to pause at *"no doubt"* so that *guidance* becomes an attribute of the Book itself, rather than merely something contained in it.

*Why is guidance restricted to the Muttaqin, if the Qur'an is guidance for all mankind?* Because **the Qur'an is guidance in itself, but only the righteous benefit from it** — as 41:44 states: for believers a guide and a healing; for disbelievers, deafness and blindness.

**Two types of Hidayah:**

| Type | Meaning | Whose it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Guidance of tawfiq** | Faith placed in the heart | Allah's alone (28:56, 2:272, 7:186) |
| **Guidance of bayan** | Explaining the truth, showing the road | The Messengers' task (42:52, 13:7) |

**Taqwa** — the root meaning is *to avoid what one dislikes*. 'Umar asked Ubayy bin Ka'b what taqwa was. Ubayy asked: "Have you walked a path with thorns?" — "Yes." — "What did you do?" — "I rolled up my sleeves and struggled." — **"That is taqwa."** And the Prophet ﷺ: *"The servant will not reach the rank of the Muttaqin until he abandons what is harmless out of fear of falling into what is harmful."*

**Iman** (2:3) is disputed among the Salaf: "to trust" (Ibn 'Abbas), "the deeds" (Az-Zuhri), "to fear Allah" (Ar-Rabi'). Ibn Jarir's synthesis — the preferred view — is that iman is **belief by tongue, deed and creed together**. Ibn Kathir adds the Ahl as-Sunnah position plainly: **iman increases and decreases.**

**Al-Ghayb** — the Unseen: Allah, His angels, Books, Messengers, the Last Day, Paradise, Fire, the meeting with Him, life after death, Resurrection. Ibn Mas'ud's striking statement: *"By He other than Whom there is no God, no person will ever acquire a better type of faith than believing in Al-Ghayb."* And the Prophet ﷺ, asked whether any could be better than the Companions: *"Yes — those who come after you, who will believe in me although they did not see me."*

**Iqamat as-Salah** is not merely praying: it is punctuality, purity, complete bowing and prostration, recitation, humility, presence. **Spending** here meant, before Zakah was legislated, spending on one's family; afterwards, the obligatory Zakah. Ibn Kathir notes why prayer and spending are so often paired: **prayer is the right of Allah; spending is kindness to His creation** — beginning with relatives, wife, servants, then everyone else.

The four descriptions are **cumulative, not alternative**: one cannot truly believe in the Unseen and pray and give while rejecting the previous Messengers and Books. Believers among the People of the Book who accept Islam gain **two rewards**; but Ibn Kathir notes fairly that an Arab convert with firmer, more encompassing faith may equal that.

### The Disbelievers (2:6–7)

*"It is the same to them whether you warn them or do not warn them."* Ibn 'Abbas: the Prophet ﷺ was eager for all people to believe; Allah informed him that **none would believe except those decreed happiness, and none stray except those decreed to stray.** So — deliver the message, and do not grieve over those who turn away: *"Your duty is only to convey."*

**Khatm (sealing)** — Mujahid demonstrated with his open palm: when the servant sins, a part of the heart rolls up (his index finger closed); another sin, another part — until the whole hand was a fist. **"Then the heart is sealed."** The Prophet ﷺ described the same as *Ran* (the stain, 83:14): a black dot engraved with each sin, polished away by repentance, accumulating until it covers the heart.

Hudhayfah's hadith: *"The trials are offered to the hearts like the straws sewn into a mat, one after another. Any heart that accepts them gets a black dot; any heart that rejects them, a white dot — until hearts become two categories: white like barren rock, which no fitnah harms as long as the heavens and earth exist; and black like an overturned cup, which recognizes no good and rejects no evil."*

Note the grammatical precision Ibn Kathir highlights: the **seal** is on hearts and hearing; the **Ghishawah (covering)** is on the eyes — each faculty gets the veil appropriate to it.

### The Hypocrites (2:8–20)

**Nifaq** means to show conformity and conceal evil. Two types: **nifaq of creed** (which lands its people in the lowest depth of the Fire eternally), and **nifaq of deed** (a major sin). Ibn Jurayj's definition: *"His deeds differ from what he publicizes, what he conceals differs from what he utters, his entrance and presence are not the same as his exit and absence."*

**Where hypocrisy came from.** There were no hypocrites in Makkah — there, the pressure ran the other way, and men concealed *faith* under a show of disbelief. In Madinah the Prophet ﷺ made treaties with the Jewish tribes and the Arabs around, and while Islam was still weak there was nothing to fear and nothing to fake. **Then came Badr.** 'Abdullah bin Ubayy bin Salul, whom the two tribes had been about to crown king, said: *"Allah's religion has become apparent"* — and pretended to be Muslim. Hypocrisy begins where power begins.

The thirteen ayat then dissect them:

- **They lie and they deny the Unseen** — the two defining marks (2:8–10). *"In their hearts is a disease"* — doubt, which Allah then increases: punishment in kind.
- **Was the Prophet ﷺ told who they were?** Only partially. He was given fourteen names to Hudhayfah at Tabuk — the plotters who tried to spook his camel off a hill. But 9:101 says plainly: *"you know them not, We know them."* He was taught their **characteristics**, and *"you will know them by the tone of their speech"* (47:30). Yet when Ibn Salul died, the Prophet ﷺ prayed over him and attended his funeral, saying: *"I would not like the Arabs to say Muhammad is killing his Companions."*
- **"We are only peacemakers"** (2:11–12). Their mischief is disobedience itself — and specifically, Ibn Jarir notes, **taking the disbelievers as allies against the believers.** They imagine they are brokering peace between two camps; that brokerage is the corruption. Ibn Kathir's sharp observation: *if the hypocrite simply remained an open disbeliever, the harm from him would be less.*
- **"Shall we believe as the fools have believed?"** (2:13) — meaning the Companions. Allah returns the word: *"Verily, they are the fools, but they do not know."* And unaware ignorance is more dangerous, a severer blindness, than ignorance that knows itself.
- **Mockery answered with mockery** (2:14–15). When they meet believers: "We believe." When alone with their *shayatin* — their leaders, the rabbis, the idolaters — "we were only mocking." Ibn Kathir explains the theological grammar carefully: **Allah's "mocking" and "deceiving" are named after the act they punish, not after any flaw in Him.** As with *"the recompense for an offense is an offense equal to it"* (42:40) — the first is injustice, the second is justice, though both carry the same name. Their punishment is drawn in 57:13: on the Day of Resurrection they will beg for light and a wall will rise between them and mercy.
- **A failed trade** (2:16). They purchased error with guidance — *"so their commerce was profitless."* Qatadah: *"By Allah! I have seen them leaving guidance for deviation, the Jama'ah for the sects, safety for fear, and the Sunnah for innovation."*

**The two parables (2:17–20)** — and Ibn Kathir's key insight is that they describe **two different kinds of hypocrite**:

| Parable | Image | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **The Fire** (2:17–18) | A man kindles a fire; it lights his surroundings; then it is snuffed out and he is left deaf, dumb, blind, unable to return | The **complete** hypocrite — believed, then disbelieved; light removed, only smoke and darkness left |
| **The Rainstorm** (2:19–20) | Rain with darkness, thunder and lightning; fingers in the ears against the thunderclap; walking when it flashes, standing still when it darkens | The **hesitant** hypocrite — the flickering one, whose light of faith is sometimes lit and sometimes out |

Ibn 'Abbas on the second: *"Whenever the hypocrites gain a share in the victories of Islam, they are content. Whenever Islam suffers a calamity, they are ready to revert."* And their fear is real fear — *"they think that every cry is against them"* (63:4) — but *"Allah ever encompasses the disbelievers"*: caution does not help against encirclement.

The parable reaches into the Hereafter: on the Sirat, **the believers' light runs before them**, some illuminating miles, some less, some flickering on and off — and the hypocrite's light is extinguished entirely. When believers see that happen, they cry out: *"Our Lord! Keep perfect our Light for us"* (66:8).

**The full taxonomy** Ibn Kathir extracts from this opening:

- **Believers:** two ranks — *the near ones (Sabiqun/Muqarrabun)* and *the righteous (Abrar)*
- **Disbelievers:** two ranks — *advocates* (compound ignorance: the mirage, 24:39) and *followers* (simple ignorance: darkness upon darkness in a deep sea, 24:40)
- **Hypocrites:** two ranks — *pure* and *partial*

And the hadith of the four hearts: **polished like a radiating lamp** (the believer); **sealed with a knot** (the disbeliever); **turned upside down** (the pure hypocrite, who knew and denied); and **wrapped** — containing both belief and hypocrisy, *"the example of faith in it is a herb sustained by pure water; the example of hypocrisy is an ulcer thriving on pus and blood. Whichever has the upper hand, prevails."*

## 4. The Universal Address (2:21–29)

**"O mankind! Worship your Lord."** Ibn Kathir treats this as **Tawhid al-Uluhiyyah** established through **Tawhid ar-Rububiyyah**: He created you and those before you; He made the earth a resting place and the sky a canopy; He sends rain and brings out fruits — *therefore* set up no rivals to Him.

The gravest sin, as Ibn Mas'ud asked and was told: **"To take an equal with Allah, while He alone created you."**

The long hadith of Yahya bin Zakariyya belongs here, because it gives the same logic in a parable. Yahya gathered the Children of Israel in Bayt al-Maqdis and gave them five commands:

1. **Worship Allah alone** — like a man who buys a servant with his own money, and the servant works but pays the profits to someone else. *Who among you would accept that?*
2. **Pray** — for Allah turns His Face toward the servant's face as long as he does not turn away. So do not turn your heads about in prayer.
3. **Fast** — like a man in a group carrying wrapped musk; all of them smell it. *The odor of the fasting person's mouth is better before Allah than musk.*
4. **Give charity** — like a captive with his hands tied to his neck, being led out to be beheaded, who ransoms himself piece by piece until he is free.
5. **Remember Allah always** — like a man pursued tirelessly by an enemy who takes refuge in a fortified fort.

**Signs of the Creator.** Ar-Razi and others took these ayat as the classical argument from design, and Ibn Kathir approves warmly. A bedouin asked for evidence of Allah's existence answered: *"The camel's dung testifies to the camel; the track testifies that someone walked. A sky holding giant stars, a land with fairways, a sea with waves — does all this not testify that the Most Kind, Most Knowledgeable exists?"*

### The Challenge (2:23–24)

*"Produce a Surah of the like thereof."* The challenge was issued in Makkah (28:49, 17:88, 11:13, 10:37–38) and repeated in Madinah, to individuals and groups, literate and illiterate — **the most comprehensive form of challenge possible**, and issued to the most eloquent nation on earth, who had every motive of hatred to answer it. And the ayah adds a second miracle inside the first: *"and you can never do it"* — a prediction of permanent failure, unfalsified to this day.

Ibn Kathir's summary of the Qur'an's inimitability is worth preserving: its expressions are perfect and its meanings explained; its stories are true and its laws just; **it does not become old with repetition, nor do scholars tire of it.** He contrasts it with Arabic poetry, in which one may find one or two lines of substance in a long poem, and where the popular saying ran: *"The most eloquent speech is the one that contains the most lies."* Warning verses would shake solid mountains; promise verses open the heart and ear.

The illustrative anecdote: before Islam, 'Amr bin Al-'As met Musaylimah the Liar, who asked what had recently been revealed in Makkah. 'Amr recited *"By Al-'Asr. Verily, man is in loss."* Musaylimah thought, then offered his own "revelation": *"O Wabr, O Wabr — you are but two ears and a chest, and the rest of you is unworthy and thin."* 'Amr — still a disbeliever — replied: **"By Allah, you know that I know you are lying."**

The Prophet's ﷺ own statement on this: *"Every Prophet was given a miracle of the type that brings mankind to faith. What I was given is a revelation. Yet I hope I will have the most following on the Day of Resurrection."* Other miracles ended with their witnesses; this one is still in the hands of every reader.

**Fuel of men and stones** — the stones being either the giant black sulfuric stones that hold the greatest heat, or the idols worshipped besides Allah (21:98). *"Prepared"* — **kept, in existence already**; many Imams of the Sunnah used this word to prove that the Fire exists now, supported by the hadith of the Fire's two breaths (winter and summer) and the stone thrown from the top of Jahannam that took seventy years to reach the bottom.

### The Reward (2:25) and the Mosquito (2:26–27)

Paradise's rivers do not run in valleys; they spring from beneath hills of musk; its sand is scented musk and its stones are pearls. The fruits are given *"in resemblance"* — the boys of everlasting youth say: *"Eat; the color is the same, but the taste is different."* Ibn 'Abbas puts it most memorably: **"Nothing in Paradise resembles anything in this life except in name."** And the wives are *Mutahharat* — purified of menstruation, waste, spit, and (as Qatadah adds) of impurity and sin.

The hypocrites mocked the parables of the fire and the rainstorm: *"Allah is far more exalted than to make such examples."* So Allah answered: **He is not ashamed to strike a parable of a mosquito, or of anything above it.** Ar-Rabi' bin Anas draws out the parable inside the parable: the mosquito lives as long as it needs food, and dies when it grows fat — *"and this is the example of people who, when they acquire the delights of this world, are taken away."*

**"By it He misleads many, and many He guides thereby."** The same parable that increases the believer's certainty increases the deviation of the one who rejects it. The **Fasiqin** are those who *break Allah's covenant after ratifying it, sever what Allah ordered to be joined, and work corruption* — the covenant being either the general covenant of obedience taken from all creation, or specifically the pledge taken from the People of the Book to follow Muhammad ﷺ when he came. (Muqatil bin Hayyan's broader reading, which Ibn Kathir calls *"very good."*)

### Two Proofs (2:28–29)

*"How can you disbelieve, seeing that you were dead and He gave you life?"* — four stages: non-existence, life, death, resurrection (cf. 40:11). And *"He created for you all that is on earth, then He turned to the heaven and made them seven heavens."*

On the order of creation: the **earth was created first**, then the heavens were made seven (Surah Fussilat 9–12). But the earth's *spreading out (daha)* — the bringing of its treasures to the surface, the bursting of its waters, the growth of its plants — came **after** the heavens, which resolves the apparent conflict with 79:27–33. Ibn 'Abbas himself gave this answer when asked.

## 5. Adam: The Khalifah (2:30–39)

**"Verily, I am going to place a Khalifah on earth."** Ibn Kathir's reading is that *Khalifah* here is not Adam personally, but **humanity succeeding one another, generation after generation** — otherwise the angels' response about bloodshed makes no sense. (Supported by 6:165, 27:62, 43:60.)

The angels' question was **not objection and not envy**. They do not speak before Him without permission. It was a request to learn the wisdom: *if the purpose is that You be worshipped, we already glorify and sanctify You without mischief — so why these?* Al-Qurtubi notes they may have inferred the bloodshed from the word *Khalifah* itself (one who judges between disputants), or from knowing man would be made of clay.

**"I know that which you do not know"** — meaning: among them will be **Prophets, Messengers, the truthful, martyrs, righteous believers, worshippers, the modest, the pious, scholars who act on their knowledge, the humble, and those who love Allah and follow His Messengers.** The benefit outweighs the harm you named.

**The Names (2:31–33).** Allah taught Adam the names of everything — Ibn 'Abbas, asked whether that included the plate and the pot, said: *"Yes, and even the terms for breaking wind."* Names, attributes, and functions. Then the objects were paraded before the angels, who could not name them, and Adam could. The angels' reply is the model of scholarly humility: **"Glory is to You, we have no knowledge except what You have taught us."**

This is why, in the great hadith of intercession, humanity comes to Adam saying: *"You are the father of mankind; Allah created you with His Own Hand, ordered the angels to prostrate for you, and taught you the names of everything"* — the ayah itself attested in the Prophet's ﷺ own words.

**The Prostration (2:34).** The obedience was to Allah; the honor was to Adam. Prostration of greeting was permitted to earlier nations (as with Yusuf's parents, 12:100) and was abrogated for this one — *"If I were to command any human to prostrate to another, I would command the wife to prostrate to her husband."*

**Iblis** was not an angel — he was of the Jinn (18:50) — but was included in the command because he was among them and imitating them. Qatadah: *"The first error ever committed was arrogance."* And the hadith: *"No person with the weight of a mustard seed of arrogance in his heart shall enter Paradise."*

**The Tree.** Some said grape, some barley, some fig. Ibn Jarir's verdict, which Ibn Kathir endorses along with Ar-Razi: **we do not know, because Allah did not tell us — and this is knowledge whose absence does no harm and whose presence brings no benefit.** Leave the vague as vague.

**The Fall and the Return.** Adam was tall with thick hair like a full date palm; when he ate, his covering fell and he ran, catching his hair in a tree, and Ar-Rahman called him: *"O Adam, are you fleeing from Me?"* — *"No, my Lord, but I am shy."*

Then: **"Adam received from his Lord Words."** The Salaf near-unanimously identify these as 7:23 — *"Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves. If You forgive us not and bestow not upon us Your mercy, we shall certainly be of the losers."* Ibn 'Abbas preserves a fuller dialogue: *Did You not create me with Your Hand? — Yes. And blow life into me? — Yes. And when I sneezed You said, may Allah grant you mercy — does not Your mercy precede Your anger? — Yes. And You destined me to this act? — Yes. Then if I repent, will You return me to Paradise? — **Yes.***

The sentence that follows the fall is the whole human condition in one line: **"Then whenever there comes to you guidance from Me — whoever follows My guidance, there shall be no fear on them, nor shall they grieve."** No fear regarding the Hereafter; no grief regarding this world.

## 6. The Children of Israel: A Sustained Case (2:40–123)

This is the longest section of Part I, and its structure is an **argument**, not merely a history. Ibn Kathir reads it as: *remember the favors → remember the covenant → here is how you broke it → therefore do not repeat the pattern with this final Messenger.*

### The Covenant and the Call (2:40–48)

*"O Children of Israel"* — Israel is Ya'qub, as the Prophet ﷺ made the Jews themselves affirm before witnesses. The address is a summons to be like their righteous father: *"O son of the generous man, do this."*

- **Believe in what I have sent down, confirming what is with you** — and do not be *the first to disbelieve therein*, i.e. the first **among the People of the Book**, since the Quraysh had already rejected him.
- **Do not sell My verses for a small price** — the whole of this life, said Al-Hasan Al-Basri, is the "small price."
- **Do not mix truth with falsehood, nor conceal the truth while you know** (2:42) — two distinct prohibitions: *don't adulterate*, and *don't suppress*. Qatadah reads it as mixing Judaism and Christianity into Islam.
- **"Do you enjoin righteousness on people and forget yourselves?"** (2:44). Ibn Kathir is careful here: **Allah is not criticizing them for commanding good** — that is an obligation on scholars. He is criticizing the contradiction. Usamah bin Zayd's hadith: a man will be thrown into the Fire, his intestines spilling out, circling like a donkey around a mill; the people of the Fire will ask, *"Did you not command us to good and forbid us evil?"* and he will say, *"Yes. I commanded righteousness and did not do it; I forbade evil and did it."* Ibrahim An-Nakha'i said he hesitated to preach because of three ayat, this being one. Prophet Shu'ayb's balance is the standard: *"I wish not, in contradiction to you, to do that which I forbid you"* (11:88).
- **"Seek help in patience and prayer"** (2:45). *Sabr* here was read as fasting by Muqatil and Mujahid — hence Ramadan is called the month of patience — and as restraint from the forbidden. 'Umar's two-tier distinction: *"There are two patiences: a good patience when disaster strikes, and a better patience in avoiding what Allah prohibited."* The prayer is heavy **except upon the Khashi'in** — those who are certain they will meet their Lord. Certainty is what makes the weight bearable.
- **"Fear a Day when no soul shall avail another"** (2:48) — no intercession accepted, no ransom taken, no help given, even between father and son (31:33).

### The Favors, and What Was Done With Them (2:49–74)

A rapid sequence, each favor followed by its betrayal:

| Favor | Response |
|---|---|
| Salvation from Pharaoh, whose slaughter of their sons was the "horrible torment" | — |
| The sea parted, Pharaoh drowned before their eyes (the day later known as **'Ashura**, which the Prophet ﷺ fasted, saying *"I have more right to Musa than you"*) | — |
| Musa's forty nights, the Tawrah and the Furqan | **The calf** — repented by killing themselves; ~70,000 died under a darkness, and both killed and killer were forgiven |
| Raised from death after the thunderbolt | *"We shall never believe until we see Allah plainly"* |
| Clouds for shade, **manna and quails** (manna: whiter than milk, sweeter than honey — the Prophet ﷺ said *truffles are a form of manna, and their liquid heals the eye*) | *"We cannot endure one kind of food"* — asking for lentils, onions, garlic, herbs |
| Twelve springs from one stone, one for each tribe | Corruption on the earth |
| The command to enter the holy city prostrating, saying *Hittah* (relieve us of our sins) | Entered on their backsides saying *Hintah* (grain seed) — mocking the word itself |
| The Mount raised over them to secure the covenant | Turned away after it |

**The Sabbath-breakers** (2:65–66). They set nets and pools on Friday, so that Saturday's fish were caught without being "fished," and collected them Saturday night. **Their punishment matched the crime**: their evil looked lawful on the surface and was wicked underneath. Ibn 'Abbas: those transformed lived only three days, ate nothing, drank nothing, left no offspring — **today's monkeys and swine are not their descendants**, but ordinary creatures made in the six days. And the Prophet's ﷺ warning to this Ummah: *"Do not commit what the Jews committed, breaching what Allah has forbidden by resorting to the lowest types of deceit."*

**The Cow** (2:67–73), from which the surah takes its name. A wealthy childless man was murdered by his nephew for the inheritance, the body moved to another man's doorstep, and the tribes nearly came to blows. Musa was commanded: *slaughter a cow.* **Had they simply slaughtered any cow, it would have sufficed** — Ibn 'Abbas and 'Ubaydah both say so. Instead they asked, and each question tightened the noose: neither old nor young; bright yellow, pleasing to behold; unworked, unblemished. In the end they found one owner with one such cow, and paid its hide's fill of gold. *"They slaughtered it, though they were near to not doing it."* Struck with a piece of it, the dead man rose, named his killer, and died again — and the killer was denied the inheritance, establishing the rule that **a murderer does not inherit from his victim.**

Ibn Kathir notes here that Al-Baqarah contains **five separate proofs of resurrection**: the revival after the thunderbolt (2:56), the cow, the thousands who fled death (2:243), the man who passed the ruined town (2:259), and Ibrahim and the four birds (2:260) — plus the dead land brought to life.

**Then the hearts hardened** (2:74) — *"like stones, or even harder."* And Ibn Kathir does not let the metaphor stay a metaphor: stones split and gush rivers; stones fall down **from fear of Allah**. He assembles the evidence that inanimate creation has a real, created awareness: the trust offered to the heavens, earth and mountains (33:72); everything glorifying Him (17:44); the shadows inclining (16:48); *"we come willingly"* (41:11); the mountain that would have humbled itself under the Qur'an (59:21); the skins that will testify; Mount Uhud *"a mount that loves us and we love"*; the stone in Makkah that greeted the Prophet ﷺ with salam before his mission; the Black Stone that will testify for those who kissed it. **The stone has more responsiveness than the closed heart.** And Allah forbade the believers from following them down this road: *"Has not the time come for the hearts of those who believe to be affected by Allah's Reminder... lest they become as those who received the Scripture before, whose hearts were hardened?"* (57:16).

### Concealment, Alteration, and Forged Books (2:75–79)

*"A party of them used to hear the Word of Allah, then change it knowingly after they understood it."* Ibn Zayd's description of the mechanism is chilling in its ordinariness: they altered the Tawrah so the lawful became unlawful and the false became right — **so that when a man came seeking truth with a bribe, they judged by the Book of Allah; when a man came seeking evil with a bribe, they produced the other, distorted book which said he was in the right; and when a man came with no bribe and no ill intent, they simply enjoined righteousness on him.**

*"When they meet the believers they say: We believe; but when alone: Will you tell them what Allah has revealed to you, so they argue with you before your Lord?"* — i.e. *do not admit what our own Book says about him, or it becomes evidence against us.*

**The Ummiyyun** (2:78) — those who cannot write, who know nothing of the Book but *Amani*: wishes, or (Mujahid) fabrications uttered by the tongue. And *"Woe to those who write the book with their own hands and then say: This is from Allah, to purchase with it a little price."*

Ibn 'Abbas's protest belongs here in full force: *"O Muslims! How can you ask the People of the Book about anything, while the Book of Allah revealed to your Prophet is the most recent from Him and you read it fresh and young? Allah told you they altered His Book and wrote another with their own hands... By Allah, we have never seen one of them asking you about what was revealed to you."*

### "The Fire shall not touch us but for a few days" (2:80–86)

The claim (some said forty days, the length of the calf-worship) is dismantled in one move: *"Have you taken a covenant from Allah? Then Allah will never break His covenant. Or do you say of Allah what you do not know?"*

The counter-principle is stated absolutely: **"Whoever earns evil and his sin has surrounded him — they are dwellers of the Fire, eternally."** Sin that *surrounds* was read as shirk (Abu Hurayrah, 'Ata', Al-Hasan), as dying unrepentant (Ar-Rabi'), or as major sins. And the necessary warning about small sins, from Ibn Mas'ud: *"Beware the belittled sins, because they gather on a person until they destroy him"* — like travellers camped on flat land, each servant collecting a stick, until there is enough to cook a meal.

Then the covenant is quoted back at them (2:83–86): **worship Allah alone; kindness to parents, kin, orphans, the poor; speak good to people; establish prayer; give Zakah** — and *"speak good to people"* Ibn Kathir reads broadly: enjoining good, forbidding evil, patience, forgiveness, and — from Abu Dharr's hadith — *"Do not belittle any form of righteousness, even if it is only meeting your brother with a smiling face."*

**"Do not shed your own blood, nor turn your own people out of their homes"** — and the historical charge is precise. In Madinah, Banu Qaynuqa' allied with Khazraj; An-Nadir and Qurayzah with Aws. When Aws and Khazraj fought, the Jewish tribes fought each other alongside their pagan allies — killing their own, expelling their own, looting — **and then, when the war ended, ransoming the prisoners according to the Tawrah.** Hence: **"Do you believe in a part of the Book and reject the rest?"** They obeyed the ransom verse and defied the killing verse, from the same page. This, says Ibn Kathir, is precisely why they cannot be trusted as custodians of the Prophet's description.

### The Prophets Killed, the Spirit Sent (2:87–91)

Musa was given the Book and followed by Messengers in succession; 'Isa was given clear signs and supported by **Ruh al-Qudus — identified as Jibril**, on the authority of Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas, As-Suddi, Qatadah and others, and by 26:193. Also invoked for Hassan bin Thabit when he defended the Prophet ﷺ in verse: *"O Allah! Aid Hassan with Ruh al-Qudus."*

*"Whenever a Messenger came with what you yourselves desired not, you grew arrogant — some you disbelieved and some you killed."* Az-Zamakhshari's grammatical note, which Ibn Kathir preserves: **Allah does not say "killed" in the past for the second clause**, because the Jews would still attempt to kill the Prophet ﷺ — by poison at Khaybar, whose effect he ﷺ said he felt until the aorta was cut in his final illness.

*"Our hearts are Ghulf"* — wrapped, screened, covered; or (Ibn 'Abbas's alternative reading) *"our hearts already contain every kind of knowledge and need nothing from you."* The reply strips the excuse: **"Nay, Allah has cursed them for their disbelief."**

The bitterest turn (2:89): **before his coming, they used to pray for him.** They would tell the Aws and Khazraj that a Prophet was due at the end of time and that they would exterminate the pagans alongside him, as 'Ad was exterminated. Then Mu'adh bin Jabal and Bishr bin Al-Bara' said: *"O Jews! You used to invoke Allah for the coming of Muhammad when we were still disbelievers, and describe him to us."* And Salam bin Mushkim of Banu An-Nadir replied: *"He did not bring anything we recognize. He is not the Prophet we told you about."*

The diagnosis is given plainly: **envy.** *"Grudging that Allah should reveal of His grace unto whom He wills of His servants."* Ibn Kathir: *"There is no envy worse than this."* Hence **"wrath upon wrath"** — Abu Al-'Aliyah: the first for rejecting 'Isa and the Injil, the second for rejecting Muhammad ﷺ and the Qur'an.

### Jibril the Enemy (2:97–98)

The occasion is preserved in Al-Bukhari through Anas: 'Abdullah bin Salam came to the Prophet ﷺ with three questions only a Prophet could answer, received the answers, and then said, *"Jibril — he, among all the angels, is the enemy of the Jews."* The ayah came down in answer.

Ibn Kathir extracts the principle: **whoever takes one Messenger as an enemy has taken them all as enemies.** Jibril does not choose his own missions — *"we descend not except by the command of your Lord"* (19:64). And the hadith Qudsi: *"Whoever takes a friend of Mine as an enemy, will have started a war with Me."*

Mikha'il is named alongside him precisely because the Jews claimed him as their friend: enmity to one is enmity to both, and to Allah.

### Suleiman and the Magic of Babylon (2:102)

One of Ibn Kathir's most careful passages. The claim being refuted was that **Suleiman ruled by sorcery**.

As-Suddi's account of how the slander arose: the devils used to eavesdrop on the angels' talk of coming events and pass it to soothsayers, mixing seventy lies with each true word; people wrote these down. Suleiman collected the books and buried them beneath his throne, saying: *"I will behead anyone who says the devils know the Unseen."* After his death, and after the generation that knew the truth had passed, a devil in human form led people to dig them up and told them: **"Solomon controlled the humans, devils and birds with this magic."** So the accusation was manufactured out of a buried archive.

Allah's answer: **"Suleiman did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved."**

On **Harut and Marut**, Ibn Kathir reports the disagreement honestly and then takes a side. Ibn Jarir argued the negation extends throughout — nothing was sent down to any two angels at all. Ibn Kathir replies flatly: **"this explanation is not plausible."** Many of the Salaf — 'Ali, Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn 'Umar, Ka'b al-Ahbar, As-Suddi, Al-Kalbi — held they were angels sent down as a **test**, reconciled with angelic infallibility the same way Iblis's case is: Allah knew eternally what would occur, and what they did was in any case far less than what Iblis did.

The trial's terms are explicit in the text: **they taught no one until they had said, "We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve."** Ibn 'Abbas's account of what happened to the one who insisted: he was directed onward, met Satan, learned magic — *"and the light of faith departed him, and he saw it shining as it flew away into the sky, and he cried: O my sorrow! Woe unto me! What have I done?"*

Rulings drawn:
- **Learning and practising magic is Kufr.** *"Whoever came to a soothsayer or sorcerer and believed what he said has disbelieved in what was revealed to Muhammad."*
- The characteristic effect named is **separating a man from his wife** — and the hadith of Iblis's throne on the water shows why: of all the devils reporting back, **the one Satan embraces is the one who separated a husband and wife.**
- *"But they could not harm anyone except by Allah's leave"* — Sufyan Ath-Thawri: *"except by Allah's appointed term."* Sorcery operates only inside decree.
- And the trade itself is a loss: *"They learn what harms them and does not profit them... they knew the buyer of it has no share in the Hereafter."*

### Naskh (Abrogation) (2:106–107)

The Jews denied that any of Allah's rulings could be repealed. Ibn Kathir answers on two levels.

**Linguistically and legally**, *Nansakh* was glossed as: to abrogate (Ibn 'Abbas), to erase (Mujahid, As-Suddi), to keep the words and change the meaning, or to change the ruling. And *Nunsiha* as: to uphold unchanged, to delay, or to cause to be forgotten. Abrogation applies to **commandments, prohibitions and permissions — never to reports or stories.** The replacement is *"better or similar"* — better meaning **more beneficial and easier for the one addressed** (Ibn 'Abbas).

**Rationally and historically**, the denial collapses: *"Do you not know that Allah is Able to do all things? Do you not know that to Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth?"* He commands a thing containing a benefit He knows, then prohibits it — and **perfect obedience is realized precisely in following the change.** And abrogation had already occurred in their own history, which they admit: Adam's children marrying one another, then forbidden; all animals permitted to Nuh after the ark, then restricted; two sisters married to one man permitted to Israel, then forbidden in the Tawrah; the command to Ibrahim to sacrifice his son, repealed before it was executed; the command to kill the calf-worshippers, repealed before they were all destroyed.

### Ra'ina, and the Prohibition of Imitation (2:104–105)

The Jews would twist *Ra'ina* ("attend to us") into an insult with a turn of the tongue (4:46), and greet Muslims with *As-samu 'alaykum* ("death be upon you") — hence the instruction to reply *wa 'alaykum*.

Believers were told to say *Unzurna* instead, and Ibn Kathir draws the broad principle: **Allah forbade the believers from imitating the disbelievers in tongue or deed.** *"Whoever imitates a people is one of them."*

### Asking Too Many Questions (2:108)

*"Or do you want to ask your Messenger as Musa was asked before?"* This is placed beside 5:101 and read as a prohibition on **manufacturing difficulty**: *"The greatest criminal among the Muslims is the one who asks whether a thing is prohibited, which is not prohibited, and it becomes prohibited because of his asking."*

The Prophet ﷺ: *"Leave me as I leave you; those before you were destroyed only because of their excessive questioning and disputing with their Prophets. When I command you, do as much as you can; when I forbid you, avoid it."* He said this after a man asked whether Hajj was obligatory *every year*, and repeated it three times — *"No. Had I said yes, it would have been ordained, and you would not have been able to bear it."* Anas: *"We were forbidden from asking, so we were delighted when a bedouin came and asked while we listened."*

### Forgive, Until the Command Comes (2:109–110)

*"Many of the People of the Scripture wish they could turn you back into disbelievers, out of envy from their own selves, even after the truth has become manifest to them."* The prescribed response for that period: **"But forgive and overlook, till Allah brings His command"** — which Ibn 'Abbas, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others hold was later abrogated by the ayat of fighting (9:5, 9:29), the phrase *"till Allah brings His command"* itself signalling an interim ruling. Usamah bin Zayd narrates that the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions forgave them as commanded, until permission came.

Meanwhile: **"Establish prayer and give Zakah, and whatever good you send forth for yourselves, you will find it with Allah."** The Day when excuses profit no one (40:52) is met with deeds sent ahead.

### "None shall enter Paradise unless he be a Jew or a Christian" (2:111–113)

*"These are their own desires. Say: Produce your Burhan — your proof — if you are truthful."*

The counter-statement is one of the most important theological formulas in the surah: **"Yes! But whoever submits his face to Allah while he is a Muhsin — his reward is with his Lord."** Sa'id bin Jubayr breaks it into the **two conditions for any deed to be accepted**:

1. **Sincerity** — *submits his face*, for Allah alone;
2. **Conformity to the Shari'ah** — *and he is a Muhsin*, following the Messenger.

Sincere but non-conforming deeds are rejected (*"Whoever performs a deed that does not conform with our matter, it is rejected"*), which is why the worship of monks and rabbis after the final Message will not be accepted, and why 25:23 describes such deeds as *"scattered floating particles of dust."* Conforming but insincere deeds are equally rejected — the hypocrite's prayer, standing lazily *to be seen by people* (4:142, 107:4–7).

And the mutual takfir: *"The Jews said the Christians follow nothing; the Christians said the Jews follow nothing — though they both recite the Scripture."* Each denies what its own Book affirms.

### Preventing People from the Masajid (2:114)

Read primarily of the Quraysh, who barred the Prophet ﷺ at Hudaybiyyah and expelled the believers from Makkah. *"Striving for their ruin"* is not only demolition — **the maintenance of a masjid means remembering Allah in it, establishing His law in it, and purifying it of shirk**, so preventing that is its ruin (cf. 9:17–18).

The ayah carries good news inside the reproach: *"It was not fitting that they should enter them except in fear"* — a promise that Islam would prevail, fulfilled when after 9 AH no idolater performed Hajj and none circled the House naked. **The punishment matched the deed exactly**: they prevented, then were prevented; they expelled, then were expelled.

### "And to Allah belong the east and the west" (2:115)

Ibn 'Abbas: **the first abrogation in the Qur'an concerned the Qiblah.** But the ayah is applied more widely: to voluntary prayer while traveling (Ibn 'Umar prayed facing wherever his mount was headed, attributing it to the Prophet ﷺ); to prayer in battle; and to one who cannot determine the direction in darkness or cloud and prays sincerely in the wrong direction. And the general ruling: *"What is between the east and the west is a Qiblah."*

### "Allah has begotten a son" (2:116–118)

*Glory is to Him.* The refutation is by relation: **the son of any being is born of two comparable beings; Allah has no equal, no rival, no consort** (6:101). Everything in the heavens and earth is His and *Qanitun* to Him — which Mujahid glosses as **obedient**, and Ibn Kathir divides into two Qunut: **legislated** (willing worship) and **destined** (the compelled submission of all creation, whose very shadows prostrate, 13:15).

The hadith Qudsi: *"The son of Adam has denied Me, and that is not his right; and he has insulted Me, and that is not his right. As for the denial — he claimed I am unable to bring him back as he was. As for the insult — he claimed I have a son."* And: **"No one is more patient when hearing an insult than Allah. They attribute a son to Him, yet He still gives them sustenance and health."**

*Badi'* — the Originator, who created without precedent. Ibn Kathir uses the word to make a distinction he clearly cares about: every innovation is linguistically a *bid'ah*, but religiously *"every innovation is a bid'ah and every bid'ah is misguidance"* — while 'Umar's gathering the people for Tarawih (itself an earlier practice of the Prophet ﷺ) was the *linguistic* sense when he said *"What a good bid'ah this is."*

*"Be — and it is."* Ibn Jarir's elegant closing of the argument: **He created the heavens and earth without precedent; likewise He created 'Isa, by His power, without a father.**

## 7. Ibrahim: The Alternative Ancestry (2:124–141)

Having dismantled the claim of the People of the Book to exclusive inheritance, the surah goes over their heads to the common father.

### The Testing (2:124)

*"When his Lord tried him with Kalimat (words), which he fulfilled."* The words are variously identified:

- **The rites of Hajj** (Ibn 'Abbas, one report)
- **The acts of Fitrah** — ten, listed in 'A'ishah's hadith: trimming the moustache, growing the beard, siwak, rinsing the nose, cutting the nails, washing the finger joints, plucking the underarm, shaving the pubic hair, washing after relieving oneself, rinsing the mouth. And Abu Hurayrah's five: circumcision, shaving the pubic hair, trimming the moustache, cutting the nails, plucking the underarm.
- **The great trials of his life** (Ibn Ishaq from Ibn 'Abbas): leaving his people; disputing with Nimrod; patience in the fire; migration; hosting guests at his own cost; and the command to slaughter his son. *"When Allah tested him with these and he was ready for the major test, Allah said: **Submit!** And he said: I have submitted to the Lord of all that exists"* — though it meant standing apart from everyone.

The reward: **"I am going to make you an Imam for mankind."** Ibrahim asked the same for his offspring, and was granted it — *"And We ordained among his offspring Prophethood and the Book"* (29:27) — **with one exclusion: "My covenant includes not the wrongdoers."** Ibn Jarir: **the unjust are not qualified to be Imams.** Ibn Khuwayz Mindad extends it: *"The unjust person does not qualify to be a Khalifah, a ruler, one who gives verdicts, a witness, or even a narrator of hadith."*

### The House (2:125–129)

**Makkah's sanctity is not Ibrahim's invention; it is Allah's decree from the day He created the heavens and the earth**, which Ibrahim conveyed. The Prophet ﷺ: *"Allah, not the people, made Makkah a sanctuary... It was not lawful to fight in it before me, and it was lawful for me for a few hours of one day; and today its sanctity has returned as it was before."* No thorn uprooted, no game chased, no lost property picked up except to announce it, no tree cut — except, at Al-'Abbas's request, lemon-grass.

The narrative of Hajar is preserved in full from Al-Bukhari: Ibrahim leaving her in the barren valley; her question *"Has Allah commanded you to do this?"* and his *"Yes"*; her reply — **"Then I am satisfied that Allah will never abandon us."** Her seven runs between Safa and Marwah, the angel digging with his heel, and the Prophet's ﷺ remark: *"May Allah grant mercy to the mother of Isma'il — had she left the water alone, it would have been flowing on the surface of the earth."*

Ibn Kathir draws the devotional lesson explicitly: **whoever performs Sa'i should remember her meekness, need and helplessness before Allah, and ask for the same providence** — that his own affairs be brought to success, his sins forgiven, his heart kept firm until death.

Then Ibrahim and Isma'il raise the foundations, saying: **"Our Lord! Accept this from us."** Ibn Kathir stops on this: two Prophets, building the House of Allah by His command, and **still afraid it will not be accepted.** *"O Khalil of Ar-Rahman! You raise the foundations of the House of Ar-Rahman, yet you fear He will not accept it from you?"* This is the disposition of 23:60 — *"those who give what they give, with their hearts full of fear."*

**Maqam Ibrahim** — the stone he stood on as the walls rose, Isma'il handing him stones. 'Umar's famous "agreements with his Lord" belong here: *"O Messenger of Allah, I wish you would take the Maqam as a place of prayer"* — and the ayah came down. (The other two: the ayah of hijab, and 66:5.)

And the supplication that closes it: **"Our Lord! Send amongst them a Messenger of their own."** Ibn Kathir's synthesis of the Prophet's ﷺ own genealogy of his mission: *"I am the supplication of my father Ibrahim, the glad tidings of 'Isa son of Maryam, and my mother saw a light that radiated from her illuminating the castles of Ash-Sham."*

Note what Ibrahim asks the Messenger to do — **recite the verses, teach the Book, teach the Hikmah (the Sunnah), and purify them** — and note that *purification* is asked for alongside instruction. Knowledge without purification was not the request.

### The Religion of Ibrahim (2:130–141)

*"And who turns away from the religion of Ibrahim except him who fools himself?"* Ibn Kathir's rhetorical force: is there anything more insane than abandoning the path of the one chosen as Imam in this life and counted among the righteous in the next?

**"Submit!" — "I have submitted to the Lord of all that exists."** This word was bequeathed: by Ibrahim to his sons, and by Ya'qub to his — *"O my sons! Allah has chosen for you the religion, then die not except as Muslims."* Meaning: **live upon it, so that you die upon it** — for one usually dies upon what he lived on, and is resurrected upon what he died on. (Balanced against the hadith of the man who works the deeds of Paradise until a span separates him from it, and then the Book takes precedence — the human's task is the striving, not the guarantee.)

Ya'qub's deathbed question — *"What will you worship after me?"* — and his sons' answer — *"Your God and the God of your fathers, Ibrahim, Isma'il and Ishaq, One God"* — is used by Ibn Kathir for a legal note too: **the grandfather is called a father**, which Abu Bakr took as evidence that he inherits in place of the brothers (Al-Bukhari says no opposing opinion was known); Malik, Ash-Shafi'i and Ahmad divide between grandfather and brothers.

The refrain that closes both this passage and the next (2:134, 2:141) is one of the surah's hardest lines: **"That was a nation who has passed away. They shall receive what they earned, and you what you earn — and you will not be asked of what they used to do."** Ancestry is not a credential. *"Whoever was slowed by his deeds will not be sped up by his lineage."*

Hence the Muslim creed of universal affirmation (2:136): **"We believe in Allah, and what was sent down to us, and to Ibrahim, Isma'il, Ishaq, Ya'qub and the tribes, and what was given to Musa and 'Isa and the Prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them."** The Prophet ﷺ recited this, with 3:52, in the two rak'ah before Fajr. And his instruction on their reports: *"Do not believe the People of the Book, nor reject what they say. Rather say: We believe in Allah and in what was sent down to us."*

**Sibghah** — Allah's "colour," His dye: **the religion of Allah**, per Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and the great majority — the fitrah one is dyed with and must hold to.

## 8. The Change of the Qiblah (2:142–152)

The Prophet ﷺ prayed toward Bayt al-Maqdis for **sixteen or seventeen months** in Madinah, which pleased the Jews — but he ﷺ kept looking to the sky, longing for the Qiblah of Ibrahim. Then the command came, mid-'Asr prayer. Al-Bara' narrates the scene: a man who had prayed with the Prophet ﷺ passed a mosque where people were **in ruku'**, and called out that he bore witness he had just prayed facing Makkah — *"and they turned toward the House while still bowing."* At Quba the news arrived during Fajr, and they turned mid-prayer.

Ibn Kathir extracts a usul principle: **an abrogating text takes effect only once knowledge of it arrives** — which is why no one was ordered to repeat the prayers already offered toward Jerusalem after the change had been revealed elsewhere.

**Three answers to the fools' question** — *"What has turned them from their Qiblah?"*

1. **"To Allah belong the east and the west"** (2:142) — the command, the decision and the authority are His. Righteousness is not in a direction; it is in obedience to whatever direction He names. *"If He commanded us every day to face a different place, we are His servants and we face where we are told."*
2. **"So that We may test who follows the Messenger from who turns on his heels"** (2:143). The change was heavy — *"except for those whom Allah guided."* The same event that adds doubt to the diseased heart adds faith to the sound one (9:124–125, 17:82).
3. **The reassurance**: when Companions had died praying toward Jerusalem and people asked about their prayers, Allah answered: **"And Allah would never make your faith to be lost. Truly, Allah is full of kindness, the Most Merciful towards mankind."** Ibn 'Abbas: *your prayer toward the first Qiblah, and your obedience in turning to the second — He will reward you for all of it.*

Ibn Kathir places the hadith of the captive woman here, and it is the emotional centre of the passage: the Prophet ﷺ saw a woman among the captives searching for her child, snatching up each infant to her chest until she found him and nursed him. *"Do you think this woman would throw her son into the fire?"* — "No, O Messenger of Allah!" — **"By Allah, Allah is more merciful with His servants than this woman with her son."**

**"Thus We have made you a Wasat nation"** (2:143). *Wasat* means **the best and the most honoured** — as Quraysh is the *wasat* of the Arabs, and 'Asr the *wusta* of the prayers. The Prophet ﷺ himself glossed it: **"Wasat means al-'Adl — the just."** And the office attached to the honour is testimony: on the Day of Resurrection, when earlier nations deny that their Prophets conveyed the message, **this Ummah will testify that they did, on the authority of what their own Prophet told them — and Muhammad ﷺ will attest to their testimony.**

The Jews, the ayah says, **already knew this change was coming** — it was in their Books, in the description of the Prophet ﷺ and his Ummah. *"They recognize him as they recognize their sons"* (2:146) — and 'Umar asked 'Abdullah bin Salam whether that was true, and he replied: **"Yes, and even more. The Honest One descended from heaven upon the Honest One on earth with his description, and I recognized him — though I know nothing of his mother's story."**

**Why the ruling is stated three times** (2:144, 149, 150) — Ibn Kathir's reading of the repetition:

| Instance | Function |
|---|---|
| 2:144 | Allah **grants the Prophet's ﷺ wish** — *"a Qiblah that shall please you"* |
| 2:149 | Allah affirms it is **the truth from your Lord** — not merely a concession, but what He loves |
| 2:150 | Allah removes the **arguments** of others — the People of the Book, who knew it was coming; and the Quraysh, who honoured the Ka'bah and could no longer say he followed a foreign Qiblah |

And *"except those of them that are wrongdoers"* — the Quraysh, whose objection (*if he follows Ibrahim, why did he ever face Jerusalem?*) has an answer that is the whole point: **he faced Jerusalem because Allah commanded it, and turned to the Ka'bah because Allah commanded it. He obeys in both cases; that is what following Ibrahim means.**

Then the bounty is named (2:151): the Messenger who recites, purifies, teaches the Book and the Hikmah, and **teaches you what you did not know.** Ibn 'Abbas: *"Allah's favour means Muhammad."* People who in Jahiliyyah uttered foolishness became, by his teaching, **the deepest in knowledge, the most pious in heart, and the most truthful in tongue** among mankind.

The response required (2:152) is the shortest and most quoted line in the section: **"Remember Me — I will remember you. And be grateful to Me, and never be ungrateful."** Al-Hasan: *"Remember Me regarding what I commanded you, and I will remember you regarding what I have obliged Myself with for your benefit."* And the hadith Qudsi: *"If you mention Me to yourself, I mention you to Myself; if you mention Me in a gathering, I mention you in a better gathering. If you draw near to Me a hand span, I draw near a forearm's length... and if you come to Me walking, I come to you running."*

## 9. Trial, Patience, and What Righteousness Actually Is (2:153–177)

### The Two Tools (2:153)

*"Seek help in patience and prayer."* Ibn Kathir's frame: **the servant is always in one of two states — enjoying a bounty to be thanked for, or suffering a trial to be borne.** Hence the hadith: *"Amazing is the believer! Whatever Allah decrees for him is better for him. If tested with a bounty, he is grateful, and that is better for him; if afflicted with hardship, he is patient, and that is better for him."*

Three kinds of *sabr*: patience in **avoiding the prohibited**, patience in **performing the obligatory** (which carries more reward), and patience **under affliction** (which is obligatory).

### The Living Dead (2:154)

**"Say not of those killed in the way of Allah, 'They are dead.' Nay, they are living, but you perceive it not."** The hadith of Muslim: their souls are in green birds moving through Paradise wherever they wish, then taking refuge in lamps hanging beneath the Throne. Their Lord asks what they wish for; they answer *"What more could we wish, when You have favoured us above all Your creation?"* — until, pressed, they say: **"We wish You would send us back, so we could be killed in Your cause again."**

Ibn Kathir adds the general hadith — *"the believer's soul is a bird feeding on the trees of Paradise until Allah returns it to its body"* — and notes that the ayah's singling out of martyrs is therefore **honour, not exclusion.**

### The Guaranteed Test (2:155–157)

**"And certainly We shall test you"** — not *may*. With something of:

- **fear** and **hunger** — and Ibn Kathir notes the word *Libas* (clothing) used elsewhere for these two (16:112), because they show on a person outwardly;
- **loss of wealth**;
- **lives** — losing friends, relatives, the loved;
- **fruits** — gardens and farms failing to produce.

Each preceded by *"something of"* — **a little of each.**

The response that earns the title *Sabirin*: **"Truly, to Allah we belong, and truly, to Him we shall return."** Not a formula but a recognition — that we are His property, that He does as He wills with His servants, that no atom's weight of deed is lost, and that the return is to Him.

The reward is triple, and 'Umar's comment catches it: *"What righteous things, and what a great height!"* — **the blessings (Salawat) and the mercy** are the two righteous things; **"and it is they who are the guided ones"** is the height, meaning more than what was promised.

Umm Salamah's story is the practical demonstration. Abu Salamah told her he had heard the Prophet ﷺ say: *"No Muslim is struck with an affliction and says Istirja', then says 'O Allah, reward me for my loss and give me what is better than it,' but Allah does just that."* She memorized it. When Abu Salamah died she said it — and then thought, *"Who could be better than Abu Salamah?"* When her 'iddah ended, the Prophet ﷺ proposed. She objected: she was jealous by nature, she was old, she had children. He ﷺ answered each: *"As for the jealousy, Allah will remove it from you. As for your age, I have suffered what you have suffered. As for your children — they are my children too."* She said afterwards: **"Allah compensated me with someone better than Abu Salamah: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ."**

### Safa and Marwah (2:158)

'Urwah read *"there is no sin on him who performs Tawaf between them"* as implying it was optional. 'A'ishah corrected him sharply: **if that were the meaning, the wording would have been "there is no sin if one does not."** The ayah was revealed for the Ansar, who in Jahiliyyah assumed ihlal for the idol Manat and therefore felt hesitant about the two hills; another report notes idols (Isaf and Na'ilah) had stood on them. The ayah removed the hesitation; **the Sunnah then established the Sa'i, and no one may abandon it.**

*"I start with what Allah started with"* — hence Safa first. And the Prophet ﷺ, seen with his garment twisted at the knees from the fast walking: *"Perform Sa'i, for Allah has prescribed Sa'i on you."*

### Concealing Knowledge (2:159–162)

**"Those who conceal the clear proofs and guidance We sent down... they are the ones cursed by Allah and cursed by the cursers."** Revealed, Abu Al-'Aliyah says, about those who hid the description of Muhammad ﷺ — but the principle is universal, and Ibn Kathir states its inverse: **everything asks forgiveness for the scholar, even the fish in the sea and the bird in the air; and everything curses the one who hides knowledge.**

The hadith: *"Whoever was asked about knowledge he has and hid it, a bridle of fire will be tied around his mouth on the Day of Resurrection."* Abu Hurayrah: *"If it were not for an ayah in the Book of Allah, I would not have narrated a single hadith to anyone"* — and he recited this one.

The door left open (2:160): **"Except those who repent, and do righteous deeds, and openly declare"** — *declare*, because the sin was concealment, so the repentance must be publication. Ibn Kathir notes this covers even those who called to innovation or disbelief: **if they repent, Allah forgives them.**

Note the structure: **curse (159) → exception for repentance (160) → the sealed curse for those who die on disbelief (161–162)**, whose punishment is *neither lightened nor postponed for an hour*. On cursing: cursing disbelievers as a class is agreed upon; cursing a **named individual** is disputed, since we do not know how his end will be — and the Prophet ﷺ stopped a man from cursing a repeatedly-flogged drunkard, saying **"Do not curse him; he loves Allah and His Messenger."**

### The Signs for People of Understanding (2:163–164)

**"And your God is One God; there is no deity but He, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful."** The hadith of Asma' bint Yazid: **Allah's Greatest Name is in these two ayat** — 2:163 and the opening of Al 'Imran.

Then the proofs, eight of them, offered as a single sweep (2:164): the creation of the heavens and the earth; the alternation of night and day, each taking from the other's length without ever overtaking it (36:40, 57:6); the ships carrying what benefits mankind between the shores; the rain reviving the dead earth; the moving creatures of every kind scattered through it, none of whose provision is unknown to Him (11:6); the veering of the winds — mercy sometimes and torment sometimes, herding clouds, bringing news; and the clouds suspended between sky and earth.

*"Are indeed signs for people of understanding"* — and Ibn Kathir pairs it with 3:190–191, where those who reflect on exactly this are described as remembering Allah **standing, sitting, and lying on their sides.**

### Rivals to Allah, and the Day They Disown Each Other (2:165–167)

*"Of mankind are some who take others besides Allah as rivals, loving them as they love Allah. But those who believe love Allah more."* The greatest sin, again by Ibn Mas'ud's hadith: **"to appoint a rival to Allah while He alone created you."**

Then a scene assembled from across the Qur'an: the angels disowning those who claimed to worship them (28:63, 34:4); the false deities becoming enemies of their worshippers and denying the worship (46:5–6, 19:81–82); Ibrahim telling his people *"the love between you is only in the life of this world, but on the Day of Resurrection you shall disown and curse each other"* (29:25); the weak and the arrogant trading blame (34:31–33); and Shaytan's final speech: **"Allah promised you a promise of truth, and I promised you and betrayed you. I had no authority over you except that I called you, and you responded. So blame me not, but blame yourselves"** (14:22).

*"Then all their relations will be cut off from them"* — Ibn 'Abbas: **the friendship.** And the followers' wish to return and disown their leaders is a lie, *"because if they were given the chance to go back, they would only return to what they were forbidden."*

**"Thus Allah will show them their deeds as regrets for them."** Their works do not merely fail; they *appear* — as scattered dust (25:23), as ashes in a storm wind (14:18), as a mirage the thirsty man walks toward and finds to be nothing (24:39).

### Eat the Lawful; Don't Follow the Footsteps (2:168–171)

**"O mankind! Eat of what is lawful and good on the earth, and follow not the footsteps of Shaytan."** The "footsteps" are the fabricated prohibitions of Jahiliyyah — *Bahirah, Sa'ibah, Wasilah* — the she-camels dedicated to idols and withdrawn from human use. In the hadith Qudsi: **"I created My servants Hunafa' (upright), but the devils came to them and led them astray from their religion and prohibited them what I had allowed for them."** Qatadah and As-Suddi generalize: **every act of disobedience is among the footsteps of Shaytan.**

His programme has an ascending order (2:169): **evil, then Fahishah (the sinful/lewd), then — worst — saying about Allah what you do not know.** Ibn Kathir: *"So this includes every innovator and disbeliever."*

And the reflexive defence of the polytheist: **"Nay, we shall follow what we found our fathers following"** — answered by a question that dissolves the whole appeal: *even though their fathers understood nothing and were not guided?* Then the parable: they are like a flock that hears the shepherd's call as unintelligible sound. **Deaf, dumb, blind — so they do not understand.**

### The Food Rulings (2:172–173)

*"Eat of the lawful things We have provided you with, and be grateful."* And the great hadith of purity of income: **"O people! Allah is Tayyib and only accepts what is Tayyib"** — then the man on a long journey, dishevelled and dust-covered, raising his hands to the sky crying *"O Lord! O Lord!"* — *"yet his food is unlawful, his drink is unlawful, his clothing is unlawful, and he was nourished by the unlawful. So how can it be accepted?"*

The prohibited list here is short and closed: **Maitah (the dead animal), blood, swine, and what is offered to other than Allah.** With the exceptions:

- **Sea creatures** are excluded from *Maitah* (5:96, and *"its water is pure and its dead are permissible"*).
- *"We have been allowed two dead things and two bloody things: fish and locusts, liver and spleen."*
- On items with residual traces (cheese made with rennet from unslaughtered animals), Al-Qurtubi's principle: a minute amount mixed into a large quantity does not matter. And the hadith of Salman: **"The allowed is what Allah allowed in His Book; the prohibited is what He prohibited in His Book; and what He did not mention is part of what He has pardoned."**

**Necessity** (2:173): *"But if one is forced by necessity, without willful disobedience nor transgressing due limits, then there is no sin on him."* Mujahid ties the phrase to purpose: if abstaining would drive him to highway robbery or rebellion, the permission applies; if he eats in disobedience, transgression, or habitually, it does not, **even in dire need**. Ibn 'Abbas: *"without willful disobedience means eating the dead animal and not continuing to do so."*

Two further rulings: if food belonging to another is available and can be taken without risk of harm, **the dead animal is not permitted** (with the hadith of 'Abbad bin Shurahbil, whom the Prophet ﷺ defended against the garden owner: *"You have not fed him when he was starving, nor taught him when he was ignorant"*). And Masruq's stark conclusion: **"Whoever is in dire need but does not eat until he dies will enter the Fire."** Eating the forbidden here is not merely permitted — **it is required.**

### Selling the Truth for a Small Price (2:174–176)

Why did they conceal? Ibn Kathir gives the motive without ornament: **they feared losing the authority, the position and the gifts they enjoyed among the Arabs.** So they hid what they knew to keep the little they were getting.

*"They eat into their bellies nothing but fire"* — as with the property of orphans (4:10), and as with the hadith of gold and silver vessels. *"Allah will not speak to them on the Day of Resurrection, nor purify them."* And **"How bold they are for the Fire!"** — Ibn Kathir reads it as the astonishment of the observer: how could anyone bear to walk so calmly toward that?

The irony completes itself: the people they feared would leave them **followed the Prophet ﷺ anyway**, and became his supporters against them. They lost the truth and the position both.

### Al-Birr — What Righteousness Is (2:177)

The capstone of this whole movement, and Ibn Kathir calls it an ayah *"containing many great wisdoms, encompassing rulings and correct beliefs."*

The Qiblah dispute had made a question urgent: *is righteousness a direction?* The answer:

> **"It is not Birr that you turn your faces towards east or west; but Birr is..."**

And then the definition, in four registers:

**1. Creed** — belief in *Allah, the Last Day, the Angels, the Book, the Prophets.* (Ath-Thawri recited only the first clause and said: *what follows are the types of Birr.*)

**2. Wealth given "in spite of love for it"** — Ibn Kathir stresses the phrase: giving while still wanting it. *"The best charity is what you give while healthy and thrifty, hoping to get rich and fearing poverty."* To eight recipients, in order:
- **kinsfolk** — *"charity to the poor is charity; charity to a relative is both charity and Silah"*
- **orphans** — the fatherless who cannot yet earn
- **the poor (Masakin)** — and the Prophet's ﷺ correction of the definition: *"The Miskin is not the one who roams and is turned away with a date or two. The Miskin is he who does not have what suffices him, and to whom people pay no attention, so he is not given charity."*
- **the wayfarer** — including the guest
- **those who ask**
- **freeing servants**

(Ibn Kathir notes the higher station still — 59:9, those who *prefer others over themselves though they are in need* — a rank above giving what one merely covets.)

**3. Worship and covenant** — establishing prayer *on time and with its due rights*, giving Zakah, **fulfilling covenants when they make them.** The opposite of this last is hypocrisy itself: *"if he speaks he lies; if he promises he breaks it; if he is entrusted he breaches the trust."*

**4. Patience in three specific places** — *in extreme poverty, in ailment, and at the time of fighting* (i.e. on the battlefield facing the enemy). Ibn Kathir notes that calling them *"the patient"* here is itself praise, because of what those three situations cost.

**"Such are the people of the truth, and they are Al-Muttaqun."** Ibn Kathir's closing note explains the double title: they are **truthful** because faith was realized in the heart, on the tongue, and in deed together; and **Muttaqun** because they avoided the prohibitions and performed the obediences.

## 10. The Law Begins (2:178–185)

The surah now turns to legislation, and each ruling arrives with its wisdom attached.

### Qisas — Equality in Retribution (2:178–179)

The context: Banu An-Nadir had defeated Banu Qurayzah in Jahiliyyah and imposed a two-tier law — **a Nadir killer paid dates; a Qurayzah killer was executed; and Nadir's blood money was double.** So the ayah's phrasing — *the free for the free, the slave for the slave, the female for the female* — is a **levelling**, abolishing a hierarchy of blood.

Rulings Ibn Kathir records:

- Some held this clause abrogated by *"life for life"* (5:45); but **the majority agree the Muslim is not executed for a disbeliever he kills** — *"The Muslim is not killed for the disbeliever,"* recorded by Al-Bukhari. (Abu Hanifah held otherwise, on the general wording of 5:45.)
- **A group is executed for one victim**, per the four Imams and the majority. 'Umar on a boy killed by seven men: *"If all the residents of San'a' collaborated on killing him, I would kill them all."* No Companion is known to have disagreed — a near-consensus. (An opinion attributed to Ahmad, and to Mu'adh, Ibn Az-Zubayr, Az-Zuhri, Ibn Sirin and others, holds only one is killed for one.)
- **Pardon** here means **accepting Diyah (blood money)** in cases of intentional murder — with a duty on both sides: the claimant *"should seek it in a good manner"* and the killer *"should pay it respectfully"*, without further harm or resistance.
- *"This is an alleviation and a mercy from your Lord"* — and Ibn 'Abbas explains the comparison. **The Tawrah's people had execution or pardon, but no blood money. The Injil's people were required to pardon. This Ummah alone has all three options.** Ibn Kathir underlines this: the mercy is in the *range*.
- Whoever kills after taking the Diyah has *"a painful torment"*.

**"And there is life for you in Qisas, O men of understanding."** The Arabs had a proverb: *killing stops further killing.* Ibn Kathir notes how much more concentrated the Qur'an's phrasing is. Abu Al-'Aliyah: *"How many a man thought about killing, and this law prevented him, for fear he would be killed in turn."* The law that takes one life is the law that saves many.

### The Will (2:180–182)

The command to bequeath to parents and next of kin was **obligatory before the ayat of fixed inheritance shares were revealed** — and Ibn 'Abbas himself said, reciting it, *"This ayah was abrogated"* (by 4:7). Fixed heirs now take their shares by right: **"Allah has given each heir his fixed share; so there is no will for a deserving heir."**

What remains:

- **A will is recommended for relatives who do not inherit** — up to a third. *"It is not permissible for any Muslim who has something to bequeath to stay two nights without his will written and kept with him"* — and Ibn 'Umar said that from the day he heard it, no night passed without his will beside him.
- **A third is the ceiling, and even that is much.** Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas asked to will all, then half, then a third: *"A third — and even a third is too much. It is better to leave your inheritors wealthy than to leave them poor, begging from others."* Ibn 'Abbas recommended a **fourth** instead.
- **Altering a will is a sin on the alterer, not the deceased** — Ibn 'Abbas: *"the dead person's reward is preserved for him by Allah, while the sin is acquired by those who change it."*
- **But correcting an unjust will is not "altering"** (2:182). If a testator errs — favouring one heir indirectly, or inflating a daughter's share through her son — whether from misplaced kindness or sinful intent, **the executor may correct it and reconcile the parties, and there is no sin on him.** Allah excluded this case explicitly so it would not fall under the prohibition.

The warning that closes it, from Abu Hurayrah: *"A man might do the works of righteous people for seventy years, then commit injustice in dictating his will — and so end with the worst of his deeds and enter the Fire. And a man might do the works of evil people for seventy years, then dictate a just will — and so end with the best of his deeds and enter Paradise."* Then he recited: **"These are the limits ordained by Allah, so do not transgress them."**

### Fasting (2:183–185)

**"Fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may acquire Taqwa."**

Ibn Kathir notes the rhetorical work the comparison does: knowing the obligation is ancient should make this Ummah **more** vigorous in it, not less. And the stated purpose is not the hunger but the taqwa — fasting *"purifies the souls and cleanses them of the evil that might mix with them,"* and it **narrows the paths of Shaytan**: *"O young people! Whoever can afford marriage, let him marry; whoever cannot, let him fast, for it is a shield for him."*

**The stages of the legislation** — Ibn Kathir traces three:

| Stage | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Before Ramadan was obligatory | **'Ashura** was fasted; the Prophet ﷺ found the Jews fasting it and said *"I have more right to Musa than you have"* |
| 2:184 | Fasting **or** paying Fidyah (feeding a poor person per day) was a **choice** for those able to fast |
| 2:185 | *"Whoever sights the month must fast it"* — **the choice is abrogated** for the healthy resident |

**Who still pays Fidyah instead of fasting.** Ibn 'Abbas insisted this part was **not** abrogated: *"It is for the old man and the old woman who are able to fast only with difficulty."* They feed a poor person per day and **do not make up the days**, because there is no expectation they will improve. Anas, when he could no longer fast, made a plate of *tharid* and fed thirty poor people. **The same ruling applies to the pregnant and breastfeeding woman who fears for herself or her child.**

**The month itself** (2:185). Allah singled Ramadan out by revealing the Qur'an in it — and Wathilah bin Al-Asqa' narrates that all the Books came in it: the Suhuf of Ibrahim on the 1st, the Tawrah on the 6th, the Injil on the 13th, and **the Qur'an on the 24th night of Ramadan.**

The Qur'an is described in three terms: **Hudan** (guidance for the hearts that follow it), **Bayyinat** (clear, unambiguous proofs for those who understand), and **Furqan** — the criterion separating truth from falsehood, guidance from misguidance, the lawful from the forbidden.

**The traveller and the ill.** They may break the fast and make up the days. Ibn Kathir presents the evidence for all three positions honestly:

- *Breaking is better* — the Prophet ﷺ broke his fast at Kadid on the march to Makkah and ordered those with him to do so. And: *"Hold to Allah's permission that He has granted you."*
- *They are equal* — Hamzah bin 'Amr Al-Aslami asked whether he should fast while travelling, and was told: **"Fast if you wish, or do not fast if you wish."** And on those Companions who fasted and those who didn't on the same journey: **"Those who did not fast have done good, and there is no harm for those who fasted"** — neither group criticizing the other.
- *Breaking is required when it becomes hard* — the Prophet ﷺ saw a man being shaded while fasting on a journey and said: **"It is not a part of Birr to fast while travelling."**

And a warning to those who ignore the permission out of a belief that using it is disliked: **they are required to break the fast and are not permitted to fast.**

Two more rulings: **the make-up days need not be consecutive** — consecutiveness is required only within Ramadan itself, which is why the ayah says merely *"the same number from other days."*

**"Allah intends for you ease, and He does not want to make things difficult for you."** Ibn Kathir gathers the hadiths of ease around it: *"Treat the people with ease and do not be hard on them; give them glad tidings and do not fill them with aversion"* — the instruction given to Mu'adh and Abu Musa when they were sent to Yemen, with *"and love each other, and do not differ"* attached. And: **"I was sent with the easy Hanifiyyah."**

The month closes as every act of worship closes — **with remembrance**: *"and that you must magnify Allah for having guided you, so that you may be grateful."* Ibn Kathir places this in a pattern: Takbir after completing the Hajj rites (2:200), remembrance after Jumu'ah (62:10), glorification before sunrise and sunset and after the prayers (50:39–40), and the Takbir by which Ibn 'Abbas said the Companions knew the Prophet ﷺ had finished praying. **The Takbir of 'Id al-Fitr is drawn from this ayah.**

## 11. In One Breath

Part I of Al-Baqarah opens by sorting all of humanity into four kinds — the believer, the disbeliever, the complete hypocrite and the flickering one — and then spends the rest of its length showing how each kind is *made*. It shows a nation given every conceivable favour — rescue, revelation, food from the sky, water from stone, a mountain held over their heads — and shows their hearts hardening past the responsiveness of rock, until they conceal the very Prophet they had been praying for, out of envy that he came from the other branch of the family. Against that, it sets Ibrahim: tested with everything, obedient in all of it, made an Imam — and told plainly that the covenant does not run through wrongdoers, however impeccable their lineage.

Between the two stands the change of the Qiblah, which is the hinge of the whole part. It is small as a ruling and enormous as a test: *who follows the Messenger, and who turns on his heels?* Righteousness, the surah then says outright, was never a direction — it is creed, and money given while you still want it, and prayer on time, and covenants kept, and patience in poverty, in illness, and under fire. And then the law arrives to build that character into a society: retribution that preserves life, wills that protect heirs from their own testator's sentiment, and a month of hunger whose stated purpose is not hunger at all but **taqwa**.

The thread running through all of it is a single question the surah asks in a dozen forms: **when Allah commands, and the command is hard, or unfamiliar, or contrary to what your fathers did — do you obey?** The Children of Israel are shown answering no, ingeniously and repeatedly. Ibrahim is shown answering *"I have submitted to the Lord of all that exists."* And the Companions are shown turning toward Makkah **mid-bow**, without finishing the rak'ah they had started.
