*Revealed in Makkah · 35 ayat · the last of the seven "Ha Mim" surahs · named for the sand-dunes of 46:21, where Hud warned the people of 'Ad*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Ahqaf is short, and it is built around a single demand: **produce your evidence**. Everything the idolaters believe is put on trial and asked for its warrant — *show Me what your gods created of the earth; show Me their share in the heavens; bring Me a scripture from before this, or some trace of inherited knowledge, if you are truthful.* Ibn Kathir's gloss is blunt: you have nothing, neither textual nor rational.

Around that demand the surah arranges four kinds of witness. **Creation** testifies — the heavens and earth were not made in idle play, and the One who made them without fatigue can certainly raise the dead. **Scripture** testifies — a witness from the Children of Israel recognises what he is hearing, because the earlier books prophesied it. **History** testifies — 'Ad were far more firmly established than you, had hearing, sight and hearts, and it availed them nothing when a wind they mistook for rain-clouds erased them. And, most strikingly, **the jinn** testify: a group of them overhear a single Fajr recitation at Nakhlah, recognise it instantly, and go home as warners — while the Prophet ﷺ, reciting, does not even know they were there.

Between these, the surah plants its most-quoted passage: the **best and worst of children**. One prays for his parents at forty and is told his worst deeds will be overlooked; the other says *"Uff"* to them and denies he will ever be raised. And it closes by telling the Prophet ﷺ to be patient the way the Messengers of firm resolve were patient, and to stop asking for the punishment to come quickly — because when it comes, this whole life will feel like an hour of one day.

---

## 1. Ask Them for Their Evidence (46:1–6)

> *We created not the heavens and the earth and all that is between them except with truth, and for a specified term.*

Not in idle play, says Ibn Kathir — and the "specified term" is **fixed, neither increasing nor decreasing**. Against that seriousness, the disbelievers are simply *distracted* from what has been sent for their benefit. A Book has been revealed to them, a Messenger sent to them, and they turn deliberately away from both.

Then comes the cross-examination, and it is worth reading as four separate questions:

| The demand | What it means |
|---|---|
| *Show me what they have created of the earth* | Point to a single square of ground they independently made |
| *Or have they a share in the heavens?* | They own not even the thin membrane over a date-stone |
| *Bring me a scripture prior to this* | Produce one revealed book, given to any prophet, commanding worship of these idols |
| *Or some trace of knowledge* | Produce one shred of clear evidence for the path you chose |

Ibn Kathir notes an alternative recitation, *atharah* — **"or some inheritance of knowledge"** — which Mujahid reads as: anyone at all who inherited real knowledge on the matter. Either way the verdict is the same: *you have absolutely no evidence, neither revealed nor rational.*

**Who is more astray?** (46:5) Not the man who worships a rival power, but the man who calls on something that **cannot answer him until the Day of Resurrection and does not even know it is being called** — because it can neither hear, nor see, nor act. It is inanimate, senseless stone.

And on the Day of Gathering, the relationship inverts completely: the objects of worship become **enemies** of their worshippers and deny the worship ever happened. Ibn Kathir sets this beside two other verses — *"No! They will deny their worship of them, and will be opponents to them"* (19:81–82), and Ibrahim's warning that *"the love between you is only in the life of this world"* (29:25). They betray you at the exact moment you need them most.

## 2. "Magic." "He Made It Up." (46:7–8)

The clear signs are recited, and the response is *plain magic* — in which, Ibn Kathir says, they lied, invented, strayed and disbelieved in one breath.

The answer to "he fabricated it" is a self-incriminating oath: **if I invented this against Allah, you could not save me from Him.** Compare 72:22–23 (*"None can protect me from Allah"*) and, most severely, 69:44–47:

> *And if he had made up a false saying concerning Us, We surely would have seized him by the right hand, then cut off his life artery — and none of you could have withheld Us from him.*

The passage ends where you would least expect a threat to end: **"And He is the Oft-Forgiving, the Most Merciful."** Ibn Kathir reads this as a door held open — *despite all of this, if you turn back, He will accept your repentance, pardon you, forgive you and have mercy on you.* The same move appears at 25:5–6, where "legends of the ancients" is answered by "He is ever Forgiving and Merciful."

---

## 3. "I Do Not Know What Will Be Done With Me" (46:9)

Two claims, both humbling:

**"I am not a new thing among the Messengers."** I am not an unprecedented event that should make you baulk. Messengers came before me, to nation after nation.

**"Nor do I know what will be done with me or with you."** Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan and Qatadah held this was later abrogated by *"that Allah may forgive you your past and future sins"* (48:2) — and the confirmed report is that when 48:2 came, the Muslims said, *"May you enjoy that, O Messenger of Allah — but what about us?"* and 48:5 came in reply.

The scene that fixes the ayah's meaning is Umm Al-'Ala's, recorded by Ahmad and Al-Bukhari. Her household nursed **'Uthman bin Maz'un** until he died. When the Prophet ﷺ came in she said, *"May Allah have mercy on you, Abu As-Sa'ib — I bear witness that Allah has honoured you."* He asked her: *How do you know that Allah has honoured him?* She said she did not know. He said:

> *As for him, certainty has come to him from his Lord, and I truly wish him well. But by Allah, though I am the Messenger of Allah, I do not know what will be done with me.*

She replied, *"Never will I claim anyone to be pious after this."* Later she dreamt 'Uthman owned a running spring, and the Prophet ﷺ told her: **that was his deeds**.

From this Ibn Kathir derives a firm rule: **do not declare of any specific person that he is in Paradise**, except those Allah or His Messenger named — the Ten, Ibn Sallam, Al-Ghumaysa', Bilal, Suraqah, 'Abdullah bin 'Amr bin Haram, the Seventy Reciters killed at Bi'r Ma'unah, Zayd bin Harithah, Ja'far, Ibn Rawahah, and their like.

## 4. The Witness from the Children of Israel (46:10–12)

> *Say: Tell me — if it is from Allah and you disbelieved in it, while a witness from among the Children of Israel testified to something similar and believed, while you turned away in arrogance?*

The earlier scriptures **prophesied things like this**, so a man who knows them recognises the Qur'an when he hears it. Masruq's line is the sharpest: *that witness believed in his Prophet and his Book — while you disbelieved in your Prophet and your Book.*

Ibn Kathir handles the identification carefully. Sa'd narrated that the Prophet ﷺ never said of anyone walking the earth that he was of the people of Paradise except **'Abdullah bin Salam**, and named this ayah in that connection; Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ikrimah, Ath-Thawri, Malik and others agree. But Ibn Kathir keeps the wording general — *the witness here refers to any witness* — because **the ayah is Makkan, revealed before 'Abdullah bin Salam accepted Islam**. Compare 28:53 and 17:107–108, where those given knowledge fall on their faces at the recitation.

**"Had it been any good, they would not have got there before us."** (46:11) The status argument again: they meant Bilal, 'Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab and the other poor, the servants and slave-women. The pagans said it because they assumed their own standing with Allah was high — the error answered by *"Is it these whom Allah has favoured from among us?"* (6:53).

Ibn Kathir then flips the same sentence into a **principle of Sunnah**. Ahl us-Sunnah say of any practice not reported from the Companions: *it is an innovation — for if there were any good in it, they would have preceded us to it, since they left no good quality untaken.* The disbelievers' sneer, turned right way up, becomes a rule of religion.

Calling the Qur'an *"ancient falsehood"* — old borrowed material — is diagnosed with the hadith: **arrogance is rejecting the truth and belittling people.** Against it, 46:12 sets the lineage: the Scripture of Musa as a *guide and mercy*, and this Book **confirming what came before it, in the Arabic tongue** — eloquent and clear — a warning to wrongdoers and glad tidings to the doers of good.

## 5. "Our Lord Is Allah," and Then Stood Firm (46:13–14)

Ibn Kathir refers the explanation back to 41:30, and reads the promise in two directions:

- **No fear** — concerning what is ahead of them.
- **No grief** — over what they have left behind.

Paradise, abiding forever, *a reward for what they used to do.*

## 6. The Good Child (46:15–16)

> *We have enjoined on man kindness to his parents. His mother bore him with hardship, and delivered him with hardship.*

Ibn Kathir places this deliberately: having established Allah's oneness and sincere worship, revelation turns immediately to **parents** — as at 17:23 and 31:14.

The occasion is **Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas**, whose mother told him: *hasn't Allah commanded you to obey your parents? Then I will neither eat nor drink until you disbelieve.* She held out until they had to prise her mouth open with a stick — and the ayah came down. Obedience to parents does not extend to disbelief; kindness to them is not thereby cancelled.

**The hardship is itemised**: fatigue, sickness, vomiting, heaviness, distress through the pregnancy; then the pains of labour and their severity.

### The thirty months

'Ali, may Allah be pleased with him, read *"his gestation and weaning is thirty months"* against *"his weaning is in two years"* (31:14) and *"mothers suckle their children two complete years"* (2:233), and concluded that **the minimum term of pregnancy is six months**. Ibn Kathir calls the conclusion strong and valid; 'Uthman and a number of Companions accepted it.

The case that settled it is grim. A Juhaynah woman delivered six months after her marriage; her husband complained, and 'Uthman summoned her for stoning. As she dressed, her sister wept; she said, *"Why do you cry? By Allah, none of Allah's creation has ever approached me but him. So let Allah decree as He wills."* 'Ali arrived and asked 'Uthman, *"Don't you read the Qur'an?"* — then did the subtraction: thirty months minus two full years leaves six. 'Uthman said, *"By Allah, I did not see that — bring the woman back."* **She had already been killed.** The child grew to be the image of his father; the father, who had accused her, was later afflicted with an abscess in his face that ate him until he died.

Ibn Abbas ran the same arithmetic forwards: nine months' gestation leaves twenty-one months of suckling; seven months leaves twenty-three; six months leaves the full two years.

### At forty

*Full strength* is maturity of ability; *forty years* is maturity of intellect, understanding and patience — and, it has been said, **the age past which a man does not usually change his ways**. The prayer given for that moment asks for four things: gratitude for His favour on me *and on my parents*, righteous deeds that please Him **in future**, goodness in my offspring, and acceptance of repentance. Ibn Kathir draws the instruction out plainly: **whoever reaches forty should renew his repentance and turn to Allah with strong resolve.**

The reward is disproportionate on purpose — *We shall accept the best of what they did and overlook their evil deeds.* Many errors forgiven; a modest quantity of deeds accepted.

## 7. The Bad Child (46:17–20)

> *But he who says to his parents: "Uff to you! Do you promise me that I will be raised, when generations before me have passed?"*

His parents beg Allah to guide him and plead, *"Woe to you — believe! Allah's promise is true."* He answers with the stock phrase: *legends of the ancients.*

**On the claim that this was revealed about 'Abdur-Rahman bin Abi Bakr:** Ibn Kathir rejects it as weak, since 'Abdur-Rahman embraced Islam *after* this was revealed and was among the best of his contemporaries. The claim's source is political. Marwan bin Al-Hakam, governor of the Hijaz, was pressing the people to pledge allegiance to Yazid; 'Abdur-Rahman objected; Marwan ordered his arrest, and when he escaped into 'A'ishah's house declared, *"This is the one about whom Allah revealed..."* From behind the curtain 'A'ishah answered: **"Allah revealed no Qur'an about us at all, except the declaration of my innocence."** In An-Nasa'i's version, after Marwan called the succession *"the tradition of Abu Bakr and 'Umar"* and 'Abdur-Rahman replied *"rather, the tradition of Heraclius and Caesar,"* 'A'ishah said flatly: **"Marwan is lying."**

The verse describes **a type, not a man** — Ibn Kathir notes that the switch to the plural *"Those are the ones"* in 46:18 proves it. Al-Hasan and Qatadah: it applies to the disbelieving, sinful person undutiful to his parents who denies resurrection.

**Degrees on both sides** (46:19): punishment is calibrated exactly to deeds, with no injustice by so much as a speck. 'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam: *the levels of the Fire descend, and those of Paradise ascend.*

**"You received your good things in the life of the world, and took your pleasure in them"** (46:20) is said by way of reproach. It frightened the best of men: **'Umar bin Al-Khattab** gave up many pleasures of food and drink saying, *"I fear being among those whom Allah reproached with this."* Abu Mijlaz added that some people will find their good deeds already spent in this life. The punishment then matches the crime — they enjoyed, were arrogant, rebelled; so it is **humiliation** they are recompensed with.

## 8. 'Ad and the Rain That Wasn't (46:21–25)

*Remember the brother of 'Ad* — Hud, sent to the first 'Ad, who lived in **Al-Ahqaf**. *Ahqaf* is the plural of *haqf*: a sand-dune (Ibn Zayd), or a mountain or cave (Ikrimah). Qatadah: a tribe in Yemen, dwelling among the sands, overlooking the sea in a land called **Ash-Shihr**. The Prophet ﷺ once prayed: *May Allah have mercy on us and on the brother of 'Ad.*

Warners had passed *before him and after him* — Messengers sent to the towns around 'Ad (cf. 41:13–14). Their reply was the standard one: *have you come to turn us from our gods? Then bring us what you threaten us with, if you are truthful* — asking for the punishment to be hastened because they were certain it would never come (cf. 42:18). Hud's answer separates the two jobs neatly: **the knowledge is with Allah; my task is only to convey.**

Then the image the surah is famous for:

> *When they saw it as a dense cloud approaching their valleys, they said: "This is a cloud bringing us rain!"*

They were in drought. They saw the punishment and **rejoiced**, because it looked like mercy. *Nay — it is what you asked to be hastened: a wind carrying a painful torment*, destroying everything it was permitted to destroy (cf. 51:42, which leaves what it touches like rotten ruins), until **nothing could be seen but their dwellings**.

### The Prophet's face when clouds came

'A'ishah reported that she never saw the Prophet ﷺ laugh so fully that his uvula showed — he only smiled — and that when he saw clouds or wind, **discomfort showed on his face**. She asked him why, when people rejoice at clouds hoping for rain. He said:

> *O 'A'ishah, what guarantees me that there is no punishment in it? A people were punished with wind. A people saw the punishment and said: "This is a cloud bringing us rain."*

He would break off whatever he was doing, even prayer, to say: *O Allah, I seek refuge with You from the evil it contains* — and if rain fell: *O Allah, make it pouring and beneficent.* And with wind: *O Allah, I ask You for its good, the good in it, and the good it was sent with; and I seek refuge from its evil, the evil in it, and the evil it was sent with.* His colour would change and his anxiety would not lift until the rain came.

## 9. Better Equipped Than You, and It Saved Them Nothing (46:26–28)

> *We had firmly established them in that in which We have not established you.*

Addressed to the Makkans: the earlier nations had wealth and offspring you have not been given anything close to. **They also had hearing, vision and hearts** — and none of it availed them, because they used those faculties to deny Allah's signs. What they mocked closed in around them completely.

The warning is geographical, and deliberately local. Allah destroyed the nations **around Makkah**:

- **'Ad** — at Al-Ahqaf in Hadramawt, near Yemen
- **Thamud** — between Makkah and Ash-Sham
- **Saba'** — in Yemen
- **Madyan** — on the route to Ghazzah
- **The people of Lut** — by the Dead Sea, which the Makkans passed on the road to Jordan

Signs were shown *in various ways, so that they might return*. And the gods taken **as a means of approach to Allah** — the exact justification the idolaters gave — were absent at the one moment they were needed. *They vanished completely from them.* That was their lie, and their fabrication.

## 10. The Jinn at Nakhlah (46:29–32)

Two accounts, and Ibn Kathir gives both.

**Az-Zubayr** (via Ahmad): they were at a place called Nakhlah while the Prophet ﷺ prayed 'Isha', and they *crowded on top of one another* to hear him (72:19) — Sufyan said, like piled wool.

**Ibn Abbas** (in Al-Bukhari and Muslim): the Prophet ﷺ **never recited to the jinn and never saw them**. The devils had been barred from eavesdropping on the heavens and were being struck by burning flames; their people told them to search the earth east and west for the cause. A party reached Tihamah and found the Prophet ﷺ leading **Fajr** at Nakhlah on the road to the 'Ukaz market. They heard the recitation, stopped, and said: *by Allah, this is what has barred you.* They went home and told their people they had heard a wondrous recitation guiding to the right path — and then 72:19 was revealed to inform the Prophet ﷺ of what had happened. **What was revealed to him was the jinn's own speech.**

Ibn Mas'ud adds: they came down on him while he recited at Nakhlah, said *"Listen quietly!"* — meaning *hush* — and were **nine in number**, one named Zawba'ah. Only afterwards did they come to him deliberately, group after group.

### What the jinn said, and what it establishes

**"A Book sent down after Musa."** Why not after 'Isa? Because the Injil was largely admonition and exhortation, with few rulings — in practice a complement to the Tawrah, which remained the legal reference. Ibn Kathir notes **Waraqah bin Nawfal** spoke the same way when the Prophet ﷺ described Jibril: *this is the one who used to come to Musa.*

**"It guides to the truth and to the straight path."** Two clauses, two domains: the truth in **belief and information**; the straight path in **deeds**. The Qur'an is information and command — its information true, its commands just (6:115).

**"O our people, respond to Allah's Caller."** Proof that Muhammad ﷺ was sent to **both jinn and mankind**.

**"He will forgive you some of your sins."** Some scholars treat *"some"* as merely auxiliary; Ibn Kathir finds that questionable, since the particle rarely strengthens an affirmative. Others read it as partial forgiveness.

**Do the jinn have Messengers?** Warners from among themselves, yes — but no Messengers. Allah sent Messengers only from *men of the townships* (12:109), who ate food and walked the markets (25:20), and after Ibrahim placed prophethood and scripture **in his descendants** (29:27). The address *"O company of jinn and men, did there not come to you Messengers from among you?"* (6:130) covers both collectively but applies specifically to one — just as pearls are said to emerge *"from both"* seas (55:22) though they come from only one.

## 11. He Was Not Wearied (46:33–35)

> *Do they not see that Allah, who created the heavens and the earth and was not wearied by their creation, is able to give life to the dead?*

Creation cost Him nothing: He said *Be*, and they were, **without refusal or delay**. And the greater work is already done — *the creation of the heavens and the earth is greater than the creation of mankind, but most people do not know* (40:57). So the question answers itself: **Yes, He surely is able to do all things.**

On the Day they are exposed to the Fire they will be asked, *Is this not the truth? Is this magic? Or do you not see?* — and they will have no option but to confess: **"Yes, by our Lord."**

### The command to the Prophet ﷺ

> *Be patient as the Messengers of firm resolve were patient, and be in no hurry for them.*

The **Ulul-'Azm** are Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa and Muhammad ﷺ — named together at 33:7 and 42:13. They were patient with their peoples' rejection; so be patient with yours. And *do not rush the punishment for them* (cf. 73:11, 86:17).

Because the wait, seen from the other end, is nothing. On the Day they see what they were promised, it will seem **as though they had stayed only an hour of a day** (cf. 79:46, 10:45).

**"A clear message."** Then the closing justice: *will any be destroyed except the defiantly rebellious?* Ibn Kathir: **Allah destroys no one but those who choose the way of destruction**, and punishes no one who does not deserve it.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Ask for the evidence.** *Show me what they created of the earth. Bring me a scripture. Bring me a trace of knowledge.* Ibn Kathir's verdict on idolatry is that it has no warrant — not revealed, not rational.
- **The gods you lean on will deny you.** They neither hear nor answer now, and on the Day of Gathering they turn hostile and disown the worship entirely.
- **"I do not know what will be done with me."** The Prophet ﷺ said it over the body of a good man. Hope for people, pray for them — but do not certify anyone into Paradise.
- **"If there were any good in it, they would have preceded us to it."** The pagans' sneer at Bilal and 'Ammar, turned around, becomes the Sunni test for innovation.
- **Thirty months, and a woman killed for arithmetic no one had done.** The mother's hardship is itemised because it is meant to be felt — and 'Ali's reading of two verses together saved every woman who came after her.
- **At forty, renew your repentance.** The prayer of 46:15 asks for gratitude, righteous deeds *going forward*, good offspring, and acceptance — and the answer is that the best deeds are taken and the worst overlooked.
- **The cloud they cheered was the punishment.** 'Ad saw relief coming and rejoiced. The Prophet ﷺ, who knew that story, could not look at a cloud without unease.
- **Equipment is not protection.** 'Ad had hearing, sight, hearts, wealth and power beyond anything Makkah had — and none of it counted, because of what they did with it.
- **Nine jinn heard one Fajr and became warners.** The reciter did not know they were there. You never know who is listening, or what a single recitation sets in motion.
- **He was not wearied by making the heavens.** Which settles the resurrection question completely — and leaves only patience, of the kind the Messengers of firm resolve had, for the short hour that remains.
