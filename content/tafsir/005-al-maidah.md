## 1. When It Was Revealed, and Why That Matters

Ibn Kathir opens not with virtues but with **chronology**, because with this surah chronology *is* the virtue.

- **Abdullah bin `Amr:** the last surahs revealed were Al-Ma'idah and Al-Fath (48). At-Tirmidhi graded it *Hasan Gharib*.
- **Ibn Abbas** held that the last surah revealed was An-Nasr (110) — Ibn Kathir records the disagreement rather than resolving it.
- **Aishah to Jubayr bin Nufayr:** *"Do you read Al-Ma'idah? It was the last Surah to be revealed. Whatever permissible matters you find in it, treat them as permissible; whatever impermissible, treat as impermissible."*

The legal weight of this is enormous and Ibn Kathir means it to be: **a late surah abrogates, it is not abrogated.** Its rulings are final. This is why Jarir bin Abdullah's testimony — *"I embraced Islam after Al-Ma'idah was revealed, and I saw the Messenger ﷺ wipe over his Khuffs"* — settled a legal dispute; the scholars valued that hadith precisely because of its date.

In the same narration Jubayr asks Aishah about the Prophet's ﷺ character, and she answers in two words: **"The Qur'an."**

## 2. Fulfil Your Obligations (5:1–2)

**"O you who believe! Fulfil your obligations."**

Ibn Mas`ud's advice to a man who asked him for counsel sets the register for the whole surah: *"When you hear Allah's statement 'O you who believe', pay full attention — for it is a righteous matter He is ordaining or an evil thing He is forbidding."* Khaythamah adds that where the Qur'an says *O you who believe*, the Tawrah reads *O you who are in need*.

**'Uqud (obligations)** — Ibn Abbas and Mujahid: treaties and covenants; Ibn Jarir reports consensus on this. Ibn Abbas widens it: everything Allah permitted, prohibited, ordained and limited in the Qur'an. **Do not commit treachery. Do not break covenants.**

**5:2 — The sanctities.** Do not violate the *Sha'a'ir* (Ibn Abbas: the rites of Hajj; Mujahid: Safa, Marwah, the sacrificial animal), the Sacred Month, the *Hady*, the garlands, or those travelling to the Sacred House. The four sacred months, per the Farewell Hajj sermon: Dhul-Qa'dah, Dhul-Hijjah, Muharram, and Rajab of Mudar — *"the division of time has returned as it was when Allah created the heavens and the earth."*

**The moral centre of the ayah** is the clause about hatred. At Hudaybiyyah the idolators barred the Muslims from the House; later idolators passed by heading to Umrah and the Companions said, *let us bar them as we were barred.* The ayah came down: **let not the hatred of a people who once stopped you lead you to transgress.** Ibn Kathir pairs it with 5:8 — *"be just, that is nearer to Taqwa"* — and states the principle flatly: **justice is ordained for everyone, in all situations, friend or enemy.**

**"Help one another in Al-Birr and At-Taqwa, but not in sin and transgression."** *Birr* is righteous action, *Taqwa* is abstaining from sin; Ibn Jarir: *Ithm* is abandoning what Allah ordained, *`Udwan* is overstepping His limits. The Prophet ﷺ: *"Support your brother whether he is unjust or the victim of injustice"* — and when asked how one supports an oppressor, he said: **"Prevent him from committing injustice — that is your support to him."**

## 3. The Forbidden Foods, and the Day the Religion Was Perfected (5:3)

The prohibited list, and what each term covers:

| Prohibited | Ibn Kathir's explanation |
|---|---|
| **Al-Maytah** | Any animal dead without proper slaughter — harmful because the blood congeals in its veins. **Exceptions: fish and locusts** (*"Its water is pure and its dead are permissible"*) |
| **Blood** | Only *flowing* blood (Ibn Abbas, Sa`id bin Jubayr; cf. 6:145). Ibn Abbas permitted the spleen: *"you are only prohibited blood that was poured forth."* Liver and spleen are the "two bloods" permitted |
| **Swine** | Domestic and wild, and the entire animal including fat — *Lahm* in Arab usage means the whole body |
| **Sacrificed to other than Allah** | Unlawful by consensus |
| **Strangled / beaten / fallen / gored / partly eaten** | All lawful species, made unlawful by *how they died* |
| **Slaughtered on An-Nusub** | The 360 stone altars around the Ka'bah. Forbidden **even if Allah's Name was pronounced** — because the act itself is a form of Shirk |
| **Al-Azlam** | Divination arrows |

**"Unless you are able to slaughter it"** rescues the previous five categories: if you reach the animal while life remains — Ali: *"while it still moves a foot or a leg"* — slaughter it and eat. Rafi` bin Khadij's hadith gives the instrument rule: *"use what makes blood flow… but not teeth or claws — teeth are bone, and claws are what the Ethiopians slaughter with."*

**Al-Azlam and the alternative Allah gives.** Three arrows: *Do*, *Do not*, blank; they would redraw until a directive came up. Quraysh kept seven such arrows by Hubal inside the Ka'bah. When the Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah he found pictures of Ibrahim and Isma'il holding divining arrows and said: **"May Allah fight them! They know these two never used the Azlam."** Ibn Kathir's point is not merely that the practice is forbidden but that **Allah replaced it** — hence he inserts the full text of the *Istikharah* supplication: the servant who wants to know what to do prays two rak'ah, and asks the One who knows while he does not.

### "This day I have perfected your religion for you"

Ibn Kathir calls this **the greatest favour Allah conferred on this Ummah**: the religion completed, no further prophet or law needed, Muhammad ﷺ made the Final Prophet to all humans and jinn — so the lawful is what he permits, the unlawful what he forbids, the Law what he legislates.

Two reactions preserved side by side, and Ibn Kathir keeps both:

- **A Jewish man to Umar:** *"There is a verse in your Book which, had it been revealed to us, we would have made that day a festival."* Umar replied: *"By Allah, I know when and where it was revealed — the Day of Arafah, and I was at Arafah."* (Al-Bukhari, Muslim, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i; the Farewell Hajj, and the scholars of Sirah agree it was a Friday.)
- **Umar wept** when it was revealed. The Prophet ﷺ asked why. *"Because our religion is being perfected. Now it is perfect — and nothing is perfect but it is bound to deteriorate."* The Prophet ﷺ said: **"You have spoken the truth."** Ibn Kathir supports this with: *"Islam began strange and will return strange; so Tuba for the strangers."*

**Necessity.** *"Whoever is forced by severe hunger, with no inclination to sin"* — Allah is Oft-Forgiving. Ibn Kathir corrects a popular error: **one need not wait three days** before eating from a carcass; the Prophet ﷺ told Abu Waqid, *"When you find neither lunch nor dinner nor produce, then eat from it."* And: *"Allah likes that His Rukhsah be used, just as He dislikes that He be disobeyed."* The condition is that the heart does not *incline* toward the forbidden thing.

## 4. Hunting, the Food of the People of the Book, and Marriage (5:4–5)

**Trained hunting animals (*Jawarih*).** From *jarh*, "to earn" — dogs, falcons, and any trained bird or beast of prey. The conditions, from `Adi bin Hatim's hadiths:

- Allah's Name is mentioned **when the animal is released**, not only at eating.
- If you find the game alive, slaughter it; if dead, eat — *"the dog has caused its slaughter to be fulfilled."*
- **If the dog ate from it, do not eat** — *"I fear it caught it for itself."*
- If a second, unnamed dog joined the hunt, do not eat.
- With the *Mi'rad* (a hunting stick): **sharp edge, eat; broad side, do not** — that is beating to death.

Az-Zuhri, asked about drinking urine medicinally, answered that it is not among the *Tayyibat*.

**"The food of the People of the Scripture is lawful to you."** Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa`id bin Jubayr, Ata', Al-Hasan and others: **their slaughtered animals.** Agreed on by the scholars, because the People of the Book hold that slaughtering for other than Allah is forbidden and pronounce Allah's Name — *even though they hold beliefs about Allah unbefitting His majesty.* Ibn Kathir uses the Khaybar hadith (the poisoned roasted leg) to argue that the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions ate from Jewish food **without asking whether the parts Jews forbid themselves, such as the fat, had been removed** — a point the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis pressed against the Malikis.

**"And your food is lawful to them"** — Ibn Kathir prefers the reading: *you are permitted to feed them from your slaughtered animals*, as fair and equal treatment.

**Marriage to chaste women of the People of the Book.** Permitted; the word *Muhsanat* means chaste — free of fornication. Ibn Abbas notes the sequence: after 2:221 (*do not marry idolatresses until they believe*) people stopped marrying pagan women; when 5:5 came, they married women of the People of the Book. **Ibn Umar dissented**, advising against marrying Christian women: *"I know of no worse Shirk than her saying that `Isa is her lord."* Ibn Kathir preserves the disagreement without flattening it.

The chastity requirement is **mutual**: *"desiring chastity, not illegal sexual intercourse, nor taking them as lovers"* applies to the men as much as the women — no mistresses, no adultery on either side. And the *Mahr* must be given willingly: *"just as these women are chaste and honourable, then give them their Mahr with a good heart."*

## 5. Purification: Wudu, Ghusl, Tayammum (5:6)

This is the Qur'an's foundational ablution verse, and Ibn Kathir treats it as a fiqh manual.

**Obligation and scope.** Wudu is obligatory in a state of impurity, **recommended** when already pure. It was once required for every prayer; on the Day of Victory the Prophet ﷺ prayed five prayers with one Wudu and told Umar, *"I did that intentionally."* Ibn Umar continued performing it for every prayer to his death — which Ibn Kathir reads as proof that it is **encouraged, not obligatory**.

**Intention.** Derived from the grammar itself: *"When you stand for the prayer, then wash…"* means *stand for it* — as the Arabs say, *"When you see the leader, then stand."* Reinforced by: *"Actions are judged by their intentions."*

**The limbs:**
- **Face** — from the hairline to the end of the chin, ear to ear. Uthman passed his fingers through his beard three times.
- **Forearms *ilā* the elbows** — *including* the elbows (parallel usage in 4:2). *"On the Day of Resurrection my Ummah will be called 'those with the radiant appendages' because of the traces of Wudu; whoever can increase his radiance, let him do so."*
- **Head** — Abdullah bin Zayd's demonstration (front to back and back again) indicates **the entire head** is wiped.
- **Feet, washed to the ankles.** Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas`ud, Ata', Ikrimah, Mujahid, Az-Zuhri and others: **washing, not wiping the bare foot.** The Prophet ﷺ shouted at men wiping their feet in a rush: **"Perform Wudu thoroughly. Save your heels from the Fire."** A dry spot the size of a fingernail was cause to repeat the Wudu — and in one narration, the prayer.
- **Between the fingers**, and rinsing the nose thoroughly *"unless you are fasting."*

**Wiping over the Khuffs** is established Sunnah at the level of *Mutawatir* — and, crucially, the Prophet ﷺ was seen doing it **after** Al-Ma'idah was revealed (Jarir bin Abdullah), which forecloses the claim that this ayah abrogated it.

**Tayammum.** The occasion is Aishah's lost necklace at Al-Bayda': the caravan halted, dawn broke, there was no water, and the ayah came down. Usayd bin Al-Hudayr said to her family: *"O family of Abu Bakr! This is not the first blessing you have brought the people."* Ibn Kathir reads the closing clause as the interpretive key to the whole verse: **"Allah does not want to place you in difficulty, but He wants to purify you."**

**After Wudu.** *"Whoever performs Wudu properly and then says the Shahadah, the eight gates of Paradise are opened for him to enter by whichever he wishes."* And the washing itself is expiation: every sin the eyes looked at departs with the water, every sin the hands committed, every sin the feet walked to — *"until he ends up sinless."*

## 6. Standing Firm for Allah (5:7–11)

**"Stand out firmly for Allah as just witnesses."** In truth for Allah's sake — not for people, not for reputation. **"And let not the enmity of others make you avoid justice. Be just — that is nearer to Taqwa."**

Ibn Kathir handles the word *nearer* with precision: Allah says justice is *nearer* to Taqwa, **but there is no other course available** — so *nearer* here functions as *is*. (Compare 25:24.)

The illustrative hadith is domestic, not political: An-Nu`man bin Bashir's father asked the Prophet ﷺ to witness a gift he had given only to this one son. *"Have you given the like to every one of your children?"* No. **"Have Taqwa of Allah and treat your children equally. I shall not be a witness to injustice."** The gift was taken back.

**On the promise of Paradise (5:9):** they will not earn it *on account of* their deeds, but **on account of His mercy** — though the deeds qualify them for that mercy, because Allah made them the cause and the path to it. *"All of this is from Allah alone."*

**5:11 — "when some people desired to stretch out their hands against you."** Two accounts, both kept:
- The bedouin **Ghawrath bin Al-Harith**, who seized the Prophet's ﷺ hanging sword and said, *"Who will protect you from me?"* — *"Allah."* Asked three times, answered three times the same. The man lowered the sword, and the Prophet ﷺ **did not punish him.**
- **Banu An-Nadir's** plot to drop a stone on the Prophet ﷺ from a roof while he sat below seeking help with blood money; Allah informed him and he left.

## 7. The Covenants Broken: Israel and the Christians (5:12–14)

**Twelve leaders (*Nuqaba'*)** were appointed among the Children of Israel — one per tribe, pledging on their people's behalf. Ibn Kathir immediately draws the parallel to **the twelve leaders of the Ansar at Al-'Aqabah** and names them all: three from Aws (Usayd bin Al-Hudayr, Sa`d bin Khaythamah, and Rifa'ah bin Abdul-Mundhir *or* Abu Al-Haytham bin At-Tayhan), and nine from Khazraj (Abu Umamah As`ad bin Zurarah, Sa`d bin Ar-Rabi', Abdullah bin Rawahah, Rafi` bin Malik, Al-Bara' bin Ma`rur, Ubadah bin As-Samit, Sa`d bin Ubadah, Abdullah bin `Amr bin Haram, Al-Mundhir bin `Amr).

**The terms of the covenant** — prayer, Zakah, belief in the Messengers, honouring and assisting them, "a good loan to Allah" (spending in His cause) — and the promise: sins remitted, Gardens beneath which rivers flow.

**The consequence of breaking it:** cursed, hearts hardened, words moved from their places, a good part of the message abandoned. Ibn Kathir explains the *tahrif*: their comprehension became corrupt, so they altered the Book from its apparent meanings and attributed to Allah what He did not say.

**And then the turn:** *"But forgive them and overlook their misdeeds. Verily, Allah loves the doers of good."* Ibn Kathir calls this **"the ultimate victory and triumph,"** and quotes the Salaf: *"You would never treat those who disobey Allah concerning you better than by obeying Allah concerning them."* He also records Qatadah's view that the clemency was abrogated by 9:29 — again preserving the dispute.

**The Christians (5:14):** they took the same covenant and abandoned the same portion of it, and the stated consequence is **enmity and hatred among themselves until the Day of Resurrection.** Ibn Kathir points to the mutual excommunications of the sects of his own day — Melkite, Jacobite, Nestorian, Arian — each barring the others from its churches.

## 8. "A Light and a Plain Book" (5:15–19)

The Messenger ﷺ has come **explaining much of what they concealed and passing over much** — passing over what would bring no benefit to expose. Ibn Abbas names one thing concealed: **the ruling of stoning.** *"He who disbelieves in stoning has inadvertently disbelieved in the Qur'an."*

**"Wherewith Allah guides those who seek His pleasure to ways of peace, and brings them out of darkness into light."**

**5:17 — the refutation of divinity for the Messiah.** If Allah willed to destroy the Messiah, his mother, and everyone on earth together — who could prevent Him? The argument is from *contingency*: what can be destroyed is not God.

**5:18 — "We are the children of Allah and His loved ones."** Ibn Kathir's reply is the ayah's own: *"Why then does He punish you for your sins?"* He notes fairly that the People of the Book have in their Book Allah's address to Israel — *"You are my firstborn"* — and that **converts from among them explained it as an expression of honour, common in their idiom**, not literal sonship.

**5:19 — "after a break in the series of Messengers."** The gap between `Isa and Muhammad ﷺ: six hundred years (Abu Uthman An-Nahdi, Qatadah, and Al-Bukhari from Salman), or 560, or 540, or 620. Ibn Kathir reconciles them elegantly: **solar versus lunar reckoning** — about three years' difference per century, which is exactly why 18:25 says *three hundred years, adding nine.*

*"I, among all people, have the most right to the son of Maryam, for there was no Prophet between him and me."*

He then describes the world into which the Prophet ﷺ came: idol-worship, fire-worship, cross-worship, and only scattered remnants of true teaching among some rabbis, priests and monks — *"the bounty of sending Muhammad was the perfect bounty at the time it was needed most."* The long hadith of Iyad bin Himar is quoted, including: **"I created all My servants Hunafa (monotheists), but the devils came and deviated them."**

## 9. Musa and the Refusal to Enter the Holy Land (5:20–26)

**"Remember the favour of Allah: He made Prophets among you, made you kings…"** *Kings*, per Ibn Abbas, in a domestic sense: **a servant, a wife, and a house** — with the hadith attached: *"He who wakes healthy in body, safe in his family, with the provision for that day, it is as if the world and all in it were gathered for him."*

Preferred *"above the nations"* means **above the nations of their own time**; this Ummah, Ibn Kathir argues, is more honoured before Allah (2:143, 3:110).

**The refusal.** Musa orders them to enter and fight; they answer that the people there are mighty, *"we shall never enter until they leave."* **Yuwsha' bin Nun and Kalib bin Yufna** — the two who feared Allah — urge them: *"Assault them through the gate; when you are in, victory is yours. And put your trust in Allah if you are believers indeed."* It changes nothing. The reply comes: **"So go, you and your Lord, and fight — we are sitting right here."**

**Ibn Kathir's structural move is to set this beside Badr.** When the Prophet ﷺ sought his Companions' opinion, Sa`d said: *"By He who sent you with the Truth! If you sought to cross this sea, we would follow you and none would remain behind… march forward with the blessing of Allah."* And Al-Miqdad said explicitly: **"We will never say to you what the Children of Israel said to Musa. Rather, march on and we are with you."** The Messenger ﷺ was gladdened. **The same test, the opposite answer.**

**The sentence: forty years wandering.** During which the Tawrah was revealed, the Law established, the Tabernacle erected, the clouds shaded them, manna and quails descended, water sprang from rock. Musa and Harun died, and everyone over forty. **Then Yuwsha' led the second generation** and besieged Jerusalem, conquering it on a Friday afternoon — the famous account of the sun halted at his request (*"You are commanded and I am commanded"*), the command to enter bowing and saying *Hittah* (which they altered), and the golden cow's head stolen from the spoils, discovered when one leader's hand stuck to Yuwsha's.

**"So do not grieve for the rebellious people."** Ibn Kathir's commentary here turns sharply polemical — his register throughout the surah's Israelite passages is that of medieval religious dispute, and a modern reader should register it as such rather than as description of a living community.

## 10. The Two Sons of Adam (5:27–31)

**"Recite to them the story of the two sons of Adam *in truth*"** — Ibn Kathir stresses *bil-haqq*: **clearly, without ambiguity, alteration, addition or deletion.** The Qur'anic account is the corrective one (cf. 3:62, 18:13, 19:34).

**The cause.** Adam married each son to the twin sister of the other; Qabil's own twin was beautiful and he wanted her for himself. Adam made the marriage contingent on a sacrifice. Habil offered a sheep and it was accepted; Qabil offered produce and it was not. Ibn Kathir notes Ibn Abbas's chain here is "better than good."

**"Verily, Allah accepts only from those who have Taqwa."** Abu Ad-Darda': *"If I knew for certain that Allah had accepted from me even one prayer, it would be better for me than this world and all it contains."*

**The refusal to retaliate.** *"If you stretch your hand against me to kill me, I shall not stretch mine against you — for I fear Allah."* Abdullah bin `Amr: **"By Allah! Habil was the stronger of the two. But fear of Allah restrained his hand."**

This becomes Ibn Kathir's text for civil strife (*fitnah*). The Prophet ﷺ: *"When two Muslims meet each other with their swords, both the killer and the killed are in the Fire"* — because the one killed **intended** to kill. And Sa`d bin Abi Waqqas, at the onset of the trouble around Uthman: *"He who sits during it is better than he who stands, and he who stands is better than he who walks…"* Asked what to do if someone enters your home to kill you: **"Be just like the pious son of Adam."**

**The crow.** Shaytan showed Qabil how to kill (by smashing an animal's head with a stone); a crow showed him how to bury. *"Woe to me! Am I not even able to be as this crow?"* Al-Hasan Al-Basri on his regret: **"Allah made him feel sorrow after the loss he had earned"** — remorse that came too late to be repentance.

*"Any soul unjustly killed — the first son of Adam carries a share of the burden of its shedding, for he was the first to establish murder."*

## 11. One Life and All Mankind; Hirabah; Theft (5:32–40)

**5:32 — "Because of that, We ordained for the Children of Israel…"** The *that* is Qabil's murder. **Whoever kills one soul without justification, it is as if he killed all mankind; whoever saves one life, as if he saved all mankind** — Ibn Kathir's explanation: *there is no difference between one life and another.*

Mujahid and Ibn Abbas give the reciprocal reading: **he who forbids himself the shedding of one life has effectively made all lives safe from him.** And the living example — Abu Hurayrah came to defend Uthman under siege; Uthman asked: *"Would it please you to kill all the people, including me?"* No. *"If you kill one man, it is as if you killed all people. Go back."* **He went back and did not fight.**

**5:33 — the punishment for *hirabah*** (waging war on Allah and His Messenger, spreading terror on the highways): killing, crucifixion, cutting hand and foot from opposite sides, or exile. Ibn Kathir holds the ayah is **general** in meaning, covering idolators and all who commit these crimes. Because *aw* ("or") signals choice — as it does in 5:95, 2:196, 5:89 — Ibn Abbas, Sa`id bin Al-Musayyib, Mujahid, Ata', Al-Hasan and others hold **the ruler chooses** among the penalties. On *exile*: pursued until captured, or driven from the land of Islam; Ad-Dahhak and Muqatil said exile within Islamic land; others said imprisonment.

**5:34 — "Except those who repent before you overpower them."** Ibn Kathir gives three case histories: Harithah bin Badr, granted safe conduct by Ali; the man from Murad who came to Abu Musa; and Ali Al-Asadi, a highway robber who heard someone reciting **39:53** — *"O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, despair not of the mercy of Allah"* — asked for it to be recited again, laid down his sword, went to Madinah in repentance, and was absolved by Abu Hurayrah's testimony before Marwan.

**5:35 — Taqwa, Wasilah, Jihad.** *Wasilah* is **the means of approach** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others): *"Seek the means of approach to Him by obeying Him and doing what pleases Him."* It is **also** the name of the highest station in Paradise — the Prophet's ﷺ own, nearest to the Throne — which is why the du'a after the Adhan asks for it, and why *"whoever asks Allah for the Wasilah for me will earn my intercession."*

**5:38 — theft.** The threshold is **a quarter of a Dinar** (Aishah's hadith), which Ibn Kathir harmonises with the "three Dirhams" reports since a Dinar then equalled twelve Dirhams. Abu Hanifah and his companions, with Ath-Thawri, held ten Dirhams; Ibn Kathir prefers the first. The small threshold is deliberate — *"so that people refrain from theft; a wise decision to those with sound comprehension."*

**5:39 — and the door left open.** The Makhzumi woman: Quraysh sent Usamah bin Zayd to intercede, and the Prophet's ﷺ face changed colour. **"Do you intercede in a punishment prescribed by Allah?"** Then, from the pulpit: *"Those before you were destroyed because when a noble person stole they let him go, and when a weak one stole they punished him. By Him in Whose Hand is my soul, if Fatimah bint Muhammad stole, I would cut off her hand."* The sentence was carried out. And Aishah's coda: **"Her repentance was sincere afterwards; she married, and used to come to me so I could convey her needs to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ."** To another woman who asked whether repentance remained open: *"Yes — this day you are free of your sin as the day your mother bore you."*

## 12. Judging by What Allah Has Revealed (5:41–50)

This is the surah's legal and theological pivot, and Ibn Kathir gives **two occasions of revelation and accepts both**:

1. **The two adulterers.** The Jews had substituted flogging and public parading for the Tawrah's stoning. They resolved: *"Take his ruling if he says flog; reject it if he says stone."* The Prophet ﷺ called for the Tawrah; the reader covered the verse with his hand; Abdullah bin Salam told him to lift it, and the stoning verse was underneath. In Abu Dawud's version the Prophet ﷺ placed the Tawrah on a cushion and said, **"I believe in you and in Him who revealed you."** The sentence was carried out; Ibn Umar: *"I saw the man shielding the woman from the stones with his body."*
2. **The blood money dispute** between Banu An-Nadir and Banu Qurayzah — full Diyah for one tribe's dead, half for the other's, a Jahiliyyah settlement imposed by the stronger. They sent hypocrites to sound out what the Prophet's ﷺ ruling would be before agreeing to arbitration. **He equalised the Diyah.**

Ibn Kathir notes the second fits the sequence better, since 5:45 (*life for life*) follows immediately.

**Why he judged by the Tawrah:** not to honour their book over his own Law, but because Allah commanded it, and **to make them admit publicly to what they had concealed.**

**Suht** (5:42) — Ibn Mas`ud and others: **bribes.** *"If one is like this, how can Allah cleanse his heart or answer his supplication?"*

**"Either judge between them or turn away"** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others hold this was **abrogated by 5:49** (*"And so judge between them by what Allah has revealed"*). Ibn Abbas recorded elsewhere as one of two abrogated ayat in this surah, the other being the garlands verse (5:2).

### The three refrains, and the great question

The same clause closes three consecutive passages with three different predicates:

| Ayah | *Whosoever does not judge by what Allah revealed…* | Said of |
|---|---|---|
| **5:44** | …such are **the disbelievers** (*Kafirun*) | The Tawrah and the Jews |
| **5:45** | …such are **the unjust** (*Zalimun*) | Retaliation, *life for life* |
| **5:47** | …such are **the rebellious** (*Fasiqun*) | The Injil and the Christians |

**Ibn Kathir's resolution is one of the most consequential passages in all his tafsir**, and he reports it as a spectrum of Salaf opinion rather than a single verdict:

- **Al-Bara', Hudhayfah, Ibn Abbas, Abu Mijlaz, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan Al-Basri:** revealed about the People of the Book. **Al-Hasan adds: "and it applies to us as well."** Ibrahim: *"revealed about the Children of Israel, and Allah accepted it for this Ummah."*
- **Ibn Abbas (via Ali bin Abi Talhah):** *"Whoever **rejects** what Allah revealed has committed Kufr; whoever **accepts** it but does not rule by it is a Zalim and a Fasiq and a sinner."*
- **Ibn Abbas again (via Tawus):** *"It is an act of Kufr"* — and Ibn Tawus qualifies: **"not like disbelief in Allah, His angels, His Books and His Messengers."**
- **Ata':** *"There is Kufr and Kufr less than Kufr; Zulm and Zulm less than Zulm; Fisq and Fisq less than Fisq."*
- **Tawus:** *"This is not the Kufr that expels one from the religion."*

**5:45 — retaliation.** Life for life, eye for eye, nose, ear, tooth, and wounds equal for equal. **The man is killed for a woman he kills** — consensus, and in the letter to `Amr bin Hazm: *"the man is killed for the woman."* Free Muslim men and women are equal in this. **Retaliation for wounds must wait until the wound heals** — the man who insisted early and was left with a limp was told: *"I asked you to wait and you disobeyed; your limp has no compensation."*

**And the pardon.** *"But if anyone remits it by way of charity, it shall be an expiation for him."* Ibn Abbas: **expiation for the aggressor and reward for the victim.** The Prophet ﷺ: *"Any man who suffers a wound and forfeits his right as charity — Allah will pardon him the like of what he forfeited."* (The Rabi' incident is Ibn Kathir's illustration: Anas bin An-Nadr swore his sister's tooth would not be broken; the family forgave; and the Prophet ﷺ said, **"There are servants of Allah who, if they swear by Allah, Allah fulfils their oath."**)

**5:48 — Muhaymin.** The Qur'an is *Muhayminan* over the previous Scriptures. Ibn Abbas gives three glosses that Ibn Kathir says are all contained in the word: **trustworthy / entrusted over it**, **witness**, and **dominant**. Ibn Jarir draws the practical rule: **whatever in the earlier Books agrees with the Qur'an is true; whatever disagrees is false.** And what no earlier Book had: *"Verily We have sent down the Dhikr and We will surely guard it"* (15:9).

**"To each among you We prescribed a law and a clear way."** Every Prophet had his own law, partially or wholly abrogated by the next, and all abrogated by the Law of Muhammad ﷺ. If Allah had willed, one nation and one unchanging law — but the difference is **a test**: *"so compete in good deeds."*

**5:50 — "Do they seek the judgment of Jahiliyyah?"** Ibn Kathir's contemporary example is the **Mongols and Al-Yasiq** — Genghis Khan's compiled code, drawing selectively on Judaism, Christianity, Islam and his own opinion, which his descendants preferred over the Book and the Sunnah. His verdict on that is severe. And on the alternative: *"Who is more just in decision than Allah, for those who know that He is more merciful to His creation than a mother to her own child?"*

---

## 13. Allegiance (5:51–58)

**"Do not take the Jews and Christians as *Awliya'*, as they are Awliya' of one another."** The word carries protective alliance and inner loyalty, not ordinary human dealing. Ibn Kathir's illustrating incident is administrative: Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari employed a Christian scribe whose work impressed Umar — until Umar learned he was Christian, poked Abu Musa's thigh, ordered the man dismissed from Madinah, and recited this ayah. Abdullah bin `Utbah's warning follows: *"Let one of you beware that he may be a Jew or a Christian while unaware."*

**5:52 — the hedging of the hypocrites.** *"We fear lest some misfortune befall us"* — insurance taken out against a Muslim defeat. Allah's answer: perhaps a victory will come (As-Suddi: the conquest of Makkah) and they will be left regretting what they concealed.

**5:54 — the great replacement verse.** *"Whoever turns back from his religion, Allah will bring a people whom He loves and who love Him."* Their four marks:

- **humble toward the believers** (cf. 48:29 — the Prophet ﷺ described as *the smiling fighter*, smiling to his allies, fighting his enemies)
- **stern toward the disbelievers**
- **striving in Allah's way**
- **never fearing the blame of any blamer**

Abu Dharr's seven commands sit here, including *"to say the truth even if it is bitter,"* *"to look at those below me and not those above me,"* and *"not to fear the blame of anyone for Allah's sake."* Balanced immediately by: **"The believer is not to humiliate himself"** — i.e., by taking on trials he cannot bear.

**5:55–56** — the true *Wali* is **Allah, His Messenger, and the believers who establish the prayer and give Zakah**. On *"and they bow down"*: Ibn Kathir firmly rejects the reading that they give Zakah *while bowing in prayer* — *"no scholar from whom rulings are taken says this, as far as we know"* — and reads it as attending congregational prayer and spending on the needs of the Muslims.

**5:57–58 — mockery of the Adhan.** Ibn Kathir places the Adhan among the highest of acts and pairs the mockers with Shaytan, who flees at the call, returns after it, flees again at the Iqamah, and returns to whisper *"remember this and that"* until a man forgets how much he has prayed.

---

## 14. The Reply to the People of the Book (5:59–66)

**"Do you criticise us for anything except that we believe in Allah, and what was sent down to us and before us?"** — *this is no cause for blame at all.* (Compare 85:8 — *they had no fault except that they believed in Allah.*)

**5:64 — "Allah's Hand is tied up."** Ibn Abbas is careful: **they did not mean it literally.** They meant *miser* — that He does not spend what He has. Ibn Kathir cross-references 17:29, where the same idiom (*let not your hand be tied to your neck*) plainly means stinginess.

The answer: **"Nay, both His Hands are widely outstretched. He spends as He wills."** And the hadith: *"Allah's Right Hand is full, and no amount of spending diminishes it, though He spends night and day. Consider how much He has spent since He created the heavens and the earth — it has not diminished what is in His Right Hand."* And: **"Spend, and I will spend on you."**

**"Every time they kindled the fire of war, Allah extinguished it."**

**5:65–66 — the counterfactual, and it is generous.** *Had* they believed and had Taqwa, their sins would have been expiated and they would have entered Gardens; *had* they acted upon the Tawrah, the Injil, and what was sent down to them now, **"they would surely have eaten from above them and from beneath their feet"** — provision descending from the sky and growing from the earth (cf. 7:96).

**And the qualification Ibn Kathir insists on:** *"Among them is a **Muqtasid** Ummah"* — a moderate, balanced community. He grades it: *Iqtisad* is the middle course, and above it is the rank of the *Sabiqun*, the foremost (35:32–33). The condemnation in this surah is **never total**, and Ibn Kathir marks the exception every time the text does.

---

## 15. "O Messenger! Convey" (5:67)

**"And if you do not, then you have not conveyed His Message. Allah will protect you from mankind."**

Aishah's testimony is the anchor: **"Whoever tells you that Muhammad concealed any part of what was revealed to him is lying"** — and she cites this very ayah. Elsewhere: *"If he had concealed anything, he would have concealed 33:37"* — the verse that reproved him.

Ibn Abbas: *"If you conceal even one ayah revealed to you from your Lord, you have not conveyed His Message."*

Az-Zuhri, in one line: **"From Allah comes the Message; the Messenger's part is its delivery; ours is submission to it."**

**The protection clause is datable.** Before it, the Prophet ﷺ was guarded — the night Sa`d bin Abi Waqqas came in armour and he slept until Aishah heard him breathing in sleep. After it, he **put his head out of the room and said: "O people, go away — Allah will protect me."**

And the receipt was taken publicly: at the Farewell Hajj, before more than forty thousand, *"You will be asked about me — what will you say?"* **"We bear witness that you conveyed, fulfilled the trust, and offered sincere advice."** He raised his finger to the sky and pointed at them: *"O Allah, did I convey? O Allah, did I convey?"*

---

## 16. `Isa: Servant, Not God (5:72–77)

Ibn Kathir's argument is built entirely from `Isa's own reported speech.

- **His first words** were *"Verily, I am the servant of Allah"* (19:30). Not *I am Allah*, not *I am the son of Allah*.
- **His preaching as a man:** *"Worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord"* (19:36; 5:72).
- **"His mother was a Siddiqah"** — she believed with complete trust. Ibn Kathir notes this is **the highest rank she was given, which itself proves she was not a Prophet.**
- **"They both used to eat food"** — needing nourishment, and everything that follows from it. The argument is bodily and unanswerable: **what requires food is not God.**

**"Allah is the third of three"** — Mujahid holds it addresses the Christians generally; As-Suddi, that it addresses taking `Isa and Maryam as two gods beside Allah, linking it to 5:116.

**And then, immediately after the severest charge, the open door:** *"Will they not repent to Allah and ask His forgiveness? For Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful."* Ibn Kathir pauses on this deliberately — **that Allah invites to repentance even those who have said this** is, he writes, a demonstration of His generosity and mercy.

**5:77 — "Do not exceed the limits in your religion beyond the truth."** *Ghuluww* — excess — is defined precisely: **exaggerating in praising the one you were commanded to honour**, raising a Prophet to the rank of a god. The cause is following teachers who went astray first.

---

## 17. "Nearest in Love" (5:82–86)

**Occasion:** the delegation sent by **An-Najashi** of Ethiopia to observe the Prophet ﷺ; he recited the Qur'an to them, they wept, believed, and returned to their king. (Ata' bin Abi Rabah: they were Ethiopians who accepted Islam when the Muslim emigrants lived among them.)

*"You will find the nearest in love to the believers those who say: We are Christians. That is because among them are priests and monks, and they are not proud."*

Ibn Kathir explains the difference in terms of **disposition formed by teaching**: mercy, compassion and monasticism were placed in the hearts of those who followed `Isa (57:27); their book teaches turning the other cheek; fighting was not part of their creed. He describes them with **knowledge, worship, humility, and following the truth**.

*"You see their eyes overflowing with tears because of the truth they recognised"* — recognition, Ibn Kathir says, of **the tidings they already had of the Prophet's ﷺ coming.**

This passage matters structurally: it is Ibn Kathir's own proof that the surah's harshest verses are **directed at conduct and covenant-breaking, not at communities as such** — and he links it to 3:199 and 28:52–53.

---

## 18. No Monasticism, and the Law of the Middle (5:87–89)

**"Do not make unlawful the good things Allah has made lawful, and do not transgress."**

**The occasion is Companions going too far.** Some resolved to castrate themselves, renounce worldly desire and wander the earth *"as the monks do."* The Prophet ﷺ summoned them: **"I fast and I break my fast; I pray and I sleep; and I marry women. Whoever turns away from my Sunnah is not of me."** Others swore off meat, or marriage, or sleeping on a bed, and received the same answer.

Ibn Kathir draws the rule out in both directions: *transgress not* forbids **excess in the permissible** as much as prohibition of it (7:31; 25:67). **"So Allah legislated a middle way between those who are extreme and those who fall short — it allows neither excessive application nor deficiency."**

**5:89 — expiation for broken oaths.** *Laghw* (unintentional oaths — *"No by Allah," "Yes by Allah"* said without intent) carries nothing. For deliberate oaths, **any one of three, by the swearer's choice**:

- **feed ten poor persons** at the average of what you feed your own family (Ata' Al-Khurasani: from the best of it)
- **or clothe them** — a garment sufficient to pray in, per person
- **or free a believing slave** — with the hadith of the slave girl asked *"Where is Allah?"* and *"Who am I?"*, and the verdict: **"Free her, for she is a believer."**

And if unable: **fast three days** (read by Ubayy and Ibn Mas`ud as *three consecutive days*). Ibn Kathir notes the ordering is from **easiest to hardest**. *"And protect your oaths"* — do not leave a broken oath unexpiated.

---

## 19. Khamr and Maysir (5:90–93)

**"Khamr, Maysir, Ansab and Azlam are a *Rijs* of Shaytan's handiwork. So avoid it, that you may be successful."**

Ibn Kathir lays out **the three-stage prohibition** through Abu Hurayrah and Umar:

1. **2:219** — *"In them is great sin, and some benefit for men."* People concluded it was not forbidden and kept drinking.
2. **4:43** — *"Do not approach the prayer while intoxicated."* People then drank only outside prayer times. (An emigrant had led Maghrib and jumbled the recitation.)
3. **5:90–91** — the final, unconditional prohibition.

**Umar's du'a runs right through the sequence:** each time an ayah was recited to him he said, *"O Allah! Make the verdict on Khamr clear to us."* When he reached **"So will you not then abstain?"** he said: **"We abstained, we abstained."**

Ibn Kathir gathers the surrounding material:

- **Definition (Umar, from the Prophet's ﷺ pulpit):** Khamr was made from grapes, dates, honey, wheat and barley — **"and Khamr is whatever intoxicates the mind."** Ibn Umar: *"Every intoxicant is Khamr, and every intoxicant is unlawful."*
- **The ten cursed** in connection with it: the drink, the drinker, the server, the seller, the buyer, the brewer, the one who has it brewed, the carrier, the one it is carried to, and whoever consumes its price.
- **Maysir** is gambling of every kind; Ali called chess a form of it; Ata', Mujahid and Tawus included children's games of chance. **Ansab** are the sacrificial altar-stones; **Azlam** the divining arrows.
- **The obedience was instant.** Anas was serving drink at Abu Talhah's house when the herald passed; he was ordered to spill it, and *"it flowed in the streets of Al-Madinah."* They broke the vessels, performed ghusl, put on perfume and went to the mosque.
- **Uthman's warning:** *"Avoid Khamr, for it is the mother of all sins"* — with the story of the worshipper coerced into choosing among three sins, who chose the drink, and then committed the other two. **"It is never combined with faith but one of them expels the other."**
- **Sa`d's account** of drinking before the prohibition, the boasting between Ansar and Muhajirun, and the blow with a bone that scarred his nose for life — the injury that made the point about *"enmity and hatred between you."*

**5:93 — the question that had to be asked.** *"O Messenger of Allah, what of our brothers who died while still drinking it?"* The answer: *"Those who believe and do righteous deeds — there is no sin on them for what they ate…"* Ibn Kathir pairs it with the identical question after the Qiblah change, answered by **"And Allah would never let your faith be lost"** (2:143). **A law does not reach backwards to condemn those who died before it.**

---

## 20. Ihram, Game, and the Ka'bah (5:94–99)

**5:94 — the test.** Game came within reach of hands and lances — Muqatil bin Hayyan places it at **Al-Hudaybiyyah**, where wild game and birds crowded the camp as the Muslims had never seen. Ibn Abbas: weak and young game, catchable by hand. **The point of the test is stated by the ayah itself: "that Allah may know who fears Him in the unseen."** Obedience in public is not proof; obedience when it would be easy and unobserved is.

**5:95 — the penalty.** An equivalent animal, judged by **two just Muslim men**, brought to the Sacred Area and distributed to its poor (consensus). Companion precedents: camel for ostrich, cow for wild cattle, goat for deer. Umar's practice was to make the offender find two judges — once judging alongside him personally. If no equivalent exists: **feeding the poor, or fasting an equivalent number of days**, scaled by Ibn Abbas (antelope → sheep, or six poor, or three days; deer → cow, or twenty poor, or twenty days; ostrich → camel, or thirty poor, or thirty days).

Mujahid held "intentionally" means *while forgetting one's Ihram*; Ibn Kathir calls this odd and gives the majority view: **expiation is due either way.** Az-Zuhri's formulation is the neat one: *"The Book requires expiation for the intentional; the Sunnah added the forgetful."*

**The five (or seven) *Fawasiq* that may be killed in Ihram:** crow, kite, scorpion, mouse, rabid dog — and by extension, since they are more dangerous, the wolf, lion, leopard and tiger; Nafi` on the snake: *"there is no doubt that killing it is allowed."*

**5:96 — water game is lawful throughout.** *"Its water is pure and its dead are lawful."* Illustrated with Jabir's account of the expedition under Abu Ubaydah, reduced to one date a man per day, who then found a fish on the shore like a small hill and ate from it for eighteen days — and a mounted camel passed beneath its raised ribs.

**Game hunted by a non-Muhrim:** lawful for the Muhrim to eat **unless it was hunted for him specifically.** Two hadiths in tension, resolved by intent: As-Sa`b's zebra was refused (*"we only returned it because we are in Ihram"*), while Abu Qatadah's was eaten after the Prophet ﷺ asked, *"Did any of you point at it or help kill it?"* — **No.** *"Then eat"* — and he ate.

> **A note Ibn Kathir's editors preserve:** the manuscript tradition breaks off here. Ibn Kathir commented on part of 5:96 and left 5:97–99 unexplained in *every extant copy*; the printed text supplies a summary from **At-Tabari**. Ibn Kathir's own honesty about gaps is worth noting — the substituted material covers the Ka'bah as an *asylum of security*, the sacred months and offerings as landmarks that held pre-Islamic Arabia together and became the symbols of Hajj, and the closing warning: **"The Messenger's duty is but to convey."**

---

## 21. Do Not Ask (5:100–104)

**"Not equal are the bad and the good, even though the abundance of the bad may please you."** Ibn Kathir: **the little that is permitted is better than the abundant that is evil.**

**5:101 — "Ask not about things which, if made plain to you, may cause you trouble."**

This is adab, not obscurantism, and the incidents are startling:

- The Prophet ﷺ gave a speech unlike any before it: *"If you knew what I know, you would laugh little and weep much."* The Companions covered their faces and the sound of weeping came from their chests. A man asked, **"Who is my father?"** — and was told. Umar rose and said, *"We accept Allah as our Lord, Islam as our religion, Muhammad as our Messenger — I seek refuge with Allah from the evil of trials."* The Prophet ﷺ said: *"I have never witnessed goodness and evil like I have today; Paradise and the Fire were shown to me beyond that wall."*
- On Hajj (3:97), they asked three times, *"Is it every year?"* He would not answer, then said: **"No. And had I said yes, it would have become obligatory — and had it become obligatory, you could not have borne it."**

The governing hadith: **"Allah has ordained obligations, so do not neglect them; set limits, so do not transgress them; forbidden things, so do not commit them; and He left some things without ruling — out of mercy, not forgetfulness — so do not ask about them."** And: *"The worst offender among the Muslims is he who asks whether a thing is unlawful, and it becomes unlawful because of his asking."*

**5:102 — a people before you asked such questions and then disbelieved** — because they asked not to be guided but to defy.

**5:103 — Bahirah, Sa'ibah, Wasilah, Ham.** Four categories of livestock consecrated to idols by pre-Islamic custom, with ears slit or bodies exempted from work, milk, wool or riding — and characteristically, **the meat reserved for men and withheld from women.** The definitions vary between Sa`id bin Al-Musayyib, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ibn Ishaq and Malik, and Ibn Kathir reports the variants without forcing them into one.

*"Allah has not instituted these"* — **the idolators invented them and made them acts of worship, and they draw no one nearer to Allah; they only harm.** Ibn Kathir names the man who began it: **`Amr bin Luhayy** of Khuza'ah, first to bring idol-worship to the Hijaz and to alter the religion of Ibrahim.

**5:104 — the only argument they have left:** *"Enough for us is what we found our fathers following."* Allah's reply is a single clause: **even though their fathers knew nothing and were not guided.**

---

## 22. "Take Care of Yourselves" (5:105)

*"O you who believe! Take care of yourselves. If you follow the right guidance, no hurt can come to you from those who are in error."*

Ibn Kathir immediately blocks the quietist misreading — with **Abu Bakr's own sermon**:

> *"O people! You recite this ayah and **you explain it the wrong way.** I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: 'If the people witness evil and do not change it, then Allah is about to send His punishment to encompass them all.'"*

The verse is a command to **reform yourself first**, not a licence to abandon *amr bil-ma'ruf*. Ibn Kathir has already stacked the evidence for this earlier in the surah, at **5:63** — the ayah Ibn Abbas called **"the most severe admonition in the Qur'an"**: *Why do the Rabbaniyyun and the Ahbar not forbid them?* — and at **5:78–79**, where those cursed by the tongues of Dawud and `Isa were cursed for exactly this: *"They did not forbid one another from the evil they committed."*

The supporting hadiths:

- *"By He in Whose Hand is my soul! You will enjoin good and forbid evil, or Allah will send a punishment upon you, and then you will supplicate and He will not answer you."*
- *"Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue; if he cannot, then with his heart — and that is the weakest faith."*
- *"The best Jihad is a word of truth before an unjust ruler."*
- Ali's sermon: *"Enjoin righteousness and forbid evil before what struck them strikes you. And know that enjoining good and forbidding evil neither reduces provision nor shortens a term of life."*
- Abu Sa`id, weeping: *"By Allah, we have seen wrongs, but we feared the people."*

---

## 23. Witnesses to a Will (5:106–108)

A ruling Ibn Kathir calls **glorious** — and it is unusual: **two just witnesses "of your own folk," or two others "from outside"** — non-Muslims, i.e. the People of the Book (Ibn Abbas).

**Two conditions** must coincide: **travel**, and **death approaching**, with no Muslims available. Shurayh the judge: *"The witness of Jews and Christians is not accepted except while travelling, and even then only for the dictation of a will."*

**The procedure:** detain the two after the prayer (Ibn Abbas and others: **the `Asr prayer**; Az-Zuhri: after any congregational prayer). If there is doubt, they swear by Allah: *"We wish no worldly gain by this, even if he were our near relative; and we will not hide the testimony of Allah."* Ibn Kathir notes the phrase **"the testimony of Allah"** is an honouring of testimony itself.

If their sin is then discovered, **two of the rightful heirs swear instead**, that their testimony is truer.

**Why it works** (5:108): being made to swear by Allah — a Name they revere — *"might compel them to give the testimony in its true form"*, and they also fear the exposure that follows if the heirs' counter-oath is admitted after theirs.

---

## 24. The Day the Messengers Are Questioned (5:109–118)

**"What was the response you received?"** — and the Messengers answer: **"We have no knowledge; only You are the Knower of all that is hidden."**

Mujahid, Al-Hasan and As-Suddi: this is **from the terror of that Day**. Ibn Abbas gives the other reading, and Ibn Kathir prefers to hold both: it is **adab before the Lord** — *"we have no knowledge compared to Your knowledge; ours grasped only their outward behaviour, not the secrets of their hearts."*

**5:110 — the favours recounted to `Isa**, every one of them followed by *bi-idhni* (by My permission), which is the theological point of the passage:

| The favour | Ibn Kathir's note |
|---|---|
| Created without a father | A sign and clear proof of Allah's power |
| Speaking in the cradle | He testified to his mother's chastity, absolving her of what the liars accused her of |
| Supported by *Ruh al-Qudus* | Jibril |
| Taught the Book, Hikmah, Tawrah, Injil | *"The Book"* — the power of writing and understanding |
| The bird of clay | Shaped, breathed into, **and it flew — by Allah's permission** |
| Healing the blind and the lepers | By Allah's permission |
| Raising the dead | *"You called them and they rose from their graves by Allah's leave, power, intent and will"* |
| Restrained the Children of Israel from him | They called it *evident magic*, tried to kill him by crucifixion, **and Allah raised him and purified him from them** |

The verbs are **past tense for a future event** — indicating, Ibn Kathir says, a matter already settled that will certainly occur.

### 5:112–115 — The Table (Al-Ma'idah)

**The surah's name comes from here.**

The disciples ask: *"Can your Lord send down to us a table from heaven?"* Some scholars say they were poor and deprived, and wanted daily provision so as to be freer to worship.

**`Isa's reaction is caution, not eagerness:** *"Have Taqwa of Allah, if you are indeed believers"* — do not ask for this; **it may become a trial for you**; trust Allah for your provision.

They press, and their four reasons are given: to eat, to have hearts made firmer, to know he told the truth, and to be witnesses to it. `Isa then makes the supplication — asking that the day be **a festival for the first and the last of us**, a sign, and provision — *"for You are the best of sustainers."* As-Suddi: a day held sacred; Sufyan Ath-Thawri: a day of prayer.

**And the answer carries a condition unlike any other in the Qur'an:** *"I am going to send it down to you — but whoever disbelieves after that, I will punish him with a torment such as I have not inflicted on anyone among the worlds."* Abdullah bin `Amr names the three who will receive the severest torment: **the hypocrites, the deniers among the people of the Table, and the people of Fir'awn.**

What was on it: seven fish and seven loaves (Ibn Abbas) — *"the last group ate as the first group did."* And Ishaq bin Abdullah's ending: they ate as much as they wished, **until some stole from it, saying "it may not come tomorrow" — and the table ascended.** The gift withdrawn by the failure of trust that asked for it.

### 5:116 — "Did you say: Worship me and my mother as two gods?"

Ibn Kathir calls this **a public chastisement**, staged on the Day of Resurrection in the presence of those who worshipped them. Abu Hurayrah's narration says `Isa **will be taught his answer** — and the answer is a masterpiece of disavowal:

> *"Glory be to You! It was not for me to say what I had no right to say. **Had I said it, You would have known it.** You know what is in my inner self, though I do not know what is in Yours."*

> *"I said to them only what You commanded me: Worship Allah, my Lord and your Lord. **And I was a witness over them while I dwelled among them; but when You took me up, You were the Watcher over them.**"*

> *"If You punish them, they are Your servants; and if You forgive them, verily You are the Almighty, the All-Wise."*

**And then the hadith that makes the whole passage land.** The Prophet ﷺ described the gathering — barefoot, naked, uncircumcised; Ibrahim the first to be clothed — and said that men of his own Ummah would be taken to the left, and he would cry, *"They are my followers!"* And it will be said: *"You do not know what they innovated after you."* **And he said he would answer exactly as the righteous servant `Isa answered** — reciting these same words.

Ibn Kathir's observation on the closing clause: **"If You punish them… and if You forgive them"** — not *You are Forgiving, Merciful* as one might expect, but **the Almighty, the All-Wise.** All matters return to Allah; He does as He wills, and none may question Him, while He will question them.

---

## 25. The Close (5:119–120)

**"This is a Day on which the truthful will profit from their truth."** Ibn Abbas: **the Day when Tawhid will benefit those who held it.**

Gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding forever — **"Allah is pleased with them, and they with Him."** Ibn Kathir stops on the mutuality: of everything named, *"the greatest bliss is the good pleasure of Allah"* (9:72). *That is the great success* — *"other than which there is no greater success."* For this, *let the workers work* (37:61); *for this let those who strive, strive* (83:26).

And the final verse returns everything to its owner: **"To Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and all that is therein, and He is able to do all things"** — He created all, owns all, governs all. *There is none like Him, no rival, no ancestor, no son, no wife, no lord or god besides Him.*

Ibn Kathir ends the surah where he began it, with Abdullah bin `Amr: **"The last revealed Surah was Surah Al-Ma'idah."**

## 26. In One Breath

Al-Ma'idah is **the surah of the fulfilled covenant.** It opens with *fulfil your obligations* and closes with the Messengers being asked what answer they received — and in between it shows, again and again, what happens to a people entrusted with a covenant.

It legislates heavily because it came last: food, slaughter, hunting, marriage, purification, theft, retaliation, oaths, wine, pilgrimage, bequests. It narrates heavily because law without memory does not hold: a generation that would not enter a land it was promised; a brother who would not raise his hand and a brother who did; a table sent down and withdrawn. And it argues heavily, because the community it addressed lived among older communities holding older scriptures — communities it charges with concealing, altering, and abandoning what they had been given, while insisting in the same breath that **among them is a balanced Ummah**, that some of them weep when they hear the truth, and that the door of repentance stands open even to those who said the gravest thing that can be said about God.

Three commands hold it together. **Be just — even toward those you hate, because justice is nearer to Taqwa and there is no other road.** **Judge by what Allah revealed — and know that Kufr, Zulm and Fisq each admit of a lesser degree.** **Convey — and do not conceal a single ayah.**

Its two most-quoted verses face each other across the whole book: *"This day I have perfected your religion for you"* — and *"whoever saves one life, it is as if he saved all mankind."* The first says the Law is complete. The second says what the Law is for.
