*Revealed in Makkah · 52 ayat · "The Pen" — named for the oath in its first verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Qalam answers a single insult. Makkah had called the Prophet ﷺ **majnun** — mad — and this surah is Allah's reply, delivered not as argument but as counter-testimony.

The structure follows from that. It opens by swearing an oath — *by the pen and what they write* — and immediately denies the charge (68:2). Then it does something more than deny: it **reverses** it. If the question is who is deranged, the surah answers by drawing two portraits side by side. First the Prophet ﷺ: *you are on an exalted character.* Then his accuser: a ten-item catalogue of moral deformity — swearer, slanderer, tale-carrier, hoarder, transgressor, cruel, of doubtful lineage — a man whose only credentials are wealth and sons.

Between the two portraits sits the surah's centrepiece, **the People of the Garden**: men who owned an orchard, resolved overnight to harvest it before the poor could arrive, and woke to find it burnt black. It is a parable aimed squarely at the Quraysh, who had been given a Messenger the way those men were given a garden.

The surah closes where it began — *they say: verily, he is a madman* — but by then the reader knows exactly who is afflicted.

---

## 1. The Pen (68:1)

**Nun** is one of the disjointed letters, like *Sad* and *Qaf*; Ibn Kathir refers back to his long discussion at the start of Al-Baqarah rather than repeat it.

On *the pen*, he takes the apparent meaning first: **the actual pen used for writing**, as in *Read! And your Lord is the Most Generous, Who has taught by the pen; He has taught man that which he knew not* (96:3–5). The oath is therefore Allah alerting His creatures to a favour they take for granted — **the skill of writing, through which knowledge is attained**.

*And by what they Yastur* — "what they write" (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah). As-Suddi: **the angels, and the deeds of the servants that they record.**

Others read it as *the* Pen — the one that wrote the decree. Al-Walid bin Ubadah bin As-Samit narrated that his father called for him while dying and told him he had heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say:

> *Verily, the first of what Allah created was the Pen, and He said to it: "Write."*
> *The Pen said: "O my Lord, what shall I write?"*
> *He said: "Write the decree, and whatever will be throughout eternity."*

This took place **fifty thousand years before the heavens and the earth**. Recorded by Ahmad through several routes; At-Tirmidhi graded it *Hasan Sahih Gharib*.

## 2. You Are Not Insane — and Your Reward Is Uncut (68:2–3)

*You, by the grace of your Lord, are not insane.* Ibn Kathir: **and all praise is due to Allah** — you are not crazy as the ignorant among your people claim. They deny the guidance and the clear truth you brought, and so they attribute madness to you *because of it*.

*A reward that is not Mamnun* — **not cut off**, never severed, never perishing. Compare *a gift without an end* (11:108) and *a reward without end* (95:6). Mujahid: *without reckoning*.

And the reason it is uncut is stated plainly: **because you conveyed the Message of your Lord to creation, and you were patient with their abuse.**

---

## 3. An Exalted Character (68:4)

This is the verse Ibn Kathir treats most lovingly.

Al-Awfi from **Ibn Abbas**: *you are on a great religion, and it is Islam* — likewise Mujahid, Abu Malik, As-Suddi, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas, Ad-Dahhak, Ibn Zayd.

Then Qatadah's report, preserved in Sahih Muslim at full length. Sa'd bin Hisham asked **A'ishah** about the character of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. She replied:

> *"Have you not read the Qur'an?"*
> He said: *"Of course."*
> She said: **"Verily, the character of the Messenger of Allah was the Qur'an."**

Ibn Kathir unpacks it: he acted by the Qur'an's commands and prohibitions, **and he abandoned his natural disposition** — the carnal nature. Whatever the Qur'an commanded, he did; whatever it forbade, he avoided. And to this Allah added **modesty, kindness, bravery, pardoning, gentleness** and every other good quality.

The evidence he stacks up:

- **Anas** (Two Sahihs): *"I served the Messenger of Allah for ten years, and he never said a word of displeasure to me, nor did he ever say concerning something I had done: 'Why did you do that?' And he never said concerning something I had not done: 'Why didn't you do this?' He had the best character. I never touched any silk softer than his palm, and I never smelled any musk or perfume with a better fragrance than his sweat."*
- **Al-Bara'** (Al-Bukhari): the most handsome face of all people, the best behaviour of all people — **neither tall nor short.**
- **A'ishah** (Ahmad): he never struck a servant with his hand, nor a woman, nor anything at all except in Jihad. Given a choice between two things, **he always took the easier** — unless it involved sin, and then he was the farthest of all people from it. He never avenged himself for anything done to him — **except when the limits of Allah were transgressed; then he avenged for Allah's sake.**
- **Abu Hurayrah** (Ahmad, alone in recording it): *I have only been sent to perfect righteous behaviour.*

Ibn Kathir notes the hadiths on this are too many to gather, and points readers to At-Tirmidhi's dedicated volume, **Kitab Ash-Shama'il**.

## 4. Which of You Is Afflicted (68:5–7)

*You will see, and they will see, which of you is Maftun.*

**Maftun**, literally, is one who has been *charmed or lured away from the truth and has strayed from it*. Ibn Abbas: *which of you is crazy.*

The verse is a deferred verdict: *you will know, O Muhammad — and those who oppose you and reject you will know — who is insane and misguided among you.* Ibn Jurayj from Ibn Abbas: **on the Day of Judgement.** Compare *Tomorrow they will come to know who is the liar, the insolent one* (54:26), and the devastating courtesy of *And verily, we or you are rightly guided or in plain error* (34:24).

Then the matter is handed over: **your Lord is the best Knower of who has strayed from His path, and the best Knower of those who are guided.**

## 5. Ten Marks of the Man You Must Not Obey (68:8–16)

*So do not obey the deniers. They wish that you should compromise with them, so they would compromise with you.*

**Ibn Abbas** on the compromise: *that you would permit them their idolatry, and they would permit you your religion.* **Mujahid**: *that you should be quiet about their gods and abandon the truth you are upon.* The offer is always mutual silence — and it is always refused.

Then the portrait, item by item:

| Term | Ibn Kathir's reading |
|---|---|
| **Hallaf** (swears much) | He swears constantly by Allah's Names, *out of place and unnecessarily* |
| **Mahin** | The liar / worthless one — *because of his weakness and disgracefulness, he can only seek protection in false oaths.* Ibn Abbas: *Al-Mahin means the liar* |
| **Hammaz** | Slander (Ibn Abbas, Qatadah) |
| **Namim** | Going about **instigating discord**, carrying tales to corrupt relations between people *when they are good and pleasant* |
| **Hinderer of good** | He refuses to give; withholds the good he has |
| **Mu'tad** (transgressor) | Even in what Allah made lawful for him, **he exceeds the legislated bounds** |
| **Athim** (sinful) | He delves into the forbidden |
| **'Utul** | Cruel, harsh, strong, greedy, stingy |
| **Zanim** | See below |

Two hadiths on the tale-carrier, both severe:

- **Ibn Abbas** (Two Sahihs): the Prophet ﷺ passed two graves and said, *"Verily, these two are being punished, and they are not being punished for something major. One of them was not careful about protecting himself from urine. The other used to spread Namimah."*
- **Hudhayfah** (Ahmad, and the Group except Ibn Majah): *"The slanderer will not enter Paradise."*

And on **'Utul**, Al-Harithah bin Wahb narrated (Bukhari and Muslim):

> *Shall I inform you of the people of Paradise? Every weak and oppressed person — when he swears by Allah, He fulfils his oath.*
> *Shall I inform you of the people of the Fire? Every 'Utul, Jawwaz and arrogant person.*

The Arabic scholars: **Ja'zari** = rude and harsh; **Jawwaz** = greedy and stingy.

**Zanim** gets the sharpest gloss. Al-Bukhari records Ibn Abbas describing him as *"a man from the Quraysh who stands out among them like the sheep that has had a piece of its ear cut off"* — i.e. **famous for his evil**, marked and unmistakable. In the language, a *Zanim* is also **one adopted among a people but not truly of them** (Ibn Jarir).

*Because he had wealth and children.* Ibn Kathir: **this is how he repays the favours** — by disbelieving in Allah's Ayat and calling them tales of the ancients. He then quotes the whole passage of 74:11–30 (*Leave Me alone with whom I created lonely… I shall oblige him to face a severe torment*), the portrait of a man given everything who asks for more and denies the Giver.

*We shall brand him on the snout.* Ibn Jarir: **We will make his matter clear and evident**, so he cannot be hidden from them — as a branding mark on an animal's snout. Others: it is the mark of the people of the Fire — *We will blacken his face on the Day of Judgement*, the face being called here a snout.

## 6. The People of the Garden (68:17–33)

*Verily, We have tried them as We tried the People of the Garden.*

Ibn Kathir states the parable's target at once: this is **the Quraysh**, granted the enormous mercy of Muhammad ﷺ, who met him with denial and opposition. The garden stands for the gift; the ash stands for what happens when a gift is hoarded.

**What they did.** They owned a garden of many fruits, inherited from a father who fed the poor from it. At night they swore to harvest at dawn — *specifically so that the poor and the beggars would not know*, and none of it would have to be given away. And they swore **without saying "if Allah wills."**

**What happened.** *A Ta'if from your Lord passed over it while they were asleep* — a heavenly destruction. By morning it was **like As-Sarim**: Ibn Abbas, *like the dark night*; Ath-Thawri and As-Suddi, *like a crop harvested, withered and dry.*

**The walk out.** They called to one another at dawn, and departed **whispering** — speaking privately so no one would overhear. Allah, *the All-Knower of secrets and private discussions*, then reports the whisper verbatim: *"No poor person shall enter upon you into it today."* They went out *with Hard Qadirin* — **with strength and power**, believing they had the power to do what they intended.

**The five stages of their collapse** — the finest sequence in the surah:

1. *"Verily, we have gone astray."* — Seeing the black ruin, they assume they took the **wrong path** (Ibn Abbas).
2. *"Nay! Indeed we are deprived."* — Certainty returns: this is the place. *We have no portion and no share.*
3. *"Did I not tell you: why do you not Tusabbihun?"* — spoken by **the Awsat among them**, which Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ikrimah, Muhammad bin Ka'b, Ar-Rabi', Ad-Dahhak and Qatadah all gloss as **the most just and best of them**. *Tusabbihun* here means **"why did you not say: if Allah wills?"** (Mujahid, As-Suddi, Ibn Jurayj) — As-Suddi notes that in that era, making the exception for Allah's will *was* an act of glorification. It has also been read as: *why don't you glorify Allah and thank Him for what He gave you?*
4. *"Glory to our Lord! Verily, we have been wrongdoers."* — And here Ibn Kathir is merciless: **they became obedient when it was of no benefit to them, and confessed when it was of no use.**
5. *Then they turned one against another, blaming.* — Each accusing the other over the resolve to deny the poor their right. Their entire response was **to confess the sin to each other.**

*"Woe to us! Verily, we were Taghin"* — we **transgressed, trespassed, violated and exceeded the bounds** until this happened. *"We hope our Lord will give us in exchange better than it."* Two readings: they hoped for a better garden **in this life**, or for its **reward in the Hereafter**. And Allah knows best.

**Who were they?** Some of the Salaf said Yemenis; **Sa'id bin Jubayr** named the village of **Darawan, six miles from San'a**. Another report: Ethiopians from the People of the Book, whose father managed the garden well — reinvesting what it needed, storing a year's food for his dependants, **giving the surplus in charity**. When he died, his sons said: *"Our father was foolish to give some of this harvest to the poor. If we prevent them, we will have more."* The moment they resolved on it, **Allah took away all of it — wealth, gain and charity alike. Nothing remained for them.**

*Such is the punishment* — for whoever opposes Allah's command, is stingy with what he was given, withholds the right of the poor and needy, and meets blessing with ingratitude. *But the punishment of the Hereafter is greater, if they but knew.*

## 7. Shall We Treat the Muslims Like the Criminals? (68:34–41)

For those with Taqwa: **Gardens of Delight with their Lord.** Then a rhetorical sequence that dismantles the disbelievers' assumption that they will fare equally well:

- *What is the matter with you? How do you judge?*
- *Or have you a Book wherein you learn — that you shall have whatever you choose?*
- *Or have you oaths from Us, binding until the Day of Judgement, that yours will be whatever you decide?*
- *Ask them: which of them will stand as surety for that?*
- *Or have they "partners"? Then let them bring their partners, if they are truthful.*

No scripture, no covenant, no guarantor, no gods. The claim rests on nothing.

---

## 8. The Day the Shin Is Laid Bare (68:42–47)

Having promised the Gardens, Allah names **when** — the Day of Judgement, with its horrors, earthquakes, trials and enormities.

Al-Bukhari records from **Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri** that he heard the Prophet ﷺ say:

> *Our Lord will reveal His Shin, and every believing male and female will prostrate to Him. The only ones left standing will be those who prostrated in the worldly life to be seen and heard. Such a person will try to prostrate, but his back will be made one stiff plate.*

The logic Ibn Kathir draws is exact: **they are punished with the opposite of what they did.** They were called to prostrate in this life *while they were whole* — healthy, secure, perfectly able — and refused. So in the Hereafter the ability itself is withdrawn. Every attempt bows the neck and goes no further.

**Then the istidraj.** *Leave Me alone with whoever denies this narration* — meaning **the Qur'an**. Ibn Kathir calls this a severe threat: *leave Me alone with him; I know him, and how I will gradually punish him and increase him in his falsehood.*

*We shall punish them gradually from directions they perceive not* — **and they will not be aware of it at all.** They will believe it is a noble blessing from Allah, when the very same thing is a humiliation. Compare:

> *Do they think that in the wealth and children with which We expand them, We hasten unto them with good things? Nay, but they perceive not.* (23:55–56)

> *So when they forgot that which they had been reminded of, We opened for them the gates of everything, until in the midst of their enjoyment We seized them suddenly — and lo, they were plunged into destruction.*

*And I will grant them a respite. Verily, My plan is strong* — great against whoever opposes His command, rejects His Messengers, and dares to disobey. The Two Sahihs record:

> *Verily Allah gives respite to the wrongdoer, until He seizes him — and he will not escape Him.*

And the Prophet ﷺ then recited: *Such is the punishment of your Lord when He seizes the towns while they are doing wrong. Verily, His punishment is painful and severe.* (11:102)

On the wage and the Unseen (68:46–47), Ibn Kathir refers back to At-Tur: **you call them to Allah without taking any payment**, hoping only for reward with Allah — and still they reject it, out of *ignorance, disbelief and obstinacy.*

## 9. Do Not Be Like the Companion of the Fish (68:48–50)

*So wait with patience for the decision of your Lord* — persevere against the harm and the rejection of your people, **for Allah will give you authority over them, and make the final victory yours and your followers', in this life and the Hereafter.**

*And be not like the Companion of the Fish* — **Dhun-Nun, Yunus bin Matta**, who left his people in anger. Ibn Kathir traces what followed: the ship, the swallowing, being carried into the depths, the layered darkness, and **hearing the sea and its creatures glorify the Most High** — for He is the One whose decree cannot be resisted. And from inside all of it:

> *None has the right to be worshipped but You. Glorified are You! Truly, I have been of the wrongdoers.* (21:87)

*So We answered his call and delivered him from the distress. And thus We deliver the believers.* (21:88)

The narrow escape is stated bluntly elsewhere: *Had he not been of those who glorify Allah, he would have remained inside its belly till the Day of Resurrection* (37:143–144).

**Makzum** = *distressed* (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, As-Suddi). *Then his Lord chose him and made him of the righteous.*

And lest the warning be mistaken for disparagement, Ahmad and Al-Bukhari record from Abdullah, and from Abu Hurayrah in the Two Sahihs:

> *It is not befitting for anyone to say that I am better than Yunus bin Matta.*

## 10. The Evil Eye Is Real (68:51–52)

*Those who disbelieve would almost make you slip with their eyes when they hear the Reminder.*

**Ibn Abbas and Mujahid**: *would make you slip* means **"in order to have some effect on you"** — *they will affect you by looking at you with their eyes*, out of jealousy and hatred, and were it not for Allah's protection of you, it would have harmed you.

From this verse Ibn Kathir draws his ruling: **the effect of the evil eye, by Allah's command, is real** — and he assembles an unusually long chain of hadiths to prove it:

- **Buraydah / 'Imran bin Husayn**: *There is no Ruqyah except for the evil eye and the sting.*
- **Ibn Abbas** (Muslim): *The evil eye is real. If anything were to overtake the divine decree, it would be the evil eye. And if you are asked to perform Ghusl, then wash well.*
- The Prophet ﷺ sought protection for **Al-Hasan and Al-Husayn**: *I seek protection for you two by the perfect Words of Allah, from every Shaytan and dangerous creature, and from every evil eye* — adding, *Thus did Ibrahim seek protection for Ishaq and Isma'il.*
- **Sahl bin Hunayf**, the fullest account: bathing in the valley of Kharrar, admired aloud by 'Amir bin Rabi'ah for the beauty of his skin, and struck down unconscious before he could rise. Brought to the Prophet ﷺ, who was angry with 'Amir and said: *"Would one of you knowingly kill his brother? If one of you sees something in his brother that he likes, let him supplicate for blessing upon him."* He then had 'Amir perform wudu' — face, hands, elbows, knees, feet, and the inside of his waist-wrapper — and had the water poured over Sahl from behind him, the vessel emptied and turned over. **Sahl got up and left with the people, with nothing wrong with him.**
- **Abu Sa'id**: the Prophet ﷺ used to seek refuge from the evil eye of jinn and of humans — *then when the Mu'awwidhatan were revealed, he used them and abandoned everything else.*
- **Jibril's ruqyah over the Prophet ﷺ**: *In the Name of Allah I pray over you for healing, from everything that bothers you, from every envious person and every evil eye — may Allah cure you.*
- **Asma' bint 'Umays**: *"Bani Ja'far are afflicted with the evil eye; should I seek Ruqyah?"* — *Yes, for if anything could overcome the divine decree, it would be the evil eye.*
- **'A'ishah** (Bukhari and Muslim): the Prophet ﷺ **ordered** her to have Ruqyah performed against the evil eye.

And the practical remedy the surah leaves you with:

> *If one of you sees in his brother, or in himself, or in his wealth, that which pleases him, then let him ask Allah to bless it — for verily, the evil eye is real.*

*And they say: "Verily, he is a madman."* Ibn Kathir's final image closes the circle: **they cut at him with their eyes and attack him with their tongues** — and they say it for one reason only: *because he came with the Qur'an.*

*But it is nothing else than a Reminder to all creatures.*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The surah's whole argument is a reversal.** Makkah said *madman*; Allah answered *exalted character* — and then, without raising its voice, listed what the accusers actually were: liars who swear by Allah's name, carriers of tales, hoarders of good, men known for evil the way a sheep is known by its clipped ear. The charge of derangement was returned to sender.

**"His character was the Qur'an."** A'ishah's six words are the most portable thing in the surah. Character here is not temperament — it is the Qur'an worked into a body, its commands done and its prohibitions avoided, until the natural disposition is replaced by the revealed one.

**The garden burns the night before harvest.** Not for idolatry, not for murder — for a whispered decision to keep the poor out. The wealth was theirs; the *right* of the poor within it was not. And the punishment was precisely proportionate: they moved to take everything, so everything was taken.

**Say "if Allah wills."** The men swore an oath about tomorrow with no reference to the One who owns tomorrow. That omission is what the best among them named as their failure.

**Prosperity after denial is not a verdict in your favour.** *We shall punish them gradually from directions they perceive not* — they will read the widening gates as blessing. The most frightening mercy is a long respite.

**And guard what you love with a prayer, not silence about it.** The evil eye is real, and the antidote the Prophet ﷺ taught is disarmingly simple: when something pleases you — in your brother, yourself, or your wealth — **ask Allah to bless it.**
