*Revealed in Madinah · 38 ayat · the only surah bearing the Prophet's ﷺ name — mentioned once, at 47:2*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Muhammad is a **Madinan surah of sorting**. From its first two verses to its last, it works by pairing: a deed nullified against a deed multiplied, a heart sealed against a heart given more guidance, a river of wine against a drink of boiling water, a people who turn away against a people who will replace them.

The instrument it uses to do the sorting is **Jihad**. This is the surah in which fighting is commanded, and Ibn Kathir's reading is that the command exists precisely *because* it separates people — it is the test that shows who was actually listening. The believers had asked for such a surah; when it came, some of them went pale.

Its second great theme, arriving unexpectedly at 47:22, is the **ties of kinship** — and Ibn Kathir spends more pages on the womb (*ar-rahim*) than on almost anything else here, because the alternative to obedience is not neutrality. It is a return to Jahiliyyah: blood spilled and relatives cut off.

---

## 1. The Opening Pair: Deeds Voided, Deeds Repaired (47:1–3)

The surah begins with two verdicts placed side by side.

**The disbelievers who obstruct others** — Allah *renders their deeds vain*. Ibn Kathir: He makes them futile and denies them any reward or blessing for them. This is the fate described elsewhere as:

> *We will approach what they have done of deeds and make them as dispersed dust.* (25:23)

**The believers** — those whose *hearts and souls have believed, and whose limbs and hidden and apparent acts have complied with Allah's Law*.

A precise grammatical point Ibn Kathir draws out: after "those who believe and do righteous deeds," the verse adds *"and believe in that which is sent down to Muhammad."* This is **adding a specific meaning to a general one** — and it is proof that **after Muhammad's ﷺ advent, believing in him is a required condition of true faith**. Belief in the abstract no longer suffices.

The phrase *"and He amends their Bal"* draws three near-identical glosses:

| Authority | Gloss |
|---|---|
| Ibn Abbas | "Their matter" |
| Mujahid | "Their affair" |
| Qatadah & Ibn Zayd | "Their condition" |

The same word appears in the response taught for one who sneezes: *May Allah guide you and rectify your affairs.*

**Why the difference in treatment?** 47:3 gives the whole reason for the surah: *those who disbelieve follow falsehood, while those who believe follow the truth from their Lord.* They **chose** falsehood over truth. Everything that follows is the consequence of that choice made visible.

---

## 2. The Law of War, and Its End Point (47:4)

The command: strike the necks in battle until the enemy is fully defeated, then bind the captives. Then a choice — **generosity (free release) or ransom**.

Ibn Kathir's framing is important: this is *guidance for the believers in their fighting against the idolaters*, and the choice regarding prisoners belongs to the phase **"after the war ends and the conflict has ceased."** The captive taken in battle is not held indefinitely; he is either released as an act of grace or ransomed.

He notes the verse appears to have come **after Badr**, where the believers had been reproached for taking captives too readily:

> *You desire the commodities of this world, but Allah desires for you the Hereafter... Were it not for a prior decree from Allah, a severe torment would have touched you for what you took.* (8:67–68)

**"Until the war lays down its burden"** — Mujahid read this eschatologically: *until 'Isa bin Maryam descends*. Ibn Kathir suggests he derived it from the hadith:

> *There will always be a group of my Ummah victorious upon the truth, until the last of them fight against Ad-Dajjal.*

The fuller version, from **Salamah bin Nufayl** (Ahmad, An-Nasa'i), is one of the surah's memorable passages. Salamah came to the Prophet ﷺ saying he had let his horse go and thrown down his weapon, *for the war has ended*. The reply:

> *Now the time of fighting has come... Verily, the centre of the believers' abode is Ash-Sham. And goodness is tied around the horses' foreheads till the Day of Resurrection.*

**Why the test at all?** *"Had Allah so willed, He could have taken vengeance against them"* — He could have destroyed the disbelievers directly. He legislated fighting instead **to test you with one another**. Ibn Kathir cross-references the two other surahs where this wisdom is stated: *"Or did you think you would enter Paradise before Allah made evident those of you who fight"* (3:142), and *"Fight them; Allah will punish them by your hands... and remove the fury of the believers' hearts"* (9:14–15).

---

## 3. The Martyrs (47:4–6)

Because war means believers die, the surah immediately guarantees their deeds. Ibn Kathir: **He does not let their good deeds go to waste — He multiplies and increases them**, and some continue to be rewarded for the entire length of their stay in Al-Barzakh.

**The six merits of the martyr** (Ahmad, from Qays Al-Judhami) — granted *as the first drop of his blood gushes forth*:

1. All his sins are forgiven
2. He is shown his place in Paradise
3. He is married to wide-eyed Huris
4. He is secured from the great fear of Judgement Day
5. He is secured from the torment of the grave
6. He is adorned with the adornments of Iman

And from **Abu Ad-Darda** (Abu Dawud): *a martyr is allowed to intercede for seventy members of his household.*

**"He will guide them"** — Ibn Kathir: guide them *to Jannah*, as in *"their Lord will guide them because of their Faith: beneath them rivers will flow"* (10:9).

**"Paradise, which He has made known to them"** — Mujahid gives the image that makes this concrete: the people of Paradise **will be guided to their houses and dwellings without error, without asking anyone for directions — as if they had dwelt there from the time they were first created.**

Al-Bukhari's hadith from Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri completes it: after passing the Fire, the believers are **detained on a bridge between Paradise and the Fire**, where every injustice they committed against one another in the world is settled. Only once purified are they admitted. *By the One in Whose Hand is my soul, each one of them will find his way to his dwelling in Paradise better than he found his way to his dwelling in the worldly life.*

---

## 4. Support and Misery (47:7–9)

> *If you support Allah, He will support you, and make your foothold firm.*

Ibn Kathir's principle: **the reward is comparable to the type of deed performed.** Cf. *"Indeed, Allah will surely support those who support Him"* (22:40).

The opposite of a firm foothold is *ta'san* — **stumbling, misery**. And here Ibn Kathir places one of the most quoted hadiths on attachment to wealth:

> *Misery awaits the servant of the Dinar; misery awaits the servant of the Dirham; misery awaits the servant of velvet. Misery awaits him, and degeneracy; and if he is pricked by a thorn, may he not find anyone to pull it out for him.*

Ibn Kathir's terse gloss on that last line: **"This means may Allah not cure him."**

The reason their deeds are made fruitless: *they hate what Allah has sent down* — they did not want it and did not like it.

---

## 5. "Allah Is Our Protector, and You Have No Protector" (47:10–13)

Travel the earth and look at how it ended for those before — *and a similar end awaits the disbelievers.* The reason is given as a single contrast: **Allah is the Mawla (Protector) of the believers, and the disbelievers have no Mawla at all.**

Ibn Kathir illustrates it with the exchange **after Uhud**, which is the surah's most vivid narrative moment. Abu Sufyan, calling from above the battlefield, asked after the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and Umar; hearing no reply, he announced that all three were dead. Umar broke the silence: *"You lie, O enemy of Allah! Allah has indeed saved those who will displease you."*

Abu Sufyan then chanted: *"Glory to Hubal!"*

> The Prophet ﷺ: **"Aren't you going to respond to him?"**
> The Companions: *"What should we say, O Messenger of Allah?"*
> **"Say: Allah is Most High and Most Glorious."**

Abu Sufyan: *"We have Al-'Uzza, and you have no 'Uzza."*

> **"Say: Allah is our Protector, and you have no protector."**

**The disbelievers "eat as cattle eat"** — Ibn Kathir: they enjoy this world and consume it like animals, *munching and gnawing with greed, having no concern other than that*. Confirmed in the Sahih: *A believer eats with one intestine, and a disbeliever eats with seven intestines.*

**"How many a town mightier than your town that has driven you out"** — a severe threat to Makkah. If Allah destroyed earlier nations for denying their messengers, what of a people who rejected **the chief of the Messengers and the last of the Prophets**? And if the punishment is withheld from some in this life by the blessing of the Prophet's presence, it is merely stored: *"Their torment will be doubled"* (11:20).

Here Ibn Kathir preserves the farewell that gives the verse its ache. Ibn Abbas reports that when the Prophet ﷺ left Makkah for the Hijrah and hid in the cave, he looked back and said:

> *You are the most beloved to Allah among Allah's lands, and you are also the most beloved to me among Allah's lands. Were it not that the idolaters have driven me out from you, I would never have left you.*

Ibn Abbas added his own verdict: **the worst of enemies is one who transgresses against Allah in His own sacred place, who kills a person not trying to kill him, or who kills for Jahiliyyah vengeance.**

## 6. Two Men Who Are Not Equal — and the Four Rivers (47:14–15)

> *Can then he who stands on clear evidence from his Lord be like those for whom their evil deeds are beautified, while they follow their own lusts?*

The first has **clear vision and certainty** about Allah's commands, from three sources: the guidance Allah revealed in His Book, the knowledge that came with it, and **the pure nature (fitrah) upon which Allah created him**. The second has only his desires, dressed up as virtue.

Ibn Kathir's answer is flat: **they cannot be equal.** Cf. *"Can he who knows that what was revealed to you is the truth be like him who is blind?"* (13:19), and *"Not equal are the people of the Fire and the people of Paradise"* (59:20).

Then the surah's most beautiful passage — **the four rivers of Paradise**, each defined by what it is *not*:

| River | What Ibn Kathir emphasises |
|---|---|
| **Water** *that is not Asin* | Ibn Abbas, Al-Hasan, Qatadah: it does not change. Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Ata' Al-Khurasani: it is not foul-smelling. (The Arabs say *asin* when water's scent turns.) |
| **Milk** *whose taste never changes* | Of utmost whiteness, sweetness and richness. In a hadith: *their milk did not come out of the udders of cattle.* |
| **Wine** *delicious to those who drink it* | No bad taste, no foul smell. *"It has no bad effects, nor does it cause intoxication"* (37:47); *"no headache, nor will they be intoxicated"* (56:19); *"white, delicious to the drinkers"* (37:46). In a hadith: *their wine was not pressed under men's feet.* |
| **Refined honey** | Utmost purity of colour, taste and smell. In a hadith: *their honey did not come out of the bellies of bees.* |

The rivers have a source. From **Hakim bin Mu'awiyah's father** (Ahmad; At-Tirmidhi: *Hasan Sahih*):

> *Verily, there is in Paradise a lake of milk, a lake of water, a lake of honey, and a lake of wine. The rivers then gush out of them.*

And their summit, from the Sahih:

> *When you ask Allah, ask Him for Al-Firdaws, because it is the central and highest part of Paradise, and from it gush the rivers of Paradise, and above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful.*

Then the pairing closes. **Can this be likened** to one abiding eternally in the Fire, given boiling water *that severs their intestines* — so hot it is unbearable, cutting through bowels and insides? Ibn Kathir breaks form for one line: **"We seek refuge in Allah from that."**

## 7. The Hypocrites Who Listened Without Hearing (47:16–19)

They sat before the Messenger ﷺ, heard his words, understood nothing, and on leaving asked the people of knowledge among the Companions: *"What is it that he has said just now?"* — meaning *at this moment*. Ibn Kathir: **they did not understand what he said, nor did they care about it.** Hearts sealed, because they followed their lusts; **neither sound understanding nor correct intentions**.

Against them, the counter-promise: *those who accept guidance, He increases in guidance* — Allah facilitates it, guides them to it, **makes them firm on it, and gives them more of it** — and inspires them to their Taqwa.

**The Hour's portents have already come.** Ibn Kathir stacks the verses that say the same thing: *"The approaching Day has drawn near"* (53:57); *"The Hour has drawn near, and the moon has split"* (54:1); *"The command of Allah is coming, so do not be impatient for it"* (16:1); *"The people's Day of Reckoning has approached while they turn away in heedlessness"* (21:1).

**The Messenger's own advent is one of those portents**, because he is the Finality of the Messengers. Al-Bukhari, from Sahl bin Sa'd — the Prophet ﷺ held up his index and middle fingers together:

> *I have been sent before the Hour as these two.*

And when it arrives, remembering is useless: *"On that Day the human being will remember, but what would that remembrance avail him then?"* (89:23).

**"So know that La ilaha illallah, and ask forgiveness for your sin."** Ibn Kathir notes the pairing is deliberate — knowledge of Tawhid is joined to *istighfar*. He then gives three of the Prophet's ﷺ own supplications, all from the Sahih:

> *O Allah, forgive my sin, my ignorance, my immoderation in my affairs, and all that You know better about my faults than myself. O Allah, forgive my joking and my seriousness, my unintended error and my deliberate mistakes — and I have done all of that.*

> *O Allah, forgive what I have done and what I may yet do, what I did secretly and what I did openly, what I did excessively, and all sins You know better than me. You are my God; there is no true God except You.*

> *O people! Repent to your Lord! Verily, I ask Allah for forgiveness and repent to Him more than seventy times every day.*

*"Allah knows your moving about and your place of rest"* — He knows **how you behave by day and where you settle at night** (cf. 6:60, 11:6).

## 8. The Ones Who Asked for the Command, Then Flinched (47:20–23)

The believers were **hoping Jihad would be legislated**. When it was, some turned back. Ibn Kathir opens with the parallel from An-Nisa': those told to restrain their hands and establish Salah, who then, *when fighting was ordained, feared the people as they fear Allah or more*, and asked for a postponement (4:77).

Here the same thing: when *a decisive Surah mentioning fighting* came down, **you could see the sick-hearted looking at you with the look of one about to faint for fear of death** — from terror and cowardice at the thought of meeting the enemy.

*"But it was better for them: obedience and good words"* — better, in that moment, **to listen and obey**. And *when the matter is resolved* — when the situation turns serious and the fighting actually begins — **if they had been true to Allah**, meaning sincere in intention, it would have been better for them.

### The turn to kinship (47:22–23)

> *So would you perhaps, if you turned away, spread corruption on earth and sever your ties of kinship?*

Ibn Kathir reads turning away from Jihad as reverting to the practices of the age of ignorance: **shedding blood and cutting relatives off.** The verse carries a general prohibition of corruption and a **specific** prohibition of severing kinship — and Allah has correspondingly commanded joining the ties **in speech, in action, and in spending wealth**.

He then assembles what is effectively a small treatise:

- **Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah** — after creation was completed, **the womb stood up and took hold of the lower garment of the Most Merciful**. He said, *"Stop that!"* It replied, *"My stand here is the stand of one seeking refuge in You from severance of ties."* Allah said, *"Would it not please you that I join whoever joins you, and sever whoever severs you?"* It said, *"Yes indeed!"* He said, **"You are granted that."** Abu Hurayrah then said: *Read, if you wish* — and recited 47:22.
- **Abu Bakrah** (Ahmad, Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi — *Sahih*, Ibn Majah): **no sin deserves that Allah hasten its punishment in this world**, on top of what is reserved in the Hereafter, more than **injustice and severing the ties of kinship**.
- **Thawban** (Ahmad): *Whoever likes his life to be extended and his provision increased, let him connect his ties of kinship.*
- **Abdullah bin 'Amr** (Al-Bukhari): the womb is **attached to the Throne**. And connecting its ties **does not mean dealing evenly** with kinsfolk — it means that **when they sever, you connect**.
- **Abdullah bin 'Amr** (Ahmad): on the Day of Resurrection the womb will be placed, **curved like a spinning wheel, speaking with an eloquent fluent tongue**, calling for the severing of whoever severed it and the joining of whoever joined it.
- **Abdullah bin 'Amr** (Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi — *Hasan Sahih*): *The merciful ones will be granted mercy by the Most Merciful. Have mercy on those on earth — the One above the heavens will have mercy on you.* And **Ar-Rahim (the womb) is derived from Ar-Rahman**: whoever joins it, it joins him; whoever severs it, it severs him.

The verdict on those who do otherwise: *Allah has cursed them, made them deaf and blinded their vision.*

## 9. Locks Upon the Hearts (47:24)

> *Will they not then reflect upon the Qur'an, or are there locks upon their hearts?*

Ibn Kathir treats this as a **command to ponder the Qur'an and a prohibition against turning away from it** — and reads the second half literally: **there indeed are locks on some hearts, closed so firmly that none of its meanings can reach them.**

Ibn Jarir preserves a small scene. The Prophet ﷺ recited this ayah, and **a young man from Yemen** said: *"Indeed, there are locks upon them — until Allah opens them totally or slightly."* Umar never forgot it; he kept the young man in mind, and when he came to authority, **he made him a consultant.**

---

## 10. Apostasy, Secret Plotting, and the Angels at Death (47:25–28)

Those who *turn back on their heels* after guidance became clear — **Shaytan enticed them**: he adorned and beautified apostasy for them, tempted them and deceived them, and *filled them with false hopes*.

Their method was collusion: *"We will obey you in part of the matter"* said privately to those who hate what Allah revealed. Ibn Kathir: **they plotted secretly and gave evil advice — the standard practice of hypocrites, who declare the opposite of what they conceal.** But *"Allah knows their secrets"*; cf. *"Allah records all that they plot by night"* (4:81).

And the ending: **how will it be when the angels take their souls, striking their faces and their backs?** Ibn Kathir: their souls cling to their bodies, so the angels extract them **by force, harshness and beating** — as in 8:50 and 6:93, where the angels *extend their hands* (meaning, to strike), saying:

> *Deliver your souls! This day you will be recompensed with the torment of degradation, because of what you used to utter against Allah other than the truth, and because you used to arrogantly reject His signs.* (6:93)

## 11. The Exposure (47:29–31)

> *Or do those in whose hearts is a disease think that Allah will not expose their Adghan?*

**Adghan** is the plural of **Dighn** — *what the souls harbour of envy and hatred toward Islam and toward the people who support it.*

Do they imagine it will stay hidden? **It will not.** Ibn Kathir points to Surah Bara'ah (At-Tawbah) as the fulfilment: it clarified the hypocrites' scandals and pinpointed the practices that gave them away — which is why **that surah is also called "The Exposer."**

*"Had We so willed, We could have shown them clearly to you"* — Allah could have named the hypocrites to His Messenger individually. **He did not.** Instead He **conceals His creation, lets their affairs run according to apparent purity, and leaves the inner secrets to the One who is aware of them.**

But there is a tell: *"you will know them by the tone of their speech."* A person announces his true allegiance through the drift and meaning of his words. Ibn Kathir quotes **Uthman bin Affan**:

> *Never would one conceal a secret but Allah will expose it by the look on his face and the uncontrolled words of his tongue.*

*"And We will surely try you"* — with commands and prohibitions, *until We know those who strive among you and the patient*. Ibn Kathir handles the theological difficulty directly: **Allah's knowledge precedes all events**; "until We know" means *until We know of its occurrence*. Ibn Abbas' formula for every verse of this kind: **"Except so that We may know" means "so that We may see."**

## 12. Do Not Invalidate Your Deeds (47:32–35)

Those who disbelieve, obstruct, and oppose the Messenger **cannot harm Allah in the least** — they only ruin themselves. Their apostasy **wipes away their good deeds entirely, just as good deeds would normally wipe away evil ones.** Not the weight of a mosquito of prior good survives it.

Then the command: *O you who believe! Obey Allah, obey the Messenger, and do not invalidate your deeds.*

Two Companion reports show how heavily this landed:

- **Abu Al-'Aliyah** (via Ahmad Ibn Nasr Al-Marwazi, *Kitab As-Salah*): the Companions used to think **no sin could harm a person who says *La ilaha illallah***, just as no good deed benefits one who associates partners with Allah. Then this verse came down — **and it made them fear that some sins could nullify their deeds.**
- **Ibn Umar**: *"We used to think all good deeds are accepted"* — until this verse. They asked each other what could nullify deeds and concluded: **the major sins and immoral acts**. Then *"Verily, Allah does not forgive that partners be associated with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills"* (4:48) was revealed — **and they stopped saying that**, thereafter fearing for those who committed grave sins and hoping for those who did not.

Ibn Kathir's own reading of *"do not invalidate your deeds"*: **meaning, by apostasy** — which is why the next verse is about those who die as disbelievers, for whom there is no forgiveness.

### The rule on treaties (47:35)

> *So do not lose heart and beg for peace while you are superior.*

*Do not lose heart* — do not be weak before the enemy. *Do not beg for peace* — do not seek compromise and an end to fighting **while you are in a position of power**, in numbers and preparation.

But Ibn Kathir immediately supplies the other half, and it is the passage worth noting:

> **If the disbelievers are more powerful and numerous than the Muslims, the Imam may decide to hold a treaty if he judges that it entails a benefit for the Muslims** — as Allah's Messenger ﷺ did when the disbelievers obstructed him from entering Makkah and offered a ten-year cessation of fighting. **He agreed to it.** *(Al-Hudaybiyyah)*

The prohibition, then, is on **seeking peace out of weakness of will while strong**, not on peace as such.

*"And Allah is with you"* — **good news of victory** — *"and He will never deprive you of your deeds"*: He will never nullify them or reduce them, **but will give you your rewards complete, without any reduction.**

## 13. The Closing Argument: What Is Actually Being Asked of You (47:36–38)

> *This worldly life is only amusement and diversion.*

Ibn Kathir: **that is its outcome — except for what is done for the sake of Allah.**

And then the surah ends where nobody expects a war surah to end: on **money**.

*"He will not ask you for your possessions."* Ibn Kathir: **He is in no need of you and asks you for nothing.** He ordained charity out of your wealth **so that you would help your needy brothers — and the benefit of that comes back to you, along with the reward.**

*"If He were to demand all of it and press you, you would withhold — and He would expose your ill-wills."* Qatadah's comment is unusually candid:

> *"Allah knows that extracting wealth from people brings about ill-wills."*

Ibn Kathir's response: **Qatadah spoke the truth — money is dear to people, and they do not part with it except for something dearer to them than it.**

*"Whoever acts stingily is but stingy toward himself"* — **he only reduces his own reward**, and the bad outcome returns to him.

*"Allah is Al-Ghani, and you are the poor."* Ibn Kathir states this as an ontological asymmetry, not a rebuke: **freedom from need is a necessary attribute of Allah; neediness is a necessary attribute of the creation, which they cannot escape.**

And the final warning:

> *And if you turn away, He will replace you with other people; then they will not be like you.*

Ibn Kathir: turning away means **turning from obeying Him and adhering to His Laws**. And the replacements? **"Rather, they will be people who will listen to Him and obey His commands."**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Surah Muhammad is about what survives.** Deeds are not automatically banked — they can be voided (by disbelief, apostasy, hatred of what was revealed) and they can be multiplied and preserved past death (for the martyr, still being rewarded in Al-Barzakh). The surah's repeated verb is *ahbata*: **He made their deeds fruitless.**

**The test is designed to sort, not to destroy.** Allah says outright that He could have taken vengeance Himself. He legislated struggle instead, *to test you with one another* — which is why the same command that the believers had eagerly requested is the one that made the sick-hearted look faint.

**Three things Ibn Kathir returns to, that are easy to miss in a surah about war:**

- **Reflect on the Qur'an, or accept that there are locks on your heart.** There is no third option offered.
- **Cut no relative off.** The womb is attached to the Throne; connecting ties means connecting *when the other side has severed*; and of all sins, injustice and severing kinship are the two whose punishment Allah hastens into this life.
- **Know that La ilaha illallah — and ask forgiveness.** The Prophet ﷺ, who needed it least, did it more than seventy times a day.

And the closing image, which is really the surah's whole argument in one line: **Allah is not asking for your wealth, or your life, because He needs them. He is asking whether you are the kind of people He will keep — or replace.**
