*Revealed in Makkah · 123 ayat · the surah that turned the Prophet's hair gray*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ibn Kathir opens not with the letters or the first verse, but with a hadith. At-Tirmidhi recorded from Ibn Abbas that **Abu Bakr said, "O Messenger of Allah, your hair has turned gray."** The Prophet ﷺ replied that it was **Hud, Al-Waqi'ah, Al-Mursalat, An-Naba' and At-Takwir** that had done it. In another narration: *"Surah Hud and its sisters..."*

That is the key to reading the whole surah. Its sisters are all surahs of the Hour and the reckoning, and Hud's method is to make the reckoning concrete by walking through the nations that already faced it: **Nuh, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim, Lut, Shu'ayb, Musa** — one after another, each ending in the same way. It is not a collection of stories. It is one argument told seven times, and the ending is always the same: *away with them.*

The surah then tells you plainly, at the end, why it did this — **to make the Prophet's heart firm** (11:120).

---

## 1. A Book Perfect in Wording, Detailed in Meaning (11:1–4)

On the disconnected letters, Ibn Kathir simply refers back to Al-Baqarah — *"that discussion is sufficient without any need for repetition here."*

**"Perfected, then explained in detail"** — Mujahid and Qatadah read this as **perfect in its wording, detailed in its meaning**; complete in form *and* in content. Ibn Jarir preferred this reading.

And the whole of that perfection descended for one purpose: *worship none but Allah.* Ibn Kathir stacks the cross-references — every messenger before was sent with the same sentence (21:25), and every nation received one (16:36).

**The warner and the bringer of glad tidings.** This is the hadith of As-Safa. The Prophet ﷺ climbed the mountain, gathered the Quraysh, and asked: if I told you a cavalry was going to attack you in the morning, would you not believe me? They said, *"We have not found you to be a liar."* He said: then I am a warner to you before a severe punishment.

**Seek forgiveness and turn to Him** — Ibn Kathir splits this precisely:

| Phrase | What it covers |
|---|---|
| *Seek forgiveness of your Lord* | **Previous** sins |
| *and turn to Him in repentance* | **Future** sins — then abide by that |
| *good enjoyment for a term appointed* | This worldly life |
| *bestow His grace on every owner of grace* | The Hereafter (Qatadah) |

Note the closing structure of the passage: a threat of a Great Day, then *to Allah is your return, and He is able to do all things.* Ibn Kathir observes the deliberate pairing — **"This section encourages fear, just as the previous section encourages hope."**

---

## 2. Folding Up the Breasts (11:5)

A small, strange, human verse. Ibn Abbas explains it: there were people who **disliked facing the sky uncovered** — while relieving themselves in the open, or with their wives — out of shame before Allah. So they would fold themselves up and cover their heads.

The verse's answer is not to shame them but to enlarge their picture of Him: even wrapped in their garments, **He knows what they conceal and what they reveal.** *He is the All-Knower of the innermost secrets of the breasts.*

---

## 3. Provision Is Guaranteed (11:6)

*No moving creature is there on earth but its provision is due from Allah.* Small, large, sea-dwelling, land-dwelling — all of it.

Ibn Abbas on the two words:

- **Its dwelling place** — where it resides.
- **Its deposit** — where it will die.

Both are already written *in a Clear Book*. Cross-referenced to 6:38 (*We have neglected nothing in the Book*) and 6:59 (*not a leaf falls, but He knows it*).

---

## 4. The Throne over the Water — and "Best in Deeds" (11:7)

Six days, and **His Throne was over the water** before that. Ibn Kathir gathers the hadiths on this:

- **Imran bin Husayn** — the Prophet ﷺ told the people of Yemen: *Allah was before everything, and His Throne was over the water. Then He wrote in the Preserved Tablet, mentioning everything.* (Imran's own narration ends with a marvellous detail: a man came and told him his she-camel had escaped, he went after her, and never learned what the Prophet ﷺ said next.)
- **Abdullah bin Amr** — Allah measured the sustenance of creation **fifty thousand years before** creating the heavens and the earth.
- **Abu Hurayrah** — *"Spend and I will spend on you."* Allah's Hand is full, undiminished by spending night and day. Consider what has been spent since creation — it has not diminished what is in His Right Hand. And in His Hand is the Scale, which He lowers and raises.

Then the purpose clause, and one of the most quoted observations in the whole tafsir:

> **Allah did not say "which of you has done the *most* deeds." He said "which of you is *best* in deeds."**

And a deed is not good until it meets **two conditions**: it must be done **sincerely for Allah**, and it must be **in accordance with the legislation of the Messenger ﷺ**. Missing either one, the deed is void.

---

## 5. The Word "Ummah" (11:8)

A grammatical detour that turns into a small lexicon. Since 11:8 uses *ummah* to mean a stretch of time, Ibn Kathir lays out every sense the word carries in the Qur'an and Sunnah:

| Meaning | Evidence |
|---|---|
| A specified **period of time** | 11:8; and Yusuf's cupbearer remembering *after an ummah* (12:45) |
| An **Imam**, a leader who is followed | *Verily, Ibrahim was an Ummah, obedient to Allah* (16:120) |
| A **religion or creed** | *We found our fathers upon a certain ummah* (43:23) |
| A **group of people** | The men watering their flocks at Madyan (28:23) |
| A **nation that received a Messenger** — believers and disbelievers alike | *For every Ummah there is a Messenger* (10:47) |
| The **followers** of the Messengers | *You are the best Ummah raised up for mankind* (3:110) |
| A **sect or party** | *Of the people of Musa there is an Ummah who lead with truth* (7:159) |

## 6. Man in Ease and in Hardship (11:9–11)

Ibn Kathir reads these three verses as a portrait of human character — **with one exception**.

Take a blessing away and man **despairs and denies**; he behaves as though he had never seen any good at all. Give him a blessing after hardship and he says *"Ills have departed from me"* — pleased with what is in his hand, ungrateful, and boastful towards others.

*Except those who show patience and do righteous good deeds.* Ibn Kathir maps the two halves of the reward onto the two halves of the description:

- **Forgiveness** — for the calamities that afflicted them (patience in hardship).
- **A great reward** — for what they sent forth in their times of ease (deeds in health).

Then the two hadiths that carry the point:

> *No worry, calamity, distress, illness or grief strikes a believer — even the prick of a thorn — except that Allah expiates his sins by it.*

> *Allah does not decree any matter for the believer except that it is good for him. If blessing befalls him he is thankful, and that is good for him. If harm comes to him he is patient, and that is also good for him. This is exclusively for the believer.*

---

## 7. The Prophet's Grief, and the Challenge of Ten Surahs (11:12–14)

The Quraysh's taunts were wearing on him: *why has a treasure not been sent down to him, or an angel come with him?* (cf. 25:7–8). Ibn Kathir says this verse **comforted the worries the polytheists were causing him** — the danger being that he might feel pressed to leave off part of the Message because of what they said.

The answer is short: *But you are only a warner. And Allah is a Guardian over all things.* You have an example in your brothers among the Messengers — they were rejected and harmed, and were patient until Allah's help came.

Then the challenge. Not the whole Qur'an — **ten fabricated surahs like it**, and call on anyone besides Allah to help you. Ibn Kathir's reason for the impossibility is theological, not literary:

> **The Speech of the Lord of all that exists is not like the speech of created beings, just as His attributes are not like the attributes of creation.**

And if they cannot answer, *know then that it is sent down with the knowledge of Allah* — the inability itself is the proof.

---

## 8. Whoever Wants Only This World (11:15–16)

A verse the Salaf read primarily as a verse about **showing off**.

**Ibn Abbas:** those who show off are given the reward of their good deeds *in this life*, not wronged by even the speck on a date-stone. Whoever fasts, prays, or stands at night for worldly gain — Allah says, *give him the reward of what he sought*, and the deed itself is wasted.

**Anas bin Malik and Al-Hasan:** revealed concerning the Jews and Christians.
**Mujahid and others:** revealed concerning people who perform deeds to be seen.
**Qatadah:** whoever's whole concern and goal is this world is paid here and arrives in the next life with nothing left to be rewarded — *whereas the believer is rewarded for his good deeds in both.*

Cross-referenced to 17:18–21 and 42:20.

---

## 9. The Fitrah and the Witness (11:17)

*Can they who rely on a clear proof from their Lord... and whom a witness from Him recites it?* Ibn Kathir reads the "clear proof" as **the natural religion Allah made inherent in His creatures** — their own confession that there is none worthy of worship but Him (30:30).

> *Every child is born upon the fitrah, but his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian — just as a calf is born whole. Have you noticed any born mutilated?*

> Allah says: *I created My servants hunafa', but the devils came to them and turned them from their religion, and made unlawful for them what I had made lawful.* (Iyad bin Himar, in Sahih Muslim)

**The witness** is the revealed legislation — finalized in the Shari'ah of Muhammad ﷺ — and the fitrah is what recognizes and accepts it. The believer is the one who **remains upon the fitrah**.

**And a beautiful aside on method.** Sa'id bin Jubayr said he never heard a sound hadith from the Prophet ﷺ without finding its confirmation somewhere in the Qur'an. The example he gives is *this* verse: he had heard the hadith *"there is no one of this Ummah, Jew or Christian, who hears of me and does not believe in me, except that he will enter the Hellfire,"* and searched for its Qur'anic footing — and found it in **"those of the sects that reject it, the Fire will be their promised meeting place."** *And this means from all religions.*

## 10. The Greatest Losers (11:18–22)

Those who lie against Allah will be **brought before their Lord in front of witnesses** — angels, Messengers, Prophets, and the rest of mankind and jinn — and the witnesses will say: *these are the ones who lied against their Lord.*

Ibn Kathir sets this beside its exact opposite, the hadith of **An-Najwa** (Ibn Umar):

> Allah will draw the believer near and **place His shade over him**, concealing him from the people, and make him confess his sins — *do you recognize this sin? and that one?* — until the believer thinks he is about to be destroyed. Then Allah says: **"I concealed them for you in the world, and I forgive you for them today."**

Same court. Two entirely different proceedings.

**Why their torment is doubled:** they were given hearing, sight, and hearts, and none of it benefited them — *they were deaf from hearing the truth and blinded away from following it* (cf. 67:10, 16:88).

And Ibn Kathir closes the passage with a striking accounting of what "greatest losers" actually means — the **exchange** they made:

- The highest levels of Paradise → for the lowest levels of Hell
- The pleasure of the Gardens → for fierce boiling water
- The drink of sealed nectar → for hot wind, boiling water, black smoke
- Wide-eyed maidens → for food from the filth of wounds
- Lofty castles → for Hawiyah
- Nearness to Allah and the blessing of gazing at Him → for His anger and His punishment

*Therefore it is no injustice that such people should be the greatest losers.*

## 11. The Two Parties (11:23–24)

Against that, the believers: those whose **hearts believed and whose limbs worked** — obedience performed, evils abandoned. Ibn Kathir's description of what they inherit is one of the warmest passages in the surah:

Lofty rooms, fruit near at hand, elevated couches, and **the sight of the Creator of the heavens and the earth**. They will not die, nor grow old, nor fall ill, nor sleep. There is no excrement, no spitting. **Their sweat will be the perfume of musk.**

Then the parable: *the blind and the deaf, and the seer and the hearer.* The disbeliever is blind to the truth — he is not guided to good and does not recognize it — and deaf to the proofs. **The believer is smart, bright and clever**: he sees the truth, distinguishes it from falsehood, hears the argument and separates it from the doubt, so falsehood does not overcome him.

*Are they equal when compared? Will you not then take heed?*

## 12. Nuh — The Objection to His Followers (11:25–31)

Nuh was **the first Messenger sent to the people of the earth**, who by then were polytheists worshipping idols.

The chiefs' objection has three parts, and Ibn Kathir dismantles each:

**(i) "You are only a human being like us."** The standing objection of every rejecting nation.

**(ii) "Only the meanest among us follow you — merchants, weavers — and they followed you without thinking."** Ibn Kathir's response is one of the most quotable passages in the surah:

> **The truth is not to be rejected because of the lowly status of those who follow it. The truth is correct in itself.** The followers of the truth are the noble ones even if they are poor; those who reject it are the lowly wretches even if they are wealthy.

And the historical confirmation: when **Heraclius** questioned Abu Sufyan about the Prophet ﷺ, he asked whether his followers were the noble or the weak. Abu Sufyan said, *the weakest of them.* Heraclius said: **"They are the followers of the Messengers."**

As for *"without thinking"* — Ibn Kathir turns the insult inside out. **When the truth is made clear it does not leave room for second-guessing.** No one continues doubtfully pondering the truth after it is clear except one who is ignorant and excessively critical.

**(iii) "We see no merit in you above us."** They saw nothing, Ibn Kathir says, because they were the ones who could not see.

**Nuh's reply** sets out what a Messenger is and is not:

- *I ask of you no wealth for it* — no payment; the reward is from Allah alone.
- *I will not drive away those who have believed* — the chiefs had demanded he separate himself from the poor believers, exactly as the Quraysh later demanded of the Prophet ﷺ (6:52–53). **"Who will help me against Allah if I drove them away?"**
- *I do not say I have the treasures of Allah, nor that I know the Unseen, nor am I an angel* — he is a human Messenger, aided by miracles, and no more.
- And on the believers they despise: **"Allah knows what is in their inner selves."** To speak evil of them is to speak without knowledge — *in that case I should indeed be one of the wrongdoers.*

## 13. Nuh — The Ship, the Flood, and the Son (11:32–48)

They demanded the punishment: *bring upon us what you threaten us with.* His reply: only Allah brings it, and **my advice cannot profit you if Allah's will is to keep you astray.**

**The interruption (11:35).** In the middle of the story, a verse about *Muhammad ﷺ* — "or do they say he has fabricated it?" Ibn Kathir notes the placement is deliberate: **it is inserted mid-narrative to affirm the narrative itself.**

**The revelation:** *none of your people will believe except those who have believed already — so do not grieve.* Then: build the ship, **under Our Eyes** (under Our vision) **and with Our revelation** (We will teach you what to do). And a hard command: *do not address Me on behalf of those who did wrong.*

**The ship's specifications**, which Ibn Ishaq relates from the Tawrah: Indian oak, **eighty cubits long, fifty wide, thirty high**, coated inside and out with tar, a slanted bow to part the water, three levels — **animals lowest, humans in the middle, birds at the top**.

**The mockery.** As he built, the chiefs passed and laughed. His answer — *if you mock at us, so do we mock at you* — Ibn Kathir calls **a severe threat and a serious warning**.

**The flood.** *At-Tannur gushed forth* — Ibn Abbas: **At-Tannur is the face of the earth**; the whole surface became gushing springs, water pouring even from the places where fire would normally be. This, Ibn Kathir notes, is the view of the majority of the Salaf and the later scholars.

Loaded aboard: a pair of every creature possessing a soul (some said plants too). It has been said **the first bird to enter was the parrot, and the last animal was the donkey**.

**The believers were few.** After **nine hundred and fifty years** of calling. Ibn Abbas: *eighty people, including their women.*

**Bismillah.** *Embark: in the Name of Allah will be its course and its anchorage.* Ibn Kathir draws the general ruling from it — it is preferred to mention Allah's Name **at the beginning of all affairs**, and specifically when boarding a ship or mounting an animal (43:12–13, 23:28–29). And he notices the mercy in the verse's ending — *surely my Lord is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful* — said precisely while the whole world is being drowned.

**The son.** Yam, the fourth son, a disbeliever, standing apart. *Embark with us and be not with the disbelievers.* His answer — *I will betake myself to some mountain, it will save me from the water* — was, Ibn Kathir says, **ignorance**: he believed the flood would not reach the mountaintops. The water rose fifteen cubits above them. *And waves came in between them.*

**The end.** *O earth, swallow your water; O sky, withhold.* Ibn Kathir: **not a single one of them remained upon the earth.**

**Mount Judi**, in Al-Jazirah. Mujahid: on the day of the drowning **all the mountains sought to tower above each other** to escape the water — and this one **humbled itself before Allah**, and so was not drowned, and so received the ship. Qatadah: it rested there a month before they descended, and Allah left it there as a sign — *even the early generations of this Ummah saw it.* Ibn Kathir adds, with feeling: **how many ships have come after it and been destroyed and become dust.**

**"O Nuh, come down with peace from Us and blessings on you and on the people with you."** Muhammad bin Ka'b: **every believing man and woman until the Day of Resurrection is included in that salutation of peace** — and every disbeliever, in the warning that follows it.

*(Ibn Kathir also relates the Tawrah's chronology as the People of the Book report it: the raven that never returned, the pigeon that came back with nothing, then with an olive leaf, then not at all — a full year from the beginning of the flood.)*

## 14. "He Is Not of Your Family" (11:45–47)

The most theologically weighted moment in the surah, and Ibn Kathir handles it carefully.

Nuh's question is **a request for information**, not an objection: *my son is of my family, and Your promise is true, and You are the Most Just of the judges.*

The answer draws the line that the whole surah is about:

> **"O Nuh! Surely, he is not of your family; verily, his work is unrighteous."**

Meaning: *I only promised to save those of your family who believe* — as the original command itself had said, *except him against whom the Word has already gone forth* (11:40).

**Ibn Abbas:** *"He was the son of Nuh, but he opposed him in deeds and intention."*
**Ikrimah** notes a mode of recitation rendering it: *"Verily, he worked deeds that were not righteous."*

And then the warning — *ask not of Me that of which you have no knowledge; I admonish you, lest you should be of the ignorant* — and Nuh's immediate response, which is the model: **"My Lord, I seek refuge with You from asking You that of which I have no knowledge. And unless You forgive me and have mercy on me, I would indeed be one of the losers."**

## 15. Hud and 'Ad (11:50–60)

Hud's call is the same call, with a distinctive promise attached:

> *Ask forgiveness of your Lord and turn to Him in repentance — **He will send you abundant rain from the sky, and add strength to your strength.***

Ibn Kathir generalizes it: **whoever has these two characteristics — seeking forgiveness for the past and repentance for the future — Allah will make his sustenance easy for him, grant him ease in his affairs, and guard over his situation.**

**Their answer** was that his own gods had driven him mad. Hud's reply is one of the boldest speeches in the Qur'an:

> *I call Allah to witness, and bear you witness, that I am free of what you associate. **So plot against me, all of you, and give me no respite.***

You and your idols together — and don't even give me the blinking of an eye.

And the ground he stands on: *I put my trust in Allah, my Lord and your Lord. **There is not a moving creature but He has the grasp of its forelock.** Verily, my Lord is on the straight path.* Ibn Kathir: this argument contains **a far-reaching proof** — everything is under His power, so nothing besides Him deserves worship; the idols are inanimate objects that cannot hear, see, befriend, or make enmity.

**Their destruction:** the barren wind, to the last of them.

Ibn Kathir picks out one phrase from the verdict — *they followed the command of every proud, obstinate one* — and one principle from another: **whoever disbelieves in one Prophet has disbelieved in all of them**, which is why 'Ad, who rejected only Hud, are described as having *disobeyed His Messengers*, plural.

## 16. Salih and Thamud (11:61–68)

Thamud lived in **cities carved from the rock between Tabuk and Al-Madinah**, after 'Ad.

Salih's call adds a phrase Ibn Kathir lingers on: *He brought you forth from the earth and settled you therein* — He began your creation from it, and from it He created your father Adam; and He made you prosperous in it. Then: **"Certainly, my Lord is Near, Responsive"** — cross-referenced to 2:186, *I am indeed Near; I respond to the invocation of the supplicant when he calls on Me.*

**Their answer** is the saddest objection in the surah: *"You have been among us as a figure of good hope until this!"* — meaning, **we had hope in your strong intellect before you started saying this.**

The she-camel, the sign, the slaughter, and the three-day reprieve: *This is a promise that will not be belied.* Then As-Sayhah, and they lay lifeless in their homes — *as if they had never lived there.*

Ibn Kathir keeps this section deliberately short: *"A discussion of this story has already preceded in Surah Al-A'raf and it is sufficient without having to be repeated here."*

## 17. Ibrahim and the Guests (11:69–76)

**"Salaman." — "Salamun."** The scholars of tafsir note that Ibrahim's reply was **better than the greeting he received**, because the subjective case (*Salamun*, not *Salaman*) implies **affirmation and permanence**. He returned more than he was given.

**The roasted calf.** *Hanidh* means roasted upon heated stones (Ibn Abbas, Qatadah). He went **in haste** to fetch it — and Ibn Kathir notes the verse *contains many aspects of the etiquettes of hosting guests* (cf. 51:26–27: he turned to his household and *brought it out*, rather than announcing it).

As-Suddi's version adds a scene worth keeping: the guests said they do not eat food without a price. Ibrahim said this food has a price — **that you mention the Name of Allah before eating it, and praise Him when you finish.** At which **Jibril looked at Mika'il and said: "This man has the right that his Lord should take him as an intimate friend."**

**The fear.** Angels have no interest in food; when their hands did not reach for it, Ibrahim mistrusted them. Sarah, meanwhile, was serving them and laughing at the absurdity — *what amazing guests, we serve them ourselves and they will not eat.*

**Sarah's laugh and the glad tidings.** Ibn Kathir reads her laughter as **delight at the news of the destruction** of a people whose corruption and disbelief were severe — and for that, she was rewarded with the news of a son after despair. Then a son beyond the son: *and after Ishaq, of Ya'qub.*

**A famous argument embedded here.** Ibn Kathir uses this verse as **the clearest evidence that the sacrificed son was Isma'il, not Ishaq**: Ishaq was promised, in advance, a son named Ya'qub. Allah's promise does not fail. So Ibrahim could not have been commanded to sacrifice Ishaq as a small child, before that promised grandson existed. *And this is the best, most correct and clearest evidence of that.*

**The negotiation over Lut's people** (Sa'id bin Jubayr). Ibrahim asked: will you destroy a town that has three hundred believers in it? *No.* Two hundred? *No.* Forty? Thirty? *No.* Down to five. *No.* Then: **what if there is one Muslim man in it?** *No.* So Ibrahim said, *but Lut is in it* — and they replied, *we know better who is there; we will save him and his family, except his wife* (29:32). **Then Ibrahim was silent, and his soul was at rest.**

Allah's commendation follows: **forbearing, given to invoking Him with humility, and repentant.** And then, gently: *O Ibrahim, forsake this. The commandment of your Lord has gone forth.*

## 18. Lut (11:77–83)

*He was grieved on account of them and said: **"This is a distressful day."*** Ibn Abbas: a severe test. They had come in the form of **handsome young men** — which Ibn Kathir explicitly calls **a test from Allah containing much wisdom and firm evidence**. Lut's fear was that if he did not host them, someone else would.

Qatadah's account: he walked ahead of them toward his house, and along the way said, *"By Allah, I do not know any people on the face of the earth more wicked than the people of this town"* — hoping they would turn back. **He repeated it four times.** Qatadah adds that the angels had been ordered not to destroy the people **until their own Prophet testified against them**, which is exactly what those four statements were.

**"Here are my daughters, they are purer for you."** Mujahid: these were not his own daughters but the **women of the nation** — *every Prophet is like a father to his nation* — and he was directing them to what is lawful (cf. 26:165–166).

**"Is there not among you a single right-minded man?"** — is there not one good man who will accept what I enjoin?

**"Would that I had strength to overpower you, or that I could betake myself to some powerful support."** And the hadith on it:

> *May Allah's mercy be upon Lut — for verily, he betook himself to a powerful support* (meaning Allah). **And Allah did not send any Prophet after him except amidst an influential family among his people.**

Then the angels revealed themselves: *they shall not reach you.* Travel by night with your family and follow behind them; **let none of you look back** — meaning, if you hear the sound of what befalls them, do not turn toward the noise, keep going. On the wife, two views: that she never left at all and was destroyed in her house, or that she left, turned at the sound, cried *"O my people!"* and was struck by a stone from the sky.

And when the mob pressed at the door: **Jibril came out and struck their faces with his wing**, blinding them, so they retreated unable to find their way (54:37).

**Sijjil** is a Persian word — stones of baked clay. *Mandud* (in an array): either that they were arranged in the heavens and prepared for this, or that they followed one another down in succession. **Musawwamah** (marked): sealed, **each stone bearing the name of the one it was for**; Qatadah and Ikrimah say each was ringed with a sprinkling of red.

The stones followed them beyond the town — *one of them would be speaking with people when a stone struck him from the sky.* Not one remained.

*And they are not ever far from the wrongdoers* — meaning **this vengeance is not far from any who do the same**.

## 19. Shu'ayb and Madyan (11:84–95)

Madyan: an Arab tribe living **between the Hijaz and Ash-Sham, near Ma'an**. Shu'ayb was **of the noblest of them in lineage** — hence *their brother*.

His call adds economic justice to tawhid: **do not short the measure or the weight.** *I see you in prosperity* — meaning, and I fear you will be stripped of this bounty by violating His prohibitions.

**"That which is left by Allah is better for you."** Ibn Jarir, from Ibn Abbas: **what you gain from honest dealing, having given full measure, is better for you than what you seize wrongfully from people.** Compare 5:100 — *not equal are the evil and the good, even though the abundance of the evil may please you.*

**"And I am not a guardian over you"** — Ibn Kathir's reading is pointed: *do this for Allah, and not to be seen by people.*

**Their mockery** is the sharpest in the surah: *"Does your **Salah** command you that we abandon what our fathers worshipped, or that we do as we like with our property?"* (Al-A'mash: *your Salah* here means *your recitation*. Ath-Thawri: the property complaint was about **paying zakah**.) And the sting in the tail — *"Verily, you are the forbearer, the right-minded!"* — which Ibn Abbas, Maymun bin Mihran, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Aslam and Ibn Jarir **all agree was said in mockery**.

**His answer** contains a line every preacher since has had to live with:

> *I wish not, in contradiction to you, to do that which I forbid you.*

Ath-Thawri: *I do not forbid you something and then contradict my own prohibition in secret behind your backs.* Qatadah: *I do not forbid you from something while I do it myself.*

And the summary of his whole vocation: ***I only desire reform to the best of my power. And my guidance cannot come except from Allah; in Him I put my trust, and unto Him I repent.***

Then the warning by precedent: *let not my* shiqaq *(my opposing you) cause you to suffer the fate of Nuh's people, or Hud's, or Salih's — **and the people of Lut are not far off from you.*** Ibn Kathir says this refers to **both time and place** — Qatadah: *they were only destroyed before you yesterday.*

**Their last threats:** *we do not understand much of what you say* (Ath-Thawri notes Shu'ayb was called **the orator of the Prophets**); *we see you weak among us*; *were it not for your family, you would have been stoned.* And his devastating reply:

> **"Is then my family of more weight with you than Allah? And you have cast Him away behind your backs."**

You would spare me out of respect for my clan — but not out of respect for the Lord of the worlds.

### The three punishments of Madyan

Ibn Kathir's finest structural observation in the surah. Madyan is described as destroyed by **three different things** in three different places. His answer: **they were one nation on whom all of these were gathered on the day of their destruction — and in each context, Allah mentioned the one that fit what they had said.**

| Surah | Their words | The punishment named |
|---|---|---|
| **Al-A'raf** | *We shall drive you out of our town* (7:88) | **Ar-Rajfah** — the quake. The land they wronged, and from which they wanted to expel their Prophet, **shook them out of it** |
| **Hud** | Disrespectful speech to their Prophet | **As-Sayhah** — the cry that overcame them and killed them |
| **Ash-Shu'ara'** | *Cause a piece of the heaven to fall on us* (26:187) | **The torment of the Day of Shadow** — the cloud they asked for |

*"This is from the intricate secrets, and to Allah belongs all praise and much bounty forever."*

---

## 20. Musa and Fir'awn (11:96–99)

The briefest of the seven, and told for one image: **he will go ahead of his people on the Day of Resurrection and lead them into the Fire.**

Ibn Kathir's comment: *just as they followed him in this life and he was their leader and chief, likewise he will lead them to the Hellfire* — and he will have a great share of that punishment (cf. 33:67–68, where the followers ask for their leaders to receive **double** torment).

*Evil indeed is the gift granted* — Mujahid: **a second curse added on the Day of Resurrection, so there are two**; Ibn Abbas: the curse of this life and of the Hereafter.

---

## 21. Standing and Reaped (11:100–105)

*Of them, some are still standing, and some have been reaped* — some ruins still visible, some erased entirely.

***We wronged them not, but they wronged themselves.*** And their gods availed them nothing — *nor did they add aught to them but destruction*, which Mujahid and Qatadah gloss simply as **loss**.

**"Such is the punishment of your Lord when He seizes the towns while they are doing wrong."** In the Two Sahihs, Abu Musa reports the Prophet ﷺ saying — *Allah gives respite to the wrongdoer until He seizes him, and then he cannot escape* — **and then reciting this very verse.**

**The Day itself:** a day *when all will be present* — angels, Messengers, all of creation gathered with their families; humans, jinn, birds, wild beasts and riding animals. *And We delay it only for a term already fixed*, which cannot be increased or decreased.

**No one will speak** except by His leave (78:38, 20:108). In the hadith of intercession: *no one will speak on that day except the Messengers, and the call of the Messengers will be, "O Allah, save us, save us."*

**Umar's question.** When *some among them will be wretched and others blessed* was revealed, Umar asked: will it be because of something a person did, or something he did not do? The Prophet ﷺ replied:

> *O Umar, it will be due to something he did and the pens wrote it down. **But every easy deed was created for its purpose.***

---

## 22. The Wretched and the Blessed (11:106–108)

**Zafir and Shahiq.** Ibn Abbas: *Az-Zafir is a sound in the throat, Ash-Shahiq a sound in the chest* — their **exhaling** is Zafir and their **inhaling** is Shahiq. Their very breathing is the torment.

**"For all the time that the heavens and the earth endure."** Ibn Jarir explains this as **an Arab idiom for permanence** — they would say *as enduring as the heavens and the earth*, or *as long as night and day are separate*, or *as long as talkers at night continue to chat*, and mean simply *forever*. Allah addressed them in the manner they already used. But Ibn Kathir adds that **the literal sense is also intended**, since there will be a heaven and an earth in the next life (14:48) — Al-Hasan Al-Basri: *a heaven other than this heaven and an earth other than this earth, and that one will be eternal.*

**"Except as your Lord wills."** The two exceptions are read differently:

- **For the Fire:** the exception refers to the **disobedient among the people of Tawhid**, whom Allah brings out by intercession — of the angels, the Prophets, and the believers — including those guilty of major sins, and finally by His own mercy, **those who never did any good except saying** *la ilaha illallah* **once in their lives**. After that, only those remain who remain forever.
- **For Paradise:** the exception is there to make clear that their eternal delight is **not mandatory in itself** but **dependent on His will** — the immortality is His favor, not their entitlement. And so it is immediately sealed: ***a gift without an end*** — Mujahid, Ibn Abbas, Abu Al-Aliyah: **it will never be cut off.**

> *Death will be brought in the form of a handsome ram and slaughtered between Paradise and the Hellfire. Then it will be said: **"O people of Paradise — eternity, and no death. O people of the Fire — eternity, and no death."***

And: *O people of Paradise, you will live and never die; you will remain young and never grow old; you will remain healthy and never fall ill; you will be happy and never grieve.*

---

## 23. Stand Firm, and the Prayers That Wipe (11:112–115)

***"So stand firm and straight as you are commanded, and those who turn in repentance with you, and transgress not."***

Ibn Kathir: **firmness is the greatest aid for gaining victory over the enemy.** And the prohibition beside it is severe — *transgression destroys the one who practises it, **even if the transgression is directed against a polytheist***.

**"And incline not toward those who do wrong."** Ibn Abbas, two reports: *do not compromise with them*; *do not side with them.* Ibn Kathir prefers a third framing — **do not seek assistance from wrongdoers, because it will be as if you are condoning their evil.**

**The prayer verse (11:114)** — *at the two ends of the day and in some hours of the night.* The Salaf differ on which prayers:

| Authority | Reading |
|---|---|
| Ibn Abbas, Al-Hasan, Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd | Subh and Maghrib |
| Al-Hasan (another narration, via Qatadah and Ad-Dahhak) | Subh and 'Asr |
| Mujahid, Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi | Subh at the day's start; Zuhr and 'Asr at its end |
| Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan (on *"hours of the night"*) | 'Isha' — or Maghrib and 'Isha' together |

Ibn Kathir adds the historical note: **this was revealed before the five daily prayers were made obligatory** on the night of Isra'. At that time there were two obligatory prayers, before sunrise and before sunset.

**"Verily, the good deeds remove the evil deeds."** The hadiths pile up: two rak'ahs after wudu' bring forgiveness (Abu Bakr, via Ali); the five prayers are like **a river at your door in which you bathe five times a day** — what dirt would remain?; and *the five prayers, Jumu'ah to Jumu'ah, Ramadan to Ramadan, are expiation for what lies between them, so long as the major sins are avoided.*

**And the reason it was revealed.** Al-Bukhari records that a man kissed a woman not lawful to him, came to the Prophet ﷺ in distress, and this verse came down. The man asked: **"O Messenger of Allah, is this only for me?"** He replied:

> ***"This is for all of my followers."***

In Ahmad's version, the man asks the same question and it is **Umar** who strikes him on the chest and says: *"No — rather it is for all of the people in general."* And the Prophet ﷺ said: **Umar has spoken the truth.**

---

## 24. The Missing Few (11:116–117)

The verse Ibn Kathir treats as **the diagnosis of every destroyed nation in the surah**:

> *If only there had been among the generations before you **persons having wisdom, forbidding corruption in the earth** — except a few of those whom We saved from among them!*

There were only ever **a small number** of that caliber, and those were precisely the ones Allah saved when the vengeance struck. **For this reason** — Ibn Kathir's connective is explicit — **Allah commanded this Ummah to always have among it those who command good and forbid evil** (3:104).

> *Whenever a group of people see an evil and do not change it, it is likely that Allah will cover them with His punishment.*

*And your Lord would never destroy the towns wrongfully, while their people were doers of good.* No town is destroyed except that it wronged itself first (11:101, 41:46).

---

## 25. They Will Not Cease to Disagree (11:118–119)

He could have made mankind one nation. He did not. *But they will not cease to disagree* — **in religions, creeds, beliefs, opinions and sects** — **except him on whom your Lord has bestowed His mercy**, and those are the ones who follow what the Messengers brought.

***And for that did He create them.*** Ibn Kathir identifies them with the **Saved Sect**:

> *The Jews split into seventy-one sects, the Christians into seventy-two, and this nation will split into seventy-three. All of them in the Fire except one.* — "Who are they, O Messenger of Allah?" — ***"The one upon what my Companions and I are upon."***

And the counterpart decree — *I shall fill Hell with jinn and men together* — brings the hadith of the dispute:

> Paradise said: *none will enter me except the weak and despised.* The Fire said: *I have inherited the haughty and the arrogant.* Allah said to Paradise, *You are My mercy, I grant mercy through you to whom I wish*; and to the Fire, *You are My torment, I take vengeance through you upon whom I wish.* **And I will fill each of you.**
>
> Paradise will always have room to spare, until Allah creates more creatures to dwell in it. **The Fire will keep saying, "Are there any more?" until the Lord of might places His Foot upon it — and then it says: "Enough! Enough! By Your might!"**

---

## 26. The Conclusion (11:120–123)

And here the surah states its own purpose, which is why Ibn Kathir titled the section simply **"The Conclusion"**:

> ***And all that We relate to you of the news of the Messengers is in order that We may make strong and firm your heart thereby.***

Every story in the surah — the argument, the rejection, the harm, the help of Allah, the disgracing of His enemies — was told **so that you may take an example from your brothers who passed before you.**

*And in this has come to you the truth* — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and a group of the Salaf, and Ibn Kathir agrees: **"this" means this Surah itself.** A comprehensive surah containing the stories of the Prophets, how Allah saved them and destroyed those who rejected them; **an admonition that restrains the disbelievers, and a reminder that makes the believers reflect.**

Then the parting words to the opposition — *act according to your ability; we are acting. And you wait; we too are waiting* (cf. 6:135) — and Ibn Kathir's note on how it turned out: **Allah fulfilled His promise, helped him, made His Word uppermost and the word of those who disbelieved lowly and disgraced.**

The final verse gathers the whole surah into one instruction: *to Allah belongs the Unseen of the heavens and the earth, and to Him return all affairs.* **So worship Him and put your trust in Him.** *And your Lord is not unaware of what you do.*

---

## The Thread

Hud is the surah of **the pattern**. Seven prophets, one script: a man rises among his own people, calls them to worship Allah alone, refuses payment, is mocked for the poverty of his followers, is told he is mad or weak or lying — and then the command comes, and the town is finished, and the survivors are the few who believed and the fewer who forbade the wrong.

Ibn Kathir keeps drawing your attention to what does *not* change between the episodes. The objection never changes ("you're only a man like us"). The refusal of a wage never changes. The seeking of forgiveness and turning in repentance never changes. And the ending never changes — *away with 'Ad; away with Thamud; away with Madyan* — until the reader stops hearing seven stories and starts hearing one law.

Which is precisely why the surah aged the Prophet ﷺ. It is not a book of the past. Twice it steps out of the narrative to say so: *this vengeance is not far from the wrongdoers* (11:83), and *such is the punishment of your Lord when He seizes the towns while they are doing wrong* (11:102). And in the middle of Nuh's story it interrupts itself to ask whether *you* say Muhammad forged it — putting the Makkans, mid-sentence, into the position of the chiefs who mocked a man building a ship on dry land.

And then, at the very end, the surah tells you it was never really about them. It was told **to make one man's heart firm** — and the two commands it leaves are the smallest and largest possible: *stand firm as you are commanded*, and *pray at the two ends of the day.*

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Hud, and all praises and thanks are due to Allah.*
