*Revealed in Makkah · 135 ayat · named for the two disconnected letters that open it*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ta Ha opens with a denial: **the Qur'an was not sent down to cause you distress.** That single line sets the temperature of the whole surah. The Quraysh had been saying that this revelation was a burden laid on Muhammad ﷺ — and Allah answers not with argument but with a story: the longest continuous narrative of Musa anywhere in the Qur'an, told from the fire on the mountainside to the sea closing over Fir'awn.

The point of the story is placement. A man lost in the cold on a winter night, with a stammer and a killing behind him, is called by name, handed two miracles, and sent to the most powerful king on earth — and told to **speak to him mildly**. Everything the Prophet ﷺ is worried about has a match in that account.

Then, having spent two-thirds of the surah in Egypt, Ta Ha pivots to Adam — who was also given a covenant, also forgot, also fell, and was also chosen again afterwards. Forgetting is the surah's real subject. As-Samiri *forgot*, Adam *forgot*, and the closing threat against those who turn away is that they, in turn, will be **forgotten**. Against that stands the only supplication in the Qur'an in which the Prophet ﷺ is commanded to ask for more of something: *My Lord, increase me in knowledge.*

---

## 1. Not Sent to Distress You (20:1–3)

> *Ta Ha. We have not sent down the Qur'an unto you to cause you distress, but only as a Reminder to those who fear Allah.*

Ibn Kathir refers the disconnected letters back to his discussion at the start of Al-Baqarah and declines to repeat it.

On the denial itself, **Ad-Dahhak** (via Juwaybir) preserves the occasion: when the Qur'an came down, the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions held to it so strictly that the idolaters of Quraysh said, *this Qur'an was only revealed to Muhammad to cause him distress.* The ayah is the reply.

- **Mujahid** links it to *So recite as much of the Qur'an as may be easy* (73:20) — people had begun hanging ropes from their chests to hold themselves up when they tired in the night prayer.
- **Qatadah**, more bluntly: *No, by Allah, He did not make it a thing of distress. He made it a mercy, a light and a guide to Paradise.*

And Ibn Kathir's own frame, from the hadith in the Two Sahihs: **whomever Allah wants good for, He gives him understanding of the religion.** Knowledge given is a sign of good intended, not a punishment.

---

## 2. The One Who Knows What Is More Hidden (20:4–8)

The revelation comes *from Him Who created the earth and the high heavens* — each sky five hundred years thick, with five hundred years between them.

On **"The Most Gracious Istawa the Throne"**, Ibn Kathir points back to Al-A'raf and states the method plainly: the safest path is the way of the Salaf — **accept what came in the Book and the Sunnah without describing it, reinterpreting it, resembling it to creation, rejecting it, or comparing it to the attributes of creatures.**

Then the reach of His knowledge. *If you speak aloud, He knows the secret and that which is yet more hidden.* **Ibn Abbas**: the *secret* is what a man conceals inside himself; *that which is more hidden* is **the deed he has not yet done**. Past and future are one complete knowledge to Him — which is why *the creation of you all and the resurrection of you all are only as a single person* (31:28).

---

## 3. A Man Lost on a Cold Night (20:9–10)

Ibn Kathir sets the scene carefully, because every detail is load-bearing.

Musa had finished the years he owed his father-in-law and was travelling with his wife, apparently towards Egypt, after more than ten years away. He **lost the road** on a cold winter night, and camped among mountain passes covered in snow, sleet, cloud and fog. He tried to strike a fire with his kindling device and it would not catch — it stopped even throwing sparks.

Then he saw a fire on the side of the mountain.

Three details are pulled out as evidence of his state:
- *that you may warm yourselves* (28:29) → **it was cold**
- *some burning brand* → **it was dark**
- *or find some guidance at the fire* → **he was lost**

**Ibn Abbas**: *they were cold and had lost their way.* So Musa said, in effect, either I will find someone to point us to the road, or at the very least I'll bring back something to light a fire with.

He was going to fetch a coal. He came back a Prophet.

---

## 4. The Sacred Valley (20:11–16)

> *He was called by name: "O Musa! Verily, I am your Lord! So take off your shoes; you are in the sacred valley, Tuwa."*

**Take off your shoes** — Ali, Abu Dharr, Abu Ayyub and others of the Salaf said his sandals were **from the skin of a donkey that had not been slaughtered**. It has also been said the command was simply respect for the blessed ground.

**Tuwa** — Ibn Abbas says it is the name of the valley, and Ibn Kathir judges this the most correct of the several opinions.

**"And I have chosen you"** — Ibn Kathir preserves a striking exchange: Allah asked Musa whether he knew why he, of all people, had been chosen to be spoken to directly. Musa said no. The answer:

> *"Because I have not made anyone humble himself as much as you have humbled yourself."*

Then comes what Ibn Kathir calls **the first obligation upon every responsible person**: *Verily, I am Allah! There is no God but Me* — to know that there is nothing worthy of worship but Allah alone.

And immediately after knowing, doing: **"and perform Salah for My remembrance."** Two readings — *pray in order to remember Me*, and *pray whenever you remember Me*. The second is supported by the hadith of Anas: whoever sleeps through a prayer or forgets it should pray it when he remembers, **and there is no expiation for it other than that** — with the Prophet ﷺ citing this very ayah.

On *the Hour is coming, and I am almost hiding it*: **Ibn Abbas** used to recite it as *I almost kept it hidden — from Myself*, then explain that nothing is ever hidden from Allah's Self; the meaning is that **no one knows its time except Him**.

---

## 5. "What Is That in Your Right Hand?" (20:17–21)

The question, Ibn Kathir notes, is interrogative for the purpose of **affirmation** — to fix in Musa's mind exactly what the object is before it changes: *that which is in your right hand is a stick you are familiar with. Now watch.*

Musa's answer is unhurried and domestic — he leans on it, he shakes branches down for his sheep, he finds other uses in it. **Imam Malik** gives a precise gloss on the beating of branches: a man puts his staff into a branch and shakes it so leaves and fruit fall **without breaking the stick** — it is not the same as striking. *(Ibn Kathir notes drily that some scholars took upon themselves the burden of cataloguing the "other uses.")*

Then: cast it down. It became a huge snake — **the size of a python, but with the darting speed of the smallest snake**. And then: *Grasp it and fear not; We shall return it to its former state.*

---

## 6. The Hand, and the Errand (20:22–24)

The second sign: the hand drawn to the side comes out **white as a half moon**, *without any disease* — meaning without leprosy, ailment or disfigurement (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi).

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri**: *He brought it out, and by Allah, it was as if it were a lamp. From this Musa knew that he had surely met his Lord.*

Then the errand, in five words: **Go to Fir'awn. Verily, he has transgressed.**

---

## 7. The Supplication (20:25–35)

> *O my Lord! Open for me my chest; and ease my task for me; and loosen the knot from my tongue, that they understand my speech; and appoint for me a helper from my family — Harun, my brother.*

Ibn Kathir spends real time on **why** Musa needed this, and the list is worth reading slowly. He was being sent to the mightiest king on earth, the most arrogant and severe of all people in his disbelief, with the largest army — a man who claimed to know no god at all, and gave his subjects none but himself. And Musa had **lived in his house as a child, slept in his room, on his bed**. And then killed one of his people and fled as an outlaw. Now he was to walk back in and call that man to worship Allah alone.

Hence: *I cannot perform this task if You do not help me.*

**The knot in the tongue** was a lisp, from the incident in childhood when he was offered a date and a hot coal and put the coal on his tongue. Ibn Kathir makes a fine observation here: Musa **did not ask for the affliction to be removed** — only for enough clarity that his message could be understood. Had he asked for the whole thing, it would have been cured. But **the Prophets do not ask for more than the task requires**, and so the remnant stayed with him — which is what Fir'awn later sneered at: *can scarcely express himself clearly* (43:52).

**Harun.** Ibn Abbas: Harun was made a Prophet at the very moment Musa was. And Ibn Abi Hatim records A'ishah, camped among some Bedouins on her way to Umrah, overhearing a man swear he knew which brother in all of history was of most benefit to his brother. She thought it presumptuous of him to swear such an oath. Then he said: **"It is Musa, when he asked for Prophethood to be bestowed upon his brother."** A'ishah: *By Allah, he has spoken truthfully.*

The purpose clause is the part people skip. Musa asks for a chest, a tongue and a brother — *that we may glorify You much, and remember You much.* **Mujahid**: a servant is not counted among those who remember Allah much until he remembers Him **standing, sitting and lying down**.

---

## 8. "You Are Granted Your Request" (20:36–40)

The answer comes immediately — and with it, a reminder of favours already given, before he had asked for anything.

Musa was born in a year when the male children were being killed. His mother put him in a case and cast him into the river — *and the heart of the mother of Musa became empty* (28:10). The river carried him to Fir'awn's own house.

Ibn Kathir draws out the design: they were killing infants **for fear of Musa's arrival**, and so Allah decreed that Musa would be raised nowhere except **on Fir'awn's own bed, on Fir'awn's food and drink, receiving the love of Fir'awn and his wife.**

*And I endued you with love from Me* — **"I made your enemy love you."** **Salamah bin Kuhayl**: *I made My creatures love you.*

He refused every wet nurse brought to him, because *We had already forbidden other foster suckling mothers for him* (28:12) — until his sister stepped forward with an offer, and his own mother was **hired** to nurse her own son. She got him back, and a wage, and *that she might cool her eyes and not grieve.*

Then, in one clause: *Then you did kill a man, but We saved you from great distress and tried you with a heavy trial.*

---

## 9. Chosen — and the Command Not to Slacken (20:41–44)

> *And I have chosen you for Myself. Go you and your brother with My Ayat, and do not, you both, slacken in My remembrance.*

Here Ibn Kathir places the famous hadith of **Adam and Musa** (Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah). Musa: *You are the one who made things difficult for mankind and got them evicted from Paradise.* Adam: *Are you the one whom Allah chose for His Message and selected for Himself and revealed the Tawrah to?* Musa: yes. Adam: *Then did you find it was preordained upon me before He created me?* Musa: yes. **So Adam defeated Musa's argument.** (The hadith returns later in the surah, at the Adam narrative — Ibn Kathir cites it twice, from both ends.)

*Do not slacken in My remembrance* — Ibn Abbas: **do not be slow**; and in another narration, **do not be weak**. The instruction is that they should keep remembering Allah **during the confrontation itself**, because that remembrance would be their strength against him.

And then the line Ibn Kathir calls a great lesson:

> *And speak to him mildly, perhaps he may accept admonition or fear Allah.*

**Fir'awn was the most insolent man alive. Musa was the friend of Allah among His creation at that time. And Musa was still commanded to speak gently** — with the soft, easy speech of a close friend, so that the message would sink deeper and do more. This is *invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and fair preaching* (16:125), applied to the hardest case imaginable.

---

## 10. "I Am With You Both, Hearing and Seeing" (20:45–48)

They were afraid — that Fir'awn would strike first, or transgress against them beyond what they deserved.

Ibn Kathir renders the reassurance almost as direct speech:

> *Do not fear him. I am with you, I hear your speech and his. I see your place and I see his. Nothing of your affair is hidden from Me. Know that **his forehead is in My Hand** — he does not speak, breathe, or use any force except by My leave and after My command.*

Their opening line to Fir'awn — *peace will be upon him who follows the guidance* — is the same formula the Prophet ﷺ used centuries later when writing to **Heraclius**: *Peace be upon him who follows the guidance. I invite you with the invitation of Islam; accept Islam and you will be safe, and Allah will give you a double reward.*

---

## 11. Fir'awn's Question, Musa's Answer (20:49–52)

> *"Who then, O Musa, is the Lord of you two?" — "Our Lord is He Who gave to each thing its form and nature, then guided it aright."*

The gloss is unusually concrete. **Ibn Abbas**: *He made the man a man, the donkey a donkey, the sheep a sheep.* **Sa'id bin Jubayr**: *He gave each of His creatures what is suitable for its creation.* Ibn Kathir expands: man was not given the form of a beast, nor the beast the form of a dog, nor the dog the form of a sheep; each was given a fitting mate and inclined towards it; **no species is exactly like another** in action, form, sustenance or mating.

Fir'awn's counter is a debating move: *What about the generations of old?* — meaning, if this is how things are, what about all those people who never worshipped your Lord?

Musa refuses the bait: *The knowledge thereof is with my Lord, in a Record.* **My Lord neither errs nor forgets.**

Ibn Kathir then makes a small, beautiful point. Created knowledge has exactly two defects: **it never fully encompasses anything, and it decays into forgetting.** The ayah denies both of Allah in a single breath.

---

## 12. The Earth as a Bed (20:53–56)

Musa keeps going — the reply is not finished. Your Lord is the One who made the earth a resting place, opened roads through it, sent water down and brought out *various kinds of vegetation* — some sour, some sweet, some bitter.

*Eat and pasture your cattle* — food for you, fodder for them, green and dry.

Then the three-verb summary of a human life: **Thereof We created you** (your father Adam, from the surface of the earth), **and into it We shall return you** (you will become dirt when you decay), **and from it We shall bring you out once again.**

And the verdict on Fir'awn: *We showed him all Our Ayat, but he denied and refused* — the proofs were established, he saw them with his own eyes, and rejected them anyway. *They belied them wrongfully and arrogantly, though they themselves were convinced thereof* (27:14).

---

## 13. The Contest Is Set (20:57–59)

Fir'awn's accusation — *have you come to drive us out of our land with your magic?* — and his counter-offer: we have magic too, name a place and time.

Musa names **the day of the festival**, in **the forenoon**, with the people assembled.

Ibn Kathir explains the choice as a matter of principle. That day was their New Year celebration, a public holiday when everyone left work and gathered — Ibn Abbas says it was their day of Ashura'; As-Suddi, Qatadah and Ibn Zayd call it their great celebration; Sa'id bin Jubayr, their great bazaar. *(Ibn Kathir: these are not contradictory. And he adds — Allah destroyed Fir'awn and his armies on a day like this one.)*

And the time was chosen for maximum visibility: full light, plain view, nothing hidden.

> **This is the way of the Prophets. Their work is always clear and apparent. It is never something hidden, or something for sale.**

Hence not at night. And **Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd bin Aslam** notes the venue was a flat place where all were on the same level with an equal view, nothing obstructing, so that no one could see what another did not.

---

## 14. The Warning Before the Throw (20:60–64)

Fir'awn summoned every magician in the kingdom — *bring me every well-versed sorcerer* (10:79) — and the trade was widespread and in demand at the time. On the day, he sat on his throne surrounded by his officials, the crowd standing to his right and left, prodding and inspiring his magicians, who bargained for their reward: *will there surely be a reward for us if we are the winners?*

Then Musa, leaning on his stick, with his brother beside him, said something to the magicians before anything was thrown:

> *Woe unto you! Invent not a lie against Allah, lest He should destroy you completely by a torment. And surely, he who invents a lie will fail miserably.*

**And it worked.** *Then they debated one with another what they must do, and they kept their talk secret.* One of them said: *this is not the speech of a magician, this is the speech of a Prophet.* Another said: no, he is only a magician.

Their public line — *these two want to drive you out of your land and take you away from your exemplary way* — is decoded by **Ibn Abbas** as concern for their **kingdom and their livelihood**. Their status and wealth came from this craft. What they were really saying to each other was: *if these two win, we lose everything.*

---

## 15. Morning Magicians, Evening Believers (20:65–70)

They offered Musa the first throw; he told them to go first — *so that we can see what magic you are going to perform, and the true state of their affair will become obvious to the people.*

They were many, and each threw a stick and a rope, **until the valley filled with snakes piled on top of each other**. *They bewitched the eyes of the people and struck terror into them* (7:116).

*So Musa conceived fear in himself* — and Ibn Kathir specifies the fear: **not for himself, but that the people would be deceived before he got the chance to throw.**

Then: *Fear not! Surely, you will have the upper hand. And throw that which is in your right hand.* The stick became a huge creature with legs, a neck, a head and fangs, and went through the ropes and sticks until **not one of them was left**, in broad daylight, in front of everybody.

And here is why the magicians, specifically, were the ones who broke. They were **the experts**. They knew every trick and method in the science. So when they saw it, they knew with certainty that this was not magic and not illusion — that **no one could do this except One who says to a thing "Be," and it is.**

> *So the magicians fell down prostrate. They said: "We believe in the Lord of Harun and Musa."*

- **Ibn Abbas and Ubayd bin Umayr**: *At the beginning of the day they were magicians, and at the end of the day they were outstanding witnesses of faith.*
- **Ibn Abbas** on the number: **seventy men.**
- **Al-Awza'i**: when they fell into prostration, **Paradise was raised up before them until they were looking at it**.
- **Sa'id bin Jubayr**, and likewise Ikrimah and Al-Qasim bin Abi Bizzah: *they saw their places in Paradise made clear before them while they were still in prostration.*

---

## 16. Fir'awn Turns on Them (20:71–73)

Beaten in public, by his own hired experts, Fir'awn does what the defeated do: he accuses.

*Verily, he is your chief who has taught you magic* — a charge which, Ibn Kathir says, **he and the magicians and every creature present knew was an outright lie.**

Then the threat: hands and feet on opposite sides, crucifixion on the trunks of date palms. **Ibn Abbas notes that Fir'awn was the first person ever to do this.**

Their reply is one of the great speeches of the Qur'an:

> *We prefer you not over what has come to us of the clear signs, and over Him Who created us. So decree whatever you desire to decree — **for you can only decree regarding this life of the world.***

Meaning, Ibn Kathir says: *you have power only in a world that is coming to an end; we are hoping in the eternal abode.*

They ask forgiveness specifically for **the magic you compelled us to do**. And **Ibn Abbas** supplies the background that makes "compelled" literal: Fir'awn had taken **forty boys from the Children of Israel** and had them taught magic at Al-Farama — *teach them knowledge no one in the land knows.* Those boys grew up to be these magicians. They had been raised as an instrument against Musa, and turned in his favour in a single morning.

Ibn Kathir's closing note: it is apparent that Fir'awn carried out the executions — **and that what he did to them was a mercy from Allah for them.**

---

## 17. The Magicians Preach (20:74–76)

Their last words are not defiance but *da'wah* — they turn and warn the king.

> *Whoever comes to his Lord as a criminal, then surely for him is Hell, wherein **he will neither die nor live**.*

Ibn Kathir gathers the parallels — *neither will it have a complete killing effect on them so that they die, nor shall its torment be lightened* (35:36); *O Malik! Let your Lord make an end of us* — *Verily, you shall abide forever* (43:77).

And then the hadith from **Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri** (Muslim), which distinguishes two populations: those who deserve the Fire will not die and not live in it. **But others** will be punished for their sins until they are burnt coals — then intercession is permitted, they are brought out group by group and spread on the rivers of Paradise, and the people of Paradise are told to pour water over them, and **they grow back like seeds on the muddy banks of a flowing river.**

For the believer: *high ranks.* **Paradise has one hundred levels, and between each is the distance between the sky and the earth; Al-Firdaws is the highest, from it spring the four rivers, and the Throne is above it — so when you ask Allah, ask Him for Al-Firdaws.**

And *the reward of those who purify themselves* — meaning purity from filth **and from associating partners with Allah**.

---

## 18. The Sea (20:77–79)

Egypt woke to find not one of them left. Fir'awn sent callers to every city and gathered his armies — *these indeed are but a small band, and they have done what has enraged us* (26:54–55).

Musa stopped with the sea in front and Fir'awn behind, and his people said *we are sure to be overtaken*. Then the command came: **strike a dry path for them in the sea.**

Ibn Kathir preserves the detail: Musa struck it and said, *Split for me, by the leave of Allah.* Each part of the water stood like a huge mountain. Then **Allah sent a wind over the seabed until the soil dried like ground on land** — hence *a dry path*, and hence the double reassurance: don't fear being caught, and **don't fear the sea drowning your people.**

Then: *the sea completely overwhelmed them and covered them up.* And the epitaph — *Fir'awn led his people astray, and he did not guide them* — which Ibn Kathir reads forward: as he went ahead of them into the sea, **so he will go ahead of them on the Day of Resurrection, leading them into the Fire.**

---

## 19. The Terms of Forgiveness (20:80–82)

The address turns to the Children of Israel: saved from your enemy, given the covenant at the right side of the Mount, fed manna and quail — a sweet substance falling from the sky and birds they could take until every pot was full.

**Al-Bukhari, from Ibn Abbas**: arriving in Al-Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ found the Jews fasting Ashura'. They told him it was the day Allah gave Musa victory over Fir'awn. He said: **"We have more right to Musa than them — so fast it."**

*Eat of the good things We have provided, and commit no transgression therein, lest My anger justly descend on you.*

Then the promise, and its four conditions in strict order:

> *And verily, I am indeed forgiving to him who **repents**, **believes**, **does righteous deeds**, and then **Ihtada** (is guided).*

- **repents** — turns away from disbelief, shirk, disobedience, hypocrisy
- **believes** — in the heart
- **does righteous deeds** — with the limbs
- **and then is guided** — **Ibn Abbas**: *he does not then doubt.* **Qatadah**: *he adheres to Islam until he dies.*

Ibn Kathir stresses the ordering is deliberate. And he notes the mercy's reach: **Allah even accepted the repentance of the ones who worshipped the calf.**

---

## 20. The Calf (20:83–89)

Musa hurried ahead to the appointment — thirty nights, extended to forty, fasted day and night — leaving Harun in charge. *And what made you hasten from your people, O Musa?* — *I hastened to You, O my Lord, that You might be pleased.*

The answer he received is one of the coldest lines in the Qur'an: **We have tried your people in your absence, and As-Samiri has led them astray.**

He came back *in a state of anger and sorrow* — the word is **Asif**, which Ibn Kathir says is used here to emphasise the severity of the anger. Mujahid: *worried.* Qatadah and As-Suddi: *sad, because of what his people had done after him.*

Their excuse is the detail worth keeping. They said they were only trying to **get rid of borrowed Coptic jewellery** — so they threw it away. And out of it came the calf.

**Ibn Abbas**: *they became religiously devoted to it, and loved it with a love they had never loved anything else with.* And on its famous sound: *by Allah, the moaning of the calf was nothing but wind entering its behind and coming out of its mouth.* **Al-Hasan Al-Basri** preserves that the calf even had a name: **Bahmut**.

Ibn Kathir's summary of the whole affair: **they set out to rid themselves of something detestable, and ended up doing something far worse.**

He then attaches a parallel that lands hard. A man from Iraq asked **Ibn Umar** whether mosquito blood on a garment invalidates the prayer. Ibn Umar's reply:

> *"Look at the people of Iraq. They killed the grandson of the Messenger of Allah, Al-Husayn — and they're asking about the blood of the mosquito."*

Then the demolition, in one question: *Did they not see that it could not return them a word, and had no power to harm them or benefit them?*

---

## 21. Harun's Defence (20:90–94)

Harun had warned them: *O my people! You are being tried in this. Your Lord is the Most Gracious — so follow me and obey my order.* They answered: **we will not stop worshipping it until Musa returns.** Ibn Kathir notes they opposed him, fought him, and **nearly killed him.**

Musa, returning, threw down the Tablets and seized his brother by the head. Ibn Kathir refers back to Al-A'raf and the hadith he cites there: **information is not the same as observation.** Musa had been *told* what happened. Seeing it was different.

Musa's charge is not that Harun failed to stop them — it is that he failed to **come and report**: *What prevented you, when you saw them going astray, that you followed me not?*

Harun's reply begins **"O son of my mother"** — and Ibn Kathir points out this is deliberate. They shared both parents; invoking the mother is *more delicate and profound in bringing about gentleness and mildness.* He feared that if he had left to find Musa, he would be accused of **causing a division among the Children of Israel**.

**Ibn Abbas**: *Harun was respectful and obedient to Musa.*

---

## 22. As-Samiri (20:95–98)

*And what is the matter with you, O Samiri?*

**Ibn Abbas** (via Muhammad bin Ishaq) gives the man a biography: he was from **Bajarma, a people who worshipped cows**, and **the love of cow-worship was still in his soul**. He had gone along with Islam among the Children of Israel. His name was Musa bin Zafar. *(Qatadah: he was from the village of Samarra.)*

His own account: *I saw what they saw not, so I took a handful from the print of the messenger and threw it. Thus my inner self suggested to me.* On the majority view — Mujahid among them — this is **a handful of dust from the hoofprint of Jibril's horse**, which he cast onto the jewellery as it went into the fire.

*Thus my inner self suggested to me* — **his soul found it pleasing and thought it a good idea.** That is the whole mechanism.

The sentence passed on him is exact retribution: *your punishment in this life is that you will say, **"Touch me not."*** Ibn Kathir: just as you took and touched what was not yours to touch, so you will live untouched and untouchable — *you will not touch people, and they will not touch you.* **Qatadah** adds that those afflicted with this condition in his own day still said *do not touch.* And beyond that, *you have a promise that will not fail* — the Day of Resurrection, with **no way to escape it**.

Then the calf is burned and its particles scattered in the sea, and Musa states the alternative in a single line: **Your God is only Allah, there is no God but Him. He has full knowledge of all things.**

---

## 23. The Reminder, and the Burden of Turning Away (20:99–101)

The story ends and the address returns to Muhammad ﷺ: *Thus We relate to you some information of what happened before* — related **exactly as it happened, without any increase or decrease.**

And then Ibn Kathir's assessment of the Book itself: **no Prophet was given a Book like it or more complete than it**, and none containing as much information about what has passed and what is to come.

*Whoever turns away from it will bear a heavy burden on the Day of Resurrection* — *burden* here means **sin**. And the warning is universal: *that I may warn you thereby, and whomsoever it reaches* (6:19) — **Arabs, non-Arabs, People of the Book and others alike.**

---

## 24. The Horn, and the Shortness of the World (20:102–104)

*The Day when the Sur will be blown.* Asked what the Sur was, the Prophet ﷺ said: **it is a horn that will be blown into** — and in the narration from Abu Hurayrah, a horn whose circumference is as large as the heavens and the earth, to be blown by Israfil.

And the hadith of the waiting angel:

> *How can I be comfortable when the one with the horn is holding it in his lips and his forehead is leaning forward, waiting to be given permission?*
> *"What should we say, O Messenger of Allah?"*
> **"Say: Allah is sufficient for us and what a good protector He is. Upon Allah we place our trust."**

The criminals are gathered **blue-eyed** — said to be from the sheer horror of what they see. And they whisper to each other (**Ibn Abbas**: *whispering among themselves*) that they stayed in the world **ten days**; the most intelligent among them says: **a single day.**

Ibn Kathir explains why. On that Day they will *feel* the shortness of the worldly life — a life of repeating nights and days and hours compressed into what seems like one day — and they are saying it **as a defence**, to argue they were not given enough time for the case against them to be established. The Qur'an answers directly: *Did We not give you lives long enough, so that whosoever would receive admonition could receive it? And the warner came to you* (35:37).

The real lesson: *if you had only known, you would have preferred the eternal life over the temporal one.*

---

## 25. When the Landscape Goes Flat (20:105–108)

They asked what happens to the mountains. *My Lord will blast them and scatter them as particles of dust.*

**Then He shall leave it as a level smooth plain** — *Qa'* is level ground, and *Safsafa* intensifies it. You will see in it **no valley, no hill, nothing low or elevated** (Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah).

And on that flat earth, *mankind will follow strictly the voice of Allah's caller, no crookedness will they show him* — they will rush wherever they are called, with no deviation left or right. Ibn Kathir's comment is quietly devastating:

> **If they had been like this in the worldly life, it would have been more beneficial for them. Here, it does not benefit them.**

*And all voices will be humbled for the Most Gracious* — **Ibn Abbas: they will be silent.** *And nothing shall you hear except Hamsa* — **the sound of footsteps** (Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Ar-Rabi', Qatadah, Ibn Zayd); in another narration from Ibn Abbas, **a hidden voice**; Sa'id bin Jubayr combines both: *secret speech, and the steps of feet.*

---

## 26. Intercession, and the Two Verdicts (20:109–112)

*No intercession shall avail, except from the one whom the Most Gracious has permitted and whose word is acceptable to Him.* Ibn Kathir stacks the parallel verses — 2:255, 21:28, 34:23, 78:38 — all making permission the precondition.

Then the hadith of the intercession itself, from the Two Sahihs:

> *I will come under the Throne and fall into prostration. I will be inspired to make praises of Allah that I cannot recall now. Then He will say: "O Muhammad, raise your head. Speak and you will be heard; intercede and your intercession will be accepted."*

A group is designated, they are entered into Paradise, and he returns to do it again — **four times**. And in another hadith, the threshold descends step by step: bring out whoever has a seed's weight of faith in his heart; then half a seed's weight; then a speck of dust; then **the smallest and tiniest particle**.

Two verdicts close the passage:

- **The wrongdoer is a complete failure.** Every right will be restored to whoever was owed it — *even the hornless ram will be given revenge against the one who had horns.* And: **beware of wrongdoing, for wrongdoing will be darknesses on the Day of Resurrection.** The greatest failure is to meet Allah having associated partners with Him — *verily, shirk is the great wrongdoing* (31:13).
- **The believer who works righteousness fears neither injustice nor curtailment** — meaning **his evils will not be increased, and his good deeds will not be decreased.** *Zulm* is having others' sins added to you; *Hadm* is having your own good taken away. Neither happens.

---

## 27. "My Lord, Increase Me in Knowledge" (20:113–114)

The Qur'an was sent down **in Arabic**, with its warnings explained in detail, for two outcomes: *that they may have Taqwa* (leaving sins and abominations) *or that it may cause them to have a lesson from it* (producing acts of obedience that bring one nearer).

Then, *High above all be Allah, the True King* — Ibn Kathir unfolds it: **He is Himself the Truth; His promise is true, His threat is true, His Messengers are true, Paradise is true, the Fire is true.** And His justice is that **He does not punish anyone before warning them**, sending Messengers, and removing every excuse.

Then the instruction that gives this section its weight. *Be not in haste with the Qur'an before its revelation is completed to you.* From Ibn Abbas: the Prophet ﷺ **used to go through great pains to retain the revelation**, moving his tongue rapidly along with Jibril out of eagerness not to lose a word. He was relieved of that: *It is for Us to collect it and to give you the ability to recite it* (75:16–19). Listen quietly; **We will gather it in your chest**; then recite it after him.

And in its place, one thing to ask for:

> ***My Lord! Increase me in knowledge.***

**Ibn Uyaynah**: *The Prophet ﷺ did not cease increasing in knowledge until Allah took him.*

---

## 28. Adam Forgot (20:115–122)

The surah's hinge. *And indeed We made a covenant with Adam before, but he forgot, and We found on his part no firm willpower.*

**Ibn Abbas** offers the etymology that unlocks the whole surah: **man was called *insan* only because he was given a covenant and forgot it — *nasiya*.** (Mujahid and Al-Hasan: *forgot* here means *abandoned*.)

Then the Garden, and the four guarantees — and Ibn Kathir notices they are two pairs of opposites:

| Guarantee | What it removes |
|---|---|
| *You will never be hungry therein* | **internal humiliation** |
| *nor naked* | **external humiliation** |
| *You will not suffer from thirst* | **internal heat** |
| *nor from the sun's heat* | **external heat** |

Shaytan's offer is precisely calibrated: **the Tree of Eternity, and a kingdom that will never waste away** — the two things that a life of ease still cannot buy. And he swore by Allah that he was sincere.

They ate, and their private parts became manifest, and they began patching the leaves of the Garden onto themselves like a garment (Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi). Ibn Kathir includes a narration from Ubayy bin Ka'b describing Adam as tall and thickly haired, **like a clothed palm tree**, whose covering fell away at the first taste — but flags honestly that **the chain is broken between Al-Hasan and Ubayy, and it is questionable whether it can correctly be attributed to the Prophet ﷺ.**

Then: *Thus Adam disobeyed his Lord, so he went astray.* **Then his Lord chose him, and turned to him with forgiveness, and gave him guidance.**

That "then" is the point. And it is where the Adam–Musa hadith returns — *are you blaming me for a matter Allah wrote upon me before He created me?* — **thus Adam defeated Musa.**

---

## 29. The Constricted Life (20:123–127)

> *Whoever follows My guidance shall neither go astray nor be distressed. But whoever turns away from My Reminder — verily, for him is a life of hardship, and We shall raise him up blind on the Day of Resurrection.*

**Ibn Abbas** on the first half: *he will not be misguided in this life, and he will not be distressed in the Hereafter.*

The second half gets Ibn Kathir's most psychologically acute passage in the surah. *A life of hardship* does not mean poverty:

> *Even if he appears to be in comfort outwardly, wears whatever he likes, eats whatever he likes and lives wherever he wants — **he will not be happy.** His heart will not have pure certainty and guidance. He will be in agitation, bewilderment and doubt, always in confusion and uncertainty. This is from the hardship of life.*

*Raised up blind* — **Mujahid, Abu Salih and As-Suddi**: he will have **no proof**. **Ikrimah**: he will be blind to everything except Hell.

And the reply to his protest — *why have You raised me blind, while I had sight?* — is exact reciprocity: *Our Ayat came to you and you disregarded them; so this Day you will be neglected.* **The punishment is built out of the crime.** *So this Day We shall forget them as they forgot their meeting of this Day* (7:51).

Ibn Kathir adds an important qualification: **forgetting the wording of the Qur'an while understanding its meaning and acting on it is not what this threat is about** — though that has its own separate and serious warning in the Sunnah.

---

## 30. The Ruins They Walk Through, and the Five Prayers (20:128–130)

*Is it not a guidance for them to know how many generations We have destroyed before them, in whose dwellings they walk?*

The evidence is the **empty houses** — the nations that showed open hostility to the Messengers, of whom no trace is left, whose homes have now been inherited and are being walked around in by the very people repeating their mistake. *It is not the eyes that grow blind, but the hearts which are in the breasts* (22:46).

That they have not already been destroyed is only because of *a Word that went forth before from your Lord, and a term determined* — **He does not punish until the proof is established, and the time is already fixed.**

Then the consolation, and the practical instruction that follows it: *So bear patiently what they say, and glorify the praises of your Lord* —

| Time named | Prayer |
|---|---|
| *before the rising of the sun* | **Fajr** |
| *before its setting* | **'Asr** |
| *during some hours of the night* | **Tahajjud** (some scholars: Maghrib and 'Isha) |
| *at the ends of the day* | the day's counterpart to the night hours |

On the first two, the hadith of **Jarir bin Abdullah Al-Bajali** (Two Sahihs), told while looking at a full moon: *you will see your Lord just as you see this moon, and you will not have to crowd together to see Him. **Therefore, if you are able not to miss a prayer before sunrise and before sunset, then do so*** — and then the Prophet ﷺ recited this very ayah. And from 'Umarah bin Ru'aybah (Muslim): **whoever prays before sunrise and before sunset will never enter the Hellfire.**

*That you may become pleased* — and Ibn Kathir takes that word all the way to its destination. In the Sahih: Allah will call the people of Paradise and ask if they are pleased. They will say, *why should we not be, when You have given us what You have given no one else of Your creation?* He will say, **I am going to give you something better than that.** They will ask what could be better. He will say: **I have allowed for you My pleasure, so I will never be angry with you after this.**

And in the other narration: the veil is lifted, and they gaze upon Him. **By Allah, He has not given them anything better than the opportunity to look upon Him — and that is the increase.**

---

## 31. Don't Strain Your Eyes (20:131–132)

> *And strain not your eyes in longing for the things We have given for enjoyment to various groups of them — the splendour of the life of this world, that We may test them thereby. But the provision of your Lord is better and more lasting.*

**Mujahid**: *various groups of them* means **the wealthy**.

Ibn Kathir's paraphrase: *do not look at what these people of luxury have. It is short-lived splendour and a feeble bounty, which We are using to test them — and very few of My servants are truly thankful.*

Then the scene from the Sahih, which is the ayah in flesh. **'Umar** entered the room where the Prophet ﷺ had withdrawn from his wives. He was lying on a sandy straw mat; there was nothing in the room but a pile of sant tree pods and some hanging equipment. 'Umar's eyes filled with tears.

> *"What makes you cry, O 'Umar?"*
> *"O Messenger of Allah — Kisra and Caesar are living in their luxury, and you are the chosen Friend of Allah among His creation."*
> **"Do you have doubt, O son of Al-Khattab? Those people have had their good hastened for them in the life of this world."**

And from Abu Sa'id: **the thing I fear most for you all is what Allah will allow you to acquire of the splendour of this world** — *the blessings of the earth.*

Then the turn inward, from other people's houses to your own: ***And enjoin Salah on your family, and be patient in offering it.*** This is *ward off yourselves and your families against a Fire* (66:6) in practice. Zayd bin Aslam's father used to stay the night at **'Umar's** house; 'Umar had a set hour for night prayer, and sometimes did not rise for it — but when he did rise, **he woke his whole family**, reciting this ayah.

And the promise attached: ***We ask not of you a provision: We provide for you.*** — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, that if you establish the prayer, **your sustenance will come from where you did not expect** (65:2–3).

Two hadiths seal it:
- *O son of Adam, perform My worship and I will fill your chest with wealth and fulfil your needs. If you do not, I will fill your chest with toil and I will not fulfil your needs.*
- **Whoever makes the worldly life his major concern**, Allah will scatter his affairs and place his poverty between his eyes, and he will get from the world only what was already written for him. **Whoever makes the Hereafter his intention**, his affairs will be gathered for him, his wealth placed in his heart — **and the world will come to him anyway.**

---

## 32. The Sign They Asked For (20:133–135)

*Why does he not bring us a sign from his Lord?*

The answer is that they are holding it. *Has there not come to them the proof of what is in the former Scriptures?* — meaning **the Qur'an**, revealed to an unlettered man who could not write and never studied with the People of the Book, yet containing the histories of vanished nations in agreement with what is authentic in the earlier Books, **acting as supervisor over them**: confirming what is correct and exposing what was falsely inserted.

From the Two Sahihs:

> *There was not any Prophet except that he was given signs that caused men to believe. That which I have been given is a revelation that Allah has revealed to me — so I hope that I have the most followers among them on the Day of Resurrection.*

Ibn Kathir notes he named only **the greatest** of his signs there; the others were innumerable.

And the demand is exposed as insincere anyway. Had they been destroyed before a Messenger came, they would have pleaded *if only You had sent us a Messenger, we would have followed Your Ayat before we were humiliated.* But now one has come, and *even if every sign should come to them, until they see the painful torment* (10:97), they will not believe.

So the surah closes not with an argument but with a wager:

> *Say: "Each one is waiting — so wait you too. And you shall know who are they that are on the straight road, and who are they that have let themselves be guided."*

---

## The Thread

Ta Ha is a surah about **forgetting, and being remembered anyway**.

It says so at its centre, in one word: *insan* — the one who was given a covenant and forgot it. Everybody in this surah forgets. Adam forgets and loses the Garden. The Children of Israel forget a sea that parted for them in living memory and, within forty nights, are devoted to a hollow statue that cannot answer when spoken to. As-Samiri forgets, which is to say he **abandons** — and Ibn Kathir's diagnosis of him is chilling in its ordinariness: *thus my inner self suggested to me.* His soul thought it was a good idea. On the Day of Resurrection the disbelievers will forget the length of their own lives and swear the whole thing took a day.

And the punishment for turning away is written in the same grammar: **you disregarded, so you will be disregarded; you forgot, so you will be forgotten.**

Against all that forgetting, the surah sets a series of things that are not forgotten. A Lord who knows the deed you have not yet done. A Record in which *my Lord neither errs nor forgets*. A man walking the wrong road on a freezing night who is called by his own name. Forty boys taken from their families and trained in sorcery as a weapon, who turn in a single morning and tell a king to his face that his decrees stop at the edge of this world. A mother paid a wage to nurse her own son.

And so the instructions the Prophet ﷺ is given are all small, repeatable, and about attention rather than victory: **speak mildly** even to Fir'awn. **Don't rush the revelation** — listen, and ask for more knowledge. **Pray before sunrise and before sunset.** **Wake your family.** **Don't stare at what the wealthy have.** Be patient with what they say.

Because the surah's opening premise was never in doubt. It was not sent down to cause you distress.

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Ta Ha, and all praise and gratitude is due to Allah.*
