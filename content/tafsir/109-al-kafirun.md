*Revealed in Makkah · 6 ayat · "The Disbelievers" · equivalent to a fourth of the Qur'an*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ibn Kathir gives this surah a single, unmistakable title: **the Surah of *Bara'ah*** — disavowal, renunciation, the formal declaration of innocence from the deeds of the idolaters. Not a debate with them. Not an invitation. A separation.

It was revealed in answer to an offer. The Quraysh, in their ignorance, proposed a deal to the Messenger ﷺ: **worship our idols for a year, and we will worship your God for a year.** It is a reasonable offer by the standards of ordinary politics — everyone concedes something, everyone keeps face, the pressure ends. Allah's response was not a counter-offer. It was six verses instructing His Messenger to disavow their religion *completely*.

The surah's method is repetition, and the repetition is not decorative. Ibn Kathir reads the two pairs of verses as covering the two things that make worship valid: **whom you worship**, and **how you worship**. The idolaters fail on both counts, and so the disavowal has to be stated twice.

Then the last verse draws the line and leaves it there: *To you be your religion, and to me my religion.*

---

## 1. The Surah the Prophet ﷺ Recited Constantly

Before any commentary, Ibn Kathir stacks up the narrations — and the striking thing is how *ordinary* and how *frequent* these occasions were. Al-Kafirun, paired always with Al-Ikhlas, was part of the daily furniture of the Prophet's ﷺ worship:

- **Sahih Muslim, from Jabir** — he recited Al-Kafirun and Al-Ikhlas in the two rak'ahs of **Tawaf**.
- **Sahih Muslim, from Abu Hurayrah** — he recited these two surahs in the two (optional) rak'ahs of the **Morning prayer**.
- **Imam Ahmad, from Ibn Umar** — in the two rak'ahs before Fajr and the two after Maghrib, "on ten or twenty different occasions."
- **Ibn Umar again** — *"I watched the Prophet twenty-four or twenty-five times"* reciting them in exactly those two places.
- **Ibn Umar again** — *"I watched the Prophet for a month"* and he would recite them there. Also recorded by At-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah and An-Nasa'i; At-Tirmidhi graded it **Hasan**.

And the weight: it has already been mentioned that **Al-Kafirun is equivalent to a fourth of the Qur'an**, as Az-Zalzalah is.

There is a quiet argument in this list. The two surahs the Prophet ﷺ chose to open and close his day with are the surah of **pure disavowal from false worship** and the surah of **pure affirmation of Allah's oneness**. Tawhid stated positively, and tawhid stated negatively — recited together, daily, at the hinges of the day.

---

## 2. "Say: O disbelievers!" (109:1)

> *Qul ya ayyuhal-kafirun*

Ibn Kathir's ruling on scope is precise, and it has two halves that must be held together:

- **It includes every disbeliever on the face of the earth** — the address is general.
- **But it is particularly directed towards the disbelievers of the Quraysh** — the specific men who made the offer.

Note also the first word: **Qul — Say.** The declaration is not the Prophet's ﷺ own initiative or temperament. He is commanded to it. Disavowal here is obedience, not hostility.

---

## 3. The First Pair: *Whom* You Worship (109:2–3)

> *I worship not that which you worship. Nor will you worship whom I worship.*

- **"That which you worship"** — Ibn Kathir: **statues and rival gods**.
- **"Whom I worship"** — **He is Allah Alone, Who has no partner.**

A small but telling grammatical note: in verse 3 the word ***Ma*** ("what") **means *Man*** ("who"). The idols are referred to with the impersonal *what*; the One the Prophet ﷺ worships is referred to with the personal *who*, even where the Arabic uses the same particle. The objects of the two worships are not two members of the same category. One is a *thing*; One is not.

---

## 4. The Second Pair: *How* You Worship (109:4–5)

> *And I shall not worship that which you are worshipping. Nor will you worship that which I worship.*

This is where Ibn Kathir explains why the statement is made a second time. The meaning shifts from object to **method**:

> *'I do not worship according to your worship, which means that I do not go along with it or follow it. I only worship Allah in the manner in which He loves and is pleased with.'*

And the reciprocal half:

> *'You do not follow the commands of Allah and His Legislation in His worship. Rather, you have invented something out of the promptings of your own souls.'*

That last phrase is the heart of the surah's critique. The idolaters' problem is not merely that they picked the wrong object — it is that the **source** of their religion is themselves. Ibn Kathir seals it with a verse:

> *They follow but a guess and that which they themselves desire, whereas there has surely come to them the guidance from their Lord!* **(53:23)**

Guesswork and desire on one side; guidance that has actually arrived on the other.

---

## 5. Why the Disavowal Has to Be Total

Ibn Kathir then states the principle underneath the whole surah:

> *For certainly the worshipper must have a god whom he worships and set acts of worship that he follows to get to him.*

Every human being has both — an object and a route. There is no neutral position. So the disavowal cannot be partial: **the Messenger and his followers worship Allah according to what He has legislated**, and the idolaters worship other than Allah with acts Allah never permitted. Both variables differ. There is nothing left to share.

This is precisely why, Ibn Kathir says, **the statement of Islam has two halves**:

> *"There is no God worthy of being worshipped except Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah."*

Which means: **there is no true object of worship except Allah** (the first pair of verses), **and there is no path to Him other than that which the Messenger came with** (the second pair). The shahadah and the surah are built to the same design.

---

## 6. "To you be your religion, and to me my religion" (109:6)

The closing line is not a shrug of indifference — it is the settlement that follows a completed separation. Ibn Kathir places it alongside two parallels:

- *And if they belie you, say: "For me are my deeds and for you are your deeds! You are innocent of what I do, and I am innocent of what you do!"* **(10:41)**
- *To us our deeds, and to you your deeds.* **(28:55)**

And **Al-Bukhari's** gloss removes any ambiguity about what the two "religions" are:

> *"To you be your religion" means **disbelief**. "And to me my religion" means **Islam**.*

The verse acknowledges a boundary; it does not pretend the two sides of it are equal.

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- The surah exists because someone proposed a **compromise in worship** — and the answer to that proposal is six verses of refusal. Some things cannot be met halfway without ceasing to be themselves.
- Valid worship requires **two** things right: the correct **object** (Allah alone, no partner) and the correct **method** (what He legislated, brought by His Messenger). This is why the shahadah has two halves.
- The idolaters' deepest error is naming the **source**: they *invented something out of the promptings of their own souls*. Sincerity of feeling does not sanctify a practice Allah never authorised.
- **"Qul"** — the disavowal is commanded, not chosen. It is an act of obedience, not of contempt.
- The Prophet ﷺ recited this with Al-Ikhlas at dawn, at sunset, and while circling the Ka'bah — pairing the **denial of everything false** with the **affirmation of the One**. Between them, a fourth of the Qur'an.
