*Revealed in Makkah · 40 ayat · "The News" — the great news they were arguing about, at 78:2*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

An-Naba' opens by **overhearing a conversation**. The idolaters of Makkah are asking each other about something, and the surah cuts in mid-question: *What are they asking about?* It does not immediately name the subject — it names the **size** of it. *The great news.* Ibn Kathir glosses that as the dreadful, horrifying, overwhelming information: the Day of Judgement.

What follows is one of the most economical arguments in the Qur'an. Rather than describe the Resurrection first, the surah **changes the subject to the world you are standing in** — the earth under you, the mountains fixing it, the pairing that produced you, the sleep that stops you, the night that hides you, the day that sends you out to earn, the seven heavens above you, the sun, the rain-heavy cloud, the grain and the tangled gardens. Eleven verses of ordinary, verifiable, daily facts. Then it returns: *Verily, the Day of Decision is a fixed time.*

The logic is never stated as a syllogism, but it is unmistakable. **The One who did all of that is not stretched by doing this.** Ibn Kathir frames the whole passage as Allah explaining "His great ability to create strange things and amazing matters... as a proof of His ability to do whatever He wishes concerning the matter of the Hereafter."

From there the surah splits, as short Makkan surahs do, into **the ambush and the success** — Hell described as something that lies in wait, then Paradise described as a garden, a cup, and the absence of stupid talk. And it closes on the quietest and most terrible image in the surah: a man who, seeing what is written about him, wishes he had been an animal, so that he could have been turned back into dust.

## 1. The Question, and the Two Camps (78:1–5)

> *What are they asking about? About the great news, about which they are in disagreement.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as **rejection of the idolaters' questioning** — they were asking about the Day of Judgement because they denied it could happen. *An-Naba' al-'Azim*, the great news, is that Day: the dreadful, horrifying, overwhelming information.

On *about which they are in disagreement*: **the people are divided into two ideas about it.** Those who believe in it, and those who disbelieve in it. There is no third camp.

> *Nay, they will come to know! Nay, again, they will come to know!*

Ibn Kathir's comment is four words long and needs no more: **"This is a severe threat and a direct warning."**

## 2. The Argument from the World You Live In (78:6–16)

This is the heart of the surah, and Ibn Kathir treats it as a single sustained proof. Each item is something the listener already accepts, and each one is a thing *Allah did*.

| Ayah | Ibn Kathir's gloss |
|---|---|
| **The earth as a bed** | An established, firm and peaceful resting place, **subservient to them** |
| **The mountains as pegs** | Pegs for the earth — to hold it in place, make it stable and firm, so it is fit for dwelling and does **not quake with those who are in it** |
| **Created you in pairs** | Male and female, each enjoying the other, and by this means procreation is achieved — as at 30:21, *He has put between you affection and mercy* |
| **Your sleep as rest** | A **cessation of movement**, so that you recover from the frequent repetition and going about in search of livelihood |
| **The night as a covering** | Its shade and darkness covers the people — as at 91:4. Qatadah: **a tranquil residence** |
| **The day for livelihood** | Made radiant, luminous and shining, so people can move about in it — for earning, business dealings and the rest |
| **Seven strong above you** | The seven heavens in their vastness, loftiness, perfection and precision, **adorned with both stable and moving stars** |
| **A shining lamp** | The radiant sun, whose light glows for all the people of the earth |

**The rain, and a nice piece of philology (78:14).** *We sent down from the Mu'sirat water Thajjaj.*

- **Mu'sirat** = the clouds. Reported from **Ibn Abbas** by Ali bin Abi Talhah, and held also by Ikrimah, Abu Al-'Aliyah, Ad-Dahhak, Al-Hasan, Ar-Rabi' bin Anas and Ath-Thawri — **preferred by Ibn Jarir**.
- **Al-Farra'** adds a precision: they are *the clouds that are filled with rain but do not yet bring rain* — the way a woman is called *mu'sir* when her cycle approaches but has not begun. The cloud is pregnant, not yet delivered. Compare 30:48, where the winds raise the clouds, spread them, then break them into fragments until the drops come forth.
- **Thajjaj**: Mujahid, Qatadah and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas — *poured out*. Ath-Thawri — *continuous*. Ibn Zayd — *abundant*. Ibn Kathir then produces the evidence from hadith: the woman with prolonged bleeding, told by the Prophet ﷺ to use an absorbent cloth, who replied that it was too much for that — *it flows profusely (thajja)*. Which fixes the word as **abundant, continuous, flowing**.

**And what the water is for (78:15–16).** So that "great abundance, goodness, benefit, and blessing" may be brought out:

- **Habb** — grain, meaning that which is stored and preserved for humans and cattle.
- **Nabat** — vegetation, meaning **vegetables eaten fresh**.
- **Jannat Alfaf** — gardens of various fruits, differing colours, and a wide variety of tastes and fragrances, **even though it is all gathered in one location of the earth**. Ibn Abbas: *Alfaf* means gathered, tangled together. The point is 13:4: neighbouring tracts, watered with the same water, yet some made more excellent than others to eat — *therein are signs for people who understand*.

That the same water and the same soil produce differing fruit is itself the argument. Something is being decided that the water is not deciding.

## 3. The Day of Decision Is on a Timetable (78:17–20)

> *Verily, the Day of Decision is a fixed time.*

**It has a set appointment. Its time cannot be added to or decreased. No one knows its exact time except Allah.** As at 11:104, *We delay it only for a term fixed*.

**The Trumpet, and the forty (78:18).** *You shall come forth in crowds* — Mujahid: **"groups after groups."** Ibn Jarir: each nation will come with its Messenger, as at 17:71.

Ibn Kathir then quotes Al-Bukhari's report from **Abu Hurayrah**, and it is worth reading for the refusal as much as the content. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"That which is between the two blowings is forty."*

- "Forty days, Abu Hurayrah?" — **"No comment."**
- "Forty months?" — **"No comment."**
- "Forty years?" — **"No comment."**

He would not fill the gap. Then the rest of the hadith: Allah sends down rain from the sky and **the dead sprout like green plants**. Every part of a person will have deteriorated except one bone — **the coccyx** — and from it the whole creation will be reassembled.

**The sky and the mountains (78:19–20).** The heaven opened until it is *as gates* — meaning **paths and routes for the descent of the angels**. And the mountains moved off, becoming *as if they were a mirage*: they appear to the one looking at them as if they are something, **but they are actually nothing**, and after that they are removed entirely — "there will be neither base nor trace of them." Ibn Kathir stacks the parallels: mountains passing like clouds (27:88), like carded wool (101:5), blasted and scattered as dust until the earth is a level smooth plain with nothing crooked or curved in it (20:105–107, 18:47).

## 4. Hell as an Ambush (78:21–30)

> *Truly, Hell is a place of ambush, a dwelling place for the Taghun.*

**Mirsad** — Ibn Kathir: **it is waiting in preparation.** Not a place you are sent to so much as a place already lying in wait on the road.

- **The Taghun** — "the disobedient rejectors who oppose the Messengers."
- **Ma'ab** — a place of return, final destination, final outcome, and residence.

**The question of Ahqab (78:23).** *They will abide therein Ahqab* — the plural of *huqb*, a long period of time. Ibn Kathir preserves the disagreement rather than resolving it:

- **Khalid bin Ma'dan** (recorded by Ibn Jarir): this ayah, and 11:107 (*except as your Lord wills*), **both refer to the people of Tawhid** — i.e. to sinning believers who eventually come out.
- **Al-Hasan**, asked directly: *Ahqab* has no specific amount of time "other than its general meaning of eternity in the Hellfire." Though it is said a huqb is seventy years, each of its days as a thousand of yours.
- **Qatadah**: it is that which has no end. **Whenever one era comes to an end, a new era follows it.** A huqb is said to be eighty years.
- **Ar-Rabi' bin Anas**: no one knows how long except Allah — though it is mentioned that a huqb is eighty years, the year 360 days, and each day equivalent to a thousand years of your reckoning.

The dominant reading is Qatadah's: the word does not measure a sentence, it describes a series that never runs out.

**What they taste (78:24–25).** *Nothing cool shall they taste therein, nor any drink* — **no coolness in Hell for their hearts, nor any good drink to partake of.** Then the exception, which Abu Al-'Aliyah and Ar-Rabi' bin Anas parse precisely:

- **Hamim** is the exception to *the coolness* — heat that has reached its maximum temperature and point of boiling.
- **Ghassaq** is the exception to *the drink* — gathered from the pus, sweat, tears and wounds of the people of the Fire, **unbearably cold with an intolerable stench**.

Ibn Kathir breaks off here, as he rarely does, to pray: *May Allah save us from that by His beneficence and grace. Amin.*

**Why it is exact (78:26–29).** *An exact recompense* — the punishment is **in accordance with their wicked deeds**, matched to them (Mujahid, Qatadah). And the charge sheet is short:

- *They used not to look for a reckoning* — **they did not believe there would be an abode in which they would be called to account.** The root of the whole thing is a diary with no deadline in it.
- *They denied Our Ayat Kidhdhaba* — they met Allah's evidences and proofs with rejection and refusal. On the odd word *Kidhdhaba*, Ibn Kathir notes it means rejection and is **a verbal noun that does not come from a verb**.
- *And all things We have recorded in a Book* — "We know the deeds of all creatures, and We have written these deeds for them."

**The harshest verse (78:30).** *So taste you. No increase shall We give you, except in torment.* Qatadah ← Abu Ayyub Al-Azdi ← **Abdullah bin Amr**: *"Allah did not reveal any ayah against the people of the Hellfire worse than this."* And then: **"They will continue increasing in torment forever."**

## 5. The Success of Those Who Have Taqwa (78:31–36)

> *Verily, for those who have Taqwa, there will be a Mafazan.*

Two readings, and Ibn Kathir chooses:

- **Ibn Abbas and Ad-Dahhak**: "a place of enjoyable recreation."
- **Mujahid and Qatadah**: they are successful, and thus saved from the Fire.

**The most obvious meaning is Ibn Abbas's** — and his reason is textual: because the very next word is *Hada'iq*, gardens. The verse is naming a place, not just an outcome.

- **Hada'iq** — gardens of palm trees and other things.
- **Kawa'ib Atrab** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and others: *fully rounded and not sagging, because they will be virgins, equal in age* — meaning all of one age. The fuller treatment, Ibn Kathir notes, was already given in Al-Waqi'ah.
- **Ka'san Dihaqa** — Ibn Abbas: *continuously filled*. Ikrimah: *pure*. Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and Ibn Zayd: *completely filled*.

**And then the thing that is easiest to skip (78:35).** *No Laghw shall they hear therein, nor lying.* As at 52:23. Ibn Kathir: **no vain, worthless speech there, nor any sinful lying** — "rather, it will be the abode of peace, and everything in it will be free of any shortcomings." A place defined partly by what is not said in it.

**Jaza'an min Rabbika 'ata'an hisaba (78:36).** A gift, given by His favour and from Him — a kindness, a mercy, a recompense — and *hisaba* here means **sufficient, suitable, comprehensive and abundant**. Ibn Kathir traces the Arabic: they say *"he gave me and he sufficed me,"* meaning he provided enough. **And from this comes the phrase *Hasbiya Allah* — Allah is sufficient for me.**

## 6. The Silence of the Court (78:37–39)

> *The Lord of the heavens and the earth, and whatsoever is in between them, the Most Gracious, with Whom they cannot dare to speak.*

Note the name chosen: at the moment of maximum awe, He is **Ar-Rahman, the Most Gracious, Whose mercy covers all things**. And still: **no one is able to begin addressing Him except by His permission** (compare 2:255, and 11:105 — *no person shall speak except by His leave*).

> *The Day that Ar-Ruh and the angels will stand forth in rows.*

**Ar-Ruh here is the angel Jibril** — Ash-Sha'bi, Sa'id bin Jubayr and Ad-Dahhak — as at 26:193–194, *which the trustworthy Ruh has brought down*. **Muqatil bin Hayyan**: the Ruh is the noblest of the angels, the closest of them to the Lord, and the one who delivers the revelation.

Even he stands in a row and says nothing. From the Sahih: *"And none will speak on that Day except the Messengers."*

And when the permitted one does speak, *he will speak what is right* — meaning **the truth**, and from the truth, say Abu Salih and Ikrimah, is **that there is no god worthy of worship except Allah**.

> *That is the True Day. So, whosoever wills, let him seek a place with his Lord.*

*The True Day* — **it will come to pass and there is no avoiding it**. And the *ma'ab* the person is told to seek is the mirror image of the Taghun's ma'ab in 78:22: here it means **a place of return, a path that leads to Him, and a way he may pass by to get to Him.** Same word, opposite destination. The surah has quietly put the choice in the reader's own hands.

## 7. "Would That I Were Dust" (78:40)

> *Verily, We have warned you of a near torment — the Day when man will see that which his hands have sent forth, and the disbeliever will say: "Woe to me! Would that I were dust!"*

**Near** — meaning the Day of Judgement, described so **because everything that is coming will certainly come to pass.** Distance in time is not distance.

**What he sees**: all of his deeds presented to him — *the good and bad, and the old and new*. As at 18:49, *they will find all that they did placed before them*; and 75:13, *man will be informed of what he sent forward and what he left behind*.

**Why dust.** The disbeliever wishes he had only ever been dust in this life — that **he had not been created and had never come into existence** — when he sees the torment and looks at his wicked deeds written down against him by the noble scribes.

And then Ibn Kathir gives the second, stranger explanation, from the well-known hadith of the Sur (with narrations from Abu Hurayrah, Abdullah bin Amr and others). Before the human judgement, **Allah passes judgement between the animals**, rectifying matters between them with a justice that wrongs no one — **even the hornless sheep is allowed to avenge itself against the sheep with horns.** When that is finished, He says to them: *"Be dust."* And they all become dust.

It is *watching this* that produces the cry. The disbeliever, seeing creatures settle their accounts and then simply cease, says **"Would that I were dust"** — meaning, in Ibn Kathir's paraphrase: *I wish I was an animal, so that I would be returned to dust.*

The surah that began with people idly asking about the great news ends with one of them wishing he had never been a person at all.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The proof is under your feet, not in the sky.** The Resurrection is argued for by a bed, a peg, a pair, a nap, a nightfall, a working day, a lamp, a heavy cloud and a tangled garden. Ibn Kathir's framing is that these are brought "as a proof of His ability to do whatever He wishes concerning the matter of the Hereafter." Nothing exotic is required as evidence — only attention to what is already ordinary.

**Everyone is in one of two camps, and there is no neutral one.** *About which they are in disagreement* means precisely that: **those who believe in it and those who disbelieve in it.** The surah does not recognise the position of not having thought about it.

**The root sin is not looking for a reckoning.** Ibn Kathir puts the disbelievers' whole indictment in one line — *they did not believe that there would be an abode in which they would be taken to account.* Everything else followed from a life organised as though no one would ever ask.

**Ahqab is not a sentence length.** Qatadah's reading is the one that lands: *whenever one era comes to an end, a new era follows it.* And the harshest verse revealed against the people of the Fire, on Abdullah bin Amr's testimony, is the one that promises **increase — but only in torment**.

**Paradise is partly defined by what is absent from it.** *No Laghw shall they hear therein, nor lying.* Beneath the gardens and the full cups, Ibn Kathir's phrase for it is *the abode of peace, free of any shortcomings* — no worthless talk, no falsehood. Consider how much of ordinary life that removes.

**Even Jibril stands in a row and says nothing.** The noblest angel, closest to the Lord, waits for permission. The one who is permitted says only the truth — and the truth, say Abu Salih and Ikrimah, is *la ilaha illa Allah*. Whatever is worth saying on that Day, you can already say today.

**The same word opens both doors.** Hell is a *ma'ab* for the Taghun (78:22); and *whosoever wills, let him seek a ma'ab with his Lord* (78:39). A place of return either way. **The surah leaves the choosing to you and moves on.**
