*Revealed in Madinah · 11 ayat · "The Friday Congregation" — recited by the Prophet ﷺ, together with Al-Munafiqun, at the Friday prayer itself*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Jumu`ah is a short surah built on a single, sharp contrast: **people who were given a revelation and did not carry it**, and **people who have been given one and are now being told exactly how to carry it.**

It moves in three movements. First, the **gift**: an unlettered nation receives a Messenger from among themselves — the answer to Ibrahim's ancient supplication — and the gift is explicitly said not to stop with them, but to run on to "others who have not yet joined them." Second, the **warning**: the image of the donkey loaded with books, and a challenge thrown at those who claim exclusive friendship with Allah. Third, the **practice**: when the call comes on Friday, drop your trade and go.

And then, in the very last verse, the surah catches its own audience doing the thing it just warned about. A caravan arrives during the khutbah, the congregation walks out, and twelve men are left standing with the Prophet ﷺ. The surah that opens with the Tawrah being carried like dead weight closes with a reminder that the new community is capable of exactly the same failure — merchandise beats remembrance, unless you decide otherwise.

---

## 1. Its Virtue

Narrated by **Ibn Abbas and Abu Hurayrah** (collected by Muslim): the Prophet ﷺ used to recite **Surah Al-Jumu`ah and Surah Al-Munafiqun** in the Friday Prayer.

The pairing is itself instructive — the surah of the congregation, and the surah of those who show up to it without belief.

---

## 2. Everything Glorifies Him (62:1)

> *Whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth glorifies Allah — the King, the Holy, the Almighty, the All-Wise.*

Ibn Kathir: **everything** praises Him — every kind of living creature and every inanimate object alike, as in *there is not a thing but glorifies His praise* (17:44).

The four names are read as a sequence:

- **Al-Malik** (the King) — Owner of the heavens and earth, with perfect control over their affairs.
- **Al-Quddus** (the Holy) — free of all shortcomings; His attributes are perfect.
- **Al-`Aziz, Al-Hakim** — the Almighty, the All-Wise: unstoppable in power, faultless in what He legislates and decrees.

---

## 3. The Favour of Sending Muhammad ﷺ (62:2)

> *He it is Who sent among the unlettered ones a Messenger from among themselves, reciting to them His Ayat, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and the Hikmah. And verily, they had been before in manifest error.*

**"Unlettered" (*ummiyyin*) here means the Arabs.** Ibn Kathir is careful to shut down the obvious misreading: singling them out **does not mean he ﷺ was sent only to them**. It means the favour done to the Arabs was greater, not exclusive. He stacks the evidence:

- *Verily, this is indeed a Reminder for you and your people* (43:44) — yet the Qur'an is a reminder for non-Arabs too.
- *And warn your tribe of near kindred* (26:214) — yet also *Say: "O mankind! I am sent to you all as the Messenger of Allah"* (7:158).
- *That I may therewith warn you and whomsoever it may reach* (6:19).
- *But those of the groups that reject it, the Fire will be their promised meeting place* (11:17).

The conclusion: **his Message is universal — to all people, mankind and Jinn alike.**

**Four things the Messenger does**, in order: recites the Ayat, purifies them, teaches the Book, teaches the Hikmah. This verse, Ibn Kathir notes, is the **acceptance of Ibrahim's supplication** — his friend had asked Allah to send the people of Makkah a Messenger from among themselves who would do precisely these things.

And He sent him **when the Messengers had ceased and the way was obscure** — at the moment of greatest need, when Allah had come to hate the people of the earth, Arab and non-Arab alike, except a remnant of the People of the Scripture still holding to what was revealed to `Isa عليه السلام.

**Why "manifest error" is not an exaggeration:** the Arabs had once followed the religion of Ibrahim. They then changed and corrupted it, **choosing polytheism instead of Tawhid, and doubt instead of certainty**, inventing a religion Allah never legislated — the same thing the People of the Scriptures had done to their Books.

What replaced it was *a great divine legislation, a perfect religion suitable for all humans and Jinns*: guidance and explanation for everything needed in this life and the next; the final judgement on every doubt, major and minor; and in Muhammad ﷺ, **all the good qualities of the Prophets before him gathered in one man**, plus what was never given to any generation before or after.

## 4. "Others Among Them Who Have Not Yet Joined Them" (62:3)

The most memorable moment in the surah, recorded by **Al-Bukhari** from **Abu Hurayrah**:

They were sitting with the Prophet ﷺ when this verse was revealed. They asked: *Who are they, O Messenger of Allah?* He did not answer — until they had asked **three times**.

**Salman Al-Farisi** was sitting among them. The Prophet ﷺ placed his hand on Salman and said:

> *"If faith were on Ath-Thurayya (the Pleiades), even then some men — or a man — from these people would attain it."*

Also collected by Muslim, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Jarir.

Two things follow from it, and Ibn Kathir names both: this proves the surah is **Madinan**, and it proves the Message is **universal**. The Prophet ﷺ explained "others among them" by pointing at **Persia** — which is why he sent letters to the kings of Persia and Rome and others, calling them to Allah. **Mujahid and others** therefore take the verse to mean **all non-Arabs who believe in the truth of the Prophet ﷺ**.

**62:4** — *That is the grace of Allah, which He bestows on whom He wills* — refers to the great Prophethood granted to Muhammad ﷺ and the qualities his Ummah was favoured with by his being sent to them.

---

## 5. The Donkey Carrying Books (62:5)

> *The likeness of those who were entrusted with the Tawrah, but did not carry it, is as the likeness of a donkey which carries huge burdens of books.*

The Jews were entrusted with the Tawrah and ordered to abide by it; they did not. The image is exact: **a donkey carrying volumes of books understands nothing of what it carries — it is only using its strength.**

Ibn Kathir then presses the comparison one step further, and it is the sharpest line in the surah:

> They read its letter but did not understand its meanings nor abide by them — rather, they corrupted and changed it. **Therefore they are worse than the donkey, because the donkey cannot understand.** They could have understood, using their minds — but their minds were of no benefit to them.

Compare *They are like cattle, nay even more astray; those! They are the heedless* (7:179).

The verse ends: *How bad is the example of people who deny the Ayat of Allah. And Allah does not guide the people who are wrongdoers.*

**Worth sitting with:** the charge is not ignorance. It is carrying a revelation without letting it change you — which is a charge available to any community that has a Book.

## 6. The Challenge: Wish for Death (62:6–8)

> *Say: "O you Jews! If you pretend that you are friends of Allah, to the exclusion of all other people, then long for death if you are truthful."*

The logic: *if you claim you are on the correct guidance and that Muhammad ﷺ and his Companions are astray, then invoke Allah to bring death upon whichever of the two groups is misguided.*

> *But they will never long for it, because of what their hands have sent before them.*

— i.e. because of the disbelief, injustice and sins they commit. *And Allah knows well the wrongdoers.*

Ibn Kathir sets this alongside **the same challenge in Al-Baqarah** (2:94–96), which adds the devastating observation: *you will find them the greediest of mankind for life, and greedier even than the idolaters — one of them wishes he could be given a life of a thousand years.*

He notes this is a recurring Qur'anic instrument, adapted per audience:

- To the **Christians** — the *mubahala*: *let us call our sons and your sons... then invoke the curse of Allah upon those who lie* (3:61).
- To the **idolaters** — *whoever is in error, the Most Gracious will extend for him* (19:75).

**The hadith that ties all three together** (Ibn Abbas, recorded by Ahmad; also Al-Bukhari, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i). Abu Jahl had said: *if I see Muhammad praying at the Ka`bah, I will step on his neck.* When the Prophet ﷺ heard, he said:

> *Had he done so, the angels would have snatched him away in public.*
> *Had the Jews wished for death, they would all have perished and seen their seats in Hellfire.*
> *Had those accepted the mubahala with Allah's Messenger, they would not have found families or property when they returned home.*

**62:8** closes the argument: *Verily, the death from which you flee will surely meet you, then you will be sent back to the Knower of the unseen and the seen, and He will inform you about what you used to do* — compare *Wheresoever you may be, death will overtake you, even if you are in fortresses built up strong and high* (4:78).

## 7. Why Friday (62:9)

**The name:** *Jumu`ah* comes from *al-Jam`* — gathering. The people of Islam gather weekly, every Friday, in the major places of worship. In the ancient language the day was called **`Arubah**.

**What happened on Fridays**, per the authentic collections:

- Allah **finished the creation** on it — the sixth day.
- **Adam was created** on it, and placed in Paradise on it — and, ironically, **taken out of Paradise** on it.
- The **Last Hour** will commence on it.
- There is **an hour within it** in which no faithful servant asks Allah for something good but Allah gives him what he asked for.

**Why this day and not another:** previous nations were informed about Friday but were led astray from it. **The Jews chose Saturday** — but Adam was not created on a Saturday. **The Christians chose Sunday** — the day creation was *begun*. **Allah chose Friday for this Ummah, because it is the day creation was completed.**

Al-Bukhari and Muslim, from Abu Hurayrah:

> *We are the last (to come) but the first on the Day of Resurrection, though the former nations were given the Scriptures before us. This was their day, the observance of which was made compulsory for them, but they differed about it. So Allah guided us to it, and all other people come after us: the Jews tomorrow and the Christians the day after tomorrow.*

Muslim's wording adds the ordering: **Friday, Saturday, Sunday — and in that order they will follow us on the Day of Resurrection.** *We are the last among the people of this world and the first among the created to be judged.*

## 8. "Hasten" Does Not Mean Run (62:9)

> *When the call is proclaimed for the Salah on Al-Jumu`ah, then hasten (fas`aw) to the remembrance of Allah and leave off business.*

Ibn Kathir is emphatic: ***sa`y* here does not mean walking quickly.** It refers to the **importance** of the thing — go to it, head for it.

**Umar bin Al-Khattab and Ibn Mas`ud** used to recite it as *"Then **proceed** to the remembrance of Allah"* (*famdu*).

Walking in haste to prayer is in fact **prohibited** — from Abu Hurayrah in the Two Sahihs:

> *When you hear the Iqamah, proceed to the prayer with calmness and solemnity, and do not rush. Pray whatever you catch, and complete whatever you have missed.*

And **Abu Qatadah**: they were praying behind the Prophet ﷺ when he heard commotion. Afterwards he asked, *What is the matter with you?* They said, *We hastened to the prayer.* He said:

> *Don't do that. When you come for prayer, there should be tranquility upon you. Pray what remains and complete what you have missed.*

**Al-Hasan**: *By Allah, hastening to the prayer is not accomplished by the feet* — they were forbidden from coming without tranquility and dignity. **It is about the hearts, the intention, and the submission.**
**Qatadah**: hasten with your heart and your actions — and **walk** to it.

## 9. How to Come to Jumu`ah

Ibn Kathir gathers the etiquette into a full checklist.

**Ghusl** — the most heavily attested item:
- *When one of you comes to the Friday prayer, let him perform ghusl* (Ibn Umar, Two Sahihs).
- *Ghusl on the day of Jumu`ah is Wajib upon every Muhtalim* (Abu Sa`id, Two Sahihs).
- *It is Allah's right upon every Muslim to bathe every seven days, washing his head and body* (Abu Hurayrah, Muslim).
- *Within every seven days every Muslim man must perform ghusl on one day — the day of Jumu`ah* (Jabir; Ahmad, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Hibban).

**Also recommended:** cleaning the body, wearing one's **best clothes**, applying **perfume**, and using the **Siwak**. Abu Sa`id's narration names ghusl, siwak and *some of his household's perfume* together.

On clothes, from the Minbar — **Abdullah bin Salam** (Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah): *What harm would it cause if one of you bought two garments for the day of Jumu`ah, other than the garment he wears daily?* And **A'ishah**, when the Prophet ﷺ saw people in worn Nimar garments: *When one of you has wealth, he should keep two garments for Friday, other than the two he wears daily.*

**Coming early** — the famous graded reward (Abu Hurayrah, Two Sahihs). Whoever bathes as for major impurity and then goes:

| Hour | Reward |
|---|---|
| First | as if he sacrificed a **camel** |
| Second | as if he sacrificed a **cow** |
| Third | as if he sacrificed a **horned ram** |
| Fourth | as if he sacrificed a **hen** |
| Fifth | as if he offered an **egg** |

> *And when the Imam appears, the angels present themselves to listen to the remembrance.*

— the register closes when the khutbah begins.

**The whole package, and its reward** — `Aws bin `Aws Ath-Thaqafi (Ahmad; the Four Sunan; graded *hasan* by At-Tirmidhi):

> *Whoever performs ghusl well on the day of Jumu`ah, leaves early, walking not riding, sits close to the Imam and listens without talking — will earn the reward of fasting and standing in prayer for an entire year, for every step he takes.*

And **Abu Ayyub Al-Ansari** (Ahmad): ghusl, perfume if he has it, best clothes, goes to the Masjid, prays voluntary prayer if he wishes, **does not bother anyone**, and listens from the Imam's appearance until the prayer —

> *then all of this will be an expiation for whatever occurs between that Friday and the next.*

## 10. Which Call, and What It Forbids (62:9)

**"When the call is proclaimed"** refers to **the Adhan called when the Prophet ﷺ came out of his house and sat on the Minbar** — called before him, near the door of the Masjid.

The **earlier Adhan** was added by **Uthman bin Affan** because the Muslims had grown in number. As-Sa'ib bin Yazid (Al-Bukhari): in the time of the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and Umar, the Adhan was pronounced while the Imam sat on the pulpit; in Uthman's later years an additional call was made **upon Az-Zawra'** — the highest house in Al-Madinah, near the Masjid.

**"And leave off business."** The scholars of Islam **agree** that it is prohibited for Muslims to engage in business transactions **after the second Adhan**.

*That is better for you if you did but know* — meaning: abandoning buying and selling and turning your full attention to Allah's remembrance and the prayer is better for you **in this life and the Hereafter**.

---

## 11. And Then: Go Back Out (62:10)

> *Then when the Salah is complete, you may disperse through the land, and seek the bounty of Allah, and remember Allah much, that you may be successful.*

Having forbidden work after the Adhan and gathered them for the prayer, **He now releases them** to spread through the earth and seek provision.

**Irak bin Malik** used to stand at the gate of the Masjid when the prayer finished and say:

> *"O Allah, I have accepted and complied with Your call, performed the prayer You ordered, and dispersed as You ordered me. Therefore grant me of Your favour — and You are the best of those who provide."*

**"Remember Allah much"** — meaning *while you are buying and selling, giving and taking*. Don't let this life keep you from what benefits you in the next.

The marketplace du`a, from the hadith Ibn Kathir cites here:

> *Whoever enters a marketplace and says, "La ilaha illallah, He is alone without partners, His is the sovereignty and His is the praise, and He is able to do all things" — Allah will record for him a thousand thousand good deeds and erase a thousand thousand evil deeds.*

**Mujahid**: a servant is not counted among those who remember Allah often **until he does so standing, sitting, and lying down.**

---

## 12. The Caravan (62:11)

> *And when they see some merchandise or some amusement, they disperse headlong to it, and leave you standing. Say: "That which Allah has is better than any amusement or merchandise! And Allah is the best of providers."*

**What happened** — Jabir (Ahmad; also the Two Sahihs): a caravan arrived at Al-Madinah while the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was giving the Khutbah. The people left. **Only twelve men remained.** Then this verse came down.

**Muqatil bin Hayyan** adds: the caravan belonged to **Dihyah bin Khalifah before he became a Muslim**, and it came in with **drums** — hence *"or some amusement."*

Two rulings Ibn Kathir draws out of the wording:

- ***"and leave you standing"*** proves the Imam should **deliver the Friday sermon standing** (so Abu Al-`Aliyah, Al-Hasan, Zayd bin Aslam, Qatadah).
- **Jabir bin Samurah** (Muslim): the Prophet ﷺ gave **two speeches and sat between them**, reciting the Qur'an and reminding the people.

**"That which Allah has"** — the reward with Him in the Hereafter — **is better**. And *Allah is the best of providers*: meaning, for those who **trust in Him and seek His provision when they are permitted to do so.**

Note where the surah lands. It does not tell them to stop trading. It tells them to trade *after* — and to know which of the two is actually feeding them.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Jumu`ah is about the difference between holding a revelation and carrying it.**

The donkey is not condemned for being loaded with books; it is condemned because the load never reaches its understanding. That failure was named against a previous community — and then, in the space of eleven verses, demonstrated by this one, when a drumbeat outside emptied the mosque of all but twelve men.

So the surah gives the practical remedy in the middle: **one fixed hour a week when the market is closed by divine command.** Wash, dress well, come early, walk calmly, sit close, and be silent while the Imam speaks. Then go back out into the world and seek your provision — remembering Allah while you buy and sell.

> *That which Allah has is better than any amusement or merchandise. And Allah is the best of providers.*
