*Revealed in Makkah · 110 ayat · the surah of the three questions*

---

## 1. Its Virtues: Light, Tranquility, and a Shield

Ibn Kathir opens with the surah's protective power — unusual in its concreteness:

- **Tranquility descends with it.** A man was reciting Al-Kahf when his animal grew restless and a fog or cloud appeared overhead. The Prophet ﺺ told him: *"Keep on reciting… for this is the tranquility (Sakinah) which descends when one reads Qur'an."* (Two Sahihs. The reciter was Usayd bin Al-Hudayr.)
- **Protection from the Dajjal.** *"Whoever memorizes ten Ayat from the beginning of Surah Al-Kahf will be protected from the Dajjal."* (Muslim, Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i, At-Tirmidhi — At-Tirmidhi's version says three Ayat.)
- **Light from Friday to Friday.** *"Whoever recites Surah Al-Kahf on Friday, it will illuminate him with light from one Friday to the next."* (Al-Hakim: Sahih chain, though not in Bukhari/Muslim.)
- **Light on the Day of Resurrection** for whoever recites it as it was revealed (Al-Bayhaqi).

---

## 2. Why It Was Revealed: The Three Questions

This is the surah's origin story, and it explains its shape.

The Quraysh sent **An-Nadr bin Al-Harith** and **Uqbah bin Abi Mu'it** to the Jewish rabbis of Al-Madinah to test Muhammad ﺺ. The rabbis gave them three questions:

| Question | Answered in |
|---|---|
| Some young men in ancient times — what was their story? | **Al-Kahf** (the People of the Cave) |
| A man who traveled to the east and west of the earth — who was he? | **Al-Kahf** (Dhul-Qarnayn) |
| What is the **Ruh** (the spirit)? | **Al-Isra' 17:85** |

The Prophet ﺺ said, *"I will tell you tomorrow"* — **but did not say "If Allah wills."** Revelation then stopped for fifteen days. Jibril did not come. Makkah began to mock him, and he grieved.

Then the surah came down: the answers, **and a rebuke** — both for the delay's cause (18:23–24) and for grieving over the idolators (18:6).

> The surah's own lesson is embedded in the story of how it arrived.

---

## 3. The Opening: A Straight Book in a Passing World (18:1–8)

**No crookedness in it.** Allah praises Himself for the greatest blessing given to the people of this earth — a Book that is *straight*, neither distorted nor confusing, bringing people out of darkness into light. Its function is twofold: **warning** to those who oppose, **good news** to those who believe and act.

**Do not destroy yourself over them (18:6).** Allah consoles His Messenger — Qatadah: *"killing yourself with anger and grief over them"*; Mujahid: *"with anxiety."* The instruction is: convey the Message. Whoever is guided is guided for himself; whoever strays, strays against himself.

**The world as an adornment for a test (18:7–8).** Ibn Kathir's summary is blunt: this world is *a place of trial, not a place of settlement.* The Prophet ﺺ said:

> *"This world is sweet and green, and Allah makes you generations succeeding one another, so He is watching what you will do."*

And then the ending: **"We shall make all that is on it bare, dry soil."** Ibn Abbas — everything on it wiped out. Qatadah — *"A plain on which there are no trees or vegetation."*

*This pairing — adornment, then dust — is the frame for everything that follows.*

---

## 4. The People of the Cave (18:9–26)

### The setup

**Ar-Raqim** — no consensus. Ibn Abbas (one report): a valley near Aylah. Ad-Dahhak: the name of the valley. Mujahid: their buildings. Ka'b: the town. Sa'id bin Jubayr: **a stone tablet on which their story was written, placed at the cave's entrance.**

Ibn Kathir's opening note is a rebuke to wonder-seeking: *"their case was not something amazing compared to Our power"* — the heavens, the earth, the alternation of night and day are greater signs. Mujahid: *"Among Our signs are things that are more amazing than this."*

### Young men, not elders

They were **youths** — Ibn Kathir notes they were *more accepting of the truth and more guided than the elders who had become stubbornly set in their ways.* He draws the parallel directly: **most of those who responded to the Prophet ﺺ were young; most of the elders of Quraysh kept to their religion.**

**"And We increased them in guidance" (18:13).** Al-Bukhari and others took this Ayah as evidence that **faith increases, varies in degree, and fluctuates** — supported by 47:17, 9:124, 48:4.

### How they found each other

They were sons of the kings and nobles of Byzantium, under a tyrant named **Decianus**. At a public idol festival they each independently saw the falsehood of it. One withdrew and sat in the shade of a tree; another joined him; then another — **none knowing the others, each hiding his belief out of fear.** Then one said: *"O people, you know by Allah that only one thing is making you leave your people… let each one say what it is."* They all said the same thing.

> *"Souls are like recruited soldiers. Those that recognize one another will come together, and those that do not recognize one another will turn away from one another."* — Al-Bukhari (incomplete chain, from A'ishah), and Muslim from Abu Hurayrah

### Fleeing as legislation

They stood before the king, told him the truth, and refused: *"Never shall we call upon any god other than Him."* Ibn Kathir notes that **"Lan" (never) implies absolute, eternal negation.**

He then extracts a *fiqh* point: **fleeing with one's religion is prescribed in times of trial and persecution** — the hadith about the best wealth being sheep, followed to mountaintops, fleeing for the sake of one's religion. But he immediately qualifies it: **seclusion is not prescribed at any other time**, because one loses congregational and Friday prayers by it.

He also draws the greater parallel: **the cave of Thawr.** When the Quraysh passed right by the Prophet ﺺ and Abu Bakr and did not see them — *"O Abu Bakr, what do you think of two who have Allah as their third?"* Ibn Kathir's verdict: **"The story of this cave (Thawr) is far greater and more wondrous than that of the people of the Cave."**

### The details of their sleep

- **The cave's orientation (18:17).** Ibn Kathir reasons it out astronomically: the entrance faced **north**. If it faced east, nothing would enter at sunset; if south, no shadow would lean either way; if west, nothing would enter at sunrise. The sun entered without touching them — *"because if it had touched them, it would have burnt their bodies and clothes."* (Ibn Abbas)
- **Why the location is unknown.** Allah described the cave but did not say where it is — *because there is no benefit in knowing, and no legislative objective behind it.* If it mattered, we would have been told: *"I have not left anything that will bring you closer to Paradise and keep you further away from Hell but I have certainly taught you about it."*
- **They were turned right and left.** Ibn Abbas: *"If they did not turn over, the earth would have consumed them."*
- **The dog at the threshold.** Ibn Jurayj: *"He was guarding the door for them."* Ibn Kathir's remark is memorable: **"The blessing they enjoyed extended to their dog… This is the benefit of accompanying good people, and so this dog attained fame and stature."**
- **Awe as a shield.** Anyone who came upon them would flee in terror — Allah made them dreadful so that no one would touch them until the appointed time.

### Waking, and the coin

After **309 years** they woke, bodies and hair and skin intact, thinking they had slept *"a day or part of a day."* They sent one with a silver Dirham to buy the **Azka** food — "purest" (same root as *Zakah*, which purifies wealth) — and warned him to conceal himself.

He walked into a city he no longer recognized. The merchant did not know the coin. *"Maybe I am crazy or deluded, maybe I am dreaming."* The people accused him of finding buried treasure and took him to the governor. He said: *"I am from this land, I was living here yesterday and Decianus was the ruler."*

**Why they were found.** Ikrimah: some people of that time believed **souls** would be resurrected but not bodies — so Allah resurrected the people of the Cave **as a physical proof of the Resurrection.** The dispute in 18:21 is precisely about the Hour.

### The mosque over the cave — a warning

When the people said, *"We verily shall build a place of worship over them,"* Ibn Kathir does not let it pass:

> *"Allah has cursed the Jews and the Christians who took the graves of their Prophets and righteous people as places of worship."*

He adds that **Umar bin Al-Khattab**, on finding the grave of Daniel in Iraq, ordered news of it withheld from the people and the inscriptions buried.

### Their number — and the discipline of not knowing

Three views (three + dog, five + dog, seven + dog). Allah calls the first two **"guessing at the unseen"** — Ibn Kathir: *like a person who aims at an unknown target; he is hardly likely to hit it, and if he does, it was not on purpose.* The third is left uncontested. **Ibn Abbas said: "I am one of the few mentioned in this Ayah; they were seven"** — chains graded Sahih.

The instruction that follows is the point: ***"Say: My Lord knows best their number"*** — refer knowledge to Allah, and **"debate not except with the clear proof"**, meaning gently and politely, *for there is not a great deal to be gained from knowing about that.*

### The length: 309 lunar = 300 solar

Three hundred **plus nine** — because the difference between a hundred lunar and a hundred solar years is three years. Ibn Kathir sides with Ibn Jarir: Allah is **stating the fact**, not merely narrating the People of the Book's claim.

## 5. "If Allah Wills" (18:23–24)

The etiquette of every future intention. The proof text is the story of **Sulayman bin Dawud**:

He swore he would visit seventy wives that night so each would bear a son who would fight for Allah. He was told, *"Say: If Allah wills"* — **but he did not say it.** Only one gave birth, to a half-formed child.

> *"By the One in Whose hand is my soul, had he said 'If Allah wills,' he would not have broken his oath, and that would have helped him to attain what he wanted."*

**"And remember your Lord when you forget."** Abu Al-'Aliyah and Al-Hasan Al-Basri: if you forget to say it, say it when you remember. **Ibn Abbas** went further: *"He may say 'If Allah wills' even if it is a year later"* — following the Sunnah of saying it. Ibn Jarir (and Ibn Kathir) accept this, but clarify: **it does not undo a broken oath or remove the obligation of expiation.**

## 6. Sit With the Poor Believers (18:27–31)

The nobles of Quraysh asked the Prophet ﺺ to sit with them alone — without Bilal, Ammar, Suhayb, Khabbab, Ibn Mas'ud. Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas (Muslim) recalls being one of six the idolators wanted removed *"so they will not offend us."*

Allah's answer is 18:28 — **keep yourself patiently with those who call on their Lord morning and afternoon, seeking His Face** — and 6:52. Ibn Abbas on *"let not your eyes overlook them"*: **do not favor others over them; do not seek the people of nobility and wealth instead of them.**

And the one whose obedience is forbidden: **"him whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance."** Ibn Kathir: *do not obey him, or admire his way, or envy what he has.*

### The two ends, contrasted by one word

Then 18:29–31 sets the two destinations against each other, and Ibn Kathir points out that the Qur'an uses the **same word, Murtafaq** (place of rest), for both:

| | The Fire | The Garden |
|---|---|---|
| **Enclosure** | A wall of fire surrounding them | Jannatu 'Adn ('Adn = lasting), rivers beneath |
| **Drink / Adornment** | Water like **Al-Muhl** — Ibn Abbas: thick, like sediment in oil; Mujahid: like blood and pus; Ad-Dahhak: black. It scalds the face so the skin falls into it | Bracelets of gold; green **Sundus** (fine cloth) and **Istabraq** (thick shiny velvet) |
| **Verdict** | *"Terrible is the drink, and an evil Murtafaq!"* | *"How good is the reward, and what an excellent Murtafaq!"* |

The freedom offered first — *"whosoever wills, let him believe; and whosoever wills, let him disbelieve"* — Ibn Kathir reads as **a threat and stern warning**, not indifference.

## 7. The Two Men and the Two Gardens (18:32–46)

Placed here deliberately: **immediately after the idolators who were too arrogant to sit with the poor.**

**The rich man's garden** was flawless — grapes ringed with date palms, crops between, a river gushing through, *"failed not in the least therein."* His speech is the diagnosis: *"I am greater than you in wealth and have a mightier entourage."* Qatadah: **"This, by Allah, is the wish of the immoral — to have a lot of wealth and a large entourage."**

His two errors compound:
1. *"I do not think that this will ever perish"* — deceived by what he could see.
2. *"And if indeed I am brought back to my Lord, I surely shall find better than this"* — assuming his wealth **proved** he was dear to Allah.

**The believer's reply** attacks the root: *"Do you disbelieve in Him Who created you out of dust, then out of Nutfah?"* — no one among Allah's creatures does not know that **he was nothing, then he came to be**, and that his existence is due neither to himself nor to any other creature.

Then the correction he should have made:

> **"It was better for you to say, when you entered your garden: 'Ma sha Allah, la quwwata illa billah' — That which Allah wills! There is no power but with Allah!"**

One of the Salaf: *"Whoever is delighted with something in his circumstances or his wealth or his children, let him say it."* And the Prophet ﺺ to Abu Musa: *"Shall I not tell you about some of the treasure of Paradise? La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah."*

**The ruin.** Husban from the sky (a punishment / a mighty rain), the garden a barren slippery earth, the water sunk beyond reach. Qatadah on his gesture of regret: *"He was clasping his hands together in grief for the wealth he had lost."* His only words left: **"Would that I had ascribed no partners to my Lord!"** — and no entourage to help him. The boast is dismantled in the same terms it was made.

**"There, Al-Walayah will be for Allah" (18:44).** Recitations differ: *Al-Walayah* (allegiance — everyone, believer or disbeliever, submits when the punishment arrives, as in 40:84 and Fir'awn's drowning) or *Al-Wilayah* (**sovereignty** belongs to Allah on that Day, as in 25:26).

**And then the parable of the world itself (18:45):** water descends, vegetation mingles and grows bright and fresh — *"then it becomes dry and broken pieces, which the winds scatter."*

### Al-Baqiyat As-Salihat — the deeds that last (18:46)

Wealth and children are *adornment*; the lasting good deeds are **better for reward and better for hope.** What are they?

- **Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr and others:** the five daily prayers.
- **Ibn Abbas (another report):** *Subhan Allah, Al-Hamdu Lillah, La ilaha illallah, Allahu Akbar.*
- **Uthman bin Affan:** those four, plus *La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah il-'Aliyy il-'Azim.*
- **Ibn Abbas (via Ali bin Abi Talhah):** all of the above plus *Tabarak Allah, Astaghfirullah, Sallallahu 'ala Rasulillah* — **and fasting, prayer, Hajj, charity, freeing slaves, Jihad, maintaining ties of kinship, and all other good deeds.**
- **Ibn Jarir's choice** (and Ibn Kathir's): **all righteous deeds.**

## 8. The Day of Resurrection (18:47–49)

Mountains moved and pulverized; the earth left **a leveled plain** — Ibn Kathir: *clear and open, with no features anyone may recognize and nothing to hide behind.* Qatadah: *"There will be no buildings and no trees."* All gathered, **not one left behind, young or old.**

**The Book produced.** *"What sort of Book is this that leaves neither a small thing nor a large thing, but has recorded it with numbers!"* — nothing omitted, recorded with utmost precision. Every traitor will have a banner by which he is known.

**"And your Lord treats no one with injustice."** Ibn Kathir's summary of the divine judgment: He will overlook, forgive, and have mercy; He will punish whom He wills by His power, wisdom and justice; **then He will rescue the disobedient and leave the disbelievers there for eternity.**

**Jabir's journey.** Jabir bin Abdullah bought a camel and traveled a month to Ash-Sham to hear one hadith from Abdullah bin Unays — afraid one of them would die first. It was about reciprocal justice:

> Allah will gather people naked, uncircumcised, with nothing. A voice will call, heard equally by the far and the near: *"I am the Sovereign, I am the Judge. None of the people of Hell should enter Hell if he is owed something by one of the people of Paradise, until I have settled the matter"* — and the same in reverse — **"even if it is only the case of a slap."**

They asked how, with nothing in their hands. He said: **"By good deeds, and by evil deeds."** Even the hornless animal will settle the score with the horned one.

## 9. Iblis, and the Enemy People Choose (18:50–54)

Iblis **was not an angel**. Al-Hasan Al-Basri: *"Iblis was not one of the angels, not even for a second. He was the origin of the Jinn just as Adam was the origin of mankind."* (Sahih chain, per Ibn Jarir.) The angels from light, Iblis from smokeless fire, Adam from what has been described.

Ibn Kathir's line on why he fell:

> **"When matters are crucial, every vessel leaks that which it contains and is betrayed by its true nature."**

He had worshipped like the angels and resembled them, so he was included in the address — but his origin surfaced under command. *Fisq* means "to step out": the same verb describes a date emerging from its flower and a mouse coming out of its hole to do damage.

**"Will you then take him and his offspring as protectors rather than Me — while they are enemies to you?"** And the false gods witnessed nothing: not the creation of the heavens and the earth, not even **their own creation**, since they did not yet exist.

**"But, man is ever more quarrelsome than anything" (18:54).** The Prophet ﺺ came at night to Ali and Fatimah and asked, *"Are you not going to pray?"* Ali answered that their souls were in Allah's hand — if He willed to wake them, He would. The Prophet ﺺ walked away **slapping his thigh** and reciting this Ayah. (Two Sahihs.)

## 10. Musa and Al-Khidr (18:60–82)

### Why the journey happened

Musa was asked who was the most learned person, and answered: **"I am."** Ibn Kathir: *"Allah rebuked him because he did not refer the knowledge to Allah."* He was then told there was a servant of Allah at the junction of the two seas with knowledge he did not have — and given the sign: **take a fish; where you lose it, you will find him.**

At the rock they slept beside a spring called **'Ayn Al-Hayat** (the Spring of Life), *"its water never touched a thing but it brought it to life."* Drops touched the salted fish; it revived, leapt into the sea, and swam away leaving a tunnel through the water. Yusha' bin Nun forgot to mention it — *"none but Shaytan made me forget."*

Note Ibn Kathir's grammatical observation: **the forgetting is attributed to them both, though only Yusha' forgot.**

### The adab of the request

***"May I follow you so that you teach me something of that knowledge which you have been taught?"***

Ibn Kathir: *"This is a question phrased in gentle terms, with no sense of force or coercion.* **This is the manner in which the seeker of knowledge should address the scholar."**

And Al-Khidr's answer defines the whole encounter — not a claim of superiority, but of **difference**:

> *"O Musa, I have some of Allah's knowledge which He has bestowed upon me but you do not know it; and you too have some of Allah's knowledge which He has bestowed upon you, but I do not know it."*

**The bird and the ocean.** A bird dipped its beak in the sea from the edge of the boat. Al-Khidr: *"My knowledge and your knowledge, and the knowledge of all creation, in comparison to the knowledge of Allah, is like what this bird has taken out of the sea."*

### The three acts, and their hidden reasons

| The act | Musa's objection | The reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Damaged the boat** after the crew gave them free passage | *"You have committed a thing **Imr**"* — Mujahid: an evil thing; Qatadah: an astounding thing | It belonged to poor men working the sea; a king ahead **seized every sound boat by force.** A flaw would save it for them |
| **Killed the boy** — the finest and most handsome of those playing | *"A thing **Nukr**"* — something clearly evil. The narrator notes: *"The second blame was stronger than the first"* | His parents were believers; he would have overwhelmed them with rebellion and disbelief. *"The boy Al-Khidr killed was destined to be a disbeliever from the day he was created."* Allah would exchange him for one better in righteousness |
| **Rebuilt the wall for free** in a town that refused them food | *"If you had wished, surely you could have taken wages for it!"* | Beneath it lay a treasure for **two orphan boys** — *"and their father was a righteous man."* It was to be preserved until they reached full strength |

**Qatadah on the boy:** *"His parents rejoiced when he was born and grieved for him when he was killed. If he had stayed alive, he would have been the cause of their doom. So let a man be content with the decree of Allah, for the decree of Allah for the believer, if he dislikes it, is better for him than if He were to decree something that he likes for him."* — *"Allah does not decree anything for the believer except it is good for him."*

**On the righteous father (18:82):** Ibn Kathir draws the general principle — **a righteous person's offspring will be taken care of, and the blessing of his worship extends to them in this world and the Hereafter.** Ibn Abbas: *"They were taken care of because their father was a righteous man, although it is not stated that they themselves were righteous."*

### The grammar of attribution

Ibn Kathir notes how carefully the three explanations assign agency:

- The boat: **"I wished** to make a defective damage in it" — the apparent harm
- The boy: **"we intended** that their Lord should exchange him"
- The treasure: **"your Lord intended** that they should attain their full strength" — the pure good attributed to Allah alone, *because no one else is able to bring them to maturity*

And **"I did them not of my own accord"** — which, with 18:65 (*"one of Our servants… whom We had taught knowledge from Us"*), Ibn Kathir presents as **evidence that Al-Khidr was a Prophet.**

**The name.** *"He was called Al-Khidr because he sat on a barren Farwah and it turned green (Khadra') beneath him."* (Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah.) *Farwah* — a patch of withered vegetation.

**The Prophet ﺺ's regret:** *"We wish that Musa had been patient so that Allah would have told us more about both of them."* And Ubayy bin Ka'b: *"May the mercy of Allah be upon us and upon Musa. If he had stayed with his companion he would have seen wonders."*

## 11. Dhul-Qarnayn (18:83–98)

**"We established him in the earth, and We gave him the means of everything."** Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ikrimah, As-Suddi, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak: *"This means **knowledge**."* Ibn Kathir: the means and power to conquer regions, defeat enemies, and humiliate the people of Shirk — everything a man in his position would need. Some say he was called "the one with two horns" because **he reached the two 'horns' of the sun, east and west.**

### Journey one — the west (18:86–88)

He reached the furthest point westward and *found the sun setting in a spring of Hami'ah* (from *Hama'ah*, mud). Ibn Kathir is emphatic and deflationary here:

> *"As for the idea of his reaching the place in the sky where the sun sets, this is something impossible, and the tales told by storytellers… are not true at all. Most of these stories come from the myths of the People of the Book and the fabrications and lies of their heretics."*

What he saw is **what anyone on a coast sees** — the sun appearing to set into the sea, though it never leaves its fixed path.

Given power over the people there — to kill and enslave, or to release — **his justice appeared in his ruling**: punishment for the wrongdoer (who will then face a worse punishment from his Lord), the best reward for the believer, *"and we shall speak unto him mild words."* Mujahid: **words of kindness.**

### Journey two — the east (18:90)

A people with **no shelter against the sun** — no buildings, no trees. Qatadah: nothing grew there, so at sunrise they went into tunnels until the sun passed its zenith, then came out to earn their living. **"And We knew all about him"** — nothing of him or his armies was hidden from Allah.

### Journey three — the barrier (18:93–98)

Between two mountains he found a people who **scarcely understood a word** — so isolated from other people that he could not understand their speech. They offered him **tribute** to build a barrier against **Ya'juj and Ma'juj** (who are among the progeny of Adam, per the Two Sahihs), then spreading destruction over crops and people.

His refusal is the mark of the man: ***"That in which my Lord had established me is better."*** Ibn Kathir compares it directly to Sulayman's *"What Allah has given me is better than that which He has given you."* He wanted **labor**, not payment.

**The construction:** blocks (*Zubar*) of iron, said to weigh a Damascene Qintar or more each, stacked from the ground to the tops of the mountains, filling width and height; then fire until the whole thing glowed; then molten **copper** (*Qitran*) poured over it — *"so it resembled a striped cloak."*

**They could neither scale it nor dig through it.** Ibn Kathir points out the Arabic itself carries the difficulty: the more intensive verb form is used for the harder action — *digging through* is harder than *climbing over*. (He notes the same principle in 18:78 vs 18:82: the more intensive form is used while the confusion is still unresolved.)

**But it will not last.** *"This is a mercy from my Lord, but when the promise of my Lord comes, He shall **Dakka'** it down to the ground"* — flatten it, as a camel's back with no hump; as the mountain was made *Dakkan* in 7:143. Zaynab bint Jahsh reported that the Prophet ﺺ woke red in the face:

> *"La ilaha illallah! Woe to the Arabs from the evil that has approached. Today a hole has been opened in the barrier of Ya'juj and Ma'juj like this"* — and he made a circle with his index finger and thumb.

She asked whether they would be destroyed with righteous people among them. He said: **"Yes, if evil increases."** (Bukhari and Muslim.)

## 12. The Close: Two Ends, and Two Conditions (18:99–110)

**The Trumpet and the gathering.** *As-Sur* is a horn; Israfil will blow it. The hadith of the one who cannot relax: *"How can I relax when the one with the Horn has put the Horn in his mouth and has knelt down, listening out for the command?"* They asked what to say. He said: **"Allah is Sufficient for us and the best Disposer of affairs; in Allah have we put our trust."**

**Hell displayed** before the disbelievers *before* they enter — to intensify their distress. Ibn Mas'ud (Muslim): *"Hell will be brought forth on the Day of Resurrection, pulled by seventy thousand reins, each held by seventy thousand angels."*

### The greatest losers in deeds (18:103–106)

Mus'ab asked his father **Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas** whether the Ayah meant the Haruriyyah. He said no — the Jews and Christians. Ali bin Abi Talib and Ad-Dahhak said it *was* the Haruriyyah.

**Ibn Kathir resolves it by refusing to narrow it.** The Ayah was revealed in Makkah — before the Qur'an addressed the Jews and Christians, and before the Khawarij existed at all. So:

> *"The Ayah is general and refers to everyone who worships Allah in a way that is not acceptable, thinking that he is right in doing that and that his deeds will be accepted, but he is mistaken and his deeds will be rejected."*

*"We shall assign no weight for them"* — the Balance is empty of goodness. *"A huge fat man will come forward on the Day of Resurrection and he will weigh no more than the wing of a gnat to Allah."* (Bukhari, Muslim.)

**Against which: Al-Firdaws** — Qatadah and others, attributed to the Prophet ﺺ: *a hill in Paradise, at its center, the best of it.* *"If you ask Allah for Paradise, then ask Him for Al-Firdaws, for it is the highest part of Paradise, in the middle of Paradise, and from it spring the rivers of Paradise."* And they will **never want to leave** — despite an eternal stay, they will never choose to move.

### The sea as ink (18:109)

If the sea were ink for the Words of the Lord, it would run dry first — and another sea after it, and another. Ar-Rabi' bin Anas: *"The parable of the knowledge of all of mankind, in comparison to the knowledge of Allah, is that of a drop of water in comparison to all of the oceans."*

Ibn Kathir: *"No one can comprehend the greatness of Allah or praise Him as He deserves to be praised, except the One Who praises Himself.* **Our Lord is as He says He is, and He is beyond what we can say."**

### The final verse: the two conditions (18:110)

***"Say: I am only a man like you."*** Ibn Kathir has the Prophet ﺺ say to the idolators: *I did not know the Unseen — the story of the people of the Cave, of Dhul-Qarnayn — I did not know any of this except for what Allah made known to me.* **The surah ends by returning to the three questions that began it.**

Then the two conditions on which every deed is accepted:

1. **"Let him work righteousness"** — in accordance with the prescribed way of the Messenger ﺺ.
2. **"And associate none as a partner in the worship of his Lord"** — intent for the sake of Allah alone.

And the warning attached to the second:

> *"What I fear the most for you is the small Shirk."* They said: *"What is the small Shirk?"* He said: **"Showing off (Ar-Riya')."** Allah will say on the Day of Resurrection, when people are rewarded for their deeds: *"Go to the one for whom you were showing off in the world and see if you will find any reward with him."*

---

## The Thread Running Through It

Ibn Kathir does not label the structure, but it surfaces on its own. **Four narratives, four things a person can be tested by, and one answer given to each:**

| The trial | The story | The answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Faith** | The People of the Cave | Flee with your religion; sleep in Allah's protection |
| **Wealth** | The Two Gardens | *Ma sha Allah, la quwwata illa billah* |
| **Knowledge** | Musa and Al-Khidr | *"Above every possessor of knowledge is one more knowing"* — ask gently, and refer knowledge to Allah |
| **Power** | Dhul-Qarnayn | *"That in which my Lord had established me is better"* — rule justly, and expect your barrier to fall |

Framing all four: **the world as adornment (18:7), the world as bare dry soil (18:8), and the deeds that last (18:46).** Everything in between is a test of whether one can tell the difference.

And the surah's two bookends are the same instruction in two forms — **"If Allah wills"** at the start, and **"associate none as a partner"** at the end. Both are the refusal to place oneself where only Allah belongs.
