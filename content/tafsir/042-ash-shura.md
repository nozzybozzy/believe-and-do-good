*Revealed in Makkah · 53 ayat · named for the mutual consultation of the believers, 42:38*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ash-Shura is a surah about **how God communicates and how people respond to it**. It opens with revelation coming down — *Likewise Allah, the Almighty, the All-Wise sends revelation to you as to those before you* — and it closes, fifty verses later, with a technical account of the three channels through which that happens. Everything between those two poles is the human reaction to a message that arrives from outside.

Two claims hold the middle together. The first is about **religion itself**: what was ordained for Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa and Muhammad ﷺ is *one* religion, and the command attached to it is to establish it and not splinter over it. Division, Ibn Kathir insists, never came from ignorance — *they divided not till after knowledge had come to them* — it came from transgression.

The second is about **what a community that has received the message actually looks like**. In 42:36–39 the surah stops arguing and simply describes: they shun major sins, they forgive when angry, they pray, they consult one another, they spend, and they are strong enough to answer oppression without being obliged to. That short passage gives the surah its name, and it is the closest thing the Qur'an has to a portrait of communal character.

Running underneath both is the surah's most quoted line, five words that Ibn Kathir treats as the boundary of all theology: **"There is nothing like Him."**

---

## 1. How Revelation Comes (42:1–3)

Ha Mim. 'Ain Sin Qaf — the disjointed letters, which Ibn Kathir passes over, having discussed them before.

Then the claim: just as this Qur'an is revealed to you, the Books and Scriptures were revealed to the Prophets before you. *Al-'Aziz* — the Almighty, meaning in His vengeance. *Al-Hakim* — the All-Wise, meaning in all He says and does.

Here Ibn Kathir places the hadith of A'ishah, on Al-Harith bin Hisham's question — *how does the revelation come to you?*

> **Sometimes it comes to me like the ringing of a bell, which is the most difficult for me; then it goes away, and I understand what was said. And sometimes the angel comes to me in the image of a man, and he speaks to me and I understand what he says.**

A'ishah adds the detail that makes it physical rather than serene: she saw him receive revelation on a bitterly cold day, and when it lifted from him his forehead was running with sweat.

---

## 2. The Sky Almost Splits (42:4–6)

> *Nearly the heavens might be rent asunder from above them, and the angels glorify the praises of their Lord, and ask for forgiveness for those on the earth.*

**Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, As-Suddi and Ka'b Al-Ahbar** all give the same cause: *out of fear of His might*.

Set against that cosmic strain is what the angels are actually doing — asking forgiveness for the people of earth, exactly as in 40:7, where those who bear the Throne pray *Our Lord! You comprehend all things in mercy and knowledge*. And immediately after, the reminder Ibn Kathir says is placed there so we take heed of it: **Verily, Allah is the Oft-Forgiving, the Most Merciful.**

As for those who take protectors besides Him: Allah is *Hafiz* over them — Witness to their deeds, recording and enumerating them precisely. And to the Prophet ﷺ: *you are not a trustee over them.* You are a warner. Allah is the Trustee of all affairs.

---

## 3. The Mother of the Towns, and Two Parties (42:7)

The Qur'an is *'Arabi* — plain, clear, manifest — sent so he may warn **Umm al-Qura**, Makkah, *and all around it*, which Ibn Kathir glosses as all the lands, east and west.

Why Makkah is the "Mother of the Towns" is answered with the Prophet's ﷺ own words, spoken as he stood in its marketplace, on the day he was leaving it:

> **By Allah, you are the best land of Allah, the most beloved land to Allah; were it not for the fact that I was driven out from you, I would never have left you.**

Then the Day of Assembling — no doubt about it — and the verse's stark division: *a party will be in Paradise and a party in the blazing Fire.*

### The two books

Ibn Kathir's longest excursion in the surah hangs on that phrase. Abdullah bin 'Amr reports that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came out holding **two books**. The one in his right hand contained the names of the people of Paradise, with their fathers and tribes, complete down to the last of them, nothing to be added or removed. The one in his left contained the same for the people of Hell.

The Companions asked the obvious question: *why should we strive, if it is already settled?* The answer:

> **Strive with your deeds as hard as you can for a middle course or close to it, for the person who is destined for Paradise will die doing the deeds of the people of Paradise, regardless of what he did before — and the person destined for Hell will die doing the deeds of the people of Hell, regardless of what he did before.**

Then he made a fist: *Your Lord has settled the matter of His servants* — opened his right hand as though casting something out: **a party in Paradise** — and the same with his left: **and a party in the blazing Fire.**

A second report gives the emotional weight of this. Abu Nadrah tells of a Companion, Abu Abdullah, found weeping by his friends. Hadn't the Prophet ﷺ told him to trim his moustache and hold to that practice until they met again? *Yes*, he said, *but I also heard him say* that Allah took a handful in one Hand and another in the other and said, **"This is for this and this is for this, and I do not care."** And I do not know which of the two handfuls I am in.

## 4. There Is Nothing Like Him (42:8–12)

Had Allah willed, He could have made them one nation — all guided or all astray. He did not; He guides whom He wills and sends astray whom He wills, and He has complete wisdom and perfect proof.

Then a dense sequence of attributes:

- **He alone is the Wali** — it is not right to worship anyone but Him, and He is the One able to bring the dead to life.
- **Whatever you differ in, the decision is with Allah** — general, applying to all things, judged by His Book and the Sunnah of His Prophet ﷺ (cf. 4:59, *refer it to Allah and His Messenger*).
- **Fatir as-samawati wal-ard** — Originator of the heavens and the earth and everything between.
- **He made mates for you from yourselves**, and eight pairs of cattle; by this means He creates you, generation after generation.

And then the verse:

> *There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearer, the All-Seer.* (42:11)

Ibn Kathir's gloss is short and absolute: there is nothing like the Creator of these pairs, **for He is the Unique, the Self-Sufficient Master, Who has no peer or equal.**

To Him belong the **keys** of the heavens and the earth (as in 39:63) — meaning He is the One controlling and governing them. He expands provision for whom He wills and straitens it, perfectly Wise and Just.

---

## 5. One Religion, Five Messengers (42:13–14)

> *He has ordained for you the same religion which He ordained for Nuh, and that which We have revealed to you, and that which We ordained for Ibrahim, Musa and 'Isa: that you should establish religion and make no divisions in it.*

The verse names the first Messenger sent after Adam (Nuh) and the last (Muhammad ﷺ), and between them the three Messengers of **strong will** — Ibrahim, Musa, and 'Isa son of Maryam. The same five appear together in 33:7.

What they all brought was one thing: *We did not send any Messenger before you but We revealed to him: none has the right to be worshipped but I* (21:25). Ibn Kathir puts the hadith beside it —

> **We Prophets are brothers and our religion is one.**

— and immediately balances it against 5:48, *to each among you We have prescribed a law and a clear way.* The **creed** is identical; the **laws and ways** differ. Unity is at the level of worship, not of legislation.

The command that follows is therefore double: establish it, **and do not splinter over it**. Allah enjoined all the Prophets to be as one and forbade them to differ.

And to the idolators, *Tawhid* is intolerable — the one thing they cannot bear to be called to.

Then the diagnosis of schism (42:14): they divided **only after knowledge had come to them**, and proof had been established against them. Nothing made them resist except transgression and stubbornness. Were it not for a Word already gone forth deferring the reckoning to the Day of Resurrection, punishment would have been hastened in this world. As for the generations who inherited the Scripture after them, they are *in grave doubt* — because they have no firm conviction, only inherited imitation of their forefathers, without evidence or proof.

---

## 6. The Verse of Ten Rulings (42:15)

Ibn Kathir notes that the scholars counted **ten separate and independent rulings** in this single verse, and said there is nothing else like it in the Qur'an except Ayat al-Kursi.

- **So unto this then invite** — call to what was revealed to you and enjoined on every Prophet before you.
- **And stand firm as you are commanded** — you and those who follow you, adhering firmly to His worship.
- **And follow not their desires** — the inventions and fabrications of idolatry.
- **I believe in whatsoever Allah has sent down of the Book** — all of the Books; *we do not differentiate between any of them.*
- **I am commanded to do justice among you** — when judging by Allah's commands.
- **Allah is our Lord and your Lord** — we affirm this willingly; and whether you do or not, everyone in the universe prostrates to Him.
- **For us our deeds and for you your deeds** — *we have nothing to do with you* (cf. 10:41).
- **There is no dispute between us and you** — Mujahid: *no argument.* As-Suddi: this was before the Ayah of the sword, which fits, since this is Makkan and 22:39–40 came after the Hijrah.
- **Allah will assemble us** — on the Day of Resurrection (cf. 34:26).
- **And to Him is the final return** — the return on the Day of Reckoning.

---

## 7. The Book and the Balance (42:16–18)

Those who dispute *about Allah after He has been responded to* are those who argue with believers and try to stop them following guidance — hoping, said **Ibn Abbas and Mujahid**, to drag them back to Jahiliyyah. **Qatadah** identifies them as those who claimed *our religion is better than yours, our Prophet came before yours, we are closer to Allah than you* — which Ibn Kathir dismisses as nothing but lies. Their dispute is futile before their Lord; on them is wrath.

Against that, what Allah actually sent down: **the Book in truth, and the Balance** — which Mujahid and Qatadah both read as **justice and fairness** (cf. 57:25, and 55:7–9, *He has set up the Balance; in order that you may not transgress the balance*).

> *And what can make you know that perhaps the Hour is close at hand?*

Ibn Kathir reads this as three things at once: encouragement to strive, a terrifying warning, and advice to think little of this world.

Those who disbelieve **seek to hasten** the Hour — *when will this promise be fulfilled?* — asking out of stubbornness, thinking it unlikely. Those who believe are **fearful of it** and know it is the very truth, so they prepare.

The Mutawatir hadith he cites makes the whole distinction practical. A man called out from a distance on a journey: *O Muhammad!* — *Here I am!* — *When will the Hour come?*

> **Woe to you! It will most certainly come. What have you done to prepare for it?**

He said: *love for Allah and His Messenger.* The reply:

> **You will be with those whom you love.**

Ibn Kathir's own comment is the point: he did not answer the question about *when*. He redirected it to *what have you prepared*.

---

## 8. Two Harvests (42:19–20)

> *Allah is Latif to His servants. He gives provision to whom He wills.*

His kindness reaches every last creature and He forgets no one; in the matter of provision, the righteous and the sinner are alike (cf. 11:6, *no moving creature but its provision is due from Allah*).

Then the choice, framed as agriculture — *harvest*:

- **Whoever desires the harvest of the Hereafter** — We give him increase in it: strength and help to do what he intends, and every good multiplied from ten to seven hundred times, as much as Allah wills.
- **Whoever desires the harvest of this world** — We give him thereof, *and he has no portion in the Hereafter*.

Ibn Kathir reinforces it with the long passage at 17:18–21, and then with Ubayy bin Ka'b's report:

> **Give the glad tidings to this Ummah of sublimity, high status, victory and power in the land. But whoever among them does the deeds of the Hereafter for the sake of worldly gain will have no portion in the Hereafter.**

The one who strives with that intention gets the worst of both deals: the Hereafter denied outright, and this world only if Allah wills it.

---

## 9. Legislating for God Is Shirk (42:21–23)

> *Or have they partners with Allah who have instituted for them a religion which Allah has not ordained?*

This is one of Ibn Kathir's sharpest passages. They do not follow what Allah ordained; they follow **what their devils among men and jinn prescribed for them** — the invented taboos of *Bahirah, Sa'ibah, Wasilah, Ham*, the permitting of carrion and blood, gambling: an entire apparatus of rulings on the lawful and forbidden, and rites of worship, manufactured in Jahiliyyah.

And the man who started it, seen by the Prophet ﷺ:

> **I saw 'Amr bin Luhayy bin Qama'ah dragging his intestines in Hell** — because he was the first to introduce the Sa'ibah.

A king of Khuza'ah, and the one who first made the Quraysh worship idols.

Against that, the contrast the surah keeps returning to. The wrongdoers will be **fearful of what they earned, and it will surely befall them** — standing in the arena of Resurrection in utter terror. The believers will be in the **flowering meadows of the Gardens**, with whatever they desire from their Lord. Ibn Kathir asks what comparison is even possible: humiliation and dread on one side; on the other, food, drink, clothing, dwellings, scenery and spouses *such as no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and has never crossed the minds of men.* **That is the supreme grace.**

### "Except love for kinship"

> *Say: "No reward do I ask of you for this except love for kinship."* (42:23)

Ibn Kathir's reading: *I ask nothing in return for this message. Only that you withhold your harm from me and let me deliver my Lord's Messages. If you will not help me, then do not obstruct me — for the sake of the ties of kinship between us.*

Al-Bukhari records that when Sa'id bin Jubayr proposed it meant *kindness to the family of Muhammad*, **Ibn Abbas corrected him**: *you have jumped to a hasty conclusion. There was no clan among Quraysh to whom the Prophet ﷺ did not have some tie of kinship.* The meaning is: **that you uphold the kinship that exists between me and you.**

And the closing promise — *whoever earns a good deed, We give him increase of good in it* (cf. 4:40) — with the two names: *Ghafur* (He forgives many bad deeds) and *Shakur* (He multiplies small good ones).

## 10. Repentance, Answered Prayer, and Measured Provision (42:24–28)

On the accusation that he fabricated the Qur'an: *if Allah willed, He could seal up your heart* — i.e. cause you to forget what has already come to you. Ibn Kathir supports this with 69:44–47, the passage about the false saying and the severed life-artery: **We would have wrought the utmost vengeance, and no one among mankind could have protected him.** Instead, *Allah wipes out falsehood and establishes the truth by His Words* — by His evidence and signs. And *He knows what is in the breasts* — all that is hidden in the hearts of men.

**On repentance** (42:25), the hadith of Anas, from Sahih Muslim — the man who loses his riding beast in a barren waste with all his food and water on it, gives up hope, lies down in a tree's shade to die, and then finds it standing beside him. Seizing the reins, delirious with joy, he says: *O Allah, You are my slave and I am Your Lord!* — getting it backwards from sheer joy.

> **Allah is more pleased with the repentance of His servant than that.**

A striking application follows: Hammam bin Al-Harith reports that **Ibn Mas'ud** was asked about a man who commits immoral sin with a woman and then marries her. He said, *there is nothing wrong with that* — and recited this verse.

**On answered prayer** (42:26): As-Suddi and Ibn Jarir read *He answers those who believe* as responding to their supplication — for themselves, their companions and their brothers — *and gives them increase of His bounty.* Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, via Qatadah, takes it further still: they intercede for their brothers, and the *increase* is that they intercede for their brothers' brothers.

**On provision** (42:27):

> *And if Allah were to extend the provision for His servants, they would surely rebel in the earth, but He sends down by measure as He wills.*

Given more than they need, they would transgress against one another in arrogance. So He gives according to what is best for them: **He makes rich those whom it is right should be rich, and poor those whom it is right should be poor.**

**On rain** (42:28): He sends it *after they have despaired* — precisely at the point of hopelessness (cf. 30:49). Qatadah preserves the scene: a man told 'Umar bin Al-Khattab that no rain had come and the people had given up. 'Umar answered, *Rain will be sent upon you* — and recited this verse.

---

## 11. Misfortune, and What It Is For (42:29–31)

Among His signs: the creation of the heavens and the earth **and whatever moving creatures He has dispersed in them both** — angels, men, jinn, and every animal, in all their shapes, colours, languages, natures and kinds, distributed across the regions of both. And despite all that scattering, He is *able to assemble them whenever He wills*: on that Day all will hear one caller and all be seen clearly.

Then a verse Ibn Kathir treats with real care:

> *And whatever of misfortune befalls you, it is because of what your hands have earned. And He pardons much.*

The second half carries the weight. *He pardons much* — of sins; He does not punish you for them (cf. 35:45, *if Allah were to punish men for what they earned, He would not leave a moving creature on the surface of the earth*).

Three hadiths follow, and together they reframe affliction as **expiation rather than mere penalty**:

> **By the One in Whose Hand is my soul, no believer is stricken with fatigue, exhaustion, worry or grief, but Allah will forgive him some of his sins thereby — even a thorn that pricks him.**

> **No physical harm befalls a believer but Allah expiates some of his sins because of it.** (Mu'awiyah bin Abi Sufyan)

> **If a person commits many sins and has nothing that will expiate for them, Allah will test him with some grief that will expiate for them.** (A'ishah)

## 12. Ships Like Mountains (42:32–35)

The sea is subjugated so that ships sail by His command — *like mountains*, said Mujahid, Al-Hasan, As-Suddi and Ad-Dahhak: mountains on the water as there are mountains on land.

*If He wills, He causes the wind to cease* — and they lie motionless on the surface, neither coming nor going. In this are signs **for everyone patient and grateful**: patient in adversity, grateful in ease.

*Or He may destroy them because of what they have earned.* Some scholars read this as drowning; others as a wind so fierce it drives the ships off course, right or left, until they are lost. Ibn Kathir accepts both, because they make the same point from opposite ends: **too little wind and the ships stop; too much and they are destroyed.** By His grace He sends exactly what is needed.

He then extends the image to rain, which is the same mercy calibrated: too much would destroy their houses, too little and their crops and fruits would not grow. And for lands like **Egypt**, which do not need rain, He sends the water from another land altogether — for if rain fell there it would collapse their walls.

*And those who dispute Our Ayat may know there is no place of refuge for them* — no escape from His vengeance; they are subdued by His power.

## 13. The Portrait: What Believers Are Like (42:36–39)

The passage that names the surah, and the closest thing here to a definition of character.

It opens by shrinking the world: *whatever you have been given is but a passing enjoyment of this worldly life* — no matter what you achieve and amass, do not be deceived; it is the lower, transient realm, and it will end. *But what is with Allah is better and more lasting* — **so do not give preference to what is transient over what endures**. For those who believe (i.e. who are patient in forgoing this world's pleasures) and who put their trust in their Lord (so that He helps them do what is obligatory and avoid what is forbidden).

Then the six traits:

1. **They shun the greater sins and Al-Fawahish.**
2. **When they are angry, they forgive.** Ibn Kathir: their very nature dictates tolerance; **vengeance is not in their character**. It is recorded in the Sahih that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ *never took revenge for his own sake* — only when the sacred Laws of Allah were violated.
3. **They answer the Call of their Lord** — following His Messenger ﷺ, obeying his commands, avoiding what he prohibited.
4. **They establish the Salah** — the greatest act of worship of Allah.
5. **Their affairs are by mutual consultation.** They make no decision without consulting one another, helping each other by sharing their views, on wars and other matters (cf. 3:159, *and consult them in the affairs*). The Prophet ﷺ consulted them so they would feel confident. And when 'Umar lay dying after being stabbed, he entrusted the choice of the next Khalifah to **six men who were to consult**: 'Uthman, 'Ali, Talhah, Az-Zubayr, Sa'd and 'Abdur-Rahman bin 'Awf — after which the Companions agreed on 'Uthman.
6. **They spend of what We have bestowed** — kind treatment of Allah's creation, beginning with those closest, then the next closest.

And then the seventh, which is not softness: **when an oppressive wrong is done to them, they take revenge.** Ibn Kathir is precise here — the point is *capacity*. They are not incapable, not helpless; they have the strength to answer aggression. And it is precisely **because they can that their forgiveness means something** — as Yusuf said to his brothers when they were entirely in his hands: *No reproach on you this day; may Allah forgive you* (12:92).

Two instances from the Prophet's ﷺ own life:
- The **eighty men** who came to harm him in the year of Al-Hudaybiyah, camped by the mountain of At-Tan'im. He overpowered them — and set them free.
- **Ghawrath bin Al-Harith**, who unsheathed a sword over him while he slept. The Prophet ﷺ woke to find it pointed at him, rebuked him, the sword dropped — and after telling his Companions what had happened, he forgave the man.

## 14. The Ethics of Retaliation (42:40–43)

> *The recompense for an evil is an evil like thereof; but whoever forgives and makes reconciliation, his reward is with Allah.*

Ibn Kathir sets the whole legal architecture out in one move. **Justice** is prescribed — equality in punishment, *Al-Qisas* (cf. 2:194; 16:126, *punish them with the like of that with which you were afflicted*). But **the better way is recommended** (cf. 5:45, *but if anyone remits the retaliation by way of charity, it shall be expiation for him*).

Forgiveness is not a loss. The hadith:

> **Allah does not increase the person who forgives except in honour.**

And *He likes not the wrongdoers* means the **aggressors** — those who initiate the evil.

Whoever takes revenge after being wronged has no blame; the burden of sin lies only on **those who oppress people and rebel in the earth without justification**. The hadith draws the line exactly:

> **When two persons indulge in abusing each other, the one who initiated it bears the blame — unless the one wronged oversteps the mark in retaliation.**

Ibn Abi Hatim preserves the story of **Muhammad bin Wasi'**, brought before Marwan bin Al-Muhallab, governor of Basrah, who asked what he needed. He asked the governor to be like *the brother of Banu 'Adiy* — Al-'Ala' bin Ziyad, who once wrote to a friend he had appointed to office:

> *If you can, only go to sleep after you make sure there is nothing on your back, your stomach is empty, and your hands are untainted by the blood or wealth of the Muslims. If you do that, then there will be no way of blame against you.*

Marwan said: *He spoke the truth, by Allah, and gave sincere advice.*

And the verdict on all of it: *whoever shows patience and forgives* — bearing the insult patiently and concealing the evil action — **that is truly from the things recommended by Allah.** Sa'id bin Jubayr: one of the things He has enjoined, for which there is great reward and much praise.

---

## 15. "Is There Any Way of Return?" (42:44–48)

Whomever Allah leaves astray has no protector after Him; whomever He guides, none can misguide (cf. 18:17).

Then the scene. The wrongdoers, seeing the torment, wishing they could go back:

> *Is there any way of return?*

Ibn Kathir answers with 6:27–28 — *Would that we were sent back! Then we would not deny the Ayat of our Lord* — and the reply the Qur'an itself gives: **if they were returned, they would revert to exactly what they were forbidden.**

They are brought forward to the Fire *made humble by disgrace* — Mujahid: in a humiliated manner — *looking with stealthy glance*, stealing sideways looks because they are afraid of it. And the thing they fear will happen, and worse.

The believers will say: *the losers are those who lose themselves and their families on the Day of Resurrection* — deprived of every pleasure of the Hereafter, separated from loved ones, companions and relatives, all of them lost at once. And the wrongdoers' torment is *lasting*: everlasting and eternal, with no way out and no escape.

Then the summons to the reader (42:47): **Answer the Call of your Lord before there comes a Day which cannot be averted.** Once the command is issued it comes to pass in the blink of an eye and nothing can prevent it. *No refuge, no denying* — no stronghold to take shelter in, no place to hide, because He encompasses you with His knowledge and power: **you have no refuge from Him except with Him** (cf. 75:10–12).

And if they turn away — *We have not sent you as a Hafiz over them.* You have no power over them (2:272, 13:40). **Your duty is only to convey.**

### The ingratitude of the moment

> *When We cause man to taste mercy from Us, he rejoices; but when evil befalls him because of what his hands sent forth, then man is ingrate!*

Ibn Kathir's diagnosis: **he forgets the previous ease and acknowledges nothing but the present moment.** In comfort he becomes arrogant; in difficulty he despairs.

The hadith he attaches to it is the Prophet's ﷺ address to the women — *give in charity, for I have seen you form the majority of the people of Hell* — with the reason given as *you complain much and are ungrateful to your husbands*:

> **If one of you were treated kindly for an entire lifetime, and then that kindness was lacking for a single day, she would say: "I have never seen anything good from you."**

Ibn Kathir explicitly limits this — *except those whom Allah guides and who are among the people who believe and do righteous deeds* — and sets against it the definition of a believer:

> **If something good happens to him he is thankful, and that is good for him. If something bad happens to him he bears it with patience, and that is good for him. This does not happen to anyone except the believer.**

## 16. Sons, Daughters, and None (42:49–50)

> *He bestows female upon whom He wills, and bestows male upon whom He wills. Or He bestows both males and females, and He renders barren whom He wills.*

Al-Baghawi supplies an example of each category from among the Prophets:

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **Daughters only** | Lut |
| **Sons only** | Ibrahim Al-Khalil |
| **Both** | Muhammad ﷺ |
| **Neither — no offspring** | Yahya and 'Isa |

*Verily, He is the All-Knower* — He knows who deserves to be in which category — *and is Able to do all things*, meaning able to differentiate between people in exactly this way.

Ibn Kathir then pairs this with a second fourfold pattern, on the other side of the generational line. Of 'Isa, Allah said *We wish to appoint him as a sign to mankind* (19:21) — a proof of His power, because human beings were created in **four different ways**:

- **Adam** — from clay, from neither male nor female
- **Hawwa'** — from a male without a female
- **All other people** — from male and female
- **'Isa** — from a female without a male, which completed the sign

One set of four concerns **children**, the other concerns **parents**. *Glory be to the All-Knower Who is Able to do all things.*

## 17. The Three Channels (42:51–53)

The surah ends where it began, now with the mechanism spelled out:

> *It is not given to any human being that Allah should speak to him unless by revelation, or from behind a veil, or that He sends a Messenger to reveal what He wills by His leave.*

**1. By revelation** — cast into the heart, with no doubt that it is from Allah. Ibn Kathir's example (Sahih Ibn Hibban):

> **Ar-Ruh Al-Qudus breathed into my heart that no soul will die until its allotted provision and time have expired — so have Taqwa of Allah, and keep seeking in a good and lawful way.**

**2. From behind a veil** — as with Musa, who asked to see Him afterwards and was not granted it. And the Prophet's ﷺ words to Jabir bin Abdullah about his father, killed at Uhud:

> **Allah never speaks to anyone except from behind a veil — but He spoke to your father directly.**

Ibn Kathir adds the necessary distinction: that occurred in the realm of **Al-Barzakh**, whereas the verse is speaking of this earthly realm.

**3. By a sent Messenger** — as Jibril and the other angels came down to the Prophets.

Then the *Ruh* of 42:52 — which Ibn Kathir identifies as **the Qur'an itself**. *You knew not what is the Book, nor what is Faith* — meaning, in the detail in which they were given to you in the Qur'an. *But We have made it a light wherewith We guide whom We will of Our servants* (cf. 41:44: a guide and healing for believers, deafness and blindness for those who reject it).

*And verily, you are guiding to a straight path* — the correct behaviour — and then the surah defines that path with its final word on the matter: **the path of Allah** — *His Laws which He enjoins* — *to Whom belongs all that is in the heavens and all that is on the earth*: their Lord and Sovereign, controlling and ruling them, **Whose decree cannot be overturned**.

> *Verily, all matters at the end go to Allah.*

All matters come back to Him, and He issues judgement concerning them. Glorified and exalted be He far above all that the evildoers and deniers say.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Ash-Shura is a surah about receiving.** It begins with revelation descending and ends with the three doors it can descend through, and everything in between is about what human beings do with something they did not author.

Three things it wants you to hold:

**The religion is one, and division is never innocent.** What was ordained for Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa and Muhammad ﷺ is a single religion — *establish it, and do not splinter over it.* Laws differ; the object of worship does not. And when people did divide, it was never for lack of knowledge: *they divided not till after knowledge had come to them, through transgression between themselves.*

**Nothing is like Him.** Five words that end all comparison, placed deliberately in the middle of a surah listing His acts — creating, provisioning, forgiving, withholding, answering, deciding — so that none of those acts can be imagined on a human scale.

**Character is the argument.** When the surah finally stops debating, it describes: they shun the great sins, they forgive when angry, they pray, **they consult one another**, they give, and they are strong enough to answer oppression without needing to. The strength matters — Ibn Kathir dwells on it — because forgiveness only counts when retaliation was available. The Prophet ﷺ freed the eighty men who came to harm him, and the man who stood over him with a drawn sword.

And underneath it all, the correction of the question everyone wants to ask. *When will the Hour come?* — **Woe to you! It will certainly come. What have you prepared for it?**
