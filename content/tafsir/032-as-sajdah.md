*Revealed in Makkah · 30 ayat · named for the prostration verse at 32:15 · one of the surahs containing a sajdah of recitation*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

As-Sajdah is short, and it moves in a straight line. It begins with a Book the Makkans called an invention, and ends with those same Makkans sneering *when is this Fath coming?* Everything between those two points is Allah's answer, and the answer is a single long argument about **origins and returns**.

The argument runs: the One who made the heavens and the earth also made you — from clay, from a drop of fluid you would not touch, and then breathed a soul into it and handed you hearing, sight and reason. The One who sends water down onto dead ground and pulls a crop out of it can also pull you out of the dust you scatter into. And in between, His command travels down and your deeds travel back up.

Placed in the middle of that argument is the surah's one moment of stillness — a description of the people who actually believe, defined not by what they say but by **what they do at night when nobody can see them**. Their reward is described the same way: hidden. That correspondence is the heart of the surah.

---

## 1. Its Virtues

Two practices of the Prophet ﷺ are recorded:

- **Fajr on Fridays.** Al-Bukhari (in the Book of the Friday Prayer) and Muslim record from Abu Hurayrah that he ﷺ used to recite this surah together with **Al-Insan (76)** in the Friday dawn prayer.
- **Never sleeping without it.** Imam Ahmad records from Jabir that he ﷺ would not sleep until he had recited **As-Sajdah** and **Al-Mulk (67)**.

---

## 2. The Book Beyond Doubt (32:1–3)

*Alif Lam Mim.* Ibn Kathir declines to repeat himself — the disconnected letters were dealt with at the opening of Al-Baqarah, and he sends the reader there.

**"The revelation of the Book in which there is no doubt"** — meaning, he says, there is no doubt whatsoever *that it was revealed*.

The idolaters answer: *he has fabricated it* — made it up himself. The rebuttal is immediate: it is the truth from your Lord, sent so that **you may warn a people to whom no warner came before you** — the Arabs, who had received no messenger in living memory — *in order that they may be guided*, i.e., that they may follow the truth.

---

## 3. Creator, Controller, and the Traffic Between Heaven and Earth (32:4–6)

Six days, then He *Istawa* over the Throne — Ibn Kathir notes he has discussed this elsewhere and does not reopen it.

**"You have none, besides Him, as a protector or an intercessor."** He is the sole Sovereign in control of all affairs, the Creator and Controller of all things. There is no creator besides Him, and **no intercessor except one He permits**. The following rebuke — *will you not then remember?* — is aimed, Ibn Kathir says, at people who worship others alongside Him and place their trust in others besides Him. He is exalted above having any equal, partner, supporter, rival or peer.

**The two-way traffic (32:5).** His command descends from above the heavens down to the furthest boundary of the seventh earth (compare 65:12). And deeds are carried *up*, to the place of recording above the lowest heaven.

The distances Ibn Kathir gives:

| | |
|---|---|
| Heaven to earth | five hundred years' travel |
| Thickness of a heaven | five hundred years |
| The angel's actual crossing | **the blink of an eye** — Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak |

**"The All-Mighty, the Most Merciful."** Ibn Kathir pauses here for one of his finest lines. He is Almighty *in* His mercy and Most Merciful *in* His might — and this is the perfection: might combined with mercy, mercy combined with might, **for He is Merciful without any hint of weakness**.

---

## 4. The Making of a Human Being (32:7–9)

**"Who made everything He has created good."** Malik, from Zayd bin Aslam: He created everything well and in a goodly fashion.

Then, having spoken of the heavens and the earth, Allah turns to man — and gives the sequence in four steps:

1. **Clay** — the father of mankind, Adam.
2. **Despised water** — his offspring reproduce from a *Nutfah*, from the loins of men and from between the ribs of women.
3. **Proportion** — He shaped him and made him upright.
4. **The soul, and the three faculties** — hearing, sight, and *al-af'idah*, which Ibn Kathir glosses in a single word: **reason**.

**"Little is the thanks you give."** These are strengths Allah provided, and Ibn Kathir defines the fortunate man precisely: the one who **uses them to worship and obey his Lord**.

---

## 5. "When We Are Lost in the Earth" (32:10–11)

The objection is physical: our bodies will be scattered, disintegrated, dispersed into the soil — and *then* we are made new?

Ibn Kathir concedes the objection is reasonable **from inside their own capabilities** — measured against human power it *is* unlikely. But the measure is wrong. The One who created them from nothing says to a thing *Be*, and it is. Their real problem is stated by Allah Himself: they deny the meeting with their Lord.

**The angel of death.** Ibn Kathir reads the verse plainly: the angel of death is a specific individual among the angels, as the hadith of Al-Bara' (quoted in his tafsir of Surah Ibrahim) also indicates. Some reports name him **`Izra'il** — the view of Qatadah and others.

He has **helpers**: they draw the soul out from the rest of the body until it reaches the throat, and then the angel of death takes it. Mujahid adds an image: the earth is gathered together for him **like a platter**, from which he takes whatever he wants.

---

## 6. Heads Hanging (32:12–14)

The criminals stand before their Lord with their heads bowed — humiliated, brought low, and, Ibn Kathir specifies, **in shame**.

Their plea has three parts: *we have now seen and heard* (now we hear what You say and we will obey); *send us back*; *we now believe with certainty*.

He gathers the parallel verses to show this is a fixed pattern:

- **19:38** — how clearly they will see and hear on the Day they appear before Us.
- **67:10** — *had we but listened or used our intelligence, we would not be among the people of the Blaze.*
- **6:27** — held over the Fire: *would that we were sent back; then we would not deny the signs of our Lord.*

And the verdict on the plea: the Lord knows that **if He returned them, they would behave exactly as before**.

**32:13 — the Word that took effect.** Had He willed, He would have given every soul its guidance (compare 10:99) — but the decree stands that Hell will be filled with jinn and men together, from both classes, with no escape and no way out. Ibn Kathir breaks off here to seek refuge with Allah from it.

**32:14 — forgotten.** Taste it *because you forgot the meeting of this Day*: you denied it, believed it would never come, tried to forget it and behaved as though you had. And *We too will forget you* means — carefully — **We will deal with you as though We had forgotten you**. Nothing escapes Allah's attention. The punishment is made to fit the crime (compare 45:34).

---

## 7. The Sajdah Verse: Who Actually Believes (32:15)

*Only those believe in Our signs who, when reminded of them, fall down prostrate, and glorify the praises of their Lord, and are not proud.*

- **They believe** = they accept them as true.
- **They fall prostrate** = they listen and obey, **in word and in deed**.
- **They are not proud** = they are not too proud to follow and submit — unlike the ignorant among the rebellious disbelievers, of whom Allah says: those who scorn My worship will enter Hell in humiliation (40:60).

---

## 8. Sides That Leave the Bed (32:16)

*Their sides forsake their beds, to invoke their Lord in fear and hope, and they spend out of what We have bestowed on them.*

This is the verse Ibn Kathir gives the most room to.

**What it means.** They pray the voluntary night prayer, forgoing sleep and the comfort of a bed. **Mujahid and Al-Hasan**: it refers to the voluntary night prayer. **Ad-Dahhak** offers a second reading: `Isha in congregation and Fajr in congregation.

**Fear and hope** — fear of His punishment, hope of His reward. **They spend** — they perform both the obligatory and the voluntary.

### The hadith of Mu`adh bin Jabal (Imam Ahmad)

Walking beside the Prophet ﷺ on a journey one morning, Mu`adh asked for a deed that would admit him to Paradise and keep him from Hell. The answer came in four stages:

**i. The great matter, made easy.** *You have asked about something great, and it is easy for the one for whom Allah makes it easy* — worship Allah and associate nothing with Him, establish the prayer, pay Zakah, fast Ramadan, make Hajj.

**ii. The gates of goodness.**
- Fasting is a shield.
- Charity wipes out sin.
- The prayer of a man in the depths of the night.

— and here he ﷺ recited this very verse, *their sides forsake their beds*, through to *as a reward for what they used to do*.

**iii. The head, the pillars, the pinnacle.** The head of the matter is **Islam**; its pillars are the **prayers**; its pinnacle is **Jihad** in the way of Allah.

**iv. The thing it all hangs on.** He took hold of his own tongue and said, *restrain this*. Mu`adh asked whether they would really be held accountable for what they say. The reply: *May your mother be bereft of you, Mu`adh — will people be thrown into Hell on their faces for anything but the harvest of their tongues?*

Also recorded by At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah; At-Tirmidhi graded it Hasan Sahih.

---

## 9. The Reward No Eye Has Seen (32:17)

*No person knows what is kept hidden for them of delights of eyes.*

No one knows the scale of what Allah has concealed for them — everlasting joy and delights no one has ever laid eyes on.

Ibn Kathir then draws the connection that gives the passage its symmetry: **because they concealed their deeds, Allah conceals their reward.** A fitting recompense, matched to the act. **Al-Hasan Al-Basri**: if people hide their good deeds, Allah hides for them what no eye has seen and what has never crossed a human mind.

**The hadith Qudsi** (Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah, who cited this verse as its proof): *I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and what has never crossed the mind of man.* Also in Muslim and At-Tirmidhi (Hasan Sahih). Another wording in Al-Bukhari adds that everything you have seen is, beside it, nothing.

And a further report from Abu Hurayrah: whoever enters Paradise lives in ease and never knows want; **his clothes never wear out and his youth never fades** (Muslim).

---

## 10. The Believer and the Rebel Are Not Equal (32:18–22)

On the Day of Judgement, in His justice and generosity, Allah will not judge those who believed and followed His Messengers the way He judges those who rebelled and rejected them. The same point recurs at **45:21**, **38:28** and **59:20**.

**Ata' bin Yasar, As-Suddi and others** mention the verse came down concerning **`Ali bin Abi Talib and `Uqbah bin Abi Mu`it** — and Allah then judged between the two types explicitly:

**The believers (32:19)** — hearts that believed in the signs, and limbs that acted on them. *Gardens of Abode*: dwellings, houses, lofty apartments. The word *nuzul* means **the welcome laid on for an honoured guest**.

**The rebels (32:20)** — the Fire, and every attempt to escape it ends with them thrown back in (compare 22:22). **Al-Fudayl bin `Iyad**: by Allah, their hands tied, their feet chained, the flames lifting them and the angels striking them down.

**32:21 — the near, lighter torment.** **Ibn Abbas**: the diseases and troubles of this world, and what befalls people in it — a test from Allah **so that they will turn back to Him**. Narrated similarly from Ubayy bin Ka`b, Abu Al-`Aliyah, Al-Hasan, Ibrahim An-Nakha`i, Ad-Dahhak, `Alqamah, `Atiyah, Mujahid, Qatadah, `Abd Al-Karim Al-Jazari and Khusayf. The purpose is stated in the verse itself: *in order that they may return.*

**32:22 — the worst wrongdoer.** No one wrongs himself more than the man whom Allah reminds of His signs, explains them clearly to him, and who then neglects, ignores and turns away — forgetting them as though he had never known them. **Qatadah**: beware of turning away from the remembrance of Allah, for whoever turns away from remembering Him will be the most misguided, the most in need, and the most laden with sin.

---

## 11. Musa's Book, and the Price of Leadership (32:23–25)

**"Be not in doubt of meeting him."** **Qatadah** says this refers to the **Night of Isra'** — and reports through Abu Al-`Aliyah Ar-Riyahi that Ibn Abbas relayed the Prophet's ﷺ own account of that night: he saw **Musa**, tall, brown-skinned, with curly hair, resembling the men of Shanu'ah; **`Isa**, of medium height, ruddy-white, with lank hair; and he saw **Malik**, the keeper of Hell, and the **Dajjal**. So the meeting was real: he saw Musa and met him.

**"And We made from among them leaders, guiding by Our command, when they were patient and had certainty in Our signs."**

Ibn Kathir reads this as a formula with conditions and an expiry date:

- **The conditions** — they were patient in keeping Allah's commands and avoiding what He forbade, they believed His Messengers and followed what was brought. So leaders arose among them, calling to good, enjoining right, forbidding wrong.
- **The expiry** — then they altered and distorted the words, moving them from their places. **They lost that position and their hearts hardened**; no sound deeds left, and no sound beliefs.

On *when they were patient*, **Qatadah and Sufyan** (and Al-Hasan bin Salih): when they patiently **shunned the temptations of this world**. Sufyan's conclusion is blunt — *a man cannot be an example to be followed unless he renounces the temptation of this world.*

Compare 45:16–17: they were given the Scripture, understanding of its laws, prophethood, good provision, and preference above the nations — and still differed. Your Lord will settle those differences on the Day of Resurrection.

---

## 12. Empty Towns and Living Ground (32:26–27)

**Two proofs, one drawn from ruin and one from growth.**

**The ruins (32:26).** Will these deniers not learn from the nations destroyed before them? The disbelievers of Makkah **walk about in the very places those people lived** — and see none of them, for they are gone, *as if they had never lived there* (11:68). Ibn Kathir lines up the parallels: can you find a single one of them, or hear so much as a whisper (19:98)? These are their houses in ruin (27:52). And 22:45–46, ending on the line he clearly wants left ringing: **it is not the eyes that go blind, but the hearts in the breasts**.

The lesson is in both directions — how the deniers ended, and how those who believed were saved.

**The growth (32:27).** Water driven to *al-ard al-juruz* — dead ground where nothing grows (compare 18:8: We shall make all that is on it a bare dry soil). Whether from the sky or carried down by rivers from the mountains, it arrives at the lands that need it **at the times they need it**. And out of that dead ground comes a crop that feeds their cattle and feeds them. *Will they not then see?* — compare 80:24–25: **let man look at his food.**

---

## 13. "When Is This Fath?" (32:28–30)

The taunt is impatience dressed as argument: *when do you get your victory over us, Muhammad? All we see of you and your companions is that you are hiding, afraid and humiliated.*

**Ibn Kathir's correction.** Those who take *Al-Fath* here to mean **the Conquest of Makkah** have, in his words, gone too far and made a grievous mistake — and his evidence is the event itself. On the day Makkah was conquered, the Prophet ﷺ **accepted the Islam of nearly two thousand** freed Makkan prisoners. If this verse meant that day, their belief would have profited them nothing and he would not have accepted it.

So *Al-Fath* here means **Judgement** — the sense the word carries in 26:118 (Nuh: *judge between me and them*), 34:26, 14:15, 2:89 and 8:19. The Day when the wrath and punishment of Allah arrive, in this world and the next, and belief comes too late — no profit, and no respite (compare 40:83–85).

**The instruction (32:30).** *So turn aside from them and await.* Turn away from the idolaters and deliver what was revealed to you (compare 6:106). Wait until Allah fulfils what He promised and grants you victory over those who oppose you — **for He never breaks His promise**.

**"Verily, they too are awaiting."** Both sides are waiting. They are waiting and plotting — *a poet; we await for him some calamity of time* (52:30). You will see the outcome of your patience and the fulfilment of your Lord's promise in your victory over them; and they will see the outcome of their waiting for something bad to befall you, in the punishment that comes upon them instead.

*Sufficient unto us is Allah, and He is the Best Disposer of affairs.*

---

## The Thread

As-Sajdah takes the largest possible frame — heavens, earth, six days, a Throne, a command that travels a thousand years in the blink of an eye — and narrows it, without a break, onto a man lying in bed at night deciding whether to get up.

That is the surah's method. The deniers' objection is about **scale**: our bodies will scatter into the earth, so how could we be reassembled? The answer is not a counter-argument but a change of measure. Look at what you were made from — clay, then a drop of fluid so unremarkable the Qur'an calls it despised — and then look at what you were given: a soul, hearing, sight, reason. Look at ground with nothing living in it, and the crop standing on it after the water comes. The One who does that is not troubled by your dust.

And between the making and the returning, the surah names exactly one distinguishing mark of the people who believe. Not a claim, not an argument — **their sides leave their beds**. It is the most private act in the religion, done in the dark, witnessed by nobody. Which is why the reward is described the same way: sealed, unseen, unimagined. *Because they concealed their deeds, Allah concealed their reward.*

Everything else in the surah is the alternative. Heads hanging, asking to be sent back. Walking through the ruins of people who asked the same thing. Diseases and troubles sent as the small punishment precisely **so that they may return** — the mercy inside the affliction — and then, for those who still turn away, the greater one. Two men, two ends, and the flat refusal at 32:18: *they are not equal.*

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah As-Sajdah; all praise is due to Allah, and all favours come from Him alone.*
