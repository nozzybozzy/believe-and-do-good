*Revealed in Makkah · 8 ayat · "The Earthquake" — named for the convulsion in its first verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Az-Zalzalah is eight verses long and it contains an entire theology of accountability. Ibn Kathir's treatment makes the shape of it unmistakable: the surah begins with the largest event imaginable and ends with the smallest unit of measurement in the language.

It opens with the **earth convulsing** and vomiting up its contents — its dead, and its buried treasure. Then it does something no other passage quite does: it gives the earth a **voice**. The ground you walked on, prayed on, sinned on, will be told *speak*, and it will testify — naming what was done on its surface, on which day. Mankind, meanwhile, proceeds outward from the Reckoning in **scattered groups**, sorted at last into what they actually were.

And then the closing couplet, which is why this surah is what it is:

> *So whosoever does good equal to the weight of a speck of dust shall see it. And whosoever does evil equal to the weight of a speck of dust shall see it.*

Ibn Kathir spends the back half of his commentary not on the earthquake but on those two lines — and every hadith he brings is about something tiny. Half a date. A cheerful face. A cup of water from your bucket. A single grape. A sheep's hoof. A small sin. The surah's argument is that **there is no such thing as an amount too small to count**, in either direction, and that people are wrong about this in both directions at once: they dismiss the good they could do as too minor to bother with, and they dismiss the wrong they do as too minor to fear.

The Prophet ﷺ called this surah **comprehensive** — and, as we will see, a man who learned only this one surah and nothing else was told he had succeeded.

## 1. The Man Who Learned Only This (Its Virtues)

Ibn Kathir opens with a long narration from **Imam Ahmad**, on the authority of **Abdullah bin Amr**, and it is worth following in full because the whole point of the surah is embedded in it.

A man came and asked the Prophet ﷺ to teach him what to recite.

- *Recite three from those that begin with* **Alif, Lam, Ra**.
- The man replied: **I have become old, my heart has hardened, and my tongue has become harsh.**
- *Then recite from those that begin with* **Ha-Mim**.
- He said the same thing again.
- *Recite three from the* **Musabbihat**.
- He said the same thing again.

Then the man asked for something else entirely: *give me something comprehensive of all of these.* So the Prophet ﷺ taught him **Az-Zalzalah**.

When he had finished reciting it to him, the man said: *By He Who sent you with the truth as a Prophet, I will never add anything else to it.* Then he turned and left, and the Prophet ﷺ said, twice:

> **Aflaha ar-Ruwaygil** — *The little man has been successful, the little man has been successful.*

**The tail of the hadith** matters as much as the head. The Prophet ﷺ called him back and mentioned that he had been commanded to celebrate `Id Al-Adha, which Allah made a festival for this Ummah. The man asked what he should do if the only animal he could get hold of was a **borrowed milking she-camel** — should he slaughter that?

> *No. But you should cut your hair, clip your nails, trim your mustache, shave your pubic area — and that will be the completion of your sacrifice with Allah, the Mighty and Majestic.*

Recorded also by **Abu Dawud** and **An-Nasa'i**.

Notice what this narration is doing before the surah has even been explained. A man with nothing — no memory, no strength of tongue, no camel — is given eight verses and a handful of grooming acts, and is pronounced **successful**. That is Az-Zalzalah's doctrine applied to a person: the small offering, sincerely given, is registered.

## 2. The Earth Convulses and Empties Itself (99:1–2)

> *When the earth quakes with its Zilzal. And when the earth throws out its burdens.*

**Ibn Abbas** on the first: it means the earth will **move from beneath it**.

On the second: it will **throw forth what is inside it of the dead**. More than one of the Salaf said this, and Ibn Kathir cross-references two verses that describe the same moment:

- *O mankind! Have Taqwa of your Lord! Verily, the earthquake of the Hour is a terrible thing* **(22:1)**
- *And when the earth is stretched forth, and has cast out all that was in it and became empty* **(84:3–4)**

### The gold and silver nobody wants

Then comes one of the most quietly devastating hadiths in the Sahih. **Muslim** records from **Abu Hurayrah** that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> *The earth will throw out the pieces of its liver — gold and silver will come out like columns.*
>
> - A **murderer** will come and say: *I killed for this.*
> - The one who **broke the ties of kinship** will say: *For this I severed the ties of kinship.*
> - The **thief** will say: *For this I got my hands amputated.*
>
> *Then they will leave it there, and no one will take anything from it.*

The wealth is finally lying in the open, in unlimited quantity, and it is **worthless**. Every price that was paid for it is itemised out loud by the man who paid it, and then everyone walks away. This is the surah showing you the true exchange rate before the Reckoning even begins.

## 3. Man's Bewilderment (99:3)

> *And man will say: "What is the matter with it?"*

Ibn Kathir: he will be **baffled by its situation** after it had always been stable, settled and firm beneath him — he had lived his whole life standing on its surface.

This is the alteration of the state of all things: the earth moving and shaking with the inescapable quaking Allah prepared for it; then throwing out its dead, from the first generation to the last. At that moment people are dumbfounded — **the earth changed into other than the earth, and the heavens as well** — and then they are presented before Allah, **the One, the Irresistible**.

## 4. The Earth Testifies (99:4–5)

> *That Day it will declare its information. Because your Lord will inspire it.*

Ibn Kathir: it means the earth will **speak of what people did upon its surface**.

**Imam Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi and An-Nasa'i** all record from **Abu Hurayrah** that the Prophet ﷺ recited this ayah and then asked: *Do you know what its information is?* They said: Allah and His Messenger know best. He said:

> *Verily, its information is that it will testify against every male and female servant about what they did upon its surface. It will say that he did such and such on such and such day. So this is its information.*

At-Tirmidhi graded it **Sahih Gharib**.

On *your Lord will inspire it*, Ibn Kathir says the apparent meaning is that **He will permit it** to speak. Three supporting reports:

| Authority | Comment |
|---|---|
| **Ibn Abbas** (via Ikrimah, via Shabib bin Bishr) | Its Lord will say to it, *"Speak"* — so it will speak |
| **Mujahid** | He commands it (i.e. to speak) |
| **Al-Qurazi** | He will command it to separate from them |

The theological weight here is easy to miss. Every private act had a **witness** the whole time, and the witness was the floor.

## 5. Scattered Groups (99:6)

> *That Day mankind will proceed in scattered groups — Ashtat — that they may be shown their deeds.*

**Proceed** means they will return from the station of the Judgement **in separate groups**. Ibn Kathir spells out the sorting: they will be divided into types and categories — **the miserable and the happy**, those commanded to Paradise and those commanded to the Hellfire.

**As-Suddi**: *Ashtat* means **sects**.

And *that they may be shown their deeds* means: so that they may see the recompense for what they did in this life, of good and of evil.

## 6. The Weight of a Speck of Dust (99:7–8)

> *So whosoever does good equal to the weight of a speck of dust shall see it.*
> *And whosoever does evil equal to the weight of a speck of dust shall see it.*

This is where Ibn Kathir slows down and gathers everything he has.

### The three kinds of horsemen — and "the single, comprehensive Ayah"

**Al-Bukhari** records from **Abu Hurayrah** that the Prophet ﷺ said the horses are for three men: for one a **reward**, for one a **shield**, for one a **burden**.

- **A reward** — for the man who keeps them in the way of Allah. They graze their whole lives in a pasture in preparation for Jihad, and whatever happens to them there is written as good deeds for him. If they are then used in a noble battle, even their **hoof prints and their dung** count as good deeds. If they pass a stream and drink, though he never intended to water them, that is counted too.
- **A shield** — for the man who keeps them to maintain himself and stay independent of begging, and who does not forget **Allah's right upon their necks and their backs** (their Zakah). They shield him from the Fire.
- **A burden** — for the man who keeps them to boast, brag, and show off.

Then the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was asked about **donkeys**, and he answered:

> *Allah has not revealed anything concerning them except this single, comprehensive Ayah:* **So whosoever does good equal to the weight of a speck of dust shall see it. And whosoever does evil equal to the weight of a speck of dust shall see it.**

Muslim also records it. That phrase — *al-Fadhdhah al-Jami`ah*, the **single, all-encompassing verse** — is Ibn Kathir's hinge. Where no specific ruling exists, this ayah covers the case. It is a universal law of accounting.

### No good deed is too small to do

- **Al-Bukhari, from `Adi**: *Fear the Fire, even if by giving half a date in charity — and even by saying a single word of good.*
- **From the Sahih**: *Do not underrate any good act, even if it is offering drinking water from your bucket to one who is seeking a drink, or meeting your brother with a cheerful face.*
- **From the Sahih**: *O party of believing women! None of you should belittle a gift sent by her neighbour, even if it is a **Firsan** of a sheep* — Ibn Kathir notes that Firsan here means its **hoof**.
- **In another hadith**: *Give something to the beggar, even if it is a burnt hoof.*
- **A'ishah** gave **a single grape** in charity, and then said: *"To how much dust is it equivalent?"* — she was counting in the surah's own units.

### No small sin is too small to fear

The mirror image, and Ibn Kathir gives it equal space:

- **Imam Ahmad**, from A'ishah (via Awf bin Al-Harith bin At-Tufayl) — the Prophet ﷺ used to say: *O A'ishah! Beware of the sins that are belittled, for indeed they will be taken account of by Allah.* Also recorded by **An-Nasa'i** and **Ibn Majah**.
- **Imam Ahmad**, from **Abdullah bin Mas`ud**: *Beware of the sins that are belittled. For verily, they are gathered in a man until they destroy him.*

And the parable the Prophet ﷺ used for those small sins, which closes the surah's commentary:

> They are like **a people who settle in barren land**. Their leader orders the men to go out, one at a time, and each to bring back **a single stick** — until they have gathered a large pile. Then they kindle a fire with it, and it **burns everything thrown into it**.

No one stick could burn anything. The pile incinerates.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **The surah is called comprehensive for a reason.** A man who could remember nothing else was given these eight verses, said he would never add to them, and was told twice over that he had **succeeded**.
- **The earth is a witness, not a stage.** It will be told to speak, and it will name what was done on it, and on which day.
- **The buried gold surfaces on that Day and nobody picks it up.** The murderer, the kin-breaker and the thief each announce the price they paid — and then walk past it.
- **Nothing is below the threshold.** Half a date, a good word, a cup of water from your bucket, a cheerful face, a sheep's hoof, one grape. The bar for a good deed is set at *the weight of a speck of dust*, which is to say there is no bar.
- **And the same rule runs in the other direction.** Small sins are gathered in a man until they destroy him — one stick at a time, until the fire is big enough to burn everything thrown in.
- **Where no other ruling exists, this ayah rules.** *Allah has not revealed anything concerning them except this single, comprehensive Ayah.* When you don't know how something will be judged, judge it by the dust-speck.
