*Revealed in Makkah · 22 ayat · named for the Buruj of 85:1 — the great stations of the sky, sworn upon in the opening oath*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Buruj is the surah for believers who **lose**.

Not who are tested and then rescued — who lose. The People of the Ditch are burned alive for no offence except faith, and the surah does not undo it. There is no last-minute deliverance, no army over the hill. The fire is lit, the believers are thrown in, and the tyrants sit at the edge and **watch**.

What the surah does instead is change the frame. It opens with three oaths, and one of them is on *the Witness and the Witnessed* — and then the word comes back, deliberately, in the worst possible place: the murderers **sat as witnesses** over what they did. The surah lets that stand for exactly one verse before answering it: **"And Allah is Witness over everything."** Nothing in that ditch went unseen. The persecutors thought they were the observers; they were the observed.

From there the surah stops arguing and simply recites **who Allah is** — Almighty, Worthy of all praise, Owner of the heavens and the earth, Oft-Forgiving, *Al-Wadud*, Owner of the Glorious Throne, **Doer of whatever He intends**. Then it produces the precedents: Fir'awn and Thamud, hosts far larger than a Yemeni king's, and where are they. And it closes on the one thing tyrants can never reach — **a Glorious Qur'an, in a Guarded Tablet**, safe from increase, decrease, distortion or change.

The believers in the ditch left no monuments. Their story is in the Preserved Tablet, and the man who burned them is a footnote in it.

## 1. The Three Oaths (85:1–3)

> *By the heaven holding the Buruj. And by the Promised Day. And by the Witness and by the Witnessed.*

Ibn Kathir brings a hadith of **Abu Hurayrah** (recorded by Ibn Abi Hatim, and by Ibn Khuzaymah) in which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ identifies each of the last two oaths:

| Oath | Identified as |
|---|---|
| **The Promised Day** | The Day of Judgement |
| **The Witness** (*Shahid*) | **Friday** |
| **The Witnessed** (*Mashhud*) | **The Day of 'Arafah** |

And about Friday specifically:

- **The sun does not rise or set on a day better than Friday.**
- In it there is **an hour** in which no Muslim servant asks Allah for some good except that Allah gives it to him.
- And he seeks refuge in it from no evil except that Allah protects him from it.

So the oath is sworn on the sky above, on the Day everything is settled, and on the two days of the year and the week when the servant is closest to being answered. Then the subject turns to men who were killed for asking of Allah at all.

## 2. The People of the Ditch (85:4–7)

> *Cursed were the People of the Ditch. Of fire fed with fuel. When they sat by it. And they witnessed what they were doing against the believers.*

**Ukhdud** — plural *Akhadid* — means **ditches dug in the ground**.

Ibn Kathir sets out the sequence plainly, and the plainness is the point:

1. A group of disbelievers **went after those among them who believed** in Allah, and tried to force them off their religion.
2. The believers **refused to recant**.
3. So they **dug a ditch**, lit a fire in it, and **prepared fuel to keep it blazing**.
4. They tried once more to make them apostatise. They still refused.
5. **So they threw them into the fire.**

*"And they witnessed what they were doing against the believers"* — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, **they were witnesses to what was done to these believers**. They sat and watched it happen.

---

## 3. "They Had No Fault Except That They Believed" (85:8–9)

> *And they had no fault except that they believed in Allah, the Almighty, Worthy of all praise!*

This is the verse the surah turns on. **They committed no crime in the eyes of their killers except faith in Allah** — and Ibn Kathir immediately unpacks the two names attached to Him here, because they are the answer to the whole scene:

- **Al-'Aziz — the Almighty** — *Who does not treat unjustly those who desire to be with Him.*
- **Al-Hamid — Worthy of all praise** — praiseworthy **in all of His statements, actions, legislation and decrees**.

And then a sentence of remarkable honesty:

> He decreed what happened to these servants of His at the hands of the disbelievers — and He is the Most Mighty, the Most Praiseworthy — **even though the reason for this decree is unknown to many people.**

Ibn Kathir does not pretend the wisdom is obvious. He affirms two things at once: it was decreed, and it was praiseworthy — and the *why* is largely hidden from us. That is the mature form of the answer, and the surah supplies it before it supplies any comfort.

> *To Whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth! And Allah is Witness over everything.*

Among His perfect attributes: He **owns** the heavens, the earth, all that is in them and between them. And His witnessing means **nothing is concealed or hidden from Him** anywhere in them.

## 4. The Sorcerer, the Monk and the Boy

This is the long hadith of **Suhayb** recorded by Imam Ahmad and by **Muslim at the end of his Sahih** — the story Ibn Kathir gives as the background to the Ditch. It is worth holding its shape, because almost every element of it is a lesson.

**The apprenticeship.** An ageing sorcerer asks his king for a boy to train. On the road to the sorcerer, the boy passes **a monk**, sits with him, and is captivated by his speech. The monk teaches him how to manage both sides: *tell the sorcerer your family delayed you, tell your family the sorcerer delayed you.*

**The test he sets himself.** A huge, terrible creature blocks the road so people cannot pass. The boy says: *Today I shall know whether the sorcerer is better or the monk is better.* He takes a stone: **"O Allah, if the deeds of the monk are more beloved to You than those of the sorcerer, kill this creature so the people can cross."** He throws it. The creature dies. The people cross.

**The monk's response** is one of the great lines in the hadith literature:

> *"O my son! Today you are better than I, and you have achieved what I see. **You will be put to trial. And when you are, do not inform them about me.**"*

The monk knows exactly what is coming, and does not warn the boy away from it.

**The healing.** The boy begins curing congenital blindness, leprosy and other diseases. A blind courtier of the king brings gifts on condition of a cure. The boy's reply is the theological centre of the story:

> *"I do not cure anybody. It is only Allah who cures. If you believe in Allah and supplicate to Him, He will cure you."*

The man believes, supplicates, and is healed.

**The unravelling.** The king asks who restored his sight. *"My Lord."* — *"I did."* — *"No: my Lord and your Lord, Allah."* — *"Do you have a lord besides me?"* — *"Yes."* He is tortured until he names the boy. The boy is tortured until he names the monk.

**The three executions.** The monk refuses to abandon his religion: a saw is placed in the middle of his head and he falls in two. The formerly blind man refuses: the same. Then the boy:

- Sent to a mountaintop to be thrown off. He prays, ***"O Allah, save me from them by any means You wish."*** The mountain shakes; his executioners fall; he walks back to the king.
- Sent out to sea to be drowned. The same prayer. **They** drown; he returns.

**The boy's bargain.** He then tells the king: *You will not be able to kill me until you do as I order you.*

> *"Gather the people in one elevated place, tie me to the trunk of a tree, take an arrow from my quiver, and say: **'In the Name of Allah, the Lord of the boy.'** If you do this, you will be able to kill me."*

The king does it. The arrow strikes the boy's temple; he puts his hand to the wound and dies. And the crowd — who have just watched the king himself invoke the Lord of the boy — cry out: ***"We believe in the Lord of the boy!"***

The boy spent his life refusing to die and then engineered his own death, because **his death in front of a crowd was worth more than his life**. That is the trade the surah is built around.

**The ditches.** The king is told: *that which you feared has taken place — by Allah, all the people have believed.* So he orders ditches dug at the entrances to the roads and fires kindled in them: **whoever abandons his religion goes free; whoever does not is thrown in.**

And the last image, the one that ends the hadith: **a woman carrying a nursing baby** hesitates at the edge of the fire — and the infant speaks:

> ***"Be patient, mother! For verily, you are following the truth."***

## 5. Ibn Ishaq's Account: Dhu Nuwas and the Twenty Thousand

Ibn Kathir then gives **Muhammad bin Ishaq's** version from his *Sirah*, which differs in detail. In it, the people of **Najran** followed the boy's religion after his murder — which by then was **Christianity**.

- **Dhu Nuwas**, king of Yemen — his name was **Zur'ah**, and in his kingship he was called **Yusuf** — came against them with his army and called them to **Judaism**: convert or die. **They chose death.** He dug a ditch and burned some in the fire, and killed others with the sword, until he had killed **close to twenty thousand** — in one morning.
- He was the son of **Tuban As'ad Abi Karib**, the Tubba' who invaded Al-Madinah and put the covering on the Ka'bah, and who brought back **two rabbis** from the Jews of Al-Madinah — at whose hands part of Yemen had accepted Judaism.
- **One man escaped**: **Daws Dhu Tha'laban**, on horseback. He reached **Caesar**, emperor of Ash-Sham, who wrote to **An-Najashi**, king of Abyssinia, who sent an army of Abyssinian Christians under **Aryat and Abrahah**. They took Yemen from the Jews; **Dhu Nuwas fled, fell into the sea and drowned.**
- Abyssinian Christian rule lasted **seventy years**, until **Sayf bin Dhi Yazin Al-Himyari** overturned it with an army Kisra of Persia sent him — some **seven hundred men taken out of the prisons** — and returned the kingdom to Himyar.

That name **Abrahah** is deliberate. Ibn Kathir signposts it forward: the rest belongs to the tafsir of ***"Have you not seen how your Lord dealt with the Owners of the Elephant?"*** (105:1). The man who came to avenge the People of the Ditch fathered the regime that marched on the Ka'bah. History is longer than any one atrocity, and Allah is dealing with all of it.

## 6. The Sentence, and the Astonishing Offer (85:10)

> *Verily, those who put into trial the believing men and believing women, and then do not turn in repentance, then they will have the torment of Hell, and they will have the punishment of the burning Fire.*

- **"Put into trial" (*fatanu*) means: they burned them.** So said **Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak and Ibn Abza**.
- **"And then do not turn in repentance"** — meaning they do not cease from what they are doing, and **do not regret what they had already done**.
- Two punishments: **the torment of Hell** and **the punishment of the burning Fire** — because, Ibn Kathir notes, **the recompense is of the same type as the deed**. They burned, so they are burned.

But the verse contains a condition, and **Al-Hasan Al-Basri** saw what was inside it:

> *"Look at this generosity and kindness. **These people killed Allah's Awliya' — and He still invites them to repent and seek forgiveness.**"*

The clause *"and then do not turn in repentance"* means the door was never shut. Men who threw believing men and women into a fire pit were still, at that moment, one act of repentance away from being forgiven.

## 7. The Names That Answer the Ditch (85:11–16)

The surah's reply to the whole scene is not an argument. It is a list of who Allah is.

> *Verily, those who believe and do righteous good deeds, for them will be Gardens under which rivers flow. That is the supreme success.* **(85:11)**

The exact opposite of what He has prepared for His enemies of Fire and Hell. The ditch was a pit of fire; the Garden runs with rivers.

> *Verily, the punishment of your Lord is severe and painful.* **(85:12)**

His vengeance upon His enemies — those who rejected His Messengers and opposed His command — is **severe, great and strong**. He is the Owner of power, Most Strong: **whatever He wants is however He wants it, in the blink of an eye or swifter.**

> *Verily, He it is Who begins and repeats.* **(85:13)**

From His perfect strength: He begins the creation and repeats it just as He began it — **without opposition or resistance**. The resurrection of those burned believers costs Him nothing.

> *And He is Oft-Forgiving, Al-Wadud.* **(85:14)**

He forgives the sin of **whoever repents to Him and humbles himself — no matter what the sin may be**. And on the name **Al-Wadud**, **Ibn 'Abbas** and others said: **it means Al-Habib, the Loving.**

Placed here, immediately after the executioners, this is not a soft name. It is the hardest thing in the surah to absorb.

> *Owner of the Throne, Al-Majid.* **(85:15)**

Owner of the Mighty Throne that is **above all of creation**. Ibn Kathir notes the word *Al-Majid* is recited two ways — with a *dhammah* (**Al-Majidu**), making glory an attribute of **the Lord**, or with a *kasrah* (**Al-Majid**), making it a description of **the Throne**. **Both meanings are correct.**

> *Doer of what He intends.* **(85:16)**

Whatever He wants, He does; **no one can counter His ruling**, and He is not asked about what He does — for His greatness, His power, His wisdom and His justice.

And Ibn Kathir attaches to this a small scene that reframes it entirely. **Abu Bakr As-Siddiq**, in his final illness, was asked: *"Has a doctor seen you?"*

> *"Yes."*
> *"What did he say to you?"*
> **"He said: 'I am the Doer of whatever I intend.'"**

The dying Khalifah was not talking about the physician.

## 8. The Precedents, and the Tablet (85:17–22)

> *Has the story reached you of the hosts, of Fir'awn and Thamud?* **(85:17–18)**

Meaning: **has the news reached you of the torment Allah caused to befall them**, and of the punishment He sent down that **no one was able to ward off**? This confirms *"the punishment of your Lord is severe and painful"* — **when He seizes the wrongdoer, He seizes him with a severe and painful punishment.** It is the seizing of One Most Mighty, Most Powerful.

> *Nay! The disbelievers persisted in denying.* **(85:19)** — they are in **doubt, suspicion, disbelief and rebellion**.

> *And Allah encompasses them from behind!* **(85:20)** — He has **power over them and is able to compel them. They cannot escape Him or evade Him.**

> *Nay! This is a Glorious Qur'an, in Al-Lawh Al-Mahfuz!* **(85:21–22)**

**Magnificent and noble** — and preserved **among the most high gathering, guarded from any increase, decrease, distortion or change.**

The surah that began by swearing on the sky ends by pointing at the one record no king can burn.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Buruj is the surah that refuses to make victory the measure.** The believers in the ditch had no rescue, no reprieve, no vindication in their own lifetimes — and the Qur'an calls them the ones for whom Gardens flow with rivers, and calls their killers *cursed*. The verdict was passed in the Preserved Tablet long before it was visible on the ground.

Three lines to carry:

- **"They had no fault except that they believed in Allah, the Almighty, Worthy of all praise."** The only charge that could be brought was faith. Ibn Kathir attaches the names *Al-'Aziz Al-Hamid* to that sentence on purpose — and then admits the wisdom behind the decree **is unknown to many people**. Faith does not require the explanation; it requires trust in the One who has it.
- **"Be patient, mother! For verily, you are following the truth."** Spoken by an infant at the edge of a fire. The comfort offered is not that the fire will be avoided, but that the direction is right.
- **"These people killed Allah's Awliya' — and He still invites them to repent."** Al-Hasan Al-Basri's observation is the mercy hidden inside a verse of punishment. If that door was open to them, it is open to you.

And the last word belongs to Abu Bakr on his deathbed, naming his physician: ***"I am the Doer of whatever I intend."*** Nothing in that ditch, and nothing in your life, is outside of that.
