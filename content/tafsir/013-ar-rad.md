*Revealed in Makkah · 43 ayat · named for the thunder that glorifies its Lord (13:13)*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ar-Ra'd is short, and it is built almost entirely out of **evidence**. Where Yunus argued, Ar-Ra'd points. Raised heavens, subjected sun and moon, neighbouring tracts of soil watered by one rain and yielding different fruit, guardian angels, lightning that frightens the traveller and cheers the farmer, thunder that praises — every few verses the surah simply gestures at something and lets it testify.

The argument underneath is always the same: **the One who did all this is One, and He can raise the dead.** Around that spine, the surah alternates between two portraits — the blessed and the wretched — and returns repeatedly to a single consolation for the Prophet ﷺ: *your job is to deliver, not to convert.*

---

## 1. "Most Men Believe Not" (13:1)

The surah opens with the disconnected letters. Ibn Kathir refers back to Al-Baqarah rather than repeating himself, but restates the principle: **surahs beginning with these letters affirm the Qur'an's inimitability** — that it is revelation, beyond doubt or denial.

Then the sting. What has been revealed to you is the truth — *but most men believe not.* Paired with 12:103 (*most of mankind will not believe even if you desire it eagerly*), Ibn Kathir reads this as a statement about the audience, not the message: after explanation this clear and unequivocal, refusal can only come from **rebellion, stubbornness and hypocrisy**.

---

## 2. The Raised Heavens and the Pillars You Cannot See (13:2)

Two readings of *without any pillars that you can see*, and Ibn Kathir prefers the second:

| Reading | Held by |
|---|---|
| There **are** pillars, but they are invisible to you | Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, and others |
| There are **no** pillars at all — "the heaven is like a dome over the earth" | Iyas bin Mu'awiyah, Qatadah |

He supports the second with 22:65 — *He withholds the heaven from falling on the earth except by His permission* — and notes plainly why it is better: **it affirms Allah's power more completely.** A ceiling held up by nothing is the greater sign.

He then gives the traditional scale: five hundred years between each heaven and the next, and five hundred years of thickness for each — the first heaven encompassing the earth from every direction, the second encompassing the first, and so on through seven.

**On *Istawa'* (rising over the Throne)** Ibn Kathir does not elaborate. He points to his treatment in Al-A'raf (7:54) and states the Salaf's position in one line: it is **accepted as it is, without altering, equating, annulling its meaning, or attempting to explain its nature.**

---

## 3. Neighbouring Tracts, One Water, Different Fruit (13:3–4)

Having established the higher world, the surah descends. Ibn Kathir's commentary here is one of the most sensory passages in his tafsir.

**Neighbouring tracts** — plots of land side by side, one fertile and productive, the next dead, salty and barren (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak). Red earth, white, yellow, black; stony, flat, sandy, thick, thin — **all adjacent, each keeping its own qualities.**

**Sinwan and ghayru sinwan** — palms growing several stems from a single root (figs, pomegranates, dates) versus one stem per tree. From this same root comes the Prophet's ﷺ words to Umar: *Do you not know that a man's paternal uncle is the sinw of his father?*

**And the point of it all:** *watered with the same water; yet some of them We make more excellent than others to eat.* Abu Hurayrah narrated the Prophet's ﷺ own gloss on this phrase — the Dagal, the Persian, the sweet, the bitter (At-Tirmidhi: *Hasan Gharib*).

One rain. One soil. And out of it come sweet and sour, red and black, fragrant and plain. Ibn Kathir's conclusion: this is not chance but **a Creator who does what He wills, and whose power makes distinctions.**

---

## 4. The Strangest Thing Is Their Astonishment (13:5)

They deny resurrection while admitting Allah originated creation from nothing — **which is the greater feat of the two.** Ibn Kathir cites 46:33: *Do they not see that Allah, Who created the heavens and the earth, and was not wearied by their creation, is able to give life to the dead?*

The wonder, in other words, is not the resurrection. **The wonder is that anyone finds it wonderful.**

---

## 5. Forgiveness and Severity, Held Together (13:6)

They demanded the punishment be hurried up — a demand recorded across the Qur'an (15:6–8, 29:53, 70:1, 38:16, 8:32). The reply comes in two halves, and Ibn Kathir insists on both:

- **Exemplary punishments have already occurred** — the destroyed nations are the lesson, delivered in advance.
- **Your Lord is full of forgiveness for mankind, in spite of their wrongdoing** — covering the errors committed night and day. Without that forbearance, 35:45 would apply: *He would not leave a moving creature on the surface of the earth.*
- **And your Lord is severe in punishment.**

Ibn Kathir names the structural principle here explicitly: **fear and hope are both mentioned, so that neither runs loose.** He lists the parallel pairings — 6:147, 7:167, 15:49–50.

---

## 6. "To Every People There Is a Guide" (13:7)

They asked why no miracle came down. The answer is not a miracle but a job description: *You are only a warner.* Guidance is not the Prophet's ﷺ to give — 2:272, *Not upon you is their guidance.*

And then a line with enormous reach: **to every people there is a guide.** Ibn Abbas: for every people there has been a caller. Ibn Kathir pairs it with 35:24 — *there never was a nation but a warner had passed among them.* No community has been left without warning.

---

## 7. What the Wombs Carry (13:8–9)

*Allah knows what every female bears, and by how much the wombs fall short or exceed.*

- **Ibn Abbas:** falling short refers to miscarriages; exceeding, to carrying the full or extended term — nine months, ten, longer or shorter — all of it by His knowledge.
- **The hadith of Ibn Mas'ud** (both Sahihs): forty days as a drop, forty as a clot, forty as a lump of flesh; then an angel writes **four things — provision, life span, deeds, and whether blessed or wretched.**
- **The five keys of the unseen** (Al-Bukhari, from Ibn Umar): tomorrow, the womb, the rain, the place of death, the Hour. **Nobody knows them but Allah.**

And the closing phrase, *everything with Him is in due proportion*, brings Ibn Kathir to the Prophet's ﷺ message to his daughter as her child was dying:

> *Whatever Allah takes is for Him and whatever He gives is for Him, and everything with Him has a limited fixed term — so let her be patient and hope for Allah's reward.*

---

## 8. The Angels in Succession — and the Verse About Change (13:10–11)

**The guardians.** Ibn Kathir's picture is precise: **four angels around each person by day, replaced by four at night — two scribes and two guards.** One recording good on the right, one recording evil on the left; one guarding from before, one from behind. The hadith of the shift-change follows: *they assemble together at the time of Fajr and Asr*, and the ascending angels are asked — though He knows better — *"How have you left My servants?"* They answer: *"As we found them praying, we left them praying."*

Also here: *every one of you has his companion from the jinn and his companion from the angels.* The Companions asked, "And you too?" — *And I too, except that Allah has helped me against him, so he only orders me to do good* (Muslim).

**And then 13:11** — *Verily, Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves.* Ibn Kathir's illustration is the one Ibrahim reported: Allah revealed to a prophet of the Children of Israel — *say to your nation: any people of a village or house who used to obey Allah and then changed to disobeying Him, He will take away what they like and exchange it for what they dislike.*

---

## 9. Thunder Praises, Lightning Warns (13:12–13)

*Ar-Ra'd glorifies and praises Him, and so do the angels because of His awe* — an instance of the universal rule in 17:44, *there is not a thing but glorifies His praise.*

A hadith from a man of Ghifar: **Allah originates the clouds, and they speak in the most beautiful voice and laugh in the most beautiful manner.** Ibn Kathir's own comment on it — *and Allah has the best knowledge* — is that **the cloud's voice is the thunder and its laughter the lightning.** Sa'd bin Ibrahim put it the same way: its smile is lightning, its voice is thunder.

**Lightning as fear and hope** (Qatadah): fear for the traveller, who dreads its harm; hope for the settled, awaiting its blessing and Allah's provision.

**The supplication on hearing thunder**, from Ibn Umar's father — *O Allah, do not kill us with Your anger, nor destroy us with Your torment, and save us before that* (At-Tirmidhi, Al-Adab Al-Mufrad, An-Nasa'i, Al-Hakim). Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr would stop mid-sentence at thunder, recite the verse itself as praise, and say: **"This is a stern warning to the people of earth."**

And the hadith with the sharpest edge: *Had My servants obeyed Me, I would have given them rain by night and sun by day, and would not have made them hear the sound of the thunder.*

**The thunderbolts.** Ibn Kathir attaches the long account of Amir bin At-Tufayl and Arbad bin Qays, who came to Madinah plotting the Prophet's ﷺ murder — Arbad's hand freezing on his sword-hilt, then Arbad killed by lightning at Riqm and Amir dying of a plague-ulcer in a stranger's house. **These verses (13:8–11) were revealed about them.**

---

## 10. The Man Reaching for Water (13:14)

For Him is **the Word of Truth** — which Ali bin Abi Talib, Ibn Abbas, Qatadah and Malik all gloss as **tawhid: *La ilaha illallah*.**

Against it, the parable of the false gods. Ali's picture: **a man stretching his hand over the edge of a deep well, reaching for water his hands cannot touch — so how would it ever reach his mouth?** Mujahid's: calling to the water with words and pointing at it, and it never comes.

The idolater is not merely wrong. He is **thirsty and gesturing**.

---

## 11. Even the Shadows Prostrate (13:15–16)

Everything in the heavens and earth prostrates — believers willingly, disbelievers unwillingly — *and so do their shadows, in the mornings and in the afternoons* (16:48).

Then the interrogation of 13:16, four questions in a row: Who is Lord of the heavens and earth? Have you taken protectors who **cannot benefit or harm even themselves**? Is the blind equal to the seeing, or darkness to light? Have your gods created anything resembling His creation, such that the two creations blur together?

Ibn Kathir notes the self-defeating thing about their own Talbiyah: *"There is no partner for You, except Your partner — You own him and he owns not."* **They conceded their gods were created and owned, and worshipped them anyway.** Intercession belongs only to those He permits (34:23); all in the heavens and earth come to Him as servants (19:93–95). *Say: Allah is the Creator of all things; and He is the One, the Irresistible.*

---

## 12. The Foam and the Water — Two Parables (13:17)

The finest passage in the surah, and Ibn Kathir treats it as two images making one point: **truth stays, falsehood is scum.**

**First:** rain falls, and *the valleys flow according to their measure* — each valley taking what its capacity allows. Ibn Kathir reads this straight into the human heart: **hearts differ, some retaining substantial knowledge, others unable to hold it at all.** The flood carries froth on its surface.

**Second:** ore heated in fire — gold and silver for ornaments, iron and copper for utensils — throws up its own scum.

In both, the froth *passes away as scum upon the banks*, sticking to trees, scattered by wind, gone; while **what benefits people remains in the earth.**

**Ibn Abbas' reading** turns the parable inward: the hearts carry knowledge and certainty in proportion; **the foam is doubt, and what remains is certainty.** As jewellery in fire is rid of its impurity, so Allah accepts certainty and discards doubt.

Ibn Kathir then gathers the Qur'an's other water-and-fire parables — the hypocrites in Al-Baqarah (2:17, 2:19), the disbelievers in An-Nur (the mirage, 24:39; the deep-sea darkness, 24:40) — and two hadiths:

- **The rain on three soils** (Abu Musa): fertile ground that drinks and produces; hard ground that holds water for people to use; and barren land that neither holds nor grows. The parable of those who learn and teach, and of those who turn away entirely.
- **The moths and the fire** (Abu Hurayrah): *I am holding you by the waist, trying to save you from the Fire, and you overwhelm me and fall into it.*

> A note Ibn Kathir preserves from the Salaf: *"When I would read a parable in the Qur'an that I could not comprehend, I would cry for myself"* — because of 29:43, *none will understand them except those who have knowledge.*

---

## 13. Two Answers to One Call (13:18–19)

Those who **answered their Lord's call** receive *Al-Husna* — the best reward (cf. 18:88, 10:26).

Those who did not: **if they owned the whole earth and its like again, they would offer it as ransom, and it would not be accepted.** They face *the terrible reckoning* — and Ibn Kathir adds the principle behind that phrase: **whoever is reckoned in detail on that Day is punished**, reckoned for the Naqir and the Qitmir, the great and the trivial alike.

Then 13:19: **the one who knows and the one who is blind are not equal.** The knower believes the whole of it is truth, each part testifying to the others, none contradicting, all its commands just — *the Word of your Lord has been fulfilled in truth and in justice* (6:115). The blind cannot find what benefits him, and would not follow it if he could.

*But it is only the men of understanding who pay heed.* Ibn Kathir ends the section with a prayer: **"We ask Allah to make us among them."**

---

## 14. Eight Qualities of the Blessed (13:20–24)

The surah now lists what the people of the good end actually do:

1. **They fulfil the covenant of Allah and do not break the trust** — unlike the hypocrite, who breaks his covenant, is most quarrelsome in dispute, lies when he speaks, and betrays what he is entrusted with.
2. **They join what Allah commanded to be joined** — kind to relatives, not severing kinship; generous to the poor and needy.
3. **They fear their Lord** — in what they do and leave undone, remembering He watches, so all their affairs run straight.
4. **They dread the terrible reckoning.**
5. **They are patient, seeking their Lord's Face** — patient in staying away from sin, doing so for His pleasure alone.
6. **They perform the Salah** — preserving its limits, times, bowing, prostration and humility.
7. **They spend from what We provided, secretly and openly** — on those they are obliged to support, and generally; in all conditions, night and day.
8. **They repel evil with good** — meeting harm with patience, forbearance, forgiveness and pardon. Ibn Kathir cites 41:34–35: the enemy becomes *as though he were a close friend* — *but none is granted it except those who are patient.*

**Their reward:** the Gardens of 'Adn (*'Adn* = continuous residence), entered together with **whoever among their fathers, spouses and offspring was righteous** — Allah gathering loved ones and **raising the lower grades to the higher without lowering anyone** (52:21). And the angels entering from every gate: *Salamun 'alaykum, for you persevered in patience.*

Ibn Kathir closes with the hadith of who enters Paradise first: **the poor emigrants**, by whom the frontiers were secured and afflictions warded off, *one of whom would die with his need still in his chest, unable to satisfy it.* The angels object — *we are the residents of Your heaven; do You command us to go and greet them?* And Allah answers: **they are My servants who worshipped Me and associated nothing with Me.**

---

## 15. And the Wretched, in Mirror Image (13:25)

Exactly inverted: **they break the covenant after ratification, sever what Allah commanded to be joined, and spread corruption in the land.** Ibn Kathir attaches the two hadiths of hypocrisy's signs — lying, breaking promises, betraying trusts; entering covenants treacherously and disputing viciously.

Their portion: **the curse — being cast away from Allah's mercy — and the evil home.**

---

## 16. Provision Is a Test, Not a Verdict (13:26)

He expands provision for whom He wills and restricts it for whom He wills. **When the disbelievers rejoice in what they were given, they do not know they are being tested** (23:55–56).

Then three measures of this world against the next:
- *Short is the enjoyment of this world* (4:77); *the Hereafter is better and more lasting* (87:16–17).
- **The finger in the sea** (Al-Mustawrid): dip a finger in the ocean and consider what it carries back — that is this life against the Hereafter.
- **The dead sheep** with the small ears, cast aside by its owners: *this world is more insignificant to Allah than that carcass was to them.*

---

## 17. Why No Miracle Came (13:27–29)

They ask again for a sign. Ibn Kathir brings the hadith of the offer: the idolaters asked for As-Safa to be turned to gold, springs to gush, the mountains around Makkah replaced with fields and rivers. Allah gave the Prophet ﷺ a choice — **give them exactly what they asked, and destroy them with an unprecedented punishment if they then disbelieved; or leave open the door of repentance and mercy.** He chose:

> *Rather, open for them the door to repentance and mercy.*

The verse's own answer is that **miracles were never the mechanism.** He misguides whom He wills and guides to Himself **those who turn to Him** — who repent, beg, seek His help, humbly submit. Signs do not manufacture faith (10:101, 10:96–97, 6:111).

**"Verily, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find rest."** Ibn Kathir's gloss is brief and warm: hearts find comfort on His side, become tranquil when He is remembered, and are **pleased to have Him as Protector** — *and surely, He is worthy of it.*

**Tuba** — the Salaf's glosses stack rather than compete: happiness and refreshment of the eye (Ibn Abbas); *how excellent is what they earned* (Ikrimah); a joy for them (Ad-Dahhak); better for them (An-Nakha'i); *you have earned a good thing* (Qatadah). Ibn Kathir: **these are synonymous, not contradictory.** And in the hadith, Tuba is also **a tree in Paradise** whose shade a rider could not cross in a hundred years, and from whose bark the garments of Paradise are taken. To the man who said *Tuba for whoever saw you and believed* — **"Tuba, and another Tuba, and another Tuba, for he who believed in me but did not see me."**

---

## 18. A Qur'an That Could Move Mountains (13:30–31)

*If there had been a Qur'an with which mountains could be moved, or the earth cloven, or the dead made to speak* — **it would have been this one.** No prior Book, no other speech; its eloquence already defies mankind and jinn together to produce even one surah like it. And still they reject it.

**But the decision of all things is with Allah.** Ibn Abbas: *He will only do what He wills — and He decided that He would not do that.*

Then the point Ibn Kathir wants remembered above all here:

> *Every Prophet was given a miracle of the kind that would make people believe. What I was given is a revelation from Allah — and I hope I will have the most followers among them on the Day of Resurrection.*

**Every other prophet's miracle died with him. This one remains.** It never ages with repetition, and scholars never exhaust it. Ibn Kathir adds the warning: *any tyrant who abandons it, Allah destroys; whoever seeks guidance elsewhere, Allah misguides.*

Also here: the disbelievers of Makkah refused, at Hudaybiyyah, to write *Ar-Rahman Ar-Rahim* — **"We do not know Ar-Rahman"** — which is what *they disbelieve in the Most Gracious* refers to.

---

## 19. Respite Is Not Reprieve (13:32–35)

Messengers were mocked before you; **I granted the disbelievers respite, and then I seized them.** The hadith attached is stark:

> *Allah gives respite to the unjust until, when He seizes him, He never lets go of him* — and the Prophet ﷺ recited 11:102.

**On the false gods (13:33):** He who stands over every soul and knows what it earns, versus idols that cannot hear, see, think, benefit or protect — and the comparison is left unfinished in the verse because it needs no finishing. *Say: Name them!* Uncover them, make them known — **for they do not exist at all.** They are *but names which you have named, you and your fathers* (53:23).

**Paradise described (13:35):** rivers flowing beneath, **provision eternal and shade eternal.** The hadith of the eclipse prayer — the Prophet ﷺ reaching for a cluster of fruit, *and had I kept it, you would have eaten from it as long as this life remains.* Its people eat and drink without waste, their food passing as a musk-scented sweat, *and they are inspired to praise and glorify Allah as spontaneously as they breathe.*

---

## 20. Deliver, and Leave the Reckoning (13:36–43)

The surah's last movement returns to the Prophet's ﷺ role, three times over.

**Some of the People of the Book rejoice at what was revealed** — because their own Books testify to it and foretold it (2:121; 17:107–109). But *among the confederates are those who reject part of it* (Mujahid: the Jews and Christians who reject a portion of the truth). And a warning aimed squarely at **people of knowledge**: follow their desires after what has come to you, and *you will have no protector against Allah.*

**The messengers were men** — with wives and offspring, eating food and walking in markets. *Say: I am only a man like you* (18:110). And the Prophet's ﷺ own line: *I fast and break the fast, I pray at night and sleep, I eat meat and marry women; whoever turns away from my Sunnah is not of mine.* **No prophet ever brought a sign except by Allah's leave.**

**"Allah blots out what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book" (13:39)** — the surah's most discussed verse. Ibn Kathir records the range:
- One view: the abrogation of earlier revealed Books, until the Qur'an abrogated them all.
- **Mujahid:** on Laylatul-Qadr, Allah decides the coming year's provisions and disasters, advancing and delaying what He wills — **but the record of the blessed and the wretched does not change.**
- **Ibn Abbas, Umar, Ibn Mas'ud, and Abu Wa'il's practice** all point the other way: the man who obeys long and dies astray is what He blots out; the man who disobeys long and dies obedient is what He confirms. Abu Wa'il's supplication: *if You wrote us among the wretched, remove it and write us among the blessed; if You wrote us among the blessed, let us stay.* **Mujahid, asked about this same prayer, said: "This supplication is good."**
- And the hadith of Thawban: **a man may be deprived of provision by a sin he commits; only supplication changes the decree; and only righteousness increases the life span** — with the parallel report that maintaining kinship ties lengthens life.

**The closing consolation (13:40–43):** whether We show you part of what We promised them or take you first, *your duty is only to convey — and on Us is the reckoning* (88:21–26). *See they not that We gradually reduce the land from its outlying borders?* Ibn Abbas: **"See they not that We are granting land after land to Muhammad?"** And when they say *you are not a messenger* — **Allah is sufficient as witness, and so are those who have knowledge of the Scripture**: the scholars of the earlier Books who find his description written with them (7:157, 26:197).

---

## The Thread

Ar-Ra'd asks you to look at ordinary things until they stop being ordinary. **Two fields side by side, one green and one salt. One rain, and a hundred flavours. Froth on a flood that will be gone by evening, and water that stays.** The surah never argues that these prove God; it assumes that anyone who genuinely looks will not need the argument.

And its answer to every demand for something more spectacular is the same: the spectacle was offered, and **mercy was chosen over it.** The Prophet ﷺ was given the option of As-Safa turned to gold with annihilation attached, and asked instead for the door of repentance. What remained in its place is the Book itself — the one miracle that did not die with the man who carried it.

*This is the end of Surah Ar-Ra'd, and all praise is due to Allah and all favours are from Him.*
