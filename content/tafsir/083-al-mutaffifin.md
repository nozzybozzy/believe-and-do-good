*Revealed in Makkah · 36 ayat · "Those Who Deal in Fraud" — named for the cheats condemned in its opening verse*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Mutaffifin begins with something startlingly small — a merchant tilting a scale — and ends with the sorting of all humanity. That is the whole argument of the surah: **the small dishonesty and the great Day are the same subject.**

It opens with *Woe*, and the woe is not aimed at murderers or idolaters but at people who take a full measure when receiving and give a short one when paying. Then it asks the question that turns the whole thing over: *Do they not think that they will be resurrected?* Cheating by a handful of grain is possible only for someone who has quietly concluded that no one is counting.

From there the surah is built almost entirely out of **mirrors**, and reading it as a set of paired opposites is the fastest way in:

| The wicked (Fujjar) | The righteous (Abrar) |
|---|---|
| Record in **Sijjin** — a prison, beneath the earth | Record in **'Illiyyin** — from *'uluw*, height |
| Hearts covered by **Ran** | Faces showing the **brightness of delight** |
| **Veiled** from their Lord that Day (83:15) | On thrones, **looking** (83:23, 83:35) |
| Entering the blazing fire | Drinking sealed **Rahiq**, mixed with **Tasnim** |
| They **laughed** at the believers in this world | The believers **laugh** at them on that Day |

And the final mirror is the one that closes the circle: the surah opens with men who **short the measure** and ends with the promise that the disbelievers will be **paid in full, completely and perfectly**. The people who would not give a whole measure receive one.

---

## 1. Woe to Those Who Short the Measure (83:1–3)

> *Woe to Al-Mutaffifin. Those who, when they have to receive by measure from men, demand full measure. And when they have to give by measure or weight to men, give less than due.*

**The occasion of revelation.** An-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah record from Ibn Abbas: when the Prophet ﷺ arrived in Al-Madinah, its people were **the worst of people in giving measure** — they cheated. This surah came down, *and after it they began to give good measure.*

**What Tatfif actually is.** Ibn Kathir defines it as being stingy with measurement and weight — and note that it runs in **both directions**:

- **increasing** the measure when it is owed *to* you, and
- **decreasing** it when it is owed *by* you.

It is not one act but a permanent tilt of the self toward its own advantage.

**The standard the Qur'an sets instead**, gathered by Ibn Kathir from three places:

> *And give full measure when you measure, and weigh with a balance that is straight. That is good and better in the end.* (17:35)

> *And give full measure and full weight with justice. We burden not any person, but with that which he can bear.* (6:152)

> *And observe the weight with equity and do not make the balance deficient.* (55:9)

And the precedent that should make the point land: **Allah destroyed the people of Shu'ayb and wiped them out because of their cheating in weights and measures.** An entire nation erased over the tilt of a scale.

## 2. The Question Underneath It (83:4–6)

> *Do they not think that they will be resurrected, on a Great Day, the Day when all mankind will stand before the Lord of all that exists?*

Ibn Kathir reads this as the diagnosis, not merely a threat: do these people **not fear** standing before the One who knows the hidden matters and the innermost secrets? Fraud in small things is a theological position, held quietly.

**What the standing is like.** They will stand *barefooted, naked and uncircumcised*, at a station that will be difficult, hard and distressful for the criminals — covered by a command from Allah that the strength and the senses cannot bear.

Ibn Kathir then stacks the narrations, and they are among the most vivid in the tafsir:

- **Ibn Umar** (Malik ← Nafi'; in Bukhari and Muslim): mankind will stand until one of them **sinks up to the middle of his ears in sweat**.
- **Al-Miqdad bin Al-Aswad** (Ahmad; also Muslim and At-Tirmidhi): the sun will be brought within **a mile or two**, and people will be submerged in sweat **according to their deeds** — some to their heels, some to their knees, some to their groins, and some **bridled in it** up to their necks.
- **Ibn Mas'ud**: they will stand for **forty years**, heads raised toward the sky, no one speaking to them.
- **Ibn Umar**: one hundred years. (Both via Ibn Jarir.)
- In Abu Dawud: the Prophet ﷺ **used to seek refuge with Allah from the hardship of the standing** on the Day of Judgement.

**A practice worth keeping.** From Aishah (Abu Dawud, An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah), the Prophet ﷺ would open his late-night prayer with **ten takbir, ten praises, ten glorifications, and ten seekings of forgiveness**, then say:

> *"O Allah! Forgive me, guide me, provide for me, and protect me."*

— and then seek refuge from the hardship of that standing.

## 3. Sijjin: The Record of the Wicked (83:7–10)

> *Nay! Truly, the Record of the wicked is in Sijjin. And what will make you know what Sijjin is? A Register inscribed.*

**Sijjin** is derived from *sijn* — **prison** — and carries the sense of straitened, constricted circumstances. Ibn Kathir: a great matter, an **eternal prison**, and a painful torment. Some said it lies **beneath the seventh earth**, and the long hadith of Al-Bara' bin 'Azib supports this: of the disbeliever's soul Allah says, *"Record his book in Sijjin"* — and Sijjin is beneath the seventh earth.

The logic is spatial and moral at once: the destination of the wicked is the lowest of the low —

> *Then We reduced him to the lowest of the low, save those who believe and do righteous deeds.* (95:5–6)

> *And when they shall be thrown into a narrow place thereof, chained together, they will exclaim therein for destruction.* (25:13)

**A careful point of exegesis.** *A Register inscribed* is **not** the answer to *what will make you know what Sijjin is?* It describes the **record** that will be filed there: inscribed, written and completed — **no one can add to it and no one can remove anything from it** (Muhammad bin Ka'b Al-Qurazi).

**On "Woe."** Basically it means destruction and devastation. Ibn Kathir attaches here the hadith of Bahz bin Hakim ← his father ← his grandfather:

> *"Woe unto whoever speaks, and lies in order to make the people laugh. Woe unto him, woe unto him."*

---

## 4. Who Denies, and Why (83:11–14)

The deniers are described in two words, and Ibn Kathir separates them precisely:

- **Mu'tad** — the transgressor beyond bounds: transgresses **in his actions**, doing what is forbidden and exceeding the limits even in what is permitted.
- **Athim** — the sinner: sins **in his statements** — *he lies whenever he speaks, breaks his promises whenever he makes them, and behaves in an abusive and wicked manner whenever he argues.*

Their verdict on revelation is a shrug: *"Tales of the ancients!"* — the same line recorded at 16:24 and 25:5, where they add that it is *dictated to him morning and afternoon*.

### The Ran — the covering they earned (83:14)

> *Nay! But on their hearts is the Ran which they used to earn.*

The obstruction is not intellectual. It is **a dark covering cast over the heart by accumulated sin** — and, crucially, it is something they *earned*. Ibn Kathir cites Abu Hurayrah (Ibn Jarir, At-Tirmidhi — *Hasan Sahih* — An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah):

> *"Verily, when the servant commits a sin, a black spot appears in his heart. If he repents from it, his heart is polished clean. But if he increases in it, the spot will continue to increase"* — until, in An-Nasa'i's wording, **it overcomes his entire heart, and that is the Ran.**

Ibn Kathir also preserves a graded distinction: **Rayn** overcomes the hearts of the disbelievers; **Ghaym** is for the righteous; **Ghayn** is for those near to Allah. Even the closest have something that passes over the heart — the difference is what is allowed to settle.

---

## 5. The Veil, and Ash-Shafi'i's Argument (83:15–17)

> *Nay! Surely, they will be veiled from seeing their Lord that Day.*

Alongside their lodging in Sijjin, they are **screened from Allah**. Then the fire, and then the rebuke: *"This is what you used to deny!"* — said, Ibn Kathir notes, by way of scolding, belittling and humiliation.

**Imam Ash-Shafi'i's inference** from this verse is famous and is the hinge of the surah's theology:

> *"In this Ayah is a proof that the believers will see Him, the Mighty and Sublime, on that Day."*

A punishment stated as deprivation only makes sense if the thing withheld is granted to others.

---

## 6. 'Illiyyin: The Record of the Righteous (83:18–21)

> *Nay! Verily, the Record of Al-Abrar is in 'Illiyyin.*

The exact inverse of Sijjin. Ibn Kathir reports:

- **Ka'b**, asked by Ibn Abbas: Sijjin is the seventh earth, holding the souls of the disbelievers; **'Illiyyin is the seventh heaven, holding the souls of the believers.**
- **Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah): it means **Paradise**.
- Others: it is located at **Sidrat Al-Muntaha**.
- Ibn Kathir's own reading: the word comes from ***'Uluw* — highness**. The more a thing ascends, the greater it becomes.

Their register too is **inscribed** — and *witnessed by those nearest*: **the angels** (Qatadah); or, per Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas, those nearest to Allah **in every heaven** bear witness to it. Where the wicked man's record is filed in a prison, the righteous man's is countersigned in public by the highest company there is.

---

## 7. The Delight of the Abrar (83:22–28)

**On thrones, looking.** Thrones beneath canopies. Two readings are given for what they look *at*: their kingdom and the bounties that will never end or perish — or **at Allah Himself**, which Ibn Kathir sets directly against 83:15. The veiled and the looking are the same picture from two sides.

**Their faces.** *You will recognize in their faces the brightness of delight* — a glow you can see. Ibn Kathir lists what it conveys: **opulence, decorum, happiness, composure, and authority.**

**Their drink**, described in three ascending stages:

1. **Rahiq** — a name for the wine of Paradise (Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd), *pure* and *sealed*.
2. **Sealed with musk** — mixed with musk (Ibn Mas'ud); or, per Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas, **the last thing placed into it is musk**, so that the closing note of the drink is its fragrance.
3. **Mixed with Tasnim** — a spring that is **the most excellent and exalted drink of the people of Paradise** (Abu Salih, Ad-Dahhak). Those **nearest to Allah drink it pure**; for everyone else it is the measure mixed into their cup.

Set between the description of the wine and the naming of Tasnim is the one imperative in this entire section:

> *And for this let those strive who want to strive.*

Ibn Kathir: for a situation like this, **let the competitors compete and race** — compare *For the like of this let the workers work* (37:61). It is the surah's single positive command, and it is an invitation to ambition.

---

## 8. Who Laughs Last (83:29–36)

> *Verily, those who committed crimes used to laugh at those who believed... and when they saw them, they said: "Verily, these have indeed gone astray!"*

Ibn Kathir catalogues the behaviour with unusual attention, because it is so recognisable: they **mocked and despised** the believers; they **winked at one another** as they passed, in contempt; they went home to their families **pleased with themselves** — finding whatever they asked for, and, rather than being grateful for that favour, busying themselves with **despising and envying** the believers instead.

**The rebuttal is one line:** *But they were not sent as watchers over them.* No one appointed them guardians over the believers' deeds and words. So why are these people the focus of their attention?

The parallel Ibn Kathir brings is the whole scene in miniature — the mockers addressed in the Fire:

> *"Remain you in it with ignominy, and speak you not to Me!... But you took them for a laughing stock, so much so that they made you forget My remembrance while you used to laugh at them. Verily, I have rewarded them this Day for their patience: they are indeed the ones that are successful."* (23:108–111)

**And then the reversal:** *But this Day those who believe will laugh at the disbelievers — on thrones, looking.* Ibn Kathir: **looking at Allah**, as recompense for having borne the false charge of being misguided. They were never misguided; they were the close *Awliya'* of Allah, and they will be looking at their Lord in the place of His honour.

**The closing verse** — *Are not the disbelievers paid for what they used to do?* — is a rhetorical question whose answer Ibn Kathir gives flatly: **they surely will be paid in full, completely and perfectly.**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**A short measure is a statement of belief.** The man who tilts the scale has decided, without ever saying so, that nothing is being recorded. Al-Mutaffifin's entire structure is the reply: *there is a register, it is inscribed, and nothing can be added to it or taken from it.*

Everything else in the surah follows from that first tilt.

- The cheat's heart does not go dark at once — it accumulates **a black spot at a time**, and repentance polishes it clean each time, until one day the spots have joined and become **Ran**.
- The worst punishment named here is not the fire. It is the **veil**: *they will be veiled from seeing their Lord that Day* — and Ash-Shafi'i read that deprivation as the proof of the gift withheld.
- The believers' reward is stated in the same grammar as the mockers' crime, exactly inverted: they were laughed at, so **they laugh**; they were called astray, so **they look**.
- And the closing note is the perfect answer to the opening one. The people who would not give a full measure are **paid in full — completely and perfectly.**

*And for this let those strive who want to strive.* (83:26)
