*Revealed in Madinah · 29 ayat · "The Victory" — revealed on the road back from Al-Hudaybiyyah, Dhul-Qa'dah, 6 AH*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Fath is the Qur'an's answer to a moment that felt, to the people living through it, like a humiliation.

The Prophet ﷺ had set out for 'Umrah with some fourteen hundred Companions. The Quraysh blocked him. After tense negotiation a treaty was signed on terms that looked lopsided: no 'Umrah this year, come back next year; any Makkan who defects to Madinah must be returned, but not the reverse; and the document itself was stripped of the words *Bismillah ar-Rahman ar-Rahim* and *Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah* because the Quraysh refused them. The Companions were so distressed that when the Prophet ﷺ ordered them to slaughter their animals and shave their heads, **not one man moved.**

Then, on the return journey, this surah came down and called that treaty **a manifest victory**.

Everything in the surah flows from that reversal. It vindicates the men who stayed and pledged; it exposes the Bedouins who found reasons not to come; it explains *why* Allah held back both armies from fighting; it promises the spoils and conquests that followed; and it closes with the most famous portrait of the Companions in the Qur'an. The surah's whole argument is a single principle: **you judge outcomes by what you can see, and you see almost nothing.**

---

## 1. Its Merit, and the Ayah Dearer Than the World

Imam Ahmad records from **Abdullah bin Mughaffal** that on the day Makkah was conquered, the Prophet ﷺ recited Surah Al-Fath while riding his she-camel, in a vibrating, beautiful tone. Mu'awiyah, a sub-narrator, added: *were I not afraid the people would crowd around me, I would try to reproduce that recitation.* (Bukhari and Muslim.)

**Umar bin Al-Khattab's** account of the revelation is one of the most human passages in the tafsir. He had asked the Prophet ﷺ a question three times on a journey and received no answer. He rode off ahead, scolding himself — *may your mother lose you, son of Al-Khattab* — genuinely afraid that Qur'an was about to be revealed condemning him. Then he heard a voice calling: *O Umar!* He went back in dread, and the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *Last night, a Surah was revealed to me that is dearer to me than this life and all that it contains* — "Verily, We have given you a manifest victory. That Allah may forgive you your sins of the past and the future…"

**Anas bin Malik** adds the sequel. When the Prophet ﷺ recited the verse of forgiveness, the Companions said: *Congratulations, O Messenger of Allah! Allah has told you what He will do with you. So what will He do with us?* And 48:5 came down in answer — gardens beneath which rivers flow, sins expiated, *supreme success*. Their share was written into the same surah.

And **Al-Mughirah bin Shu'bah** reports the question that follows naturally: if all your sins past and future are forgiven, why do you still pray until your feet swell?

> *Should I not be a thankful servant?*

---

## 2. Which "Victory"?

Ibn Kathir is emphatic, and so were the Companions: **Al-Fath here is Hudaybiyyah, not the conquest of Makkah.**

- **Abdullah bin Mas'ud:** "You consider the conquering of Makkah to be Al-Fath, while to us, Al-Fath is the treaty at Al-Hudaybiyyah."
- **Jabir bin Abdullah:** "We only considered Al-Fath to be the day of Hudaybiyyah."
- **Al-Bara' bin Azib** (in Bukhari): "You consider Al-Fath to be the conquest of Makkah, which was indeed a victory. But we consider Al-Fath to be the pledge of Ar-Ridwan on the day of Al-Hudaybiyyah."

Al-Bara' then tells the story of the well: fourteen hundred men drank it dry, not a drop left. The Prophet ﷺ sat on its edge, called for a bucket, made ablution, rinsed his mouth, made du'a and poured the water back in — and the well supplied every man and every animal, in whatever quantity they wished.

Why was a treaty the victory? Ibn Kathir gives the reason plainly: **it opened the doors.** Peace meant the two sides could mix openly, Muslims could preach, beneficial knowledge and faith spread, and people entered Islam *in large crowds*. Two years later Makkah fell with barely a fight — because the treaty had already won the argument.

### The forgiveness that belongs to him alone

On *"that Allah may forgive you your sins of the past and the future,"* Ibn Kathir stops to note that this is a **unique honour**. There is no authentic hadith stating that any other person earned forgiveness for all past and future sins on account of good deeds. He explains it as fitting: no human being has ever matched the Prophet ﷺ in obedience and uprightness, nor ever will.

And the immediate context proves it. When his camel Al-Qaswa knelt and refused to move toward Makkah, he said: *He Who stopped the elephant has stopped her too.* Then he declared the position that produced the whole treaty:

> *By Him in Whose Hand my soul is, this day, if they ask me anything which respects the ordinances of Allah, I will grant it to them.*

He conceded everything they asked because none of it crossed a line Allah had drawn. The forgiveness verse follows that submission.

## 3. As-Sakinah: The Calm That Was Sent Down (48:4–7)

> *He it is Who sent down As-Sakinah into the hearts of the believers, that they may grow more in faith along with their faith.*

**Sakinah** = tranquility. Qatadah called it *grace into the hearts* — specifically the hearts of the Companions on the day of Hudaybiyyah. These were men who accepted the decision even when they hated it, and once their hearts settled into acceptance, **Allah added faith to the faith they already had.**

Ibn Kathir notes that **Al-Bukhari and other Imams used this ayah as proof that faith increases and decreases.**

Then: *to Allah belong the armies of the heavens and the earth.* One angel could have finished the disbelievers. But Allah **willed** that jihad be established for and by the believers, for wisdom He alone fully knows. The struggle is not because He needs it.

The surah then sorts humanity into two outcomes:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Believing men and women** | Gardens, eternity, sins expiated — *supreme success* |
| **Hypocrites and idolaters** | Disgraceful torment, Allah's anger, His curse, Hell |

Their defining crime is named precisely: **they think evil thoughts about Allah.** Ibn Kathir explains — they questioned the wisdom of Allah's decisions and assumed the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions were going to be wiped out.

---

## 4. The Pledge of Ar-Ridwan (48:8–10)

> *Verily, those who give pledge to you, they are giving pledge to Allah. The Hand of Allah is over their hands.*

Ibn Kathir reads this alongside *"He who obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah"* (4:80), and explains *the Hand of Allah is over their hands* as: He is with them, hearing them, witnessing them, knowing them inwardly and outwardly. **The pledge was taken by Allah, through His Messenger.**

### Why the pledge happened

From Ibn Ishaq: the Prophet ﷺ wanted to send **Umar** to Makkah to explain that he had come only for 'Umrah. Umar declined — no clan left in Makkah to protect him, and the Quraysh knew his hostility — and nominated a better man: **Uthman bin Affan**, who had powerful relatives there. Uthman went, protected by Aban bin Sa'id.

When the Quraysh offered Uthman the chance to perform Tawaf himself, he refused: *I would not do that before the Messenger of Allah performs it.* They detained him. Then word reached the camp that **Uthman had been killed.**

The Prophet ﷺ said: *We will not leave until we fight the people* — and called for the pledge. It was taken under a **Samurah** (thorn) tree.

- **Jabir:** the pledge was **not to flee**, not a pledge to die. Ma'qil bin Yasar, who held a branch away from the Prophet's head while it was taken, says the same.
- **Salamah bin Al-Akwa'** (Bukhari): asked what he pledged to — *to die.* He pledged **three separate times** — first, middle, and last — because the Prophet ﷺ kept calling him back. When the Prophet ﷺ later gave him armour and found he had given it away to 'Amir, he laughed and said: *You are just like the man of old who said, "O Allah! Give me a dear person dearer to me than myself."*
- **Al-Jadd bin Qays** of Banu Salamah was the one man who did not pledge. Jabir: *it is as if I am still looking at him, hiding behind the shoulder of his camel.*
- **Umar** was arming for battle and missed the call; his son Abdullah pledged first and came back to tell him. (This is why some wrongly thought Ibn Umar became Muslim before his father.)

Then the news of Uthman's death turned out to be false.

**How many?** Jabir says 1,400; Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib's father says 1,500. Al-Bayhaqi reconciles: Jabir at first said 1,500, then later recalled the correct figure of 1,400. Ibn Kathir prefers **1,400**.

### What they were promised

- *Today, you are the best people on the earth.*
- *None among those who gave the pledge under the tree shall enter Hellfire.*
- Whoever ascends the pass of **Al-Murar** will have his sins relieved as the Children of Israel were relieved — *all of you are forgiven, except the owner of the red camel*, a man too busy hunting his lost animal to care.
- When a slave complained that **Hatib bin Abi Balta'ah** would surely enter Hell: *You lie. He shall never enter the Fire; he participated in Badr and Al-Hudaybiyyah.*

### The tree that disappeared

One of the quiet lessons of the surah. **The next year the Companions went back and could not find the tree.** They disagreed over which one it was. Sa'id bin Al-Musayyib, told that people were now praying at a site claimed to be it, said with cutting irony:

> *The Companions of Muhammad forgot where the tree was, but you know where it is. Therefore you have better knowledge than them!*

## 5. The Men Who Stayed Home (48:11–17)

> *"Our possessions and our families occupied us, so ask forgiveness for us."*

The Bedouins who lagged behind rehearse their excuse in advance — and Allah pre-empts it. They ask for istighfar **not out of faith in the Prophet's du'a, but as a show.** *They say with their tongues what is not in their hearts.*

The real reason is then stated: *you thought the Messenger and the believers would never return to their families.* Their absence was not weakness — it was **hypocrisy plus a calculation that the Muslims were going to be exterminated.**

They are called **Bur** — a people gone to destruction (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid); *corrupt*, said Qatadah; and some noted it as a word from the dialect of Oman.

### The punishment that fits the crime

When the Prophet ﷺ later set out for **Khaybar**, the same Bedouins asked to come — the fighting was over and the spoils were rich. Allah ordered him to refuse:

> *Say: "You shall not follow us; thus Allah has said beforehand."*

Khaybar's spoils had already been promised to the people of Hudaybiyyah alone. They protest that this is envy; Allah answers: *they understand not except a little.* Ibn Kathir notes the elegance — **the legislation matched the destiny.** They wanted to change Allah's word; they could not.

But the door is not shut permanently. They are told they will be **called again**, to fight a people of great warfare, and if they obey this time they get a fair reward. Ibn Kathir catalogues the scholarly guesses at who that people is — Hawazin, Thaqif, Banu Hanifah, the Persians, the Romans, both, the idolaters generally — and reports that **Ibn Jarir preferred Mujahid's undefined reading: simply "men given to great warfare."** The call keeps coming; the identity changes.

### Who is genuinely excused

> *No blame is there upon the blind, nor upon the lame, nor upon the sick.*

Real incapacity is a real excuse — including temporary illness, which excuses a man **until it lifts.** The distinction the surah draws is not between the strong and the weak; it is between the incapable and the unwilling.

---

## 6. "Allah Was Pleased With Them" (48:18–21)

> *Indeed, Allah was pleased with the believers when they gave the pledge to you under the tree. He knew what was in their hearts…*

What was in their hearts, Ibn Kathir says: **truthfulness, trustworthiness, obedience, adherence.** So He sent down Sakinah and rewarded them with *a near victory* — the treaty itself — and *abundant spoils* — Khaybar, then Makkah, then province after province.

On 48:20–21, the scholars divide over the reference:

- *"He has hastened for you this"* — Khaybar (Mujahid) or the Hudaybiyyah treaty itself (Ibn Abbas).
- *"And other victories not yet within your power"* — Khaybar (Ibn Abbas), the conquest of Makkah (Qatadah, preferred by Ibn Jarir), Persia and Rome (Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Ibn Abi Layla), or **every victory and every spoil until the Day of Resurrection** (Mujahid).

Ibn Abbas's comment on the verse is the one that lands: *they are the victories that are continuing until this day.*

And the restraining of hands is read generously: no enemy attack touched them, **and the men left behind near their families and children were also restrained from harming them.**

The lesson Ibn Kathir attaches is the surah's thesis in miniature — Allah alone knows the consequences of matters, and the best decisions are the ones He chooses for His servants **even when they look unfavourable from outside**:

> *It may be that you dislike a thing that is good for you.* (2:216)

---

## 7. Why Nobody Fought (48:22–26)

Had the Quraysh fought at Hudaybiyyah, they would have turned and fled with no protector and no helper — *the way of Allah already with those who passed away before*. Whenever faith and disbelief meet at a decisive juncture, faith is given the victory. Badr is the proof: few and lightly armed against many and well-equipped.

So why no battle?

### The seventy captives

**Salamah bin Al-Akwa'** describes four Makkans insulting the Prophet ﷺ near where he rested; a shout came that Ibn Zunaym had been killed; Salamah seized the men's weapons while they slept and marched them in. With others taken, the prisoners numbered **seventy**. The Prophet ﷺ looked at them and said:

> *Let them go, for theirs will be the initiation of hostilities and its burden.*

He forgave them — and 48:24 was revealed about it. (**Anas** gives a parallel report: eighty armed men came down from Tan'im to ambush the camp at dawn, were captured after the Prophet's du'a against them, and were pardoned.)

### The believers hidden in Makkah

> *Had there not been believing men and believing women whom you did not know, that you may kill them…*

This is the hinge of the whole episode. There were Muslims **living secretly inside Makkah**, concealing their faith out of fear. Had the armies met, the Muslims would have killed their own brothers without knowing it, and incurred sin *without knowledge*. So Allah held both sides back — *that Allah might bring into His mercy whom He wills*, delaying punishment on Makkah so that its hidden believers survived **and so that many of the idolaters would themselves become Muslim.**

> *If they had been apart, We would have punished with painful torment those of them who disbelieved.*

The peace, in other words, was not a concession. **It was mercy operating on people who could not yet be told apart.**

### Their pride, and the word of Taqwa

The *pride and haughtiness of the time of ignorance* is identified concretely: **their refusal to allow "In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful" and "Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah" into the document.**

Against that, Allah gave the believers Sakinah and *made them stick to the word of Taqwa* —

- **sincerity** (Mujahid)
- **"La ilaha illallah, alone without partners"** (Az-Zuhri ← 'Urwah ← Al-Miswar; and Ata' bin Abi Rabah)

*and they were well entitled to it and worthy of it.*

## 8. The Day Itself: The Long Narration

Ibn Kathir then reproduces Bukhari's full account (Al-Miswar bin Makhramah and Marwan bin Al-Hakam). The details worth carrying:

- **Consultation.** The Prophet ﷺ asked the people's opinion twice about striking the families of Quraysh's allies. **Abu Bakr** answered: we came to visit the House, not to kill anyone — head for the House, and fight only whoever stands in the way. *Go forth then.*
- **Khalid bin Al-Walid** led the Quraysh cavalry; the Prophet ﷺ took the right-hand path and Khalid knew nothing until he saw the army's dust.
- **Al-Qaswa knelt.** *She has not become stubborn; that is not her habit. She was stopped by Him Who stopped the elephant.*
- **The arrow in the pit.** The little water was exhausted; an arrow from his quiver was placed in it and the water rose until every man drank and left satisfied.
- **Budayl bin Warqa'** of Khuza'ah carried the Prophet's offer to Quraysh: a truce, non-interference, and — *if they wish* — the chance to enter Islam like everyone else; otherwise, *I will fight for this cause until I am killed, and Allah will make His cause victorious.*
- **'Urwah bin Mas'ud** came to negotiate, taunted the Prophet ﷺ that his followers would desert him, and was verbally savaged by Abu Bakr. He kept reaching for the Prophet's beard as he spoke, and **Al-Mughirah bin Shu'bah** — helmeted, sword in hand — rapped his hand back each time. But 'Urwah returned to Quraysh with the most valuable report of the day:

> *By Allah, I have been to kings, to Caesar, Khosrau and An-Najashi. I have never seen any of them respected by his courtiers as Muhammad is respected by his Companions.* — they caught his spittle and rubbed it on their skin, raced for his leftover ablution water, lowered their voices before him, and would not stare at his face out of reverence.

- **The Kinanah envoy** saw the garlanded sacrificial camels and said it would be wrong to keep these people from the Ka'bah. **Mikraz bin Hafs** came next — *here is Mikraz, and he is a vicious man.*
- **Suhayl bin 'Amr** arrived, and the Prophet ﷺ played on his name: *Now the matter has become easy (suhayl/sahl) for you.*
- **The document.** Suhayl rejected *Ar-Rahman* — "I don't know what it means" — so it became *In Your Name, O Allah.* He rejected *Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah* — so it became *Muhammad bin Abdullah.* The Prophet ﷺ to him: *By Allah, I am the Messenger of Allah even if you deny me.*
- **Abu Jandal** — Suhayl's own son — arrived in chains, having escaped, and had to be handed back under a clause not yet even written. He cried out to the Muslims. It was the hardest moment of the day.
- **Umar's protest.** *Aren't you truly the Messenger of Allah? Isn't our cause just? Then why should we be humble in our religion?* The answer: *I am Allah's Messenger, I do not disobey Him, and He will make me victorious.* And: *Did I tell you that you would visit the Ka'bah this year?* He went to Abu Bakr and got the identical answers, word for word. Umar later said: *I performed many good deeds as expiation for the improper questions I asked that day.*
- **The frozen camp.** Ordered to slaughter and shave, nobody moved — three times. The Prophet ﷺ went to **Umm Salamah**, who advised: say nothing to anyone, go out, slaughter your animal and call your barber. He did. The entire camp erupted into action behind him.
- **The women.** Believing women who emigrated were *not* returned — 60:10 was revealed. Umar divorced two wives who remained disbelievers.
- **Abu Basir.** Returned to Quraysh under the treaty, he killed one of his escorts and fled. The Prophet ﷺ said of him: *Woe to his mother! What an excellent war kindler he would be, if he only had supporters.* Abu Basir understood he would be handed back, so he made for the coast — Abu Jandal and every new convert from Quraysh joined him, and they began cutting the Syrian caravan route to pieces. **Quraysh eventually begged the Prophet ﷺ to take them in**, waiving the very clause they had insisted on. The clause that looked like surrender destroyed them.

### The postscript at Siffin

Years later, at Siffin, **Sahl bin Hunayf** used Hudaybiyyah to warn 'Ali's camp against confidence in their own judgement:

> *Do not feel certain in the reliability of your own opinions. On the day Abu Jandal came, I saw myself willing to reject the command of Allah's Messenger, had I had the chance.*

And **Ibn Abbas** used the same day against the Khawarij: when 'Ali was told to erase *Rasulullah* from the document, the Prophet ﷺ ordered it erased — *O Allah, You know that I am Your Messenger* — and erasing the title did not erase the prophethood.

## 9. The Vision Was True (48:27–28)

The Prophet ﷺ had dreamt of entering Makkah and performing Tawaf, and told the Companions in Madinah. When they were turned back, some struggled badly. Note the precision of the correction: **the dream was never dated.** *Have I told you that you would go this year?*

The next year — **Dhul-Qa'dah, 7 AH, 'Umrat al-Qada** — they entered exactly as promised: secure, some shaven, some with hair shortened, *having no fear*. Ibn Kathir supplies the surrounding chronology: back to Madinah, **Khaybar in Safar** (its spoils divided among the Hudaybiyyah participants alone, with Ja'far bin Abi Talib's returning party and Abu Musa Al-Ash'ari's people the exceptions), then Makkah.

Details from that entry:

- The Prophet ﷺ prayed **three times** *"O Allah, be merciful to those who shave their heads"* before adding *"and those who shorten."*
- Quraysh sent Mikraz to accuse him of breaking the treaty by arriving armed. He had already sent the weapons to the valley of **Ya'jaj**, keeping only sheathed swords as agreed. Mikraz: *this is the way we knew you to be — trustworthy.*
- The idolaters had spread word that the fever of Yathrib had weakened the Muslims, so the Prophet ﷺ ordered **Ramal** (brisk, vigorous walking) for the first three circuits, and normal walking between the two corners where the watchers could not see — *out of pity for them.* The idolaters, watching from Qu'ayqi'an: *these are stronger than so-and-so.*
- **Abdullah bin Rawahah** led the camel by its bridle, reciting poetry through the streets of Makkah: *For today we shall impose on you the fulfilment of its interpretation, just as we fought you to accept its revelation.*

Then the promise that outruns Arabia entirely:

> *He it is Who has sent His Messenger with guidance and the religion of truth, that He may make it superior to all religions.*

Ibn Kathir: guidance = beneficial knowledge, religion of truth = righteous deeds. **Islamic law has these two components** — its reports and creed are true, its commands are just. And *superior to all religions* means over the religions of everyone on earth, Arab and non-Arab, ideologue, atheist and idolater alike.

## 10. The Portrait of the Companions (48:29)

> *Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah. And those who are with him are severe against disbelievers, and merciful among themselves.*

Ibn Kathir pairs this with 5:54 — *humble towards the believers, stern towards the disbelievers* — and describes it as a single character with two faces: unsmiling before hostility, **beaming with pleasure before a believing brother.** Two hadiths anchor it:

> *The parable of the believers in their kindness and mercy for each other is that of the body: when one organ falls ill, the rest of the body responds with fever and sleeplessness.*

> *A believer to another believer is like a building whose parts reinforce each other* — and he interlaced his fingers.

**The mark on their faces.** Ibn Abbas explained *the traces of prostration* as a **graceful appearance**; Mujahid and others as **humility and reverence**. Others said: righteous deeds bring light to the heart, brightness to the face, ampleness in provision, and love in people's hearts. **Uthman bin Affan**: whoever hides a secret, Allah will show it on his face or let it slip from his tongue. And the hadith: *good conduct, graceful appearance and moderation are one part of twenty-five parts of Prophethood.*

Imam Malik was told that when the Christians of Ash-Sham saw the Companions who conquered it, they said: **"These people are, by Allah, better than the Disciples of 'Isa, according to what has been conveyed to us."**

**The two parables.** In the Tawrah, one description; in the Injil, another — a seed that sends out its shoot, is strengthened by it, thickens, and stands straight on its stem, *delighting the sowers, that He may enrage the disbelievers with them.* The shoot that stiffens the plant is the Companions around the Messenger ﷺ.

From the final clause — *that He may enrage the disbelievers with them* — Imam Malik derived his famous ruling that **the Rawafid are disbelievers**, because they are enraged by the Companions, and this ayah makes rage at the Companions a mark of disbelief. Ibn Kathir notes that several scholars agreed with him.

And the promise closing the surah — *forgiveness and a mighty reward* — with Ibn Kathir's own comment: those who follow the Companions' guidance will be *like* them, but the Companions keep the better virtue, the obvious lead, and a perfection of character **that no one in this Ummah will ever reach.**

> *Do not abuse my Companions. By the One in Whose Hand is my soul! If one of you spent the weight of Uhud in gold, it would never reach the level of one of them spending a Mudd — or half of it.*

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Fath is the surah of the misread outcome.** A treaty signed on humiliating terms, without the Bismillah, without the Prophet's title, sending a chained believer back into torture — and Allah called it *a manifest victory* before its consequences had unfolded.

Every thread confirms this. The Companions couldn't see why they were being stopped; **Allah was protecting Muslims hidden in Makkah whom they would have killed by mistake.** They couldn't see why the clause on returning defectors was accepted; **it created Abu Basir's band, which broke Quraysh's trade until Quraysh themselves begged to cancel it.** Umar couldn't see why the dream hadn't come true; **it hadn't been dated.** The Bedouins calculated that this expedition was a death sentence and stayed home — and lost Khaybar, permanently.

The two responses the surah divides people by are not strength and weakness. They are **Sakinah and suspicion** — the heart that settles into Allah's decision and grows in faith, and the heart that *thinks evil thoughts about Allah* and calls its own cowardice caution.

And when the Companions finally could see nothing at all — frozen in the camp, unable to obey an order that felt like defeat — what moved them was not an argument. It was the Prophet ﷺ walking out in silence and slaughtering his own animal first.
