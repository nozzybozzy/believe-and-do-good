*Headed "Revealed in Makkah" · 78 ayat · the only surah with two prostrations · named for the pilgrimage proclaimed by Ibrahim*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Hajj is the surah that refuses to sit in one category. Its heading says Makkan, but Ibn Kathir points out that **22:25 must be Madinan** (it addresses the blocking of Muslims from the Sacred Mosque), and **22:39 is the first ayah of the Qur'an to permit fighting** — a Madinan concern by definition. So the surah spans the hinge of the Prophet's ﷺ life: the last of the persecution and the first of the response.

Its architecture follows from that. It opens with the **terror of the Hour** and closes with a **command to strive and hold fast**. In between it runs three interlocking arguments: *the Resurrection is rationally obvious* (from the womb and from the rained-on earth); *the House at Makkah belongs to Allah alone and is open to all* (from Ibrahim's foundation to the rites of Hajj); and *those who were driven out will now be defended*. The sacrifice sits at the centre as the surah's own commentary on itself — **neither meat nor blood reaches Allah, only taqwa** — which is the principle the whole surah is applying to everything else.

---

## 1. The Earthquake of the Hour (22:1–2)

> *O mankind! Have Taqwa of your Lord! Verily, the earthquake of the Hour is a terrible thing.*

Two readings are preserved:

- **Alqamah** (via Ibn Jarir): this earthquake comes **before** the Hour, at the end of the world's lifespan.
- **Others**, and the view **Ibn Jarir himself favoured**: it describes the terror in the arena of Judgement, **after** the resurrection from the graves.

Ibn Jarir's evidence is the hadith of **Imran bin Husayn**. The Prophet ﷺ was travelling, some Companions had fallen behind, and he raised his voice reciting these two ayat. They hurried to catch up, knowing he meant to say something. He said: this is the Day Adam will be called and told to send forth those of his progeny destined for the Fire — *from every thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine.*

The Companions stopped smiling. What follows is one of the most tender moments in the tafsir — **the Prophet ﷺ immediately reframing the number**:

- Those thousands are counted together with **Ya'juj and Ma'juj** and all who died of the progeny of Adam and of Iblis.
- *"In comparison to mankind you are like a mole on the flank of a camel, or a mark on the foreleg of a beast."*
- Then, rising: *I hope you will be a **quarter** of the people of Paradise.* — "Allahu Akbar!" — *a **third**.* — "Allahu Akbar!" — *a **half**.* — "Allahu Akbar!"

Ibn Kathir notes the nursing mother's distraction is deliberate wording: Allah says *every nursing mother*, at the very moment of feeding — the horror severs the strongest bond in creation at its most intense point.

---

## 2. Two Kinds of Disputer (22:3–4, 22:8–10)

The surah names them separately, and Ibn Kathir reads this as the whole anatomy of misguidance:

| | The Follower (22:3) | The Leader (22:8) |
|---|---|---|
| Fault | disputes *without knowledge* | disputes *without knowledge, guidance, or a Book giving light* |
| Behaviour | follows every rebellious Shaytan | **bends his neck in pride** |
| Result | driven to the Fire | disgrace **in this world**, then burning |

**"Bending his neck in pride"** — Ibn Abbas: too proud to follow the truth when called to it. Mujahid, Qatadah and Malik (from Zayd): twisting the neck away, the physical posture of contempt. Ibn Kathir gathers the parallels: Fir'awn turning away with his hosts, the hypocrites averting their faces, Luqman telling his son not to turn his face from people in arrogance.

Abu Malik (via As-Suddi) and Ibn Jurayj both say 22:3 was revealed about **An-Nadr bin Al-Harith**.

---

## 3. The Argument From the Womb and From the Rain (22:5–7)

This is the surah's central proof, and it is empirical rather than abstract: *if you doubt the Resurrection, look at how you got here the first time.*

**Stage by stage** — dust (what Adam was made from), then Nutfah, then a clot, then a lump of flesh *"some formed and some unformed"* (Ibn Kathir: this is why some miscarriages are shaped and some are not). Mujahid adds that after forty days as a lump, an angel breathes the soul in and forms it, then writes its provision, its lifespan, and whether it is blessed or wretched.

The hadith of **Ibn Mas'ud** in the Two Sahihs anchors it: forty days collected, forty a clot, forty a lump — then an angel writes **four words**: *his provision, his deeds, his life span, and whether he will be blessed or wretched.*

**Then the reverse arc**: brought out an infant, *weak in body, hearing, sight, senses, stamina and mind*; given strength to the vitality of youth; then either death while strong, or the *"miserable old age"* in which a person **knows nothing after having known**.

**And the parable of the earth** — *Hamidatan*, which Qatadah glosses as eroded and dusty and As-Suddi simply as **dead**. Water falls; it stirs, swells, and puts forth *every lovely kind* — beautiful in appearance and fragrance. Ibn Kathir's conclusion is a single line: **He Who gives life to this is surely able to give life to you.**

---

## 4. Worshipping Allah "Upon the Edge" (22:11–13)

> *And among mankind is he who worships Allah as it were upon the edge.*

Two readings of *upon the edge*: Mujahid and Qatadah say **in doubt**; others say literally on the rim, like the edge of a mountain — *he enters Islam on the edge, and if he finds what he likes he stays, otherwise he leaves.*

**Al-Bukhari records Ibn Abbas's explanation**, and it is startlingly concrete. People would come to Al-Madinah to declare Islam. If their wives bore sons and their mares bore foals, they said *this is a good religion.* If not — *this is a bad religion.* Al-'Awfi's version adds the detail that Madinah was then an unhealthy place: if he stayed well, and got a son, and a foal, he was content. If he got the fever, and a daughter, and his charity was late in coming, the Shaytan would come and say: *by Allah, since you followed this religion you have had nothing but bad.* **That is the Fitnah.**

Mujahid on *he turns back on his face*: he apostatises. And so he loses both worlds — Ibn Kathir calls it *the losing deal.* The idols he turns to are, in Mujahid's word, **an evil Mawla and an evil 'Ashir** — an evil protector and an evil companion to spend one's life beside.

---

## 5. The Rope to the Ceiling (22:15)

> *Whoever thinks that Allah will not help him in this world and in the Hereafter, let him stretch out a rope to the ceiling...*

Ibn Abbas — followed by Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ata', Abu Al-Jawza and Qatadah — reads the pronoun as the Prophet ﷺ: **whoever thinks Allah will not help Muhammad**. The ayah is rhetorical scorn, not instruction: *let him go and see whether that removes his rage.* Ata' Al-Khurasani puts it exactly — *let him see whether that will cure the rage he feels in his heart.*

The point is the certainty on the other side of the taunt: *Verily, We will indeed make victorious Our Messengers and those who believe* (40:51). The help is not in question; only the enemy's ability to bear it is.

---

## 6. Six Communities, One Judge (22:17)

Believers, Jews, Sabians, Christians, Majus, and idolaters. Ibn Kathir refers back to Surah Al-Baqarah for the definitions and the disagreements over who the Sabians were, and lets the ayah do its own work: **Allah will judge between them all on the Day of Resurrection**, for He is Witness over their deeds, what they say aloud and what they conceal in their breasts.

---

## 7. Everything Prostrates (22:18)

The list is deliberate: heavens, earth, **sun, moon, stars**, mountains, trees, *Ad-Dawabb* (all animals), and many of mankind. Ibn Kathir notes the celestial bodies are **named because they were worshipped** — so Allah discloses that they too prostrate to their Creator.

Three narrations give the section its texture:

- **Abu Dharr** (Two Sahihs): *Do you know where this sun goes?* — it sets and prostrates beneath the Throne, then awaits the command to return the way it came.
- **Ibn Abbas**: a man dreamt he prayed behind a tree, prostrated, and the tree prostrated with him, saying: *O Allah, write for me a reward, remove from me a sin, store it with You, and accept it from me as You accepted it from Your servant Dawud.* The Prophet ﷺ then recited an ayah of prostration, prostrated, and said the very same words.
- **Abu Hurayrah** (Muslim): when the son of Adam recites a verse of prostration and prostrates, **the Shaytan withdraws weeping**: *he was commanded to prostrate and he prostrated, so Paradise is his; I was commanded and I refused, so I am doomed.*

**Al-Hajj's two prostrations.** Khalid bin Ma'dan (in Abu Dawud's *Al-Marasil*): *Surah Al-Hajj has been favoured over the rest of the Qur'an with two prostrations.* Umar performed both at Al-Jabiyah and said the same. (Compare 22:77 later: Uqbah bin Amir reports the Prophet ﷺ saying that whoever will not prostrate them should not recite them.)

---

## 8. The Two Disputants, and the Punishment (22:19–22)

**Abu Dharr swore** — recorded in the Two Sahihs — that *These two opponents dispute with each other about their Lord* was revealed about the single combat at **Badr**:

> **Hamzah, Ali and Ubaydah** vs. **Utbah bin Rabi'ah, Shaybah bin Rabi'ah and Al-Walid bin Utbah.**

Al-Bukhari also records **Ali** saying: *"I will be the first to kneel before the Most Merciful so that the dispute may be settled on the Day of Resurrection."*

Mujahid and Ata' read it more broadly — believers and disbelievers disputing about the Resurrection — and **Ibn Kathir prefers this**, noting it *includes* Badr rather than replacing it: the believers want to support Allah's religion, the disbelievers to extinguish its light.

**Then the punishment**, which the tafsir does not soften:

- *Garments of fire cut out for them* — Sa'id bin Jubayr: of **copper**, because it is the hottest thing when heated.
- Boiling water poured on the head: Abu Hurayrah's hadith (via Ibn Jarir, graded Hasan Sahih by At-Tirmidhi) — it penetrates the skull, melts what is inside down to the feet, then the person is restored to his former state and it begins again.
- *Hooked rods of iron* — Ibn Abbas: with each blow a limb is severed, and they cry out for oblivion.
- **Salman**, on the ayah about being driven back every time they try to escape: *"The fire of Hell is black and dark; its flames and coals do not glow or shine."*

---

## 9. The Answering Reward (22:23–24)

Deliberately mirrored against the section above: garments of fire → **garments of silk**; scorn and rebuke → **guidance to goodly speech**.

- **Bracelets of gold and pearls** — and the agreed-upon hadith: *the jewelry of the believer will reach as far as his Wudu' reached.*
- Abdullah bin Az-Zubayr's blunt gloss: those who do not wear silk in the Hereafter are those who will not enter Paradise.
- *Guided unto goodly speech*: some say the Qur'an, some say *La ilaha illallah* and the prescribed words of remembrance. Ibn Kathir sees no contradiction — in Paradise *they will be inspired with glorification and praise just as they are inspired with breath*, while in this world it means guidance to the straight path.

---

## 10. The Sacred Mosque Belongs to Everyone (22:25)

> *...which We have made (open) to (all) men, the dweller in it and the visitor from the country are equal there.*

Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Abu Salih, Qatadah and others: **the people of Makkah and everyone else have equal right to be there.** This is the ayah Ibn Kathir cites as proof that this passage is Madinan.

It also generates one of the tafsir's most interesting legal disputes — argued between **Ash-Shafi'i and Ishaq bin Rahwayh in the Masjid of Al-Khayf, with Ahmad bin Hanbal present**:

- **Ash-Shafi'i**: the houses of Makkah can be owned, inherited and rented. Evidence: the hadith of Usamah bin Zayd (*"Has Aqil left us any property?"*), and Umar buying a house in Makkah from Safwan bin Umayyah for four thousand Dinars and turning it into a prison.
- **Ishaq bin Rahwayh** (with Mujahid and Ata'): they may not be inherited or rented. Evidence: Alqamah bin Nadlah — through the deaths of the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and Umar, nobody claimed property in Makkah; whoever needed to live there did, and whoever didn't let others live there.
- **Abdullah bin 'Amr**, severely: *"Whoever charges rent for the houses of Makkah, consumes fire."*
- **Ahmad bin Hanbal's middle path** (via his son Salih): they may be owned and inherited, **but not rented** — reconciling all the proofs.

A vivid supporting report: Umar forbade the people of Makkah to put **gates** on their houses, so pilgrims could sleep in the courtyards. The first man to fit a gate was **Suhayl bin 'Amr**, who explained he was a trader and needed to protect his goods. Umar said: *then you may do that.*

---

## 11. The Haram Punishes Intention Alone (22:25)

> *...and whoever inclines to evil actions therein or to do wrong, him We shall cause to taste from a painful torment.*

*Or to do wrong* means **deliberately**, not by mistake (Ibn Abbas, via Ibn Jurayj). The suggested meanings stack up rather than compete: Shirk (Ibn Abbas); permitting in the Haram what Allah forbade — mistreating and killing those who have done nothing to you (Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas); insulting a servant *and anything above that* (Sa'id bin Jubayr); hoarding goods in Makkah.

**Mujahid states the principle** that makes this ayah unique in the Qur'an: *this is one of the special features of Al-Haram — the person who intends an evil action there should be punished for the intention, even if he has not yet begun the act.*

Ibn Mas'ud says the same (Ibn Abi Hatim, also Ahmad; Ibn Kathir judges the chain Sahih by Bukhari's conditions, and more likely Mawquf than Marfu'). And the historical demonstration is the **Army of the Elephant** — they merely *planned* to destroy the House and were made *like an empty field of stalks.* The future demonstration is the hadith of the army that will attack the House and be swallowed by the earth in open ground, *the first of them and the last of them.*

---

## 12. Ibrahim, the Site, and the Call (22:26–27)

Allah *showed* Ibrahim the site — Ibn Kathir: guided him to it, entrusted it to him, gave him permission to build. Many scholars take this as evidence that **Ibrahim was the first to build the House**, though Abu Dharr's hadith (Al-Masjid Al-Haram first, Bayt Al-Maqdis forty years later) and *the first House appointed for mankind was that at Bakkah* (3:96) sit alongside it.

Two commands attach to the foundation: **build it in My Name alone**, and **purify it** — Qatadah and Mujahid: *purify it from Shirk*. Ibn Kathir notes that *Tawaf* and *prayer* are named together here because they are prescribed together in **only one place on earth**: Tawaf is done at the Ka'bah and nowhere else, and prayer is offered facing it.

**And proclaim to mankind the Hajj.** The narration gathered from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah and Sa'id bin Jubayr: Ibrahim asked how his voice could possibly reach them, and was told — *call them, and We will convey it.* He stood and called; the mountains lowered themselves so his voice reached every region; and everyone whom Allah has decreed will make pilgrimage until the Day of Resurrection answered — **including those still in their mothers' wombs and their fathers' loins** — *"Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk."*

**On foot or riding?** The ayah mentions *on foot* first, and some scholars took that as preference. **Ibn Abbas**: *"I do not regret anything except that I wish I had performed Hajj on foot."* But the majority hold riding to be better, because the Messenger of Allah ﷺ performed Hajj riding while physically able.

*Fajj 'Amiq* — every route, from every distant place. Ibn Kathir closes with Ibrahim's other prayer, *fill some hearts among men with love towards them* (14:37), and the observation that **there is no Muslim who does not long to see the Ka'bah**.

---

## 13. The Benefits, the Days, and the Ending Tawaf (22:28–29)

*That they may witness things that are of benefit to them* — **Ibn Abbas: benefits in this world and the next.** The Hereafter's benefit is Allah's pleasure; this world's includes the sacrificial animals and lawful trade.

**The appointed days** are, per Ibn Abbas and a long list after him, **the ten days of Dhul-Hijjah**:

- Al-Bukhari from Ibn Abbas: *No deeds are more virtuous than deeds done on these days.* — "Not even Jihad?" — *Not even Jihad, unless a man goes out risking himself and his wealth and comes back with nothing.*
- Ibn Umar (Ahmad): no days greater or more beloved to Allah — *so increase your Tahlil, Takbir and Tahmid.*
- Ibn Umar and Abu Hurayrah would go out into the **marketplace** during the ten days saying Takbir, and the people would take it up from them.
- The ten include **'Arafah** — fasting it, the Prophet ﷺ hoped, expiates the previous year and the coming one — and the **Day of Nahr**, the greatest day of Hajj.

*Then let them complete their prescribed duties* — Ibn Abbas: ending Ihram, shaving, ordinary clothes, trimming the nails. *And circumambulate the 'Atiq House* — Mujahid: the obligatory Tawaf on the Day of Sacrifice. Ibn Abbas to Abu Hamzah: **"The end of the rites is the Tawaf around the 'Atiq House."** Menstruating women are exempt from the farewell circuit.

**Why "Atiq"?** Three answers, all preserved: Al-Hasan Al-Basri — because it is the first House established for mankind; Ikrimah — because it *survived* (*U'tiqa*) the flood of Nuh; Khusayf — **because no tyrant ever conquered it.**

Also noted here: **Al-Hijr** is part of the House. Quraysh left it out when they rebuilt, being short of lawful funds; the Prophet ﷺ included it in his Tawaf, and did not touch the two Shami corners because they were not on Ibrahim's foundations.

---

## 14. Honouring What Is Sacred; Shunning Idols and Lies (22:30–31)

*Whoever honours the sacred things of Allah* — Ibn Kathir: whoever avoids disobedience and treats sin as a serious matter. **The one who avoids sin earns reward just as the one who performs obedience does.**

*So shun the Rijs of the idols, and shun false speech.* Ibn Kathir dwells on the pairing — abomination and lying named in one breath:

- **Abu Bakrah** (Two Sahihs): *Shall I not tell you of the worst of major sins?* — associating others with Allah, disobeying one's parents — then he **sat up** from reclining — *and false statements, and bearing false witness* — and he kept repeating it until they wished he would stop.
- **Khuraym bin Fatik Al-Asadi** (Ahmad): after Fajr the Prophet ﷺ stood and said *bearing false witness is on a par with associating others with Allah* — and then recited this very ayah.

*Hunafa' Lillah* — sincerely submitting to Him alone, shunning falsehood, seeking truth.

**The parable of the idolater** is one of the most violent images in the Qur'an: *as if he had fallen from the sky, and the birds caught him in midair, or the wind had thrown him to a remote place.* Ibn Kathir links it to the hadith of Al-Bara' — when the disbeliever's soul is carried up, the gates of heaven are not opened, and **his soul is thrown back down**.

---

## 15. The Sha'a'ir and the Taqwa of Hearts (22:32–33)

*Whosoever honours the Sha'a'ir of Allah* — His commands — *then it is truly from the Taqwa of the hearts.*

Applied to sacrifice, "honouring" becomes concrete:

- Ibn Abbas (via Miqsam): **choosing fat, healthy animals.** Abu Umamah bin Sahl: *"We used to fatten the Udhiyyah in Al-Madinah, and the Muslims used to fatten them."* (Al-Bukhari)
- The Prophet ﷺ sacrificed two castrated, fat, horned rams.
- Ears examined. Disqualified: the **Muqabilah** (ear cut at the front), **Mudabirah** (cut at the back), **Sharqa** (split), **Kharqa'** (pierced with a hole).
- Al-Bara' (Ahmad, the Sunan, Sahih per At-Tirmidhi): **four are not permitted** — the obviously one-eyed, the obviously sick, the obviously lame, and those with broken bones that no one would choose.

*In them are benefits for you for an appointed term* — the sacrificial camels may be milked, used for wool and hair, and **ridden**. Anas: the Prophet ﷺ saw a man driving his sacrificial camel and said *Ride it.* — "It is a sacrificial camel." — ***Ride it, woe to you!*** Jabir's version: *Ride it gently, according to your need.*

---

## 16. Sacrifice Was Prescribed for Every Nation (22:34–35)

Ibn Abbas glosses the *religious ceremonies* as **festivals**; Ikrimah as **sacrifices**. The point is that shedding blood in Allah's name is not an Arab custom but a universal institution — *your God is One God*, even though the Laws of the Prophets varied and abrogated one another. All of them called to the same thing.

Then the **Mukhbitin**, whom Mujahid defines as *those who find contentment in their faith* and Ath-Thawri as *those who accept the decree of Allah and submit* — but Ibn Kathir says the ayah defines them itself, in four clauses:

1. **hearts that fear** when Allah is mentioned
2. **patience** under whatever befalls
3. **establishing the Salah**
4. **spending** from what they have been given — on family, servants, the poor and needy, treating people kindly within Allah's limits

He notes these are the exact inverse of the hypocrites described in Surah Bara'ah.

---

## 17. Slaughtering the Budn (22:36)

*So mention the Name of Allah over them when they are drawn up in lines.* Ibn Abbas: standing on three legs, the left foreleg tied — *Bismillah, Allahu Akbar, La ilaha illallah, Allahumma minka wa laka.* Ibn Umar corrected a man who had made his camel kneel: **make it stand fettered — this is the Sunnah of Abu Al-Qasim ﷺ.**

Jabir records the Prophet's ﷺ words over his two rams — *I turn my face to the One Who created the heavens and the earth... my prayer, my sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah* — and, crucially, **the intention he attached to them**: one *on behalf of me and anyone of my Ummah who has not offered a sacrifice*, the other for himself and his family. Abu Rafi's version: *O Allah, this is on behalf of all of my Ummah, whoever bears witness to Your Tawhid and that I have conveyed.*

**Then, when they are down on their sides** — Mujahid: fallen to the ground; Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd: **when they have died**. One may not eat until movement has ceased. Umar's rule was *do not rush until you are sure the animal is dead*, and behind it stands Shaddad bin Aws in Sahih Muslim:

> *Allah has prescribed proficiency in all things. If you kill, kill well; and if you slaughter, slaughter well. Let each of you sharpen his blade and spare suffering to the animal he slaughters.*

And: **whatever is cut from an animal while it is still alive is Maytah.**

**Qani' and Mu'tarr.** Ibn Abbas has two reported positions — that Qani' is the poor man content in his house who does not ask and Mu'tarr the one who comes near without asking; or that Qani' is the one too proud to ask and Mu'tarr the one who does. Some scholars derived the familiar **division into thirds** (eat, gift, give away) from this ayah, but **Ibn Kathir says the ayah does not actually establish it.** What is established: *I used to forbid you to keep the meat beyond three days — now eat, keep, and give in charity*; and, on the skins, *make use of them, but do not sell them.*

**Timing**: Al-Bara' — whoever slaughters before the 'Id prayer has merely brought meat for his family, *it is not a sacrifice at all.* One waits for the Imam. The days of Tashriq are all days of sacrifice (Jubayr bin Mut'im).

---

## 18. The Verse the Surah Turns On (22:37)

> *It is neither their meat nor their blood that reaches Allah, but it is Taqwa from you that reaches Him.*

Ibn Jurayj supplies the occasion: the people of the Jahiliyyah would place the meat of their sacrifices and **sprinkle the blood on the House** — and some Companions said, *we have more right to do that.* The ayah came down against the impulse itself.

Ibn Kathir sets two hadiths beside it:

- *Allah does not look at your appearance or your colours, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds.*
- *Charity falls into the Hand of Ar-Rahman before it falls into the hand of the one asking.*

**Note on the Udhiyyah**, appended here: it is **Sunnah Mustahabbah**, and **one animal suffices for an entire household**. Abu Ayyub's testimony is the sharpest thing in the section — *"At the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ a man would sacrifice one sheep for himself and all his household, and they would eat and feed others, until people started **boasting**, and things reached the stage you see now."* Abdullah bin Hisham likewise sacrificed one sheep for his whole family. And the animals must be mature, or else a young sheep if nothing else is possible.

---

## 19. Allah Defends Those Who Believe (22:38)

The hinge into the second half of the surah. He *defends* — protects, guards, supports — those who trust Him and turn to Him, against the worst of people and the plots of sinners. *Is not Allah sufficient for His servant?* (39:36)

And the counterweight: *Allah likes not any treacherous ingrate.* **Treachery** is breaking covenants and not doing what one says; **ingratitude** is receiving blessings without acknowledging them.

---

## 20. The First Ayah of Jihad (22:39–41)

> *Permission (to fight) is given to those who are fought against, because they have been wronged.*

Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Ibn Abbas, Urwah bin Az-Zubayr, Zayd bin Aslam, Muqatil and Qatadah all state: **this is the first ayah revealed about fighting.** Ibn Abbas gives the moment — when the Prophet ﷺ was driven from Makkah, **Abu Bakr** said, *"They have expelled their Prophet; truly to Allah we belong and to Him we return — surely they are doomed."* Then this ayah came, and Abu Bakr said: **"Then I knew there would be fighting."**

**Ibn Kathir's explanation of the timing is one of the most important passages in the surah.** In Makkah the idolaters outnumbered the Muslims more than ten to one; fighting then would have been disastrous. Only after the persecution escalated, the emigrations scattered them, the Prophet ﷺ joined them at Al-Madinah, and they had *a place where Islam prevailed and a stronghold to retreat to* — only then was Jihad prescribed. And even so: *Allah is able to give them victory* **without any fighting at all** — He wills that His servants strive.

*Only because they said: "Our Lord is Allah."* — they had committed no wrong against their people **except** believing in Allah's Oneness. Which, to the idolaters, was the worst of sins.

**And then the ayah that widens the whole subject:** were it not that Allah checks one set of people by means of another, there would have been pulled down —

| Term | Identified as |
|---|---|
| **Sawami'** | monks' cells / small temples (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak); Sabian or Zoroastrian places of worship (Qatadah) |
| **Biya'** | larger churches, holding more worshippers |
| **Salawat** | churches (Ibn Abbas); Jewish synagogues (Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah); Sabian places of worship (Abu Al-Aliyah) |
| **Masjids** | the Muslims' |

**Ibn Jarir's verdict**: the correct view is that all four are meant — monasteries, churches, synagogues and mosques alike would have been destroyed. Some scholars read the sequence as ascending from smallest to greatest, ending with the Masjids as most frequented by those with correct intention.

**Duties on attaining power (22:41).** Uthman bin Affan said the ayah was revealed about them: *we were expelled unjustly only for saying "Our Lord is Allah," then we were given power, so we established the prayer, paid Zakah, enjoined good and forbade evil.* And **Umar bin Abdul-Aziz**, in a public speech, extended it past the rulers to the ruled — your right over your governor is that he check on you regarding your duties to Allah, restore your rights against one another, and guide you to the straight path as far as he can; your duty to him is obedience without cheating and without resentment, in secret as in the open.

---

## 21. Consolation by History (22:42–46)

The list of deniers — Nuh, 'Ad, Thamud, the people of Ibrahim, of Lut, the dwellers of Madyan, and Musa — is Allah consoling the Prophet ﷺ: *this is what has always happened.*

*But I granted respite... then I seized them.* Abu Musa (Two Sahihs): **Allah lets the wrongdoer carry on until, when He seizes him, He never lets him go** — followed by the recitation of *Such is the punishment of your Lord when He seizes the towns while they are doing wrong.*

The ruins are itemised: houses levelled to their roofs (Ad-Dahhak); a **deserted well** from which no one draws water though crowds once came to it; a **castle Mashid** — plastered white (Ikrimah, Ali, Mujahid, Ata', Sa'id bin Jubayr) or high and impenetrable (others). Ibn Kathir says both readings agree in effect: **the height and the plaster protected no one**.

*Have they not travelled through the land?* — travelled physically **and used their minds**. Ibn Kathir quotes Ibn Abi Ad-Dunya's *At-Tafakkur wal-I'tibar*, a passage of advice from the wise that reads like a discipline:

> Give life to your heart with lessons; illuminate it with thought; kill it with asceticism; strengthen it with certain faith; remind it of its mortality; make it aware of the calamities of this world; warn it of what life may bring; show it how suddenly things change; tell it the stories of the people of the past.

And the conclusion the surah has been building toward: **it is not the eyes that grow blind, but the hearts within the breasts.** Sound eyes that cannot draw a lesson are no use at all.

---

## 22. A Thousand Years as One Day (22:47–48)

They demand the punishment be hurried — as elsewhere they said *rain down stones on us* and *hasten our punishment before the Day of Reckoning.* The answer is not a threat but a statement of scale: **what counts as a thousand years to His creation is as a single day with Him.** He does not hasten, He does not forget, and He misses nothing though He delays.

Two hadiths on that half-day: Abu Hurayrah — *the poor among the Muslims will enter Paradise half a day before the rich — five hundred years.* And Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas — *I hope it will not be too much for my Ummah if Allah delays them half a day.* Asked what half a day meant, Sa'd said: **five hundred years.**

---

## 23. What the Shaytan Throws In — and Ibn Kathir on the Gharaniq (22:52–54)

> *Never did We send a Messenger or a Prophet before you but when he recited, Shaytan threw (some falsehood) in his recitation. But Allah abolishes that which Shaytan throws in, then Allah establishes His revelations.*

This is where many exegetes told the story of the **Gharaniq** — the so-called "satanic verses" episode, and the return of some emigrants from Ethiopia who believed Quraysh had accepted Islam. **Ibn Kathir's judgement is flat and unhedged:**

> *These reports all come through Mursal chains of narration, and I do not think that any of them may be regarded as Sahih. And Allah knows best.*

He then gives what the ayah does mean, from Al-Bukhari's narration of **Ibn Abbas**: *when he spoke, the Shaytan threw something into his speech — and Allah abolished what the Shaytan threw in.* Ali bin Abi Talhah from Ibn Abbas and Mujahid read *recite* as *speak*; Al-Baghawi and the majority read it as reciting the Book, with the interference falling in the recitation. Ibn Jarir judged the latter closer to interpretation. **Naskh** here carries its root sense: *to remove, to lift away.*

The purpose is stated in the ayat themselves — a **trial** sorting three groups: those with **disease** in their hearts (Ibn Jurayj: the hypocrites), those whose hearts are **hardened** (the idolaters), and **those given knowledge**, who recognise it as the truth from their Lord, believe in it, and whose hearts **submit to it with humility**. Ibn Kathir attaches to that last group the guarantee of 41:42: *falsehood cannot come to it from before it or behind it.*

---

## 24. Those Who Emigrated and Then Died (22:58–60)

The reward is promised equally to those **killed** in Jihad and those who simply **died** without ever fighting — Ibn Kathir stresses this twice. *Whosoever leaves his home as an emigrant unto Allah and His Messenger, and death overtakes him, his reward is then surely incumbent upon Allah* (4:100).

Two reports carry the section:

- **Salman Al-Farisi**, passing Shurahbil bin As-Simt during a long siege in Roman territory, quoted the Prophet ﷺ: *whoever dies guarding the borders of Islam, Allah will give him a reward like that of a martyr, will provide for him, and will keep him safe from trials* — then said, **recite this ayah if you wish.**
- **Fadalah bin Ubayd** sat by the grave of the man who had died, not the one struck by a catapult. Asked why he was neglecting the martyr, he answered: *"I would not mind which of these two graves Allah resurrects me from"* — and recited these two ayat.

*Most Forbearing* here means: He forgives and overlooks their sins, **accepting their Hijrah and their trust in Him as expiation.**

---

## 25. Signs, and the Restraint of the Sky (22:61–66)

A run of proofs, each ending in a name of Allah:

- **He merges night into day and day into night** — Ibn Kathir explains it as encroachment: the night long and the day short in winter, and the reverse in summer.
- **Rain, then green** — *Most Kind and Courteous, Well-Acquainted with all things*: He knows every seed in every region however small, and each receives its share of water. Ibn Kathir gathers Luqman's mustard seed in the rock, *not a leaf falls but He knows it*, and *not the weight of a speck of dust*.
- **The ships** sailing a raging sea gently, carrying people and goods between lands, because He subjugated it.
- **He withholds the heaven from falling on the earth** — *if He willed, He could give the sky permission to fall.* That He does not is named as **kindness and mercy toward mankind** — Ibn Kathir adds, pointedly, *even though they do wrong.*
- **He gave you life, will cause you to die, and will give you life again** — *and man is indeed Kafurun*, denying.

---

## 26. The Fly (22:73–74)

> *Verily, those on whom you call besides Allah cannot create a fly, even though they combine together for the purpose.*

The parable has two edges. First: **all** the false gods, pooling all their power, could not produce one fly. Ibn Kathir supplies the divine challenge from the Two Sahihs — *Who does more wrong than one who tries to create something like My creation? Let them create an ant, or a fly, or a grain of barley.*

Second, and worse: **if the fly takes something from them, they cannot get it back.** They cannot create the weakest of creatures, and cannot defend against it either.

*So weak are the seeker and the sought.* Ibn Abbas: the seeker is the idol, the sought is the fly — Ibn Jarir preferred this and Ibn Kathir says it fits the context. As-Suddi: the seeker is the worshipper and the sought is the idol.

*They have not regarded Allah with His rightful esteem* — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, they have not recognised His might, since they worship beside Him things that cannot fend off an insect.

---

## 27. He Chooses His Messengers (22:75–76)

Messengers from among the **angels** and from among **men** — chosen by His decree, He being All-Hearer and All-Seer, knowing *best with whom to place His Message.* And He knows what is before them and behind them: none of their affairs are hidden, and He guards them. Ibn Kathir cites the watching guards marching before and behind the chosen Messenger until *he knows that they have conveyed the Messages of their Lord* (72:26–28), and *Allah will protect you from mankind* (5:67).

---

## 28. The Closing Charge (22:77–78)

> *O you who believe! Bow down, and prostrate yourselves, and worship your Lord and do good, that you may be successful. And strive hard in Allah's cause as you ought to strive.*

The second sajdah of the surah lands here. **Uqbah bin Amir**: *Surah Al-Hajj has been blessed with two Sajdah, so whoever does not prostrate them should not recite them.*

*Strive as you ought to strive* — Ibn Kathir: **with your wealth, your tongues and your bodies**, on the pattern of *have Taqwa of Allah as is His due.*

Then five gifts, each of which is also an obligation:

1. **He has chosen you** — selected this Ummah above the nations, honouring it with the noblest of Messengers and the noblest of Laws.
2. **No hardship in the religion.** Ibn Kathir's proof is the prayer itself: four rak'ahs when settled, two when travelling, one in times of fear according to some Imams; prayed walking or riding; sitting if ill, and lying on one's side if that is not possible. *I have been sent with the easy Hanifi way* — and to Mu'adh and Abu Musa at Yemen: **make things easy and do not make them difficult; give good news and do not repel people.**
3. **The religion of your father Ibrahim** — Ibn Jarir offers two readings: that it is *easy, like* Ibrahim's religion, or that it is a command to *adhere to* it.
4. **He has named you Muslims** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ata', Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi, Muqatil and Qatadah agree the Namer is **Allah Himself**, before the Qur'an (in the earlier Books) and in it. An-Nasa'i records the Prophet ﷺ warning that whoever adopts the call of Jahiliyyah will crawl on his knees in Hell — *"even if he fasts and prays?"* — **yes, even so** — *so adopt the call of Allah, whereby He called you Muslims and believers and servants of Allah.*
5. **Witnesses over mankind**, with the Messenger a witness over you — this Ummah's testimony accepted on the Day of Resurrection that the Messengers delivered their Message.

The response to all five: *perform the Salah, give the Zakah, and hold fast to Allah* — seek His help, trust Him, draw strength from Him.

> *He is your Mawla — what an Excellent Mawla and what an Excellent Helper!*

---

## The Whole Surah in One Line

**Al-Hajj takes the House that Ibrahim built for all mankind, the pilgrimage he was told to proclaim to people who did not yet exist, and the animals brought to be slaughtered there — and says of every one of them that the outward act is not what arrives: neither meat nor blood reaches Allah, only taqwa from you.** It then applies that same test to everything else in the surah — to the worshipper standing on the edge who wants sons and foals, to the man too proud to bend his neck, to the false gods that cannot make a fly, and to the emigrants driven from their homes for saying *our Lord is Allah*, who are now told that they may fight, that Allah defends them, and that when they hold power they must pray, give, command good and forbid evil.

---

*End of the Tafsir of Surah Al-Hajj. May Allah bless our Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and his family and Companions, and grant them peace.*
