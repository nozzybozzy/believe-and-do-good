*Revealed in Madinah · 24 ayat · "The Gathering" — though Ibn Abbas simply called it **Surah Bani An-Nadir**, and Ibn Ishaq held that the entire surah was revealed about them*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Hashr takes a single military episode — the expulsion of one Jewish tribe from Madinah, achieved in six days without a battle — and uses it to teach almost everything a community needs to know about **power, property, and the heart**.

The structure is unusually clean:

- **What happened** (2–5): a treaty broken, a plot to kill the Prophet ﷺ, fortresses that proved worthless, and men demolishing their own houses with their own hands.
- **What to do with what was gained** (6–10): the law of *Fai'* — wealth acquired without fighting — and the three generations of believers who deserve a share in it, culminating in the most celebrated portrait of the Ansar in the Qur'an.
- **Who let them down** (11–17): the hypocrites who promised support and delivered nothing, likened to Shaytan, who says *"Disbelieve"* and then *"I am free of you."*
- **What it means for you** (18–24): look at what you have sent ahead for tomorrow; do not forget Allah lest He make you forget yourself; and then a closing crescendo of the Divine Names so weighty that a mountain would crumble under it.

The through-line: **the Jews trusted their forts, the hypocrites trusted their words, and the Ansar trusted Allah — and the surah ends by telling you which of the three is worth being.**

---

## 1. Everything Glorifies Him (59:1)

> *Whatsoever is in the heavens and whatsoever is on the earth glorifies Allah. And He is the Almighty, the All-Wise.*

Everything in existence praises, glorifies, reveres, prays to Him and affirms His Oneness. Ibn Kathir pairs this with 17:44 — *there is not a thing but glorifies His praise, but you understand not their glorification.*

**Al-'Aziz** — of invincible majesty. **Al-Hakim** — All-Wise in what He decrees and legislates. The surah opens and closes on this same pair of Names, framing everything in between.

---

## 2. The Expulsion of Bani An-Nadir (59:2)

> *He it is Who drove out the disbelievers among the People of the Scripture from their homes at the first gathering.*

**The background.** When the Prophet ﷺ migrated to Madinah he made a peace treaty with the Jewish tribes: he would not fight them, they would not fight him. Bani An-Nadir soon betrayed it.

**The immediate trigger.** After the massacre of the seventy Qur'an-teachers at Bi'r Ma'unah, the sole survivor — 'Amr bin Umayyah Ad-Damri — killed two men of Bani 'Amir on his way home, not knowing they held a promise of safe passage from the Prophet ﷺ. He ﷺ said: *"You have killed two men; I shall pay the blood money for them."* Bani An-Nadir were allies of Bani 'Amir by treaty, so the Prophet ﷺ went to their settlement, a few miles east of Madinah, to ask their contribution.

**The plot.** They answered warmly — *"Yes, O Abu Al-Qasim, we will help you"* — then conferred privately: ***"You will not find a better chance with this man than this."*** He ﷺ was sitting beside the wall of one of their houses. 'Amr bin Jihash bin Ka'b volunteered to climb the roof and drop a stone on him. Abu Bakr, 'Umar and 'Ali were sitting with him.

**News of the plot was conveyed to him from heaven.** He stood up and walked back to Madinah without a word. When the Companions eventually went looking, a man told them he had seen the Prophet ﷺ enter the city. He told them what the Jews had planned and ordered them to march.

**The siege.** Six days. Their forts were formidable, and no one expected them to fall so quickly — *You did not think that they would get out.* They had every confidence in their walls: *And they thought that their fortresses would defend them from Allah!*

> *But Allah reached them from a place whereof they expected it not, and He cast terror into their hearts.*

Ibn Kathir's aside here is worth pausing on: **why would terror not fall on them, when the man laying siege to them was the one Allah aided by frightening his enemies a month's distance away?**

He sets beside it 16:26 — *Allah struck at the foundation of their building, and then the roof fell down upon them, and the torment overtook them from directions they did not perceive.*

**Their own hands.** Allowed to leave with whatever their camels could carry, they stripped their own houses — pulling down roofs and doors, one man demolishing the wall around his door so he could load the door itself onto his camel. Hence:

> *They demolished their own dwellings with their own hands and the hands of the believers. Then take admonition, O you with eyes.*

Meaning, says Ibn Kathir: **contemplate the end of those who defied Allah's command, contradicted His Messenger and denied His Book — see how humiliating torment struck them in this life, and what painful torment waits in the next.**

They went to Khaybar and to Adhri'at in Ash-Sham — *the land of the grand Gathering*, which is part of why the surah carries the name it does. **Only two men of the tribe accepted Islam** — Yamin bin 'Umayr bin Ka'b and Abu Sa'd bin Wahb — and thereby kept their wealth.

## 3. Exile Instead of Slaughter (59:3–4)

> *And had it not been that Allah had decreed exile for them, He would certainly have punished them in this world; and in the Hereafter theirs shall be the torment of the Fire.*

Az-Zuhri, 'Urwah and As-Suddi: had exile not already been decreed, they would have faced killing and captivity instead. Ibn Zayd: the decree covered both — punishment here, and the Fire there.

**The reason is stated flatly:** *That is because they opposed Allah and His Messenger.* They defied him and denied the good news of Muhammad ﷺ contained in the earlier Books — news the Jews knew, Ibn Kathir says, **as well as they knew their own children.**

---

## 4. The Palm Trees (59:5)

> *What you cut down of the Linah, or you left them standing on their stems, it was by leave of Allah, and in order that He might disgrace the rebellious.*

The Prophet ﷺ ordered the date trees of Bani An-Nadir cut down and burned — at Al-Buwayrah — to break their morale. **Linah** is a fine variety of date palm (Abu 'Ubaydah: distinct from Ajwah and Barni; Ibn Jarir: all date trees).

Two objections arose, and the ayah answers both at once:

- **From the Jews**, who called out: *"O Muhammad! You used to forbid mischief in the earth and blame those who did it — why the burning?"*
- **From the Muslims themselves.** Some emigrants discouraged the cutting, arguing the trees were now war spoils. The Companions hesitated, saying: *"We cut some and left some. We must ask the Messenger whether we are rewarded for what we cut or burdened for what we left."*

The answer: **both parties acted by Allah's leave.** Neither group is blamed. What was felled and what was spared were equally by His permission, will and pleasure — to disgrace the rebellious.

---

## 5. The Law of Fai' (59:6–7)

> *And what Allah gave as booty (Fai') to His Messenger from them — for this you made no expedition with either cavalry or camelry.*

**Fai'** is wealth acquired from the disbelievers **without fighting**. Bani An-Nadir's property qualified precisely because there was no battle: Allah forced them out of their forts by the fear He placed in their hearts. It therefore belonged to the Messenger ﷺ to spend at his discretion — and he spent it on righteous causes and the benefit of the Muslims.

The ruling then extends beyond that one tribe: *What Allah gave as booty to His Messenger from the people of the townships* — every settlement taken in the same manner falls under the same rule.

**The distribution list:** Allah, His Messenger, the kindred, the orphans, the poor, the wayfarer —

> *In order that it may not become a fortune used by the rich among you.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is one of the most quoted lines in the surah: the expenditures are structured **so that wealth does not remain circulating among the wealthy, spent as they wish and desire, with none of it reaching the poor.**

### 'Umar's testimony

Imam Ahmad records 'Umar's summary: the wealth of Bani An-Nadir was Fai'; the Prophet ﷺ took from it his family's expenses **for one year at a time**, and the remainder went to buying weapons and mounts for the cause of Allah.

The long version — Abu Dawud, via Malik bin Aws — is a scene worth reading whole. 'Umar, sitting on a bedstead of palm leaves with no mattress, is asked to judge between **Al-'Abbas and 'Ali**, who had come seeking the Prophet's ﷺ property as inheritance. He puts the same oath to both parties and to the four witnesses ('Uthman, 'Abdur-Rahman bin 'Awf, Az-Zubayr, Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas):

> *"Our property will not be inherited. Whatever we leave is charity."*

All confirm it. 'Umar then explains: this property was **specially given** to the Messenger ﷺ — *"yet by Allah, neither did he take possession of it and leave you, nor did he favour himself with it to your exclusion."* He took a year's expenses and left the rest in the Muslim treasury. Abu Bakr managed it that way; 'Umar managed it that way; he had handed it to the two of them only on the pledge that they would manage it the same way. **And he refuses to revisit his own ruling: *"By Allah, I will never give any decision other than what I have already given, until the Last Hour begins."***

## 6. Take What He Gives You (59:7)

> *And whatsoever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatsoever he forbids you, abstain from it. And have Taqwa of Allah; verily, Allah is Severe in punishment.*

Whatever he commands, do; whatever he forbids, avoid — **for he commands only righteousness and forbids only evil.**

The illustrating story is Ibn Mas'ud and **Umm Ya'qub**, a woman of Bani Asad. He had cursed those who tattoo and are tattooed, who pluck their eyebrows and file gaps between their teeth for beauty, *altering Allah's creation*. She confronted him: *"I have read the whole Qur'an and did not find in it what you say."*

His reply: *"If you have truly read it, you have found it. Did you not read — **and whatsoever the Messenger gives you, take it, and whatsoever he forbids you, abstain from it**?"*

She then insinuated that his own wife did these things; he said, *"Go and look at her."* She found nothing. He added: **"If my wife were as you thought, I would not keep her with me."**

Paired with it, the hadith of Abu Hurayrah:

> *When I order you to do something, do as much of it as you can. If I forbid something, then shun it.*

## 7. The Three Generations Who Deserve a Share (59:8–10)

The surah now names who the Fai' is for — and in doing so ranks the whole ummah.

### (i) The Muhajirin (59:8)

> *For the poor emigrants, who were expelled from their homes and their property, seeking bounties from Allah and His good pleasure, and helping Allah and His Messenger. Such are indeed the truthful.*

They left their homes and defied their own people for nothing but Allah's acceptance. **Truthful in statement and in deed** — and they are the chiefs of the emigrants.

### (ii) The Ansar (59:9)

> *And those who, before them, had homes and had adopted the faith, love those who emigrate to them, and have no jealousy in their breasts for that which they have been given, and give them preference over themselves even though they were in need of that.*

Three separate virtues, in order:

**They loved them.** Anas reports the Muhajirin saying: *"O Messenger of Allah, we have never met people like those we emigrated to — comforting us in scarcity, giving generously in abundance. They have sufficed for us and shared their wealth so much that we feared they might take the whole reward instead of us."* He ﷺ answered: **"No — not as long as you thank them for what they did and invoke Allah for them."**

**They did not envy.** No resentment at the higher rank and status Allah gave the Muhajirin over them.

**They preferred others to themselves — *ithar*.** When the Prophet ﷺ offered to divide Al-Bahrayn among the Ansar, they said: *"Not until you give a similar portion to our emigrant brothers."* When they asked him to divide their date palms with the emigrants and he said no, they told the Muhajirin: *"Tend the trees and share the fruits with us"* — and the emigrants said, **"We hear and obey."**

> *The best charity is that given when one is in need and struggling.*

Ibn Kathir ranks this **above** the station of those described in 76:8 (*they give food, in spite of their love for it*) and 2:177 (*gives his wealth in spite of love for it*) — because those give what they love but do not need, while these give what they themselves require.

**The illustrations he chooses:**

- **Abu Bakr** gave away all his wealth. Asked what he had left his family: *"I kept for them Allah and His Messenger."*
- **At Al-Yarmuk**, Ikrimah bin Abi Jahl and two other wounded men were each offered water, and each directed it to another of the three. By the time it reached the third, **all three had died and none had drunk.**
- **Abu Talhah Al-Ansari**, the occasion of the ayah's revelation. A starving man came to the Prophet ﷺ; none of his wives had food. Abu Talhah took him home and told his wife to honour the Messenger's guest. She said: *"By Allah, we have nothing but the children's meal."* He said: *"Put the children to sleep if they ask for supper, turn off the lamp, and we will go to bed hungry."* In the morning the Prophet ﷺ told him: **"Allah wondered — or laughed — at what you and your wife did last night."** And the ayah came down.

### The greed that ruins, and the greed that doesn't

> *And whosoever is saved from his own greed (shuhh), such are they who will be the successful.*

Jabir reports the Prophet ﷺ warning of two things together:

> *Beware of oppression, for oppression is darkness on the Day of Resurrection. And beware of shuhh, for shuhh destroyed those before you — it made them shed blood and make lawful what was forbidden to them.*

A man once came to Ibn Mas'ud in terror: *"I fear I have earned destruction — I hear this ayah, and I am a miser who barely gives anything away."* Ibn Mas'ud's answer is a genuine mercy:

> **"That is not the *shuhh* Allah mentioned in the Qur'an — that pertains to illegally consuming your brother's wealth. What you have is miserliness, and it is an evil thing indeed to be a miser."**

Stinginess is a flaw to fight. *Shuhh* is the grasping that violates other people's rights — and that is what destroys.

### (iii) Those who came after (59:10)

> *And those who came after them say: "Our Lord! Forgive us and our brethren who have preceded us in faith, and put not in our hearts any hatred against those who have believed. Our Lord! You are indeed full of kindness, Most Merciful."*

The third category: **everyone who follows the Muhajirin and Ansar in good works and beautiful conduct, and who prays for them in public and in private.** Compare 9:100.

*Hatred* here means **rage or envy** toward the believers.

'Umar's own counsel to whoever would succeed him: know the rights and virtues of the foremost Muhajirin and preserve their honour; be kind to the Ansar — accept the good from those who do good among them, and forgive those who err.

**Imam Malik** drew a striking legal conclusion from this ayah: those who curse the Companions have no share in the Fai' money, since they lack the very quality by which Allah describes the deserving here. And 'A'ishah, reciting it, said: **"They were commanded to invoke Allah to forgive them — but instead they cursed them."**

## 8. The Hypocrites' Empty Promise (59:11–14)

> *"If you are expelled, we indeed will go out with you, and we shall never obey anyone against you; and if you are attacked, we shall indeed help you." But Allah is Witness that they verily are liars.*

'Abdullah bin Ubayy and his circle — along with Malik bin Abi Qawqal, Suwayd, Da'is and others — sent word to Bani An-Nadir: *"Be firm and strong. We will never abandon you. If you are fought, we fight with you; if you are exiled, we go with you."*

**The Jews waited for this support. It never came.** Allah's verdict is delivered in three descending stages: they will not leave with them; they will not help them; and even if they did fight alongside them, **they would turn their backs and not be victorious.** Ibn Kathir calls this *good news* — a guarantee to the believers before the fact.

Then the diagnosis:

> *Verily, you are more fearful in their breasts than Allah. That is because they are a people who comprehend not.*

They fear you more than they fear Allah — as in 4:77, *a section of them fear men as they fear Allah, or even more.*

> *They fight not against you even together, except in fortified townships, or from behind walls.*

**Cowardice.** For all their threats, they will only fight from behind fortifications, and only when forced to defend themselves.

> *Their enmity among themselves is very great. You would think they were united, but their hearts are divided.*

You may see them massed and assume the alliance is solid. **In reality it is severely fractured.** Ibrahim An-Nakha'i applied this to the hypocrites and the People of the Scripture together. Compare 6:65 — *and make you taste the violence of one another.*

Their precedent: *They are like their immediate predecessors; they tasted the evil result of their conduct* — which Ibn Abbas, Qatadah and Ibn Ishaq identify as **Bani Qaynuqa'**, expelled earlier.

## 9. The Parable of Shaytan (59:15–17)

> *Like Shaytan, when he says to man: "Disbelieve." But when he disbelieves, Shaytan says: "I am free of you, I fear Allah, the Lord of all that exists!"*

The hypocrites talked the Jews into confrontation with promises of aid, then vanished when the siege closed in and left them to taste utter defeat. **That is exactly the devil's method:** he lures a man into disbelief, and the moment the man obeys, disowns him entirely.

> *So, the end of both will be that they will be in the Fire, abiding therein. Such is the recompense of the wrongdoers.*

**Both** — the one who issued the call to disbelief and the one who answered it. The instigator gains nothing by having only instigated.

## 10. Look At What You Have Sent Ahead (59:18–20)

> *O you who believe! Have Taqwa of Allah and let every person look to what he has sent forth for tomorrow, and fear Allah. Verily, Allah is All-Aware of what you do.*

Ibn Kathir's reading: **hold yourselves accountable before you are held accountable.** Examine what you have stored up for the day of your return and your parade before your Lord. Taqwa is named twice, before and after — obeying what He ordered, avoiding what He forbade.

**The hadith of Jarir** is the great illustration. A delegation of Mudar arrived barefoot, near-naked, in striped wool with swords slung at their necks. **The Prophet's ﷺ face changed colour** at their poverty. He went in, came out, ordered Bilal to call the Adhan and Iqamah, led the prayer, then addressed the people — reciting 4:1, and then this ayah: *let every person look to what he has sent forth for tomorrow.*

Then: *"Let a man donate his dinar, his dirham, his clothes, his measure of wheat, his measure of dates"* — until he said — **"even half a date."**

An Ansari came with a moneybag his hands could barely lift. Then the flood followed, until Jarir saw **two heaps of food and clothing**, and saw the Messenger's ﷺ face *shining with pleasure like gold*. He said:

> *Whoever sets a good example in Islam has its reward and the reward of everyone who acts on it after him, without any decrease in their rewards. And whoever sets an evil example in Islam bears its burden and the burden of everyone who acts on it after him, without any decrease in their burdens.*

Then the warning that gives this passage its weight:

> *And be not like those who forgot Allah, and He caused them to forget themselves. Those are the rebellious.*

**Forget the remembrance of Allah and He will make you forget the deeds that would have saved you** — because the recompense matches the action. Compare 63:9: *Let not your properties or your children divert you from the remembrance of Allah.*

And the closing contrast:

> *Not equal are the dwellers of the Fire and the dwellers of Paradise. It is the dwellers of Paradise that will be successful.*

Ibn Kathir stacks the parallels: 45:21 (*do those who earn evil deeds think We shall hold them equal with those who believe?*), 40:58 (*not equal are the blind and those who see*), 38:28 (*shall We treat the God-fearing as the wicked?*).

## 11. The Mountain That Would Crumble (59:21)

> *Had We sent down this Qur'an on a mountain, you would surely have seen it humbling itself and rent asunder by the fear of Allah. Such are the parables which We put forward to mankind that they may reflect.*

The argument is a fortiori and it is aimed at you. **If a mountain — hard, immense — were given the capacity to understand this Qur'an, it would crumble.** Then what excuse do you have, who *do* understand His command and comprehend His Book, for a heart that stays hard?

Ibn Kathir brings in the *mutawatir* hadith of **the weeping tree trunk**. The Prophet ﷺ used to lean against a palm trunk in the Masjid when he spoke. When a minbar was built and he passed the trunk to climb it, **the trunk cried out like an infant** — missing the remembrance of Allah and the revelation that had been recited beside it. Al-Hasan Al-Basri's comment after narrating it:

> **"You — mankind — are more worthy to miss the Messenger of Allah than the tree trunk."**

Compare 13:31 (*if there were a Qur'an by which mountains could be moved* — it would be this one) and 2:74 (*there are stones which fall down for fear of Allah*).

## 12. The Closing Names (59:22–24)

Three ayat, delivered as an ascending litany.

> *He is Allah, beside Whom La ilaha illa Huwa, the All-Knower of the unseen and the seen. He is the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.*

He alone is worthy of worship; everything else worshipped is false. He knows what we can see and what we cannot — nothing in heaven or earth escapes Him, **down to ants in darkness.**

> *He is Allah, beside Whom La ilaha illa Huwa, Al-Malik, Al-Quddus, As-Salam, Al-Mu'min, Al-Muhaymin, Al-'Aziz, Al-Jabbar, Al-Mutakabbir. Glory be to Allah! Above all that they associate as partners with Him.*

| Name | Ibn Kathir's gloss |
|---|---|
| **Al-Malik** | The Owner and King of all things, with full power, without resistance or hindrance |
| **Al-Quddus** | *The Pure* (Wahb bin Munabbih); *The Blessed* (Mujahid, Qatadah); *He Whom the honourable angels glorify* (Ibn Jurayj) |
| **As-Salam** | Free of any defect or shortcoming that could diminish His perfect attributes and actions |
| **Al-Mu'min** | He who granted safety to His servants by promising never to be unjust to them (Ibn Abbas); who affirms His own statements true (Qatadah); who attested to His faithful servants' faith (Ibn Zayd) |
| **Al-Muhaymin** | The Witness over His servants' actions, the Ever-Watcher (58:6, 10:46, 13:33) |
| **Al-'Aziz** | The Almighty, dominant over all things; His majesty is never violated |
| **Al-Jabbar, Al-Mutakabbir** | The only One worthy of being the Compeller and the Supreme |

On the last pair, the *qudsi* hadith:

> *Might is My Izar and pride is My Rida; if anyone disputes either of them with Me, I will punish him.*

> *He is Allah, Al-Khaliq, Al-Bari, Al-Musawwir. To Him belong the Best Names.*

**Al-Khaliq** — measuring and proportioning. **Al-Bari** — inventing and bringing into existence what He has measured. **Al-Musawwir** — giving it the form He chooses (*In whatever form He willed, He put you together*, 82:8). **None but Allah can measure, originate and create.**

> *Allah has ninety-nine Names, one hundred less one; whoever preserves them will enter Paradise. Allah is Witr (One) and He loves the Witr.*

And the surah shuts the circle it opened with:

> *All that is in the heavens and the earth glorify Him. And He is the Almighty, the All-Wise.*

Al-'Aziz — **His greatness is never humbled.** Al-Hakim — All-Wise in His legislation and His decrees.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Al-Hashr is a surah about what you are leaning on.**

Bani An-Nadir leaned on stone walls, and *Allah reached them from a place whereof they expected it not* — then made them pull those walls down themselves, by hand. The hypocrites leaned on their own words, and Allah recorded, before the fact, that not one syllable would be honoured. The Ansar leaned on nothing at all, gave away the food their children were waiting for, and had a whole ayah revealed about a dark house and an empty stomach.

Three lines hold the surah together:

> *Then take admonition, O you with eyes.* (59:2)
> *In order that it may not become a fortune used by the rich among you.* (59:7)
> *Let every person look to what he has sent forth for tomorrow.* (59:18)

The first is about **reading history correctly**. The second is about **wealth being engineered to move downward**, not pool at the top. The third is about **auditing yourself before the audit**.

And the surah's final mercy is that it does not leave you with a warning. It leaves you with **Names** — Al-Quddus, As-Salam, Al-Mu'min, the One who promised never to wrong you — and with a mountain that would have shattered under the weight of the Book you are holding in your hands.

**Do not forget Him, lest you be made to forget yourself.**
