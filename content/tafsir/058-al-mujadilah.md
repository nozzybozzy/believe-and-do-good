*Revealed in Madinah · 22 ayat · "The Woman Who Disputes" — named for a single woman whose complaint, spoken too quietly for A'ishah to hear, was answered from above the seven heavens*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Mujadilah opens with the most intimate scene in the Qur'an: an old woman, wronged by her husband, arguing her case with the Prophet ﷺ in a corner of a room — and Allah answering her by name of situation, in revelation, immediately. **"Indeed Allah has heard."** A'ishah's response to it is the surah's own thesis: *All praise be to Allah, Who hears all voices.*

From that opening the surah expands outward in a single continuous motion — from **what Allah hears** to **what Allah knows**. He hears the woman in the corner. He hears the whisper of three men who think they are alone, and He is their fourth. He hears the Jews of Madinah twisting a greeting into a curse under their breath, and He hears the hypocrites swearing oaths they know to be false. Nothing is inaudible, nothing is unrecorded: **Allah has kept account of it, while they have forgotten it.**

The practical rulings that follow — Zihar and its expiation, the manners of private counsel, the manners of sitting in an assembly, charity before consulting the Prophet ﷺ — all sit inside that frame. And the surah ends by drawing the only line that finally matters: the **party of Shaytan**, who forgot Allah's remembrance, against the **party of Allah**, who severed even blood ties rather than sever their loyalty to Him.

---

## 1. The Woman Whose Complaint Reached Heaven (58:1)

> *Indeed Allah has heard the statement of her that disputes with you concerning her husband, and complains to Allah. And Allah hears the argument between you both. Verily, Allah is All-Hearer, All-Seer.*

**A'ishah's testimony** (Imam Ahmad; also An-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Jarir; Al-Bukhari cites it without a chain in the Book of Tawhid):

> *All praise be to Allah, Who hears all voices. The woman who disputed came to the Prophet ﷺ and argued with him while I was in another part of the room, unable to hear what she said. Allah revealed this Ayah.*

In Ibn Abi Hatim's wording she says: **"Blessed is He, Whose hearing has encompassed all things. I heard what Khawlah bint Tha'labah said while some of it I could not hear."** Khawlah's complaint, as A'ishah reports it, is worth reading slowly:

> *O Allah's Messenger! He spent my wealth, exhausted my youth and my womb bore abundantly for him. When I became old, unable to bear children, he pronounced the Zihar on me! O Allah! I complain to you.*

Her husband was **Aws bin As-Samit**. She is named variously as Khawlah bint Tha'labah, Khawlah bint Malik bin Tha'labah, and Khuwaylah — Ibn Kathir notes all these are close to one another, *and Allah knows best*. Soon after she spoke, Jibril brought the verse down.

---

## 2. Khawlah's Own Account — and the Whole Ruling Delivered to One Woman (Abu Dawud, Ahmad)

This is the fullest narration Ibn Kathir gives, and the ruling of the surah is essentially dictated to her in real time.

She swears: **"By Allah! Allah sent down the beginning of Surah Al-Mujadilah in connection with me and Aws bin As-Samit."** He had grown old and difficult. In anger he said, *"You are like my mother's back to me,"* went out, sat with his people, then came back wanting her. She refused:

> *No, by the One in Whose Hand is the soul of Khuwaylah! You will not have your way with me after you said what you said, until Allah and His Messenger issue judgement about our case.*

He tried to force the matter and she pushed him away — *he was an old man*. She borrowed a garment from a neighbour and went to the Messenger ﷺ, complaining of her treatment. His first response is a fact about him worth noting: he did not take sides instantly. He said, **"O Khuwaylah! Your cousin is an old man, so have Taqwa of Allah regarding him."**

Then, before she left, revelation came. She describes the Prophet ﷺ feeling the hardship of it as he usually did, then becoming relieved, and saying: **"O Khuwaylah! Allah has revealed something about you and your spouse."** He recited the opening of the surah to her, through to *"And for disbelievers, there is a painful torment."*

Then the descending ladder of expiation, negotiated line by line against her poverty:

| The Prophet ﷺ says | She answers |
|---|---|
| *Command him to free a slave.* | He does not have any to free. |
| *Let him fast two consecutive months.* | By Allah! He is an old man and cannot fast. |
| *Let him feed sixty poor people a Wasq of dates.* | By Allah, he does not have any of that. |
| *We will help him with a basket of dates.* | And I will help him with another. |

And his closing words to her: **"You have done a righteously good thing. So go and give away the dates on his behalf and take care of your cousin."** Ibn Kathir: *This is what is correct about the reason behind revealing this Surah.*

---

## 3. What Zihar Is, and Why Allah Refused to Let It Be a Divorce (58:2)

**Zihar** derives from *az-zahr*, **the back**. In Jahiliyyah, a man who wished to declare it would say to his wife: *"To me, you are like the back of my mother."* Ibn Kathir states plainly what it was: **one of the ways they issued divorce in that time** — a woman rendered permanently untouchable and permanently unfree, by a single sentence.

Allah's ruling overturns it in two strokes:

**First, the words are simply false.** *They cannot be their mothers. None can be their mothers except those who gave them birth.* Saying it does not make it so. *And verily, they utter an ill word and a lie* — meaning, in Ibn Kathir's gloss, **false and sinful speech**.

**Second, the marriage survives.** *Allah allowed this Ummah to pay expiation for this statement and did not render it as a divorce, contrary to the case during the time of Jahiliyyah.*

And then the door is left open: *And verily, Allah is Oft-Pardoning, Oft-Forgiving* — which Ibn Kathir reads as covering **what you used to do in Jahiliyyah, and what accidentally slips out of your mouth, unintentionally.**

---

## 4. "And wish to free themselves from what they uttered" — the Scholars' Disagreement (58:3)

The condition that triggers expiation is stated in a phrase the jurists read differently. Ibn Kathir lays out the positions without adjudicating:

- **Ash-Shafi'i:** to keep her for a while after the Zihar without divorcing her, though he is able to do so.
- **Ahmad bin Hanbal:** to return to sexual relations with her, or merely to intend to — but only after paying the expiation.
- **Malik** (as quoted): the intention to have relations, or to keep her, or actually having intercourse.
- **Sa'id bin Jubayr:** if they want to return to what was forbidden between them.
- **Al-Hasan Al-Basri:** to utilise her sexual organ — and he saw no harm in anything short of that before the expiation is paid.

**"Before they touch each other"** — Ibn Abbas (via Ali bin Abi Talhah): *the touching refers here to sexual intercourse.* Ata', Az-Zuhri, Qatadah and Muqatil bin Hayyan said similarly. **Az-Zuhri added the strictest reading: he is not to kiss or touch her until he pays the expiation.**

**The man who could not wait** (the Sunan compilers, from Ikrimah ← Ibn Abbas). A man came saying he had pronounced Zihar and then had intercourse before paying. The Prophet ﷺ asked, *"May Allah grant you His mercy — what made you do that?"* He answered: **"I saw the adornment she was wearing shining in the moon's light."** The Prophet ﷺ said: *Then do not touch her until you do what Allah has ordered you to do.* At-Tirmidhi graded it Hasan Gharib Sahih.

Two further technical notes from Ibn Kathir:

- The verse mentions **any slave** — unlike the expiation for unintentional killing, it is **not restricted to a believing servant**.
- The strict order of the three penalties (free · fast · feed) is confirmed by the Hadiths on the man who had intercourse with his wife during a day of Ramadan, in the Two Sahihs.

*That is an admonition to you* — **a warning to threaten you in this case**. *That is in order that you may have perfect faith* — **We legislated this punishment so that you acquire this trait.** *These are the limits set by Allah* — the things He has forbidden, so do not transgress them.

---

## 5. Allah Is the Fourth of Any Three (58:5–7)

Those who oppose Allah and His Messenger *will be disgraced as those before them were disgraced* — **humiliated, cursed and disgraced, just as happened to their like before them.** And the clear Ayat are such that **none contradicts or opposes them except a disbeliever, rebellious, sinner.**

Then the great verse of divine encompassment:

> *There is no Najwa of three but He is their fourth, nor of five but He is their sixth, nor of less than that or more but He is with them wheresoever they may be.*

Ibn Kathir: He is **watching them, perfectly hearing their speech, whether uttered in public or secret** — and His angels record all they say, *even though He has better knowledge of it and hears them perfectly.* He cross-references two verses:

> *Know they not that Allah knows their secret ideas, and their Najwa* (9:78)
> *Or do they think that We hear not their secrets and their private Najwa? And Our messengers are by them to record* (43:80)

**The theological point he is careful to fix:** several scholars record a **consensus that "with them" here refers to Allah's knowledge** — and Ibn Kathir affirms it is true, *especially if we add to it the certainty that His hearing encompasses all things, as well as His sight.*

And Imam Ahmad's elegant observation on the structure of the verse: **"Allah began the Ayah by mentioning His knowledge and ended it by mentioning His knowledge."**

On *Allah has kept account of it, while they have forgotten it* — Allah recorded and preserved all these actions **even though they themselves have forgotten what they did**. Nothing escapes His knowledge; no matter is hidden from Him or escapes His complete observation.

---

## 6. The Greeting That Was Not a Greeting (58:8)

> *And when they come to you, they greet you with a greeting wherewith Allah greets you not.*

Mujahid (via Ibn Abi Najih) identifies those forbidden secret counsels and returning to them: **the Jews** — meaning here the specific parties in Madinah under treaty. **Muqatil bin Hayyan** supplies the context: there was a peace treaty; when a Companion passed a gathering of theirs they would whisper among themselves, so that the believer feared they were plotting to kill or harm him and would change his route. The Prophet ﷺ told them to stop; they continued; the verse came down.

Ibn Kathir separates the two sins in the phrase precisely:
- **Al-Ithm** — *sin*, which involves themselves.
- **Al-'Udwan** — *wrongdoing*, which affects others.

Plus **disobedience to the Messenger, with persistence, and recommending each other to follow their way.**

**A'ishah's report** (Ibn Abi Hatim): some came and greeted him with *As-Sam 'alayka* — a near-homophone meaning *death be upon you*. She retorted, *"wa 'alaykum as-Sam."* The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *O A'ishah, Allah does not like rudeness and foul speech.*

She protested that she had heard what they said. He answered: **"Didn't you hear me answering them back by saying, 'Wa 'alaykum'?"** In the Sahih version she says, *"And be upon you the death, disgrace and curse,"* and he tells her: **Allah accepts our supplication against them, but not theirs against us.**

**Anas bin Malik's report** (Ibn Jarir): a man passed, greeted, and was greeted back. The Prophet ﷺ asked his Companions, *"Do you know what he just said?"* They said he gave salam. He said: **"Rather he said, Sam 'alaykum"** — and had him brought back and confirmed it from his own mouth. The permanent ruling follows: *When the people of the Book greet you, say, "Wa 'alaykum."*

**Their reasoning, and its refutation.** *Why should Allah punish us not for what we say?* — that is, *had he been a Prophet, Allah would have punished us for what we said; Allah knows what we conceal, so if Muhammad were a Prophet the punishment would have come sooner, in this life.* Allah's reply is one clause: ***Hell will be sufficient for them.*** Abdullah bin 'Amr narrates the same occasion (Ahmad; chain Hasan, not collected by Al-Bukhari or Muslim).

---

## 7. The Manners of Private Counsel (58:9–10)

The believers are then taught the inverse of what they have just watched:

> *When you hold secret counsel, do it not for sin and wrongdoing, and disobedience to the Messenger, but do it for Al-Birr and Taqwa.*

Ibn Kathir: **do not hold evil secret counsels like the ignorant disbelieving People of the Scriptures and their allies among the hypocrites, who imitate their ways.** And *have Taqwa of Allah unto Whom you shall be gathered* — He will then inform you of all your deeds and statements; **He has counted and recorded them and will justly hold you accountable.**

Then the diagnosis of *why* whispering wounds:

> *Secret counsels are only from Shaytan, in order that he may cause grief to the believers. But he cannot harm them in the least, except as Allah permits.*

Those who hold such counsels do so **because of the lures of the devil**, whose aim is simply to *bother* the believers — though his plots cannot harm them unless Allah wills. Ibn Kathir's counsel to the one being whispered about is exactly the verse's ending: **seek refuge in Allah and put your trust in Him, for none of it will harm you, Allah willing.**

**And the Sunnah closes the door practically**, so no Muslim is ever the third man:

> *If you were three, then two of you should not hold a secret counsel in the presence of the third person, because that would cause him to be worried.* (Ibn Mas'ud — in the Two Sahihs)

Ibn Umar's version adds the only exception: **"except with his permission."** (Muslim)

---

## 8. Make Room, and Room Will Be Made for You (58:11)

> *When you are told to make room in the assemblies, make room. Allah will give you room. And when you are told to rise up, then rise up.*

The governing principle Ibn Kathir extracts is the one that runs through the whole Shari'ah: **the reward or recompense depends on the type of action.** Two hadiths illustrate it:

- *Whoever builds a Masjid for Allah, Allah builds for him a house in Paradise.*
- *He who relieves a difficulty for a person living in straitened circumstances, Allah will relieve his difficulties in this life and the Hereafter. Surely, **Allah helps the servant as long as the servant helps his brother.***

**Qatadah's occasion:** this was revealed about gatherings *in places where Allah is being remembered.* When someone came to join the Messenger's ﷺ assembly, people would hesitate to make space **so as not to lose their own places** — and Allah commanded them to spread out.

The prohibition that accompanies it (Ahmad and Ash-Shafi'i, from Ibn Umar; in the Two Sahihs): **"One of you should not remove someone from his place and sit in it — but instead, spread out and make room."** Abu Hurayrah's version adds the promise: *rather spread out and make room, and Allah will make room for you.*

A second reading is preserved from **Ibn Abbas and Al-Hasan Al-Basri**: the "assemblies" are **war assemblies**, and *rise up* means **"Get up to fight."** **Qatadah** generalises it: *"When you are called to any type of good, then respond."*

---

## 9. Allah Raises People by This Book (58:11b)

> *Allah will exalt in degrees those of you who believe, and those who have been granted knowledge.*

Ibn Kathir ties this directly back to the manners just taught, and the logic is beautiful: **do not think that making room for your brother, or rising when commanded, diminishes your right or honour. Rather, it increases your virtue and status with Allah** — *surely, he who humbles himself by and before the command of his Lord, Allah will elevate his status and make him known by his good behaviour.* And *Allah is Well-Acquainted with what you do* means **He is Aware of who deserves this reward and who is not worthy of it.**

**The story that proves it** (Ahmad, via Abu At-Tufayl 'Amir bin Wathilah). Nafi' bin Abdul-Harith met Umar bin Al-Khattab ؓ at Usfan. Umar asked whom he had left in charge of Makkah.

> *"I appointed Ibn Abza, one of our freed slaves, as my deputy."*
> **"You made a freed slave their governor in your absence?"**
> *"O Leader of the faithful! He has memorised Allah's Book, has knowledge of the regulations of inheritance, and is a proficient judge."*

Umar said: **"Surely, your Prophet ﷺ has said: *Verily, Allah elevates some people and degrades others, on account of this Book.*"** (Muslim)

## 10. The Command That Lasted an Hour (58:12–13)

> *When you consult the Messenger in private, spend something in charity before your private consultation. That will be better and purer for you. But if you find not, then verily, Allah is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful.*

The purpose, in Ibn Kathir's words: the charity **cleanses and purifies him and makes him worthy of having a private counsel with the Prophet ﷺ**. And the exemption is built in from the start — *if he is unable due to poverty* — because **He only commanded those who were financially able.**

**Ibn Abbas** (via Ali bin Abi Talhah) explains the underlying problem: *the Muslims kept asking questions until it became difficult on him. Allah wanted to lighten the burden from His Prophet.* When the charity was required, many were afraid to pay and stopped asking. Then came the release:

> *Are you afraid of spending in charity before your private consultation? If then you do it not, and Allah has forgiven you, then perform Salah and give Zakah and obey Allah and His Messenger.*

Ibn Kathir: **Allah abrogated the obligation.** Ikrimah and Al-Hasan Al-Basri say the same. Qatadah and Muqatil bin Hayyan (via Sa'id bin Abi 'Arubah) describe men who genuinely needed to raise a real matter with the Prophet ﷺ and could not — *and Allah sent down relief from this requirement afterwards.*

**And the one man who obeyed it.** Ali bin Abi Talib ؓ: **"No one except me implemented this Ayah, until it was abrogated"** — and he is reported to have said it stood in effect **for merely an hour**. Ma'mar reports from Qatadah that it was abrogated after being in force **for only one hour of a day**.

---

## 11. The Party of Shaytan (58:14–19)

The final movement turns to the hypocrites, whose defining trait here is **the oath they know to be false**.

They took as friends *a people upon whom is the wrath of Allah* — the Jews of Madinah, with whom they were **allied in secret**. And *they are neither of you nor of them*: **neither with the believers, nor with their allies.** Ibn Kathir places the verse beside its twin:

> *Swaying between this and that, belonging neither to these nor to those* (4:143)

**They swear to a lie while they know** — this is **Al-Ghamus**, the oath sworn in deliberate falsehood. *We seek refuge with Allah from their ways.* Ibn Kathir notes the sharp irony: when they swore to the Prophet ﷺ that they were believers, **the content of what they said about him was true in essence** — and they were still liars, because they knew they did not hold the creed they professed.

- ***They have made their oaths a screen*** — they concealed disbelief behind false oaths; many were unaware of their true stance and were deceived, and so **some people were hindered from the Path of Allah.** The humiliating torment is *recompense for belittling the significance of swearing by the Mighty Name of Allah, while lying and concealing betrayal.*
- ***Their children and their wealth will avail them nothing*** — **none of their possessions can avert the affliction when it is sent their way.**
- ***They will swear to Him as they swear to you.*** On the Day of Resurrection they will swear to Allah Himself that they were rightly guided, exactly as they used to swear to the believers. Ibn Kathir's explanation is chilling in its simplicity: **those who live following a certain path will most likely die upon it — and thus be resurrected upon it.** They will imagine their oaths will work on Allah as they worked on people. *Verily, they are liars!*
- ***The Shaytan has overpowered them, so he has made them forget the remembrance of Allah*** — he has taken over their hearts **to the point that he made them forget Allah**; this is what the devil does to those he controls.

**The hadith of the isolated three** (Abu Dawud, from Abu Ad-Darda'):

> *Any three in a village or desert among whom the Salah is not called for, will have the Shaytan control them. Therefore, adhere to the Jama'ah, for the wolf eats from the strayed sheep.*

As-Sa'ib glossed *Jama'ah* here as **praying in congregation**.

*They are the party of Shaytan* — **those controlled by the devil and, as a result, forgetful of the remembrance of Allah.**

## 12. The Party of Allah (58:20–22)

> *Allah has decreed: "Verily, I and My Messengers shall be the victorious."*

Ibn Kathir reads *kataba* at full strength: **He has decreed it, written it in the First Book, and decided it in a decree which can never be resisted, changed or prevented** — that final victory belongs to Him, His Book, His Messengers and the faithful believers, **in this life and the Hereafter.** He supports it with 11:49 (*the good end is for those who have Taqwa*) and 40:51–52 (*We will indeed make victorious Our Messengers... on the Day when the witnesses will stand forth*).

Those who oppose them, meanwhile, are **among the miserable, the cast out, banished from goodness — the humiliated ones in this life and the Hereafter.**

**Then the surah's hardest demand:**

> *You will not find any people who believe in Allah and the Last Day, making friendship with those who oppose Allah and His Messenger, even though they were their fathers or their sons or their brothers or their kindred.*

Ibn Kathir: **do not befriend the deniers, even if they are among the closest relatives** — citing 3:28 and the searching verse of 9:24 (*if your fathers, your sons, your brothers, your wives, your kindred, the wealth you have gained... are dearer to you than Allah and His Messenger, then wait*).

**And he grounds it in Badr, name by name** — because it was not theoretical for the first generation:

| The clause | The man it was said to concern |
|---|---|
| *their fathers* | **Abu Ubaydah 'Amir bin Abdullah bin Al-Jarrah**, who killed his disbelieving father at Badr |
| *their sons* | **Abu Bakr As-Siddiq**, who intended to kill his son Abdur-Rahman |
| *their brothers* | **Mus'ab bin 'Umayr**, who killed his brother Ubayd bin Umayr |
| *their kindred* | **Umar**, who killed a relative — and also Hamzah, Ali and Ubaydah bin Al-Harith, who killed Utbah, Shaybah and Al-Walid bin Utbah that day |

*Allah knows best*, Ibn Kathir adds. And the sequel: when Umar ؓ placed the Khilafah in the hands of six men after him, he said, **"If Abu Ubaydah were alive, I would have appointed him the Khalifah."**

He places beside it the consultation over the **captives of Badr**. Abu Bakr argued for ransom — the captives were cousins and kindred, the money would strengthen the Muslims, and they might embrace Islam later by Allah's help. Umar argued the opposite: *"Let me kill so-and-so, my relative, and let Ali kill Aqil, and so-and-so kill so-and-so. Let us make it known to Allah that we have no mercy in our hearts for the idolators."*

**And the reward for that severance:**

- ***He has written faith in their hearts*** — As-Suddi: **"He has placed faith in their hearts."** Ibn Kathir: He decreed faith, meaning **happiness**, in their hearts, and made faith dear to them.
- ***And strengthened them with Ruh from Himself*** — Ibn Abbas: **"He gave them strengths."**
- ***Allah is well pleased with them, and they are well pleased with Him*** — and here Ibn Kathir pauses on what he calls **a beautiful secret**: *when the believers became enraged against their relatives and kindred in Allah's cause, He compensated them by being pleased with them and making them pleased with Him — from what He has granted them of eternal delight, ultimate victory and encompassing favour.*
- ***They are the party of Allah*** — **His servants who are worthy of earning His honour.**

The surah's last word is a deliberate mirror of its second-to-last section. **The party of Shaytan: the losers. The party of Allah: the successful.** Two whispers, two allegiances, two ends.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

An old woman argued in a corner of a room, so quietly that the Prophet's ﷺ own wife, sitting a few feet away, could not make out her words. **Allah heard her, and legislated for her, and the surah is named after her.**

Everything that follows is that same fact widened out. He hears the whisper of three men who think themselves unobserved — *He is their fourth*. He hears the syllable altered in a greeting to turn it into a curse. He hears the oath sworn by a man who knows he is lying, and He records it while the man forgets it: ***Allah has kept account of it, while they have forgotten it.***

Which means the surah's ethics are not really rules about assemblies and whispering and expiation. They are the behaviour of people who have understood that **there is no such thing as an unheard word** — so make room rather than guard your seat, whisper for *Birr* and *Taqwa* rather than to wound, and never say *"you are like my mother's back to me"* to a woman who bore your children, because the sentence does not vanish when it leaves your mouth.

And the two parties are sorted by exactly one thing: **remembrance.** One party Shaytan overpowered until *he made them forget the remembrance of Allah*. The other He compensated for everything they gave up — *Allah is well pleased with them, and they are well pleased with Him.*

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Al-Mujadilah. All praise and thanks are due to Allah.*
