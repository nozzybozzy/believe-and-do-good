*12 ayat · "The Divorce" — named for its subject, which occupies the first seven verses*

*(This edition's header reads "Revealed in Makkah," but the surah's content — legislation delivered to an established community, with occasions of revelation set in Al-Madinah — places it among the Madinan surahs, as the majority hold.)*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

At-Talaq is the Qur'an's most concentrated body of divorce law, and it is also — unexpectedly — one of its most generous surahs of consolation. This is not a coincidence, and it is the key to reading it.

Three times inside the legal material, Allah interrupts the rulings to make a promise, and each promise is attached to the same condition:

> *Whoever has **Taqwa** of Allah, He will make a way out for him, and provide for him from where he never could imagine.* (65:2–3)
> *Whoever has **Taqwa** of Allah, He will make his matter easy for him.* (65:4)
> *Whoever has **Taqwa** of Allah, He will expiate his sins and increase his reward.* (65:5)

The placement is deliberate. These promises are not planted in a surah about prayer or patience under persecution — they are planted in the middle of the ugliest, most bitter, most legally contested moment in a person's life, when a marriage is ending and two people are arguing over housing, money, and a child's milk. That is precisely where Allah says: *fear Me here, and I will open a door you cannot see.*

The surah then closes by widening the lens completely — seven heavens, seven earths, and command descending between them — so that the man arguing about a room in his house understands who legislated the room.

---

## 1. Divorce at the Right Time (65:1)

> *O Prophet! When you divorce women, divorce them at their `Iddah, and count their `Iddah.*

The Prophet ﷺ is addressed first as an honour, though the ruling is for the whole Ummah.

**The incident behind it** (Al-Bukhari): `Abdullah bin `Umar divorced his wife while she was menstruating. `Umar mentioned it to the Messenger ﷺ, who **became angry** and said:

> *Order him to take her back and keep her until she is clean from her menses, then wait until she gets her next period and becomes clean again. Then, if he wishes to divorce her, he may do so before he has intercourse with her. **This is the `Iddah which Allah has fixed.***

Ibn Mas`ud's gloss on *"at their `Iddah"* is three words: **"Purity without intercourse."** Ibn `Abbas expands it: he does not divorce her during menses, nor during a purity in which he has had intercourse — he waits for the next cycle to end, and then divorces her once.

Ibn Kathir draws out the resulting classification:

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Sunnah divorce** | One pronouncement, while she is pure, with no intercourse since the menses ended — or while her pregnancy is evident |
| **Innovated divorce** | During menses; or after intercourse in that purity, when he cannot know whether she has conceived |
| **Neither** | Divorcing a wife too young to menstruate, one past the age of it, or one whose marriage was never consummated |

**Why the timing matters:** the wisdom is that he should not divorce her when he cannot see her state. `Ikrimah's point is that the ruling forces clarity — either the pregnancy is visible, or the fresh cycle proves there is none. And *"count their `Iddah"* means know its beginning and end, so the term is not stretched out and she is not left unable to remarry.

## 2. She Stays in the House (65:1)

> *And turn them not out of their homes, nor shall they leave.*

For the duration of the `Iddah, **housing is her right and his obligation**. He may not force her out; she may not leave — *because she is still tied to the marriage contract.*

The one exception is *Fahishah Mubayyinah* — manifest immorality. The Salaf give it two readings, both preserved by Ibn Kathir: **adultery** (Ibn Mas`ud, Ibn `Abbas, Sa`id bin Al-Musayyib, Mujahid, `Ikrimah and many others), or **open defiance of the husband and abuse of his family in word and deed** (Ubayy bin Ka`b, Ibn `Abbas, `Ikrimah).

*These are the set limits of Allah — and whoever transgresses them has wronged himself.*

### The reason she stays — the most beautiful line in the surah

> *You know not, it may be that Allah will afterward bring some new thing to pass.*

Ibn Kathir's reading: **she remains in his house so that he might regret it.** Proximity is the mechanism of mercy. Living under the same roof, the anger cools, the heart softens, and Allah may put it in him to want the marriage back — *and this way, returning to his wife will be easier for him.*

Fatimah bint Qays explained "some new thing" in a single phrase: **"Taking her back."** So too Ash-Sha`bi, `Ata', Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak, Muqatil and Ath-Thawri.

**The limit of this:** the *irrevocably* divorced woman (the third and final pronouncement) has no right to housing or maintenance. The evidence is Fatimah bint Qays's own case — her husband Abu `Amr bin Hafs sent her the third divorce from Yemen, and the Prophet ﷺ told her: *There is no obligation on him to spend on you*, and in Muslim's wording, *nor housing*. He explained the principle plainly:

> *Spending and housing are required from the husband **who can return to his wife**. If he no longer has that right, she has no right to spending and housing.*

He then sent her to complete her `Iddah at the house of Ibn Umm Maktum — *for he is a blind man; he cannot see you if you take off your garments.*

## 3. Part Well, and With Witnesses (65:2)

> *Then when they are about to attain their term, either take them back in a good manner or part with them in a good manner.*

At the end of the `Iddah the decision must be made, and Ibn Kathir insists that **both branches are governed by *ma`ruf***: if he keeps her, he keeps her with good companionship; if he releases her, he releases her **without abusing, cursing, or admonishing her** — on good terms, with kindness and good manners.

> *And take as witness two just persons from among you. And establish the testimony for Allah.*

`Imran bin Husayn was asked about a man who divorced, then returned to his wife, with no witnesses to either. His answer: *"His divorce and his taking her back were in contradiction to the Sunnah. Bring witnesses for both, and do not repeat your conduct."* `Ata' went further still: marriage, divorce and reconciliation should all have two just witnesses, unless there is a valid excuse.

## 4. The Promise Inside the Law (65:2–3)

> *And whosoever has Taqwa of Allah, He will make a way for him to get out. And He will provide him from where he never could imagine.*

Ibn Mas`ud called this **the greatest verse of relief in the Qur'an** (as he called 16:90 the most comprehensive). The commentary is a chorus:

- **`Ikrimah:** *"Whoever divorces as Allah commanded him, Allah will make a way out for him."* — the promise is first of all a promise about *this* subject matter.
- **Ibn Mas`ud and Masruq:** it concerns the man who knows that if Allah wills He gives and if He wills He withholds; provision then comes *from where he did not anticipate*.
- **Qatadah:** a way out **from every doubt, and from the horrors of death itself.**

> *And whosoever puts his trust in Allah, then He will suffice him.*

Here Ibn Kathir places the great hadith of Ibn `Abbas, taught to him as a boy riding behind the Prophet ﷺ:

> *O boy! I will teach you some words. **Be mindful of Allah and He will protect you. Be mindful of Allah and you will find Him before you.** If you ask, ask Allah; if you seek help, seek it from Allah. Know that if the whole Ummah gathered to benefit you, they could not benefit you except with what Allah has decreed for you; and if they gathered to harm you, they could not harm you except with what Allah has decreed against you. **The pens have been lifted and the pages are dry.***

> *Verily, Allah will accomplish His purpose. Indeed Allah has set a measure for all things.*

## 5. The `Iddah of Every Kind of Woman (65:4)

Al-Baqarah 2:228 had given the rule for menstruating women — three cycles. This verse fills the gaps, and it was revealed **because someone asked**. Ubayy bin Ka`b said: *"O Messenger of Allah, some women were not mentioned in the Qur'an — the young, the old, and the pregnant."*

| Case | `Iddah |
|---|---|
| **Past menopause** | Three months |
| **Too young to menstruate** | Three months |
| **Pregnant** (divorce **or** widowhood) | Until she gives birth |

On *"if you have doubt"*: Mujahid, Az-Zuhri and Ibn Zayd read it as doubt about whether blood seen is menstrual. Sa`id bin Jubayr — and Ibn Jarir, and Ibn Kathir with him — read it as **doubt about the ruling itself**: *if you do not know what to do, know that it is three months.*

**The case that settled the pregnant widow:** Subay`ah Al-Aslamiyyah's husband Sa`d bin Khawlah, a veteran of Badr, died during the Farewell Pilgrimage while she was pregnant. She gave birth shortly after, and then adorned herself for proposals. Abu As-Sanabil rebuked her: *"By Allah, you cannot marry until four months and ten days have passed."* She went to the Messenger ﷺ that evening, and **he ruled that she was free to marry from the moment she delivered.** Ibn `Abbas had held the longer of the two terms, until Umm Salamah reported this — and Abu Hurayrah had already sided with Abu Salamah on the verse.

## 6. Housing, Harm, and the Child's Milk (65:6–7)

> *Lodge them where you dwell, according to what you have, and do not harm them so as to suppress them.*

- **"According to what you have"** — Ibn `Abbas, Mujahid and others: *your ability*. Qatadah: **"If you can only afford a corner of your house, then do so."**
- **"Do not harm them"** — Muqatil bin Hayyan: do not make her life difficult in order to force her to buy her way out, or to drive her from the house. Ath-Thawri, from Abu Ad-Duha, names another abuse: taking her back just as the `Iddah is about to expire, purely to trap her.
- **The pregnant, irrevocably divorced woman must be maintained until she delivers** — and the revocably divorced woman is maintained regardless, pregnant or not.
- **Suckling is paid work.** If she nurses the child after birth, she is owed a wage by agreement with the father. *"Let each of you deal with the other in a mannerly way"* — Ibn Kathir cites 2:233: *no mother shall be harmed on account of her child, nor father on account of his child.*
- **If they deadlock** — she demands too much, he offers too little — he may hire another woman. But if the mother will accept what the other woman would have taken, **she has the greater right to nurse her own child.**

> *Let the rich man spend according to his means, and the man whose resources are restricted spend from what Allah has given him. Allah puts no burden on any person beyond what He has given him.*

## 7. Allah Will Grant, After Hardship, Ease (65:7)

Ibn Kathir calls this **a sure promise, and Allah does not break His promises** — the twin of *Verily, with hardship comes ease. Verily, with hardship comes ease* (94:5–6).

He then tells a story, from Abu Hurayrah, about a poor man of an earlier generation who came home from a journey hungry and exhausted and asked his wife for food. She had none.

> *"Yes — receive the good news of Allah's provision,"* she said. He pressed her. *"Wait a little longer."* She was **waiting for Allah's mercy**. When he grew desperate, she said, *"I will; I am about to open the oven — do not be hasty."*
>
> Then she said to herself: *I should look in my oven.* She found it **full of roasted lamb**, and her mortar full of grain, **grinding itself**. She shook out the mortar and took out the meat.

Abu Hurayrah swore by Allah that the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *Had she taken out what was in her mortar and not emptied it by shaking it, it would have kept grinding until the Day of Resurrection.*

The lesson sits exactly where the surah needs it: the wife's *Taqwa* and her refusal to despair were what opened the door — and her only mistake was scraping out the last of what she had been given.

## 8. The Towns That Refused (65:8–11)

The tone turns. *Many a town revolted against the command of its Lord and His Messengers* — they rebelled, rejected, and arrogantly refused — *so We called it to a severe account.* They **tasted the evil result of their own affair**, and regretted it when regret was useless; and a severe torment waits for them in the Hereafter, *in addition* to what came down on them in this life.

Then the address swings back to the listener:

> *So have Taqwa of Allah, O men of understanding, those who believe! Allah has indeed sent down to you a Reminder — a Messenger, who recites to you the Ayat of Allah containing clear explanations.*

Ibn Kathir's reading, with Ibn Jarir: the **Reminder is the Qur'an**, and the **Messenger is the one who makes it clear**. And the purpose of the whole apparatus is a single movement: *that He may take out those who believe and do righteous deeds **from the darkness to the light*** — from the darkness of disbelief and ignorance into the light of faith and knowledge.

Revelation is called *light* because of the guidance it brings, and *Ruh* (42:52) because it brings the heart to life.

---

## 9. Seven Heavens, Seven Earths (65:12)

> *It is Allah Who has created seven heavens, and of the earth the like thereof. His command descends between them, that you may know that Allah has power over all things, and that Allah surrounds all things with knowledge.*

Ibn Kathir says the verse is here for a reason: the surah's legislation is honoured and obeyed **because of who legislated it.**

*"And of the earth the like thereof"* means **seven earths** — literally. He anchors this with the hadith in the Two Sahihs: *Whoever usurps a hand span of land unjustly, his neck will be encircled with it down to the seven earths* — and Al-Bukhari's wording, *he will sink down to the seven earths*. Those who reinterpret this as the seven continents, he says bluntly, **have given an implausible explanation that contradicts the wording of the Qur'an and the Hadith, without proof.**

The surah that began with a man's private decision about his wife ends with command descending through seven heavens and seven earths. The scale is the point.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Divorce is a legislated act, not an outburst.** Its timing, its counting, its witnesses, and its manner are all fixed — and a divorce done in anger, at the wrong moment, is one the Messenger ﷺ became angry about.

**Endings are governed by *ihsan*.** *Keep her in a good manner, or release her in a good manner.* No cursing, no admonishing, no squeezing her out of the house, no using the law as a weapon.

**She stays, because Allah leaves the door open.** *You know not, it may be that Allah will afterward bring some new thing to pass.* The `Iddah is not a countdown; it is a space made deliberately for reconciliation.

**Taqwa is the escape route.** Three times, in the middle of legal fine print: fear Allah *here*, and He will make a way out, make it easy, provide from where you never calculated, and forgive you besides. Or, as `Ikrimah put it — divorce the way Allah told you to, and Allah will make a way out for you.

**And the story of the oven:** the woman waited, refused to despair of Allah's provision, and it came. She only lost what she shook out of the mortar herself.
