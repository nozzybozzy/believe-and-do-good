*Revealed in Madinah · 75 ayat · "The Spoils of War"*

---

## 1. What This Surah Is

Ibn Kathir establishes the frame in a single line from Sa'id bin Jubayr: he said to Ibn Abbas, *"Surah Al-Anfal!"* — and Ibn Abbas answered, **"It was revealed concerning Badr."**

That is the key to the whole surah. Al-Anfal is the Qur'an's commentary on a single day — the 17th of Ramadan, year 2 of the Hijrah — written while the dust was still settling. It is a battle report, a law code, a theological argument, and a rebuke, all addressed to the same three hundred men.

And notice where it begins: not with the victory, but with **the argument the victors had afterwards over the loot**. Allah's first word on Badr is a correction of the men who won it.

---

## 2. The Quarrel Over the Spoils (8:1)

**"They ask you about Al-Anfal. Say: Al-Anfal are for Allah and the Messenger."**

Ibn Kathir gives several meanings for *Anfal* — the spoils generally (Ibn Abbas, via Al-Bukhari); the extra portion a commander awards to a particular squad; the *khumus* (the fifth); the *Fay'* taken without fighting. But the reason for revelation is what carries the weight, and there are two accounts:

**Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas** asked for a sword he had earned. The Prophet ﷺ told him: *"This sword is neither yours nor mine; put it down."* Sa'd put it down and walked away thinking — with entire honesty — *he might give it to a man who did not fight as hard as I did*. Then he was called back: *"You asked me for the sword, but it was not mine to give. Now it has been given to me, and I give it to you."*

**Ubadah bin As-Samit** gives the fuller picture. After the rout, the army had split into three:

- those who **pursued** the enemy and inflicted the casualties;
- those who **collected** the spoils on the field;
- those who **stood guard around the Prophet ﷺ** in case of a counter-attack.

Each group claimed the spoils, and each had a real argument. The collectors: *we gathered it*. The pursuers: *we drove them off, you'd have nothing without us*. The bodyguard: *we protected the Messenger, we had no chance to gather anything*.

Allah's answer took the property out of all their hands and gave it to Allah and His Messenger — who then **divided it equally among them.**

Then the command: **"So have Taqwa of Allah and settle all matters of difference among you."** As-Suddi's gloss is blunt: *do not curse each other.* Ibn Kathir's own: the guidance and knowledge Allah gave you is worth more than the thing you are arguing about.

*A day of miraculous victory, and the first correction is about how you spoke to each other afterwards.*

---

## 3. Who the Believers Actually Are (8:2–4)

Immediately after the quarrel, Allah defines the believers — as if to say, *this is the standard you were falling short of*:

| The description | Ibn Kathir's commentary |
|---|---|
| **When Allah is mentioned, their hearts feel fear** | Mujahid: their hearts become afraid. As-Suddi: a man is on the verge of an injustice; someone says *have Taqwa of Allah*, and his heart trembles and he stops |
| **When His Ayat are recited, their faith increases** | Al-Bukhari and the majority took this ayah as proof that **faith increases and decreases**, and varies in strength from heart to heart. Ash-Shafi'i, Ahmad, and Abu Ubayd called it the consensus of the Ummah |
| **They put their trust in their Lord** | Sa'id bin Jubayr: *"Tawakkul of Allah is the essence of faith."* They hope in none but Him, seek refuge with none but Him, and know that what He wills happens and what He does not will never happens |
| **They perform Salah and spend from what We provided** | Qatadah: establishing prayer means guarding its times, its ablution, its bowing and prostration. Spending covers Zakah and everything beyond it |

Then the verdict: **"It is they who are the believers in truth."** For them: grades of dignity, forgiveness, and a generous provision.

Ibn Kathir attaches the hadith of the *Illiyyin* — the highest dwellers of Paradise are seen by those below them like a distant star on the horizon. The Companions assumed these must be the grades of prophets. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Rather, by He in Whose Hand is my soul, they are men who had faith in Allah and believed in the Messengers."* In another narration he added: **Abu Bakr and Umar are among them.**

## 4. Dragged Toward the Battle They Did Not Want (8:5–8)

**"As your Lord caused you to go out from your home with the truth; and verily, a party among the believers disliked it."**

The logic is a parallel, and Ibn Kathir draws it out explicitly:

> *You quarrelled about the spoils, so Allah took them from you and divided them justly — to your benefit. In the same way, you disliked meeting the armed army, so Allah made the battle happen anyway — to your benefit.*

They had marched out for a **caravan**, not an army. The Prophet ﷺ had said: *"This is the caravan of Quraysh carrying their property, so march forth to intercept it; Allah might make it as war spoils for you."* Some did not even bother to mobilise, assuming there would be no fighting.

Ibn Kathir quotes 2:216 twice in this passage: *fighting is ordained for you though you dislike it, and it may be that you dislike a thing which is good for you.*

Then **"as if they were being driven to death, while they were looking"** — the disputants had a grievance and it was not unreasonable: *you told us we were going for a caravan. You did not tell us there would be a battle, or that we should prepare.*

### The Council at Dhafiran

Abu Sufyan, sensing danger, had sent Damdam bin Amr racing to Makkah to raise the Quraysh. Word reached the Prophet ﷺ in the valley of Dhafiran that an army — not a caravan — was coming. He put the decision to the men.

Abu Bakr spoke well. Umar spoke well. Then **Al-Miqdad bin Amr** stood:

> *"March to what Allah has commanded you, for we are with you. By Allah, we will not say to you what the Children of Israel said to Musa — 'So go you and your Lord and fight, we are sitting right here.' Rather: go you and your Lord and fight, and we will be fighting alongside you both. By He Who sent you with the Truth, if you take us to Birk-ul-Ghimad, we will fight with you until you reach it."*

The Prophet ﷺ said good words to him and prayed for him. But then he asked again — *"Give me your opinion, O people!"* — and Ibn Kathir explains exactly why. Most of the men present were **Ansar**, and their pledge at Al-Aqabah had been to protect him *once he arrived in their land*. He feared they would consider themselves bound only to defend Madinah, not to march out to fight elsewhere.

**Sa'd bin Mu'adh** understood the question. *"Is it us you mean, O Messenger of Allah?"* Yes.

> *"We have faith in you, testified that what you brought is the truth, and gave you our pledges of obedience. So march. By He Who sent you in Truth, if you decided to cross this sea, we would follow you into it, and not one of us would stay behind. We do not dislike meeting our enemy tomorrow. We are patient in war, fierce in battle. May Allah show you what pleases your eyes in us."*

The Prophet ﷺ was gladdened and said: *"March with the blessing of Allah and receive the good news. For Allah has promised me one of the two camps. By Allah, it is as if I am now looking at the places where these people will fall."*

**"Allah willed to justify the truth by His Words and to cut off the roots of the disbelievers"** — Ibn Kathir: you wanted the easy prize; He wanted the decisive one. *You are surrounded by His wise planning, although people only like what appears favourable to them.*

## 5. The Help That Came Down (8:9–14)

### The invocation

Al-Miqdad's words are recorded again here, in Ibn Mas'ud's mouth, and Ibn Mas'ud says: *"I was a witness to something Al-Miqdad did that I would rather have done than almost anything else."* Al-Miqdad came to the Prophet ﷺ while he was invoking Allah against the idolators and said *we will fight to your right, to your left, before you and behind you* — and Ibn Mas'ud saw the Prophet's ﷺ face beam with pleasure.

The Prophet's ﷺ own du'a at Badr, from Ibn Abbas:

> *"O Allah! I invoke You for Your covenant and promise. O Allah! If You decide so, You will not be worshipped."*

Abu Bakr took his hand and said, *"Enough."* The Prophet ﷺ came out reciting: **"Their multitude will be put to flight, and they will show their backs."** (54:45)

### The angels

**"I will help you with a thousand of the angels Murdifin"** — *Murdifin*, per Ibn Abbas, means following one behind the other in succession. Five hundred under Jibril on one flank, five hundred under Mika'il on the other.

Umar's narration, from Muslim: a Muslim was chasing an idolator when he heard a whip crack above him and a rider call, **"Forward, O Hayzum!"** — and the idolator dropped, his nose split and his face torn as if lashed, the whole face turned green. He reported it and the Prophet ﷺ said: *"You have spoken the truth. That was from the reinforcements of the third heaven."*

Rifa'ah bin Rafi' narrated that **Jibril asked the Prophet ﷺ how the men of Badr were regarded among the Muslims.** *"Among the best Muslims."* Jibril said: *"And so it is with the angels who were at Badr."*

Ibn Kathir also notes the standing reprieve granted to that generation — from the Hatib incident: *"How do you know that Allah has not looked upon the people of Badr and said: 'Do whatever you want, for I have forgiven you'?"*

But then the corrective, which is the real point of the passage: **"Allah made it only as glad tidings, and that your hearts be at rest therewith. And there is no victory except from Allah."** He did not need the angels. They were for *you*.

### Why fighting is legislated at all

Ibn Kathir pauses here for one of his most striking arguments. Earlier nations that denied their prophets were destroyed **by Allah directly** — Nuh's people by the flood, 'Ad by the wind, Thamud by the scream, Lut's people by the earthquake, Shu'ayb's by the Day of the Shadow, Fir'awn by drowning. After Musa, the Tawrah legislated *fighting*, and that remained in the successive Laws.

Why? Because *"it is more humiliating for the disbeliever and more comforting to the hearts of the faithful that the believers kill the disbelievers by their own hands"* — citing 9:14, *fight them so that Allah will punish them by your hands, and heal the breasts of a believing people.*

His example is pointed: **Abu Jahl** was killed in battle, which was more humiliating for him than dying in bed. **Abu Lahab** died of a foul disease none of his relatives could approach; they washed him by throwing water from a distance and piled stones over his corpse.

### The slumber, and the rain

**"When He covered you with a slumber as a security from Him."**

- **Abu Talhah** (of Uhud, the same favour): *"The sword fell from my hand several times, and I kept picking it up. I saw the Companions' heads nodding in the rear guard."*
- **Ali:** *"Only Al-Miqdad had a horse at Badr. At some point I found we had all fallen asleep — except the Messenger of Allah. He was praying under a tree and weeping until dawn."*
- **Ibn Mas'ud:** *"Slumber in battle is security from Allah; slumber in prayer is from Shaytan."*
- **Qatadah:** slumber affects the head; sleep affects the heart.

Ibn Kathir generalises: this is what Allah does for believers whenever they are in distress.

Then the rain. Ibn Abbas: the Muslims camped on soft sand, with the Quraysh holding the water. Shaytan whispered — *you claim to be Allah's supporters, and they hold the wells while you pray needing purity*. Allah sent heavy rain: the Muslims drank and purified themselves, **the sand firmed under their feet**, and the ground where Quraysh camped turned to mud.

The verse names four gifts, and Ibn Kathir pairs them as **outer and inner**:

| **"to clean you thereby"** | outer purity — water for washing |
| **"to remove the Rijz of Shaytan"** | inner purity — his whispers and evil thoughts |
| **"to strengthen your hearts"** | inner courage — patience, resolve to fight |
| **"and make your feet firm"** | outer courage — literal footing |

### The command to the angels

**"Verily, I am with you, so keep firm those who have believed. I will cast terror into the hearts of those who have disbelieved."**

Ibn Kathir calls this *a hidden favour Allah has now made known* — the believers did not see this happening. And note the division of labour: the angels **steady the believers**; Allah Himself **casts the terror**.

Ar-Rabi' bin Anas: *"After Badr, people could tell which of the dead the angels had killed — by the wounds on their necks, fingers and toes, marked as if branded by fire."*

Abu Jahl had told his men: *do not kill the Muslims, capture them, so we can show them what they did in mocking our religion and shunning Al-Lat and Al-Uzza.* Abu Jahl was killed, with sixty-nine others; 'Uqbah bin Abu Mu'ayt was captured and then executed — seventy dead in all, and seventy captured.

## 6. Fleeing the Field (8:15–16)

**"When you meet those who disbelieve in a battlefield, never turn your backs to them."**

Two exceptions are written into the verse itself:

- **A stratagem of war** — feigning flight so the enemy pursues, then turning to kill them (Sa'id bin Jubayr, As-Suddi). Ad-Dahhak adds: going ahead to scout the enemy's strength.
- **Retreating to a troop** — falling back to another Muslim force to reinforce or be reinforced.

And here Ibn Kathir preserves a genuine juristic argument about **how far the verse reaches**.

**Umar**, when Abu Ubayd was overwhelmed by Persian numbers at the Bridge: *"If he retreated to me, I would be a troop for him."* And publicly: **"O people! We are the troop of every Muslim."** And: *"Do not be confused by this ayah — it was about the day of Badr, and we are a troop for every Muslim."*

**Ibn Umar**, asked by Nafi' what to do when you cannot even locate your own army, answered: *"The troop is Allah's Messenger."* Pressed with the verse, he said: **"This ayah was about Badr — not before it, nor after it."**

Ad-Dahhak reads it forward: those who retreat to the Prophet ﷺ in his lifetime, and to their commander in ours.

Outside these cases, fleeing is a **major sin** — one of the seven destructive sins in the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, listed alongside shirk, magic, unlawful killing, riba, consuming an orphan's wealth, and slandering chaste believing women.

## 7. "You Did Not Throw" (8:17–19)

**"You killed them not, but Allah killed them. And you threw not when you did throw, but Allah threw."**

Ibn Kathir opens with a theological claim, stated plainly: **Allah creates the actions His servants perform**, and whatever good they do, He is the one to be praised, because He directed and enabled it.

The throw itself: the Prophet ﷺ came out of his shelter after invoking Allah in humility and neediness, took a handful of sand, threw it at the enemy saying *"humiliated be their faces"*, and ordered the charge. Allah carried that handful into the eyes of every one of them.

Victory does not come from numbers. Ibn Kathir cites Badr itself (3:123 — *you were a weak little force*) and then Hunayn (9:25 — *you rejoiced at your great number, but it availed you nothing*), and closes with 2:249: *how often has a small group overcome a mighty host by Allah's leave.*

**"If you ask for a judgement, now has the judgement come unto you."** This is addressed to the *disbelievers*, and it is Allah answering their own prayer. Abu Jahl had said before the battle: *"O Allah! Whichever of the two camps severed the ties of the womb and brought us what we do not recognise — destroy him this day."* As-Suddi adds that the Quraysh had clung to the curtains of the Ka'bah asking for victory *for the more honoured of the two armies, the most righteous of the two tribes*.

Ibn Kathir's reading: **"I accepted your supplication — and Muhammad won."**

## 8. The Call That Gives Life (8:20–24)

**"Answer Allah and the Messenger when he calls you to that which will give you life."**

Al-Bukhari's gloss: *"Answer" means obey; "that which gives you life" means that which makes your affairs good.* Urwah bin Az-Zubayr reads it specifically of Jihad — *by which Allah gives you might after meekness and strength after weakness*.

Al-Bukhari embeds a beautiful story here: **Abu Sa'id bin Al-Mu'alla** was praying when the Prophet ﷺ called him, and he finished the prayer before answering. The Prophet ﷺ recited this very ayah to him — *has not Allah said, answer Allah and the Messenger when he calls you?* — and then promised to teach him the greatest surah in the Qur'an before leaving. It was **Al-Fatihah**.

Between these comes the harshest verse in the surah about human beings:

**"Verily, the worst of living creatures with Allah are the deaf and the dumb, who understand not."** Deaf to the truth, mute in comprehending it. Ibn Kathir: every other creature keeps to the nature Allah created in it; these were made to worship and instead disbelieved. (Some said the verse is about certain Quraysh of Bani Abd Ad-Dar; Ibn Ishaq said the hypocrites. Ibn Kathir sees no contradiction — *both are devoid of sound comprehension and good intention.*)

### "Allah comes between a person and his heart"

The commentary here is the most quoted line in the surah.

- **Ibn Abbas:** *"Allah prevents the believer from disbelief and the disbeliever from faith."*
- **Mujahid** (second narration): He leaves him without comprehension.
- **As-Suddi:** He comes between a man and his own heart, so that he can neither believe nor disbelieve except by His leave.

Then the hadiths. The Prophet ﷺ used to say constantly: **"O Turner of hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion."** The Companions objected — *we believed in you and in what you brought; do you fear for us?* He said: **"Yes. For the hearts are between two of Allah's Fingers; He turns them as He wills."**

And from An-Nawwas bin Sam'an: *"Every heart is between two of the Fingers of the Most Merciful — if He wills, He makes it straight; if He wills, He makes it stray."*

## 9. The Fitnah That Does Not Stay With the Guilty (8:25)

**"And fear the Fitnah which affects not in particular only those of you who do wrong."**

Ibn Kathir: a trial that reaches the wicked *and those around them* — it will not confine itself to the sinners if the sin is not stopped.

The most haunting comment is **Az-Zubayr's**, asked at the Battle of the Camel why he had come out: *"We used to recite this ayah in the time of the Messenger of Allah, and Abu Bakr, and Umar, and Uthman — and we never thought it was about us. Until it reached us as it did."*

Ibn Abbas: Allah commanded the believers to stop evil from spreading among them so that He does not encompass them all in the punishment.

The supporting hadiths are a sustained argument for collective responsibility:

- **Hudhayfah:** *"By He in Whose Hand is my soul, you will enjoin righteousness and forbid evil, or Allah will send a punishment upon you — and then you will supplicate and He will not answer."*
- **Hudhayfah again,** on his own generation: *"A man used to utter one word in the time of the Messenger of Allah and become a hypocrite because of it. I now hear such words from one of you four times in a single sitting."*
- **The parable of the boat** (An-Nu'man bin Bashir): passengers drew lots for seats; those below had to climb past those above for water. They proposed making a hole in their own section instead. *If those above let them, everyone drowns. If they stop them, everyone is saved.*
- **Umm Salamah** asked whether the righteous would be among those punished when sins become widespread. **"Yes."** And what becomes of them? *"They will be stricken like the rest, then end up with Allah's forgiveness and pleasure."*

## 10. From Weakness to Strength (8:26)

**"And remember when you were few and were reckoned weak in the land, and were afraid that men might kidnap you."**

Few in Makkah, practising in secret, fearing that pagans or Persians or Romans might snatch them from any corner of the earth — until Allah gave them Madinah, allies who housed and fed them, and victory at Badr.

**Qatadah's** commentary is the passage worth keeping whole:

> *"The Arabs were the weakest of the weak, with the toughest life, the emptiest stomachs, the barest skin, the most obvious misguidance. Whoever lived among them lived in misery; whoever died went to the Fire. They were being eaten up and unable to eat up others. By Allah, we know of no people on the face of the earth at that time who had a worse life. Then Allah brought Islam — and made them dominant, and gave them provision and leadership over the necks of the nations. It is through Islam that Allah granted all you see. So thank Him."*

## 11. Betrayal, and What Wealth Is For (8:27–28)

**"Betray not Allah and His Messenger, nor betray knowingly your Amanat."**

The occasion is **Hatib bin Abi Balta'ah**, who wrote to Quraysh warning them the Prophet ﷺ was marching on Makkah. Umar asked to behead him; the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Leave him. He was at Badr. How do you know Allah has not looked upon the people of Badr and said: 'Do what you wish, I have forgiven you'?"*

Ibn Kathir immediately notes the principle of interpretation: **the ayah is general even though it was revealed about a specific incident** — *rulings are dealt with by their indications, not by the specific reasons behind their revelation, according to the majority of scholars.*

Ibn Abbas defines *Amanah* broadly: the actions Allah has entrusted the servants with, including everything He ordained. *Do not betray them* means **do not abandon the obligations.**

Then: **"Know that your possessions and your children are but a trial."** Ibn Kathir stacks the parallel verses — 64:15 (*a trial, and with Allah is a great reward*), 21:35, 63:9 (*let not your properties or children divert you from the remembrance of Allah*), 64:14 (*among your wives and children are enemies for you*).

And the hadith of the three qualities that yield the sweetness of faith: that Allah and His Messenger be dearer than anything else; that one loves a person purely for Allah's sake; and that one would rather be thrown into fire than return to disbelief after being saved from it.

## 12. Furqan (8:29)

**"If you obey and fear Allah, He will grant you Furqan, and will expiate for you your sins, and forgive you."**

The Salaf read *Furqan* several ways, and Ibn Kathir keeps them all:

- **A way out** — Ibn Abbas, As-Suddi, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah and others. Mujahid adds: *in this life and the Hereafter.*
- **Salvation**, or in another narration, **aid** — Ibn Abbas.
- **A criterion between truth and falsehood** — Ibn Ishaq.

Ibn Kathir prefers Ibn Ishaq's, *because it is more general and includes the rest*. Taqwa gives you the faculty of telling true from false — which is itself the escape route, in this world and the next.

Note the three gifts stacked in one verse: **discernment, expiation, forgiveness.**

## 13. The Plot at Dar An-Nadwah (8:30)

**"And when the disbelievers plotted against you — to imprison you, or to kill you, or to expel you; they were plotting and Allah too was plotting; and Allah is the best of plotters."**

Ibn Ishaq's account, from Ibn Abbas, is one of the great set-pieces of the sirah. The chiefs of Quraysh gathered at their council house. **Iblis joined them in the form of a distinguished old man from Najd**, and they let him in for his advice.

He opened it: *"You must deal with this man. By Allah, he will soon overwhelm you."*

- *Imprison him in chains until he dies, as the poets before him died.* — The old man: **no.** His Lord will free him to his companions, who will liberate him and may drive you from your land.
- *Expel him and be rid of the trouble.* — The old man: **no.** Have you forgotten his eloquence, and how his speech captures hearts? He will gather the Arabs against you, attack you in your own land, and kill your chiefs.
- Then **Abu Jahl**: take one strong young man from **every tribe**, give each a sharp sword, and let them strike together — so the blood is on all the clans at once, and Banu Hashim, unable to fight all of Quraysh, must accept blood money.

The old man from Najd: **"By Allah, this man has spoken the best opinion, and I support no other."**

They broke up and began preparing. Jibril came that night and told the Prophet ﷺ not to sleep in his bed — and Allah gave him permission to emigrate.

The three verbs of the verse — *imprison, kill, expel* — are the three proposals, in order.

Urwah's gloss on **"Allah too was plotting"**: *"I plotted against them with My sure planning, and I saved you from them."*

## 14. The Two Shelters (8:31–33)

The Quraysh boast: **"We have heard; if we wished we could say the like of this. This is nothing but tales of the ancients."**

Ibn Kathir: *they boasted with words, not with actions.* They were challenged repeatedly to produce a single chapter and never could.

The speaker was likely **An-Nadr bin Al-Harith**, who had travelled to Persia and learned the stories of Rustum and Isfandiyar. When the Prophet ﷺ left a gathering, An-Nadr would take his place telling Persian tales and asking: *"Who has better stories to tell — Muhammad, or I?"* He was captured at Badr and executed.

Then the extraordinary prayer: **"O Allah! If this is indeed the truth from You, then rain down stones on us from the sky, or bring on us a painful torment."** Anas identifies the speaker as **Abu Jahl**.

Ibn Kathir's comment is the whole moral: *They should have said, "O Allah, if this is the truth from You, guide us to it and help us follow it." Instead they called down their own punishment.*

And Allah answered with mercy: **"Allah would not punish them while you are among them, nor while they seek forgiveness."**

Ibn Abbas: **"They had two safety shelters — the Prophet, and their seeking of forgiveness. The Prophet went away, and only seeking forgiveness remained."**

The Prophet ﷺ himself said the same (Abu Musa): *"Allah sent down to me two safe shelters for my Ummah... and when I die, I leave the seeking of forgiveness with them until the Day of Resurrection."*

And the hadith of Abu Sa'id, which Ibn Kathir offers as corroboration:

> Shaytan said: *"By Your might, O Lord, I will keep luring Your servants as long as their souls are in their bodies."*
> The Lord said: *"By My might and majesty, I will keep forgiving them as long as they keep asking Me for forgiveness."*

## 15. Whistling and Clapping at the House (8:34–35)

**"Why should Allah not punish them, when they hinder men from Al-Masjid Al-Haram, and they are not its guardians? None can be its guardians except those who have Taqwa."**

The Prophet ﷺ and his Companions are the true custodians of the sanctuary, not the pagans who hold the keys — Ibn Kathir supports this with 9:17–18 (*it is not for the polytheists to maintain the Masjids of Allah*). Mujahid extends it: the ayah is about the Mujahidin, whoever and wherever they are.

**"Their Salah at the House was nothing but Muka' and Tasdiyah."** A long list of the Salaf — Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah and others — agree: **Muka' is whistling, Tasdiyah is clapping**. Mujahid adds that they put their fingers in their mouths to whistle. Ibn Abbas: they performed Tawaf naked, whistling and clapping.

That was their "prayer" at the holiest site on earth — and they were the ones barring the believers from it.

## 16. Money Spent Against the Truth (8:36–37)

**"Those who disbelieve spend their wealth to hinder men from the path of Allah — and so will they continue to spend it; but in the end it will become an anguish for them. Then they will be overcome."**

Ibn Ishaq's account: after Badr, the men of Quraysh who had lost fathers, sons and brothers went to Abu Sufyan and those with capital in the surviving caravan. *"Muhammad has grieved you and killed your chiefs. Help us with this wealth so we may fight him."* They agreed — and that money funded **Uhud**.

Ibn Kathir sets out the arc coldly: they spend it, they lose it, they lose the war, and the money becomes pure regret. *Whoever among them lives long will see and hear what grieves him; whoever is killed returns to everlasting disgrace.*

**"In order that Allah may distinguish the wicked from the good"** — Ibn Abbas: to separate the people of happiness from the people of misery. Ibn Kathir reads the whole thing as a test run in both directions: *We tried you with combatant disbelievers whom We enabled to spend money fighting you* (cf. 3:179, 3:142).

## 17. "If They Cease" — and the Order to Fight (8:38–40)

**"Say to those who have disbelieved: if they cease, their past will be forgiven."**

Two hadiths carry this:

- *"He who becomes good in his Islam will not be punished for what he did in Jahiliyyah. He who becomes bad in his Islam will be punished for the earlier and the later."*
- *"Islam erases what came before it, and repentance erases what came before it."*

**"And fight them until there is no more Fitnah, and the religion will all be for Allah alone."**

Ibn Kathir devotes real space to what *Fitnah* means here, and he does it through a superb exchange recorded by Al-Bukhari. A man came to **Ibn Umar** and challenged him: *why don't you act on this verse and fight?*

Ibn Umar: *"We did that in the time of the Messenger of Allah, when Islam was weak and a man would be tortured to death or imprisoned for his religion. When Islam became strong and widespread, there was no more Fitnah."*

The man, changing tack, asked what he thought of Ali and Uthman. Ibn Umar: *"As for Uthman, Allah has forgiven him — but you hate that Allah forgives him. As for Ali, he is the cousin of the Messenger of Allah and his son-in-law"* — and he pointed: *"and that is his house over there."*

And to another questioner: *"Do you know what Fitnah is? Muhammad was fighting the idolators, and in that time being among the idolators was a Fitnah in one's religion. It is nothing like what you are doing — fighting to gain leadership!"*

The Salaf's readings of *Fitnah* here: **shirk** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi and others); or **the persecution of a Muslim until he abandons his religion** (Az-Zuhri, Urwah). And *the religion all for Allah* means **Tawhid practised sincerely**, without shirk.

But Ibn Kathir sets hard limits on it in the same breath:

- **"But if they cease, then certainly Allah is All-Seer of what they do"** — *even though you do not know the true reason they stopped.* Judgement of motive is not yours.
- **9:5** — *if they repent, pray and give Zakah, leave their way free.*
- **9:11** — *then they are your brethren in religion.*
- **2:193** — *if they cease, let there be no transgression except against the wrongdoers.*
- And **Usamah bin Zayd**, who killed a man after the man said *La ilaha illallah*, protesting that he only said it to save himself. The Prophet ﷺ: **"Did you cut his heart open?"** And then, again and again: *"What will you do about 'La ilaha illallah' on the Day of Resurrection?"* — until Usamah said, **"I wished I had embraced Islam only that day."**

---

## 18. The Fifth (8:41)

**"Know that whatever war booty you gain, one-fifth is for Allah, and for the Messenger, and for the near relatives, the orphans, the poor, and the wayfarer."**

Ibn Kathir first fixes the terms:

- **Ghanimah** — spoils captured from the disbelievers by force of arms.
- **Fay'** — property forfeited without fighting: what they abandon in peace, what they leave with no heir, plus Jizyah and Kharaj.

Then the rulings:

- The fifth must be surrendered **in full, however small** — *"even a yarn and a needle."*
- The share of "Allah and the Messenger" is **one share**, not two (Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Ash-Sha'bi, Ata', Qatadah and others). Ibn Abbas notes that naming Allah first is inclusive, as when He says *to Allah belongs all that is in the heavens and the earth*.
- The four-fifths go to the army, and **no fighter has more right to it than another**: *"Even if you pull out an arrow that pierced your flank, you have no more right to it than your Muslim brother."*
- The relatives' share goes to **Banu Hashim and Banu Al-Muttalib**, because Banu Al-Muttalib stood with Banu Hashim in the boycott at the mountain pass of Abu Talib.
- The Prophet ﷺ could select an item for himself — he chose the sword **Dhul-Fiqar** at Badr.

Ubadah's narration of the Prophet's ﷺ address, holding a tuft of camel hair between his fingers:

> *"This too is part of your booty. I have no share in it except my own — the fifth designated to me — and even that is given back to you. So surrender even the needle and thread, and whatever is bigger or smaller. Do not steal from the spoils: it is Fire and shame in this life and the next."*

And Ibn Kathir underlines the theological point Al-Bukhari built a chapter title around: in the hadith of the delegation of Abd Al-Qays, the Prophet ﷺ listed **surrendering the fifth as part of faith itself**, alongside the shahadah, the prayer and the Zakah.

**"The Day of Criterion, the Day when the two forces met"** — Badr is *Yawm Al-Furqan*, says Ibn Abbas, *the day Allah separated truth from falsehood.*

---

## 19. The Geometry of Badr (8:42–44)

**"When you were on the near side of the valley, and they on the farther side, and the caravan on the ground lower than you."**

Muslims camped on the Madinah side; Quraysh on the Makkah side; Abu Sufyan's caravan below, nearer the sea, slipping away.

**"Even if you had made a mutual appointment, you would have failed in the appointment."** Urwah's father's comment: *had it been arranged, and had you known their numbers against yours, you would never have come.*

Ibn Kathir includes the reconnaissance scene. Scouts brought back two water-carriers. The Companions, wanting them to be Abu Sufyan's men, beat them until they said what was wanted; when they told the truth — *we belong to Quraysh* — they were beaten harder. The Prophet ﷺ finished his prayer and said:

> *"When they tell you the truth you beat them, and when they lie you let them go! They have told the truth, by Allah — they belong to Quraysh."*

He then questioned the boys himself, and the exchange is a small masterpiece of intelligence work:

- *How many are they?* — "Many."
- *How many exactly?* — "We don't know."
- **"How many camels do they slaughter each day?"** — "Nine or ten."
- **"Then they are between nine hundred and a thousand."**

He asked which chiefs had come, and the boys named them: Utbah and Shaybah bin Rabi'ah, Abu Al-Bakhtari, Hakim bin Hizam, Nawfal bin Khuwaylid, Abu Jahl, Umayyah bin Khalaf, An-Nadr bin Al-Harith, Suhayl bin Amr, and others.

The Prophet ﷺ turned to his men: **"This is Makkah — she has thrown you her most precious sons."**

### Made few in each other's eyes

Two separate favours, and Ibn Kathir is careful to show how they fit with 3:13, where the disbelievers see the Muslims as *twice their number*:

1. **In the dream (8:43):** Allah showed the Prophet ﷺ the enemy as few, and he told his Companions, and their resolve hardened. *Had He shown them as many, you would have lost heart and quarrelled.*
2. **At the meeting (8:44):** each side saw the other as few — so both would be eager to engage. Ibn Mas'ud: *"They looked so few to us that I said to the man beside me, 'Do you think they are seventy?' He said, 'A hundred.' Later we captured one and he told us: we were a thousand."*
3. **Once the battle began** and the angels came down in succession, the disbelievers then saw the Muslims as **double** their number.

Ibn Kathir: *"This is how we reconcile these two ayat, and each of them is true."*

---

## 20. The Manners of War (8:45–47)

**"When you meet a force, take a firm stand against them and remember Allah much."**

Ibn Kathir extracts the discipline: **endure, do not flee, keep remembering Allah in the middle of it, obey Allah and the Messenger, and do not quarrel.**

The Prophet's ﷺ battlefield address, from Abdullah bin Abi Awfa — delivered after waiting for the sun to decline:

> *"O people! Do not wish to meet the enemy, and ask Allah for safety. But when you do meet them, be patient — and know that **Paradise is under the shadows of the swords**."*

Then he stood and prayed: *"O Allah, Revealer of the Book, Mover of the clouds, Defeater of the Confederates — defeat them and grant us victory over them."*

**"And do not dispute lest you lose courage and your strength departs, and be patient."**

Ibn Kathir treats this as the surah's structural spine — the same disease as verse 1, now named as a military hazard. Internal argument literally drains a force of its *rih*, its wind, its momentum.

And then a passage of pure admiration: through obedience to the Messenger ﷺ, the Companions *opened hearts as well as the eastern and western parts of the world in a very short time*, though few in number against Romans, Persians, Turks, Berbers, Ethiopians and Copts. **The Islamic state spread east and west in less than thirty years.**

**"Be not like those who come out of their homes boastfully and to be seen of men."**

The counter-model is Abu Jahl. Told the caravan was safe and they could go home, he answered:

> *"No, by Allah! We will not go back until we reach the well of Badr, slaughter camels, drink wine, and have the singing girls sing for us. Then the Arabs will always talk about our stance and what we did that day."*

Muhammad bin Ka'b confirms they brought singers and drums with them. Ibn Kathir's closing note is grim: *they proceeded to the well of Badr and brought themselves to death — and were thrown into that same well, dead and despised.*

---

## 21. Shaytan in the Shape of Suraqah (8:48–49)

**"And when Shaytan made their deeds seem fair to them and said: 'No one of mankind can overcome you this day, and verily, I am your neighbour.'"**

He came in the form of **Suraqah bin Malik bin Ju'shum**, chief of Banu Mudlij — guaranteeing that the Quraysh's rear was safe from the tribe of Bakr while they marched. Ibn Abbas says he came with a standard and troops of his own.

**"But when the two forces came in sight of each other, he ran away and said: 'I have nothing to do with you. I see what you see not. I fear Allah, for Allah is severe in punishment.'"**

Ibn Abbas: when the Prophet ﷺ threw the sand and Jibril advanced, Shaytan — holding the hand of a Mushrik at that moment — pulled his hand free and fled with his soldiers. The man cried after him: **"Suraqah! You said you were our neighbour!"** And the answer came back: *I see what you do not see.*

*He saw the angels.*

Ibn Kathir attaches 4:120: *he makes promises to them and arouses false desires — and Shaytan's promises are nothing but deception.*

**"These people are deceived by their religion"** — the line of the hypocrites and the sick-hearted, looking at three hundred men facing a thousand and drawing the obvious conclusion. Ash-Sha'bi adds a detail that stings: some Makkans who had been *considering Islam* came out with the army, saw how few the Muslims were, and said it too.

Allah's answer is the last clause: **"But whoever puts his trust in Allah — then surely Allah is All-Mighty, All-Wise."**

---

## 22. The Angels at Death, and the Law of Grace (8:50–54)

**"If you could see when the angels take away the souls of those who disbelieve; they smite their faces and their backs."**

Ibn Abbas: at Badr the Muslims struck their faces with swords, and when they turned to flee, **the angels struck their backs.** Ibn Kathir notes the verse describes Badr but is not restricted to it — it is general for every disbeliever, as in 6:93 and in Surah Muhammad.

The hadith of Al-Bara' on the death of the disbeliever: the angel of death comes in a terrifying form and says *"Get out, O wicked soul, to fierce hot wind, boiling water, and a shadow of black smoke."* The soul scatters through the body and is drawn out **like a needle pulled from wet wool.**

**"And verily, Allah is not unjust to His servants."** The hadith Qudsi from Abu Dharr:

> *"O My servants! I have forbidden injustice for Myself, and made it forbidden between you — so do not wrong one another. O My servants, it is your deeds that I keep count of for you. So whoever finds good, let him praise Allah; and whoever finds otherwise, let him blame no one but himself."*

Then the surah's great historical principle:

**"That is so because Allah will never change a grace which He has bestowed on a people, until they change what is in themselves."**

Ibn Kathir pairs it with 13:11 and applies it to Fir'awn: Allah destroyed him and stripped away *gardens, springs, plants, treasures and pleasant dwellings* — **"Allah did not wrong them; it is they who wronged themselves."**

*Blessing is not withdrawn arbitrarily. It leaves when the people who held it become different from the people who were given it.*

---

## 23. Treaties: Breaking, Deterrence, and Honest Repudiation (8:55–58)

**"The worst of living creatures before Allah are those who disbelieve — those with whom you made a covenant, but they break it every time, and they do not have Taqwa."**

Note the definition: the worst are not simply those who reject, but **those who cannot be held to an agreement**.

**"So if you gain mastery over them in war, then disperse those who are behind them, so that they may learn a lesson."** Ibn Abbas, Al-Hasan Al-Basri, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi and others: punish the treaty-breakers severely enough that others take warning. As-Suddi: *"so that they are careful not to break treaties, lest they meet the same end."*

Then the counterweight, and it is one of the noblest passages in the surah:

**"If you fear treachery from a people, throw back their covenant to them so as to be on equal terms. Certainly Allah likes not the treacherous."**

You may end a treaty on suspicion — but you must **announce** it, so both sides know a state of war exists. And Ibn Kathir states flatly: *this even includes treachery against the disbelievers.*

The story that proves it: **Mu'awiyah** was marching in Roman territory during a valid truce, timing his approach so he could strike the moment it expired. An old man on his mount began shouting **"Allahu Akbar! Allahu Akbar! Be honest and stay away from betrayal!"** — and quoted the Prophet ﷺ:

> *"Whoever has a treaty of peace with a people should not untie any part of it nor tie it harder until the treaty reaches its term. Or he should declare it null and void, so that both are on equal terms."*

Mu'awiyah, informed of it, **withdrew**. The old man turned out to be the Companion Amr bin Anbasah.

---

## 24. Preparing Power, and Accepting Peace (8:59–63)

**"And make ready against them all you can of power, including steeds of war, to threaten the enemy of Allah and your enemy."**

Uqbah bin Amir heard the Prophet ﷺ say from the minbar, reciting this verse:

> **"Verily, power is shooting! Power is shooting! Power is shooting."**

On horses, the famous hadith of the three kinds:

| **A source of reward** | tethered for Allah's cause — even what it grazes, its dung, its hoofprints, the water it drinks unbidden, all written as good deeds |
| **A means of shelter** | kept for self-sufficiency and to avoid begging, without forgetting Allah's right over it |
| **A source of sin** | kept for pride, show, and enmity toward Muslims |

Asked about donkeys, he said nothing had been revealed to him about them except *the unique, comprehensive ayah*: **"whoever does an atom's weight of good shall see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil shall see it."**

**"And others besides them, whom you may not know but whom Allah does know."** Mujahid: Banu Qurayzah. As-Suddi: the Persians. Muqatil and Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd: **the hypocrites** — supported by 9:101, *among the Bedouins around you are hypocrites, and among the people of Madinah... you know them not, We know them.*

**"Whatever you spend in the cause of Allah shall be repaid to you in full."**

### And then the pivot

**"But if they incline to peace, you also incline to it, and trust in Allah."**

Ibn Kathir's example is **Hudaybiyyah** — the Prophet ﷺ accepted a nine-year cessation of hostilities and the other terms the Quraysh brought, when they inclined to peace.

And from Ali: *"There will be disputes after me, so if you have a way to end them in peace, then do so."*

**"And if they intend to deceive you, then verily, Allah is All-Sufficient for you."** Accept the peace even so — if it is a trick to regroup, Allah will suffice you.

### The uniting of hearts

**"He it is Who has supported you with His help and with the believers. And He has united their hearts. If you had spent all that is in the earth, you could not have united their hearts — but Allah has united them."**

Ibn Kathir names the specific case: the Aws and the Khazraj, who had fought each other for generations with every reason to keep fighting. *Allah ended all that evil with the light of faith* (3:103 — *you were enemies and He united your hearts, and you were on the brink of a pit of Fire*).

And the Prophet's ﷺ address to the Ansar at Hunayn:

> *"O Ansar! Did I not find you misguided, and Allah guided you by me? Poor, and Allah enriched you by me? Divided, and Allah united you by me?"*

Every time, they answered: **"Truly, the favour is from Allah and His Messenger."**

*Money can buy an alliance. It cannot buy the end of a blood feud.*

## 25. Twenty Against Two Hundred — and Its Easing (8:64–66)

**"If there are twenty steadfast persons among you, they will overcome two hundred."** One Muslim to stand against **ten**.

Ibn Abbas: *"When this was revealed, it became difficult for the Muslims."* Then Allah lightened it: **"Now Allah has lightened your task, for He knows that there is weakness in you. So if there are a hundred steadfast, they shall overcome two hundred."** One against **two**.

Ibn Kathir's formulation is precise: **the ruling was abrogated, but the good news remained.** And Ibn Ishaq draws out the consequence — thereafter Muslims may not flee an enemy up to twice their number, but below that ratio they are not obliged to fight and may withdraw.

Between these two verses sits the Prophet's ﷺ exhortation at Badr, and the story of **Umayr bin Al-Humam**:

> *"Get up and march toward a Paradise as wide as the heavens and the earth."*
> Umayr: *"As wide as the heavens and the earth!"*
> *"Yes."*
> Umayr: *"Excellent! Excellent!"*
> *"What makes you say that?"*
> *"The hope that I might be one of its people."*
> **"You are one of its people."**

Umayr broke the scabbard from his sword, took some dates from his bag and began eating — then threw them away, saying: **"If I live long enough to finish these dates, that is indeed a long life."**

He went forward, fought, and was killed.

## 26. The Prisoners (8:67–71)

**"It is not fitting for a Prophet that he should have prisoners of war until he has fought thoroughly in the land. You desire the goods of this world, but Allah desires for you the Hereafter."**

Anas's narration is the centre of it. After the victory the Prophet ﷺ put the question to the men: *"Allah has made you prevail over them."*

**Umar** stood: *"O Messenger of Allah! Cut off their necks."* The Prophet ﷺ turned away.
He asked again, adding: *"and only yesterday they were your brothers."*
**Umar** again: *"Cut off their necks."*
He asked again.
**Abu Bakr**: *"O Messenger of Allah, I think you should pardon them and set them free for ransom."*

Ibn Kathir records the physical detail: **the grief left the Prophet's ﷺ face**, and he accepted the ransom. And then the verse came down: **"Were it not a previous ordainment from Allah, a severe torment would have touched you for what you took."**

The "previous ordainment", per Ibn Abbas and a long line of others, is that **war spoils had already been decreed lawful for this Ummah** — as in the hadith of the five things given to no prophet before: victory by awe at a month's distance, the earth as a place of prayer and purification, the lawfulness of booty, intercession, and being sent to all mankind rather than one people.

Hence: **"So enjoy what you have gotten of booty in war, lawful and good, and have Taqwa of Allah."** The ransom at Badr was fixed at four hundred dirhams a head.

The majority ruling that Ibn Kathir draws from all this: **the fate of prisoners is at the discretion of the Imam** — execution (as with Banu Qurayzah), ransom (as at Badr), exchange for Muslim captives, or captivity.

### Al-Abbas

Before the battle, the Prophet ﷺ had given orders that certain men forced into the Quraysh ranks were not to be killed — among them **Abu Al-Bukhtari bin Hisham** and **Al-Abbas bin Abdul-Muttalib**, his own uncle.

Abu Hudhayfah bin Utbah objected: *"Shall we kill our fathers, sons, brothers and kinsmen and spare Al-Abbas? By Allah, if I meet him I will put the sword in him."* When it reached the Prophet ﷺ he turned to Umar — and Umar noted it was the first time he was ever called *Abu Hafs*:

> **"Will the face of the Messenger of Allah's uncle be struck with the sword?"**

Umar asked permission to behead Abu Hudhayfah for hypocrisy. Abu Hudhayfah lived the rest of his life saying he would never feel safe from that sentence unless Allah forgave him through martyrdom — and he was martyred at Al-Yamamah.

That night, Ibn Abbas says, the Prophet ﷺ lay awake. *"Why do you not sleep?"* He said: **"I heard the cries of pain from my uncle Al-Abbas because of his shackles. So untie him."** When the crying stopped, he slept.

The Ansar offered to release Al-Abbas without ransom, as their maternal cousin. He refused: **"No, by Allah. Do not leave a single dirham of it."**

Al-Abbas protested that he had been a Muslim already, and had no money. The Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Allah knows if you are Muslim, and if what you claim is true, He will compensate you. But your outward stance was against us. So ransom yourself, and your nephews Nawfal and Aqil, and your ally Utbah."*
> *"I do not have that, O Messenger of Allah."*
> **"What about the money you and Umm Al-Fadl buried, when you said to her: 'If I am killed in this battle, this is for my children Al-Fadl, Abdullah and Quthm'?"**

Al-Abbas: *"By Allah, I know you are the Messenger of Allah — no one knew of that except Umm Al-Fadl and me."*

He paid. And the verse came: **"O Prophet! Say to the captives in your hands: if Allah knows any good in your hearts, He will give you something better than what has been taken from you, and He will forgive you."**

Al-Abbas afterward: *"After I became Muslim, Allah gave me twenty servants in place of the twenty Uwqiyah I lost. And I still hope for His forgiveness."*

Ibn Kathir closes the episode with a scene from years later: wealth arrived from Bahrain, the largest sum the Prophet ﷺ had ever received, and he had it poured out in the Masjid and prayed without so much as looking at it. Then he sat and gave to everyone who came. Al-Abbas asked for a share, *because I ransomed myself and Aqil*. He was told to take. He stuffed his garment, could not lift it, asked for help — refused; asked the Prophet ﷺ himself to help lift it — refused; finally dropped some, hoisted the rest, and staggered off. **The Prophet ﷺ watched him go, astonished at his greediness, and did not rise until the last coin was distributed.**

## 27. Who Belongs to Whom (8:72–75)

The surah ends on **Wilayah** — the bonds of loyalty, protection and inheritance — and divides the believers into three:

**1. The Muhajirin and the Ansar — "allies to one another."**
Those who emigrated and fought with their wealth and their lives; and those who gave them shelter and aid. *Each has more right to the other than anyone else.* The Prophet ﷺ forged formal ties of brotherhood between them, and **they used to inherit from each other, ahead of blood relatives** — until that was abrogated by the fixed shares.

Ibn Kathir cites 9:100 (*the foremost of the Muhajirin and the Ansar... Allah is well-pleased with them*), 9:117, and 59:8–9 — *they give them preference over themselves even though they were in need*, and *have no jealousy in their breasts for what the others were given*, which the best commentary reads as: **they did not envy the Muhajirin the reward of their emigration.** From these, Ibn Kathir reports a scholarly consensus that **the Muhajirin rank above the Ansar.**

**2. Those who believed but did not emigrate — "you owe no duty of protection to them until they emigrate."**
They have no share in the spoils or the fifth unless they join the fighting. Ibn Kathir supports this with the Prophet's ﷺ standing instructions to commanders (from Buraydah): call the enemy to Islam; then call them to move to the land of the Muhajirin, informing them that if they do, they get the Muhajirin's rights *and duties*; if they refuse and stay put, they are like Muslim Bedouins — the law applies to them fully, but **no share of booty unless they fight**; then Jizyah; and only then, battle.

But: **"if they seek your help in religion, it is your duty to help them"** — with one exception, **"except against a people with whom you have a treaty."** A valid treaty outranks the claim of a fellow believer's call for aid. Ibn Kathir: *do not betray your treaties or break your promises.*

**3. Those who came later — "and those who believed afterwards, and emigrated and strove hard along with you, they are of you."**
Ibn Kathir attaches: *"One will be with those whom he loves"* (mutawatir, in both Sahihs); *"He who loves a people is one of them"*; and in another wording, *will be gathered with them.*

Between them stands the hard line: **"And those who disbelieve are allies of one another. If you do not do so, there will be Fitnah on the earth, and great corruption."** No mutual inheritance across the line of religion. And the warning: if the believers do not hold together and keep their loyalties distinct, *confusion becomes rampant and trials spread among people.*

The final verse returns the inheritance to blood: **"But kindred by blood are nearer to one another in the decree ordained by Allah."** Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and others: this **abrogated** inheritance by treaty and by the pact of brotherhood, which had held in the earliest days of Islam.

## 28. In One Breath

Al-Anfal takes the most spectacular victory in the Ummah's history and refuses to let its people enjoy it uncritically. It begins with the winners arguing over the loot and answers by confiscating it. It reminds them they did not want the battle, did not choose the ground, did not know the numbers, did not throw the sand that blinded the enemy, and did not kill the men they killed. It tells them the angels were for their comfort, not their necessity. It then hands them a code: do not flee, do not quarrel in the ranks, do not march out to be seen of men, surrender the fifth down to the needle and thread, honour a treaty until you have openly renounced it, prepare real power and then accept peace the moment it is offered, and remember that the union of your hearts could not have been bought with everything on earth.

And underneath it all, the surah's quietest verse and its most demanding: **Allah comes between a person and his heart.** The men who won at Badr were the same men who quarrelled after it, and the same men who were promised forgiveness in advance. Their victory was never theirs, and neither was their faith — both were held for them by the One who turns hearts between two of His Fingers, and who does not remove a favour from a people until they have first changed what is in themselves.
