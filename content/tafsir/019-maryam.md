*Revealed in Makkah · 98 ayat · the surah recited to a Christian king*

---

## 1. Where This Surah Was First Heard Outside Makkah

Ibn Kathir opens not with the text but with a scene. In the narrations of Umm Salamah (via Ibn Ishaq) and Ibn Mas'ud (via Ahmad), the Muslims who migrated to Ethiopia were summoned before An-Najashi to answer the Quraysh delegation. **Ja'far bin Abi Talib recited the opening portion of this surah** to the king and his court.

The choice is not accidental. A surah that speaks of Maryam, Zakariyya, Yahya and 'Isa — and then states plainly what 'Isa was and was not — was the one piece of revelation that a Christian monarch could recognise and be moved by, and that still gave nothing away.

**Kaf Ha Ya 'Ain Sad** — Ibn Kathir refers back to his discussion of the disconnected letters at the start of Al-Baqarah and does not repeat it.

---

## 2. Zakariyya Calls Out in Secret (19:2–6)

*A reminder of the mercy of your Lord to His servant Zakariyya, when he called his Lord (with) a call in secret.*

He was a Prophet of the Children of Israel and — per Sahih Al-Bukhari — **a carpenter who ate from the work of his own hands.**

- **Why in secret?** Because that is more beloved to Allah. Qatadah: Allah knows the pious heart and hears the hidden voice.
- **"My bones have grown feeble, and gray hair has Ashta'al on my head"** — the gray has *burned* through the black. Weakness and old age, inside and out.
- **"I have never been unblessed in my invocation to You"** — meaning: I have no experience of You except that You answer me.
- **The fear:** the *Mawali* after him — his succeeding relatives (Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi). He feared the generation to come would be a wicked one, so he asked for a son who would be a Prophet to guide them.

### The inheritance was never about money

Ibn Kathir argues this at length, in three steps:

1. A Prophet is too lofty to fret over who gets his property, or to ask for a son merely to cut relatives out of an estate.
2. Allah never describes Zakariyya as wealthy — he was a carpenter living off his own labour, and amassing wealth is not the way of Prophets, who are the most abstentious of people in worldly matters.
3. The hadith in the Two Sahihs: **"We (the Prophets) do not leave behind inheritance. Whatever we leave behind is charity."**

So *who shall inherit me* means **inheritance of prophethood**, exactly as with *And Sulayman inherited from Dawud* (27:16) — which would be meaningless if it meant property, since every legal system already gives a son his father's wealth. Mujahid: his inheritance was knowledge. Abu Salih: he would be a Prophet as his forefathers were Prophets.

*And make him, my Lord, one with whom You are well-pleased* — pleasing to You and to Your creation; beloved to people in both his religion and his character.

---

## 3. Yahya — A Name Never Used Before (19:7–9)

The answer skips the granting and reports the announcement, which implies it. **"We have given that name to none before him"** — Qatadah, Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Zayd: no one had borne this name previously. Ibn Jarir preferred this.

Zakariyya's astonishment (*how can I have a son?*) is the reaction of a man whose supplication was answered beyond expectation: his wife barren her whole life, himself feeble and past capacity.

The angel's reply does two things. First, it closes off the obvious escape route: the child will come **from you and from this wife of yours**, not from another woman. Second, it answers a bigger question than the one asked:

> *Certainly I have created you before, when you had been nothing!*

Paired with 76:1 — *Has there not been over man a period of time, when he was not a thing worth mentioning?*

---

## 4. Three Nights Without Speech (19:10–11)

Zakariyya asks for a sign — not from doubt, but as Ibrahim asked to see the dead revived: *but to put my heart at ease* (2:260).

The sign is his own tongue. *You shall not speak unto mankind for three nights, though having no bodily defect* — meaning healthy, with no illness or injury, and yet unable to speak. Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Wahb, As-Suddi and Qatadah all agree: his tongue was arrested without sickness. 'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd adds that **he could still recite and glorify Allah** — he simply could not address people except by gesture.

Read alongside 3:41 (*three days except with signals*), Ibn Kathir concludes the silence covered the three nights **and their days**.

Then he came out of the Mihrab — the very place where he had received the news — and signalled to his people to glorify Allah morning and evening. A swift, subtle gesture; they were to keep the three days with him in increased worship and gratitude.

---

## 5. Yahya: Wisdom While Yet a Child (19:12–15)

*O Yahya! Hold fast to the Scripture* — learn it with strength, zeal and studious effort.

*And We gave him wisdom while yet a child* — understanding, knowledge, fortitude, diligence, and appetite for good, all granted while he was still young. That is precisely why it is worth mentioning.

Then a chain of qualities, each unpacked:

| Word | Ibn Kathir's reading |
|---|---|
| **Hananan** | Mercy, compassion, tenderness from Us. Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak: mercy. Ad-Dahhak adds: mercy no one else could give. Mujahid: gentleness from his Lord |
| **Zakatan** | Purity — from filth, wickedness and sin. Qatadah: the righteous deed. Ibn Abbas: he was a blessing |
| **And he was pious** | Pure, with no inclination toward sin |
| **Dutiful to his parents** | Not disobedient to them in speech, action, command or prohibition — *and he was not arrogant or disobedient* |

Note the order Ibn Kathir points out: obedience to the Lord, then mercy and purity, then **immediately** obedience to parents. The two are habitually joined in the Qur'an.

### Peace at the three loneliest moments

*And Salam be on him the day he was born, and the day he dies, and the day he will be raised up to life again.*

Sufyan bin Uyaynah: the loneliest a man ever is comes in three situations —

- **The day he is born** — when he sees himself emerging from what he was in.
- **The day he dies** — when he sees people he will never see again.
- **The day he is resurrected** — when he sees himself in the great gathering.

Yahya was granted security in exactly these three.

---

## 6. Maryam Withdraws (19:16–17)

Ibn Kathir explains the placement: Allah has just shown a child given to a barren couple in old age; He now shows a child given with **no father at all**. The stories are near in meaning, which is why they sit together here, in Al 'Imran and in Al-Anbiya' — all demonstrating His ability and the reach of His authority.

Maryam bint 'Imran, of the lineage of Dawud, from a good family of the Children of Israel; dedicated by her mother to the service of the Sacred House. She lived under the care of Zakariyya, her brother-in-law, who found her supplied with provision he could not account for — **winter fruit in summer and summer fruit in winter** (3:37).

*She withdrew to a place facing east* — she secluded herself on the eastern side of the Masjid in Jerusalem. Ibn Abbas: this verse is the reason the Christians took the east as their direction of worship, since it marks the site of 'Isa's birth.

*Then We sent to her Our Ruh* — **Jibril** (Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, Ibn Jurayj, Wahb, As-Suddi), appearing as a complete and perfect man.

---

## 7. "I Seek Refuge with the Most Gracious from You" (19:18–21)

Alone, screened off, confronted by a man — she assumed the worst. And her first move was not to flee or fight, but to **remind him of Allah**.

Ibn Kathir marks this out as legislated practice: *this is what is prescribed in defence against evil, so that it may be repulsed with ease.* Abu Wa'il: she knew a pious man would restrain himself when addressed this way.

It is said that at her mention of Ar-Rahman, Jibril reverted to his true form. His answer removes the fear: *I am only a messenger from your Lord.*

Her second objection is the natural one — *no man has touched me, nor am I Baghiyya* (a fornicating woman).

### The four modes of creation

The angel's answer includes *We wish to appoint him as a sign to mankind* — and Ibn Kathir draws out why 'Isa completes a set:

1. **Adam** — from neither male nor female.
2. **Hawwa'** — from a male without a female.
3. **All other people** — from male and female.
4. **'Isa** — from a female without a male.

The four together prove the perfection of His power. *And it is a matter already decreed* — Ibn Ishaq: Allah has determined it; there is no avoiding it.

---

## 8. The Palm Trunk (19:22–26)

Ibn Ishaq describes the aftermath in human detail: she conceived, returned with her water jug, her menses stopped, and she went through what pregnant women go through — sickness, hunger, change of colour, even a change in how she spoke. Word spread among the Children of Israel. **They named Yusuf**, since no one else was in the temple with her. So she veiled herself entirely: no one saw her, and she saw no one.

*And the pains of childbirth drove her to the trunk of a date palm.* On the location, the reports differ — As-Suddi places it east of the Sanctuary; Wahb once says between Ash-Sham and Egypt, and elsewhere **eight miles from Jerusalem, at Bayt Al-Lahm (Bethlehem)**. Ibn Kathir notes the Isra' hadiths (An-Nasa'i from Anas; Al-Bayhaqi from Shaddad bin Aws) support Bethlehem, "if the hadith is authentic," and that the Christians have never doubted it.

*Would that I had died before this, and had been forgotten and out of sight.* Ibn Kathir reads this as **evidence that it is permissible to wish for death when calamity strikes**. She knew what was coming: no one would help her, and no one would believe her. A woman known among them for devotion was about to be taken for a fornicator.

### The voice from below

Two readings of who called out:

- **Jibril**, from the bottom of the valley — Ibn Abbas, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak, 'Amr bin Maymun, As-Suddi, Qatadah. Their argument: 'Isa did not speak until she brought him to her people.
- **'Isa himself** — Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Ibn Zayd, and one of two reports from Sa'id bin Jubayr, who points to *then she pointed to him* (19:29). Ibn Jarir preferred this.

*Your Lord has provided a Sariy under you* — Al-Bara' bin 'Azib, Ibn Abbas, 'Amr bin Maymun, Sa'id bin Jubayr: **a small stream or river** (Mujahid: the word is Syriac). Others (Al-Hasan, Ar-Rabi', Ibn Zayd) took Sariy to mean 'Isa himself, but Ibn Kathir prefers the first — the next instruction is about food, so this one is about drink.

*Shake the trunk toward you* — 'Amr bin Maymun: **nothing is better for a woman in childbed than dried dates and fresh dates.** Then he recited this verse.

*So eat and drink and rejoice* — be happy.

### The vow of silence

*I have vowed a fast for the Most Gracious, so I shall not speak to any human being today* — she is to say this **by gesture**, not speech, so as not to break the very oath she is declaring. Anas bin Malik, Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak: a fast of silence. In their Law, fasting forbade both eating and speaking (As-Suddi, Qatadah, 'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd).

Ibn Zayd preserves a dialogue: when told *grieve not*, she answered — how can I not grieve, with you here and no husband, and no excuse to give people? And the reply came: **"I will suffice you with a statement."**

---

## 9. Carrying Him to Her People (19:27–29)

*O Maryam! Indeed you have brought a thing Fariy* — Fariy meaning **a mighty, enormous thing** (Mujahid, Qatadah, As-Suddi).

Nawf Al-Bikali preserves the search: her family went looking and found no trace, until they met a cowherd. Had he seen a girl of such-and-such description? No — but that night he had seen his cattle **prostrating toward that valley**. (Sayyar's version: he saw a radiant light.) They followed the direction and found her walking toward them; she sat down with the child in her lap, and they stood over her.

*O sister of Harun!* Two explanations:

- **A comparison** — "O one resembling Harun in worship," or in reference to a righteous man among them named Harun whom she matched in devotion.
- **A lineage** — Harun the brother of Musa, since she was of his descendants; the Arabs say "O brother of Tamim" to any Tamimi (Ali bin Abi Talhah, As-Suddi).

*Your father was not a man who used to commit adultery, nor was your mother unchaste* — meaning: you come from a house known for righteousness. How could you do this?

Then she pointed. As-Suddi records the fury: **"Her mocking us, by telling us to talk to a child, is worse to us than her fornication."** Maymun bin Mahran: they said she had come to them with a calamity and now had the nerve to send them to an infant.

---

## 10. The First Words of 'Isa (19:30–33)

Nawf Al-Bikali: he was nursing at her breast; at their accusation he released it, reclined on his left, and spoke.

**The first word out of his mouth is *'abd* — servant.** Ibn Kathir stops here: before anything else, 'Isa declared the loftiness of his Lord, His freedom from having a child, and his own status as a worshipper. *He has given me the Scripture and made me a Prophet* — which is simultaneously a declaration of **his mother's innocence**.

*And He has made me blessed wherever I be* — Mujahid, 'Amr bin Qays, Ath-Thawri: **a teacher of good**; in another report from Mujahid, a person of great benefit. Wuhayb bin Al-Ward, asked what the blessing was, answered: **commanding good and forbidding evil, wherever he was.**

*And enjoined on me Salah and Zakah as long as I live* — compare *worship your Lord until there comes to you the certainty* (15:99). Malik bin Anas draws a striking inference: Allah informed him of what his affairs would be until his death, which is **the firmest evidence against those who deny the divine decree**.

*And to be dutiful to my mother* — placed straight after obedience to the Lord, as it is in 17:23 and 31:14.

*And Salam be upon me the day I was born, and the day I die, and the day I shall be raised alive* — his own affirmation that he is a created servant who will live, die and be resurrected like everyone else, distinguished only by peace at those three points.

---

## 11. "Such Is 'Isa — A Statement of Truth" (19:34–37)

The recitations differ, and each shifts the emphasis:

- **Qawlul-Haqq** (most reciters) — the phrase describes **'Isa himself**: he is the statement of truth. Ibn Kathir finds this the most apparent grammatically, supported by 3:60.
- **Qawlal-Haqq** (Asim, 'Abdullah bin 'Amir) — it describes **the whole story** people dispute over.
- **Qalal-Haqqa** (Ibn Mas'ud) — **he spoke** the truth.

*It befits not Allah that He should beget a son* — when He decrees a thing He says *Be*, and it is. As with Adam, created from dust (3:59–60).

### The sects and what each said

Ibn Kathir lists the positions the People of the Book split into after the matter was made plain:

- He was a child of fornication and his speech in the cradle was sorcery — **the majority of the Jews**.
- It was Allah speaking, not 'Isa.
- He was the son of Allah.
- He was the third of a trinity.
- **He was the servant of Allah and His Messenger** — the statement of truth, which Allah guided the believers to.

### Why they are not punished immediately

*So woe to the disbelievers from the meeting of a great Day.* Allah grants respite out of gentleness and confidence in His own decree; He is in no hurry with those who disobey Him. The hadith: **Allah gives respite to the wrongdoer until He seizes him, and then he cannot escape** — followed by 11:102. And: **no one is more patient with something harmful He hears than Allah — they attribute a son to Him while He is the One providing them sustenance and health.**

And the promise on the other side, from 'Ubadah bin As-Samit (agreed upon): whoever testifies to Allah alone, to Muhammad as His servant and Messenger, and to **'Isa as Allah's servant and Messenger, His word cast upon Maryam and a spirit from Him**, and that Paradise and Hell are real — Allah will admit him to Paradise, whatever his deeds.

## 12. The Day of Grief and Regret (19:38–40)

*How clearly will they see and hear, the Day when they appear before Us!* — no one will hear or see better than they will, on the day it can no longer help (compare 32:12). Today, in this world, they neither hear nor see nor reflect.

### Death is slaughtered

Abu Sa'id's hadith (Ahmad, Al-Bukhari, Muslim): when the people of Paradise have entered Paradise and the people of Hell have entered Hell, **death is brought in the form of a handsome ram** and placed between them. Both sides are asked if they know what it is; both look and say yes, this is death. Then it is slaughtered, and it is announced: *O people of Paradise, eternity and no more death. O people of Hell, eternity and no more death.*

Then the Prophet ﷺ recited this verse, gestured with his hand, and said: **the people of this life are in the state of heedlessness of this life.**

Ibn Mas'ud adds the sharper grief: every soul will be shown **both** its residence in Paradise and its residence in Hell. To the people of Hell it will be said — this would have been yours, had you believed. To the people of Paradise — this would have been yours, had Allah not favoured you.

*Verily, We will inherit the earth and whatsoever is thereon.* Umar bin Abdul-Aziz wrote to his governor in Kufah citing exactly this: Allah prescribed death for His creatures when He created them, and He will inherit the earth and all who are on it.

---

## 13. Ibrahim and His Father (19:41–45)

The instruction to Muhammad ﷺ is pointed: recite this to your people who worship idols — **they are Ibrahim's descendants and claim to follow his religion.**

Four appeals, each opening with the same word: **"O my father."**

1. *Why do you worship what hears not, sees not, and cannot avail you in anything?* — these things cannot benefit you or protect you from harm.
2. *There has come to me knowledge that did not come to you* — meaning: I know you see me as your inferior because I am your son, but knowledge has reached me that has not reached you. *So follow me, I will guide you to a straight path* — one that reaches the goal and saves from what is dreaded.
3. *Worship not Shaytan* — meaning, do not obey him by worshipping these idols; he calls to this and is pleased by it (36:60; 4:117). He was a rebel against Ar-Rahman: obstinate, too arrogant to obey, and expelled for it. **Do not follow him, or you will become like him.**
4. *I fear a torment from the Most Gracious will touch you, so you become a companion of Shaytan* — no one to protect or help you but Iblis, who has power over nothing (16:63).

The tone is the point. Ibn Kathir keeps drawing attention to the gentleness of the address — a son arguing with a father he will not insult.

---

## 14. The Threat, and the Salam (19:46–48)

The father's answer: *Do you revile my gods, O Ibrahim? If you stop not, I will La'arjumannak* — I will stone, curse and revile you (Ibn Abbas, As-Suddi, Ibn Jurayj, Ad-Dahhak). *So get away from me Maliyan* — **forever** (Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, As-Suddi, Al-Hasan: for a long time), or **safely, before punishment reaches you from me** (Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, Malik; preferred by Ibn Jarir).

Ibrahim's reply is one word long before anything else: ***Peace be on you*** — meaning, "you will receive no insult or harm from me," out of the honour due to fatherhood. Compare 25:63 and 28:55.

*I will ask forgiveness of my Lord for you. Verily, He is unto me Hafiyya* — kind, attentive to my affair (Ibn Abbas, As-Suddi).

### And then that permission was withdrawn

Ibn Kathir traces the arc. Ibrahim kept seeking forgiveness for his father for years — after migrating to Ash-Sham, after building the Sacred House, after Isma'il and Ishaq were born (14:41). Early Muslims followed him in this, praying for their idolatrous relatives — **until 60:4 named that specific statement of Ibrahim's as the one exception not to be imitated**, and 9:113–114 explained that Ibrahim's prayer was because of a promise he had made, and that he dissociated himself once it became clear his father was an enemy of Allah.

*And I shall turn away from you and from what you invoke besides Allah… I hope I shall not be unblessed in my invocation to my Lord.* Ibn Kathir notes the word 'Asa (I hope) here means **a certainty**, not a wish against long odds — this is the leader of the Prophets after Muhammad ﷺ.

---

## 15. What He Got in Exchange (19:49–50)

He withdrew from his father and his people for Allah's sake, and Allah gave him better than what he left: **Ishaq, and Ishaq's son Ya'qub** — and made each of them a Prophet.

Ibn Kathir observes that Yusuf is not mentioned here, and infers from it that **Ya'qub was made a Prophet within Ibrahim's own lifetime** — otherwise the promise would have been extended further down the line. The hadith on the best of people: *the Prophet of Allah Yusuf, son of the Prophet of Allah Ya'qub, son of the Prophet of Allah Ishaq, son of the Friend of Allah Ibrahim* — in another wording, **the noble son of the noble son of the noble son of the noble.**

*And We granted them Sidqin 'Aliyyan on the tongues* — lofty, truthful praise. Ibn Jarir explains the word 'Aliyyan: **every religion commends them.**

---

## 16. Musa, Isma'il, Idris (19:51–57)

**Musa** — *Mukhlasan*, read two ways: **purely devoted** (Abu Lubabah's report has 'Isa define the Mukhlis as *one who acts for Allah and does not want people to praise him for it*), or **chosen** (7:144). He was both Messenger and Prophet, one of the five of Strong Will: Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa, Muhammad ﷺ. Called from the right side of the Mount — right *from his direction* as he approached the fire — and **brought near for a private address**.

And he was granted his brother Harun as a Prophet "out of Our mercy" — in answer to his pleading (28:34; 26:13–14; 20:36). Some of the Salaf said: **no one in this life ever interceded for another as Musa interceded for his brother to be made a Prophet.**

**Isma'il** — *true to what he promised*. Ibn Jurayj: he never made a promise to his Lord without fulfilling it; he never bound himself to an act of worship without carrying it out in full. It is also linked to his word to his father: *you will find me, if Allah wills, of the patient* (37:102) — and he was.

Ibn Kathir inverts the hadith of the hypocrite (*when he speaks he lies; when he promises he breaks it; when he is trusted he betrays*) to show that keeping one's word is a mark of true faith. He notes that the Prophet ﷺ was likewise never known to break a promise, and that **Abu Bakr honoured the Prophet's outstanding promises after his death** — filling Jabir's hands three times from the wealth of Bahrain, then doubling it.

Isma'il is described as **Messenger and Prophet**, where Ishaq is described only as Prophet — which Ibn Kathir takes as an indication of his favoured status, alongside the hadith in Sahih Muslim: *Allah chose Isma'il from the sons of Ibrahim.*

*And he would enjoin on his family and his people the Salah and the Zakah* — compare 20:132 and 66:6 (protect yourselves **and your families** from a Fire). The hadith: **may Allah have mercy on a man who rises at night to pray and wakes his wife — and if she refuses, sprinkles water on her face; and on a woman who does the same for her husband.**

**Idris** — a man of truth, a Prophet, *raised to a high station*. Mujahid: the fourth heaven, where the Prophet ﷺ passed him on the Night Journey. Al-Hasan and others: Paradise.

---

## 17. The Verse of Prostration (19:58)

*Those were they unto whom Allah bestowed His grace… of the offspring of Adam, of those We carried with Nuh, of the offspring of Ibrahim and Israel…*

As-Suddi and Ibn Jarir map the clauses:

| Phrase | Who is meant |
|---|---|
| Offspring of Adam | **Idris** |
| Those carried with Nuh | **Ibrahim** |
| Offspring of Ibrahim | **Ishaq, Ya'qub, Isma'il** |
| Offspring of Isma'il | **Musa, Harun, Zakariyya, Yahya, 'Isa** |

Ibn Jarir explains the split: Idris has to be listed separately because **he was Nuh's ancestor**, not his descendant — he could not be "of those carried in the ship." Ibn Kathir finds this the clearest reading. The whole verse works like 6:83–90, which ends with *They are those whom Allah has guided, so follow their guidance* — and Ibn Abbas' comment there: **your Prophet is among those commanded to follow them.**

*When the Ayat of the Most Gracious were recited to them, they fell down prostrate and weeping.* Bukiyyan is the plural of Baki — those weeping. And on this the scholars agree: **it is legislated to prostrate on reciting this verse**, in imitation of them.

---

## 18. Those Who Came After, and Lost the Prayer (19:59–63)

*Then there succeeded them a posterity who lost the Salah and followed lusts. So they will meet Ghayy.*

Ibn Kathir gives the strict reading first — that losing the prayer means not treating it as obligatory, which strips away the pillar and foundation of the religion — but then stacks up the reports that narrow it:

- **Al-Qasim bin Mukhaymirah:** this means not keeping to the proper times, because outright abandonment would be **disbelief**.
- **Ibn Mas'ud**, asked about this, cited 107:5, 70:23 and 23:9 and said it means *at their designated times*. When people said they had assumed it meant abandoning prayer altogether, he replied: **"That would be disbelief."**
- **Masruq:** no one who guards the five daily prayers is written among the heedless; the neglect meant here is **delaying them past their fixed times**.
- **Umar bin Abdul-Aziz**, reciting the verse: their loss was not abandonment, but not offering them in their prescribed times.

*Ghayy* — loss (Ibn Abbas), evil (Qatadah). Ibn Mas'ud: **a valley in Hell, very deep, whose food is filth**. Abu Iyad: a valley of pus and blood.

*Except those who repent* — and Ibn Kathir makes the exception generous. Repentance wipes out what came before; **"the one who repents from sin is like one who has no sin."** Those who repent lose nothing from their prior good deeds and are not held to account for what preceded, so their later reward is not diminished. He compares the identical structure in 25:68–70.

### The Gardens described

*'Adn Gardens which the Most Gracious has promised His servants in the unseen* — promised as part of the unseen they believe in without having witnessed it, out of the strength of their conviction.

*Verily, His promise must come to pass* — and Ibn Kathir notes the elegant ambiguity: it will come to them, and they are coming to it. As the Arabs say, *fifty years came to me, and I came to fifty years* — the same thing said two ways.

*They shall not hear therein any Laghw, but only Salam* (compare 56:25–26). *And they will have their sustenance morning and afternoon* — Ibn Abbas: intervals equivalent to night and day, not literal night and day.

And the hadith of the first group to enter (Ahmad, Al-Bukhari, Muslim): forms like the full moon; no spitting, no blowing of noses, no relieving themselves; vessels and combs of gold and silver; censers of aloes wood; sweat like musk; **no disputes and no hatred among them — their hearts united like the heart of one man**; glorifying Allah morning and evening. And the martyrs (Ahmad, from Ibn Abbas) on the banks of a river at the gates of Paradise, under a green dome, provided for morning and evening.

---

## 19. "And We Descend Not Except by the Command of Your Lord" (19:64–65)

Ahmad and Al-Bukhari record that the Prophet ﷺ said to Jibril: **"What prevents you from visiting us more than you do?"** — and this verse was revealed. Al-'Awfi's report from Ibn Abbas puts it the other way round: Jibril was kept from visiting, the Prophet ﷺ was grieved by it, and Jibril came with this verse as his answer.

*To Him belongs what is before us and what is behind us, and what is between those two.* The commentators split on whether the pair means this life and the Hereafter (with "between" as the two blasts of the Trumpet — Abu Al-'Aliyah, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Qatadah, As-Suddi, Ar-Rabi'), or the future and the past (with "between" as what falls between this life and the next — Ibn Abbas, Ad-Dahhak, Ibn Jurayj, Ath-Thawri; preferred by Ibn Jarir).

*And your Lord is never forgetful* — Mujahid, plainly: **"Your Lord has not forgotten you."**

*So worship Him and abide patiently in His worship. Do you know of any who is similar to Him?* — Ibn Abbas: do you know any equal or likeness to the Lord? And: **there is no one named Ar-Rahman other than Him.**

## 20. "Shall I Then Be Raised Up Alive?" (19:66–72)

Man finds his own resurrection far-fetched — the same astonishment answered in 13:5 and 36:77–79. The reply here is the simplest available argument: **He created you when you were nothing; the repetition is easier than the origination** (30:27).

The hadith Qudsi Ibn Kathir cites gives both halves of the offence at once:

> The son of Adam **denies** Me, and he has no right to. The son of Adam **harms** Me, and he has no right to. His denial is his saying that I will not repeat his creation as I created him the first time — yet the second creation is no harder on Me than the first. His harming Me is his saying that I have a son — yet I am the One, the Self-Sufficient, who neither begets nor was begotten.

*Surely We shall gather them, and the Shayatin, then bring them round Hell, Jithiyya* — kneeling or sitting (Ibn Abbas, comparing 45:28), or standing (As-Suddi, Ibn Mas'ud). *Then We shall drag out from every sect those worst in rebellion* — Ibn Mas'ud: the first will be bound to the last until the number is complete, and Allah will begin with **the greatest of them in crime** and work down (compare 7:38–39).

### Everyone passes over it

*There is not one of you but will pass over it; this is with your Lord, a decree which must be accomplished.*

Ibn Mas'ud describes the crossing: **the bridge over Hell is like the sharp edge of a sword.** The first group crosses like a flash of lightning; the second like the wind; the third like the fastest horse; the fourth like the fastest cow; and the rest pass while the angels say, *O Allah, save them, save them.*

Umm Mubashshar's exchange makes the verse's force clear. The Prophet ﷺ said **no one present at Badr or Hudaybiyyah will enter the Fire**. Hafsah objected with this very verse — *there is not one of you but will pass over it*. He answered by reciting the next verse: ***Then We shall save those who had Taqwa.***

Ibn Zayd distinguishes the two passages: for the Muslims it is crossing the bridge above it; for the idolaters it is entry. Then the intercession — of angels, Prophets and believers — brings out those with faith in their hearts, in descending order: first the one with a Dinar's weight of faith, then less, then less, down to **the tiniest atom's weight**, and finally whoever said *La ilaha illallah* even once in his life, though he never did a good deed. After that, only those obliged to remain forever will remain.

## 21. "Which of Us Has the Better Meeting-Place?" (19:73–76)

The disbelievers' argument was an argument from **furniture**: better houses, better assembly rooms, better attended gatherings. *How can we be on falsehood while we live like this?*

Ibn Kathir names the comparison they were drawing: they were saying it of people **hiding in the house of Al-Arqam bin Abi Al-Arqam** and places like it. The same reasoning appears in 46:11 (*had it been good, they would not have beaten us to it*), in Nuh's people at 26:111 (*shall we believe you, when the weakest follow you?*), and in 6:53.

The reply is historical: *how many a generation have We destroyed before them, who were better in wealth, goods and outward appearance* — Ibn Abbas glosses Maqam as dwelling and Nadi as the place of gathering, citing Fir'awn's people (44:25–26) and the people of Lut, who *practised evil in their meeting places* (29:29).

*Say: whoever is in error, the Most Gracious will extend it for him* — respite, until they see either the torment or the Hour, **and then find out who was worse in position and weaker in forces.** Ibn Kathir sets this alongside the challenges Allah issued elsewhere and that were declined: the Jews invited to long for death if they were truly Allah's friends (62:6), and the Christians invited to mutual imprecation over 'Isa (3:61). **Both refused.**

*And Allah increases in guidance those who walk aright* — placed deliberately opposite the respite given to the misguided: the one group is extended in error, the other increased in guidance. And *the righteous deeds that last are better with your Lord for reward and better for resort* — the reward, and the final outcome.

## 22. The Man Who Expected Wealth in the Hereafter (19:77–80)

Khabbab bin Al-Aratt was a blacksmith in Makkah. He made a sword for **Al-'As bin Wa'il** and went to collect his pay. Al-'As refused: *not until you disbelieve in Muhammad.* Khabbab answered: *not until you die and are resurrected.* Al-'As said — fine: **when I am dead and resurrected and you come to me, I will have wealth and children there too, and I will pay you then.**

These verses were revealed about that conversation.

*Has he known the Unseen, or taken a covenant from the Most Gracious?* — Al-Bukhari's gloss on covenant: **an agreement.** Does he know what awaits him well enough to swear to it?

*Nay, We shall record what he says* — his claim, the fantasy he has told himself, and his disbelief — *and We shall increase his torment. And We shall inherit from him all that he speaks of, and he shall come to Us alone.* Ibn Kathir sharpens the inversion: he expects to arrive with **more** wealth and children than he had here; in fact what he had here will be taken from him, and **he will arrive with nothing.**

## 23. The Gods Will Turn on Them (19:81–84)

They took gods besides Allah *that they might give them honour, power and glory* — imagining these things would make them mighty and give them victory.

*Nay — they will deny their worship of them, and will become their adversaries.* As-Suddi: severe opposition and dispute. Ad-Dahhak: enemies. Compare 46:5–6: on the Day of Gathering these objects of worship become enemies and disown the worship offered them.

*See you not that We have sent the Shayatin against the disbelievers to push them?* — Ibn Abbas: they lead them astray with temptation; in another report, they incite them against Muhammad ﷺ and his Companions. Qatadah: they harass and disturb them until they disobey Allah. Ibn Zayd cites 43:36 — whoever turns blindly from the remembrance of Ar-Rahman gets a devil appointed as his companion.

*So make no haste against them; We only count out to them a number.* As-Suddi: **years, months, days and hours.** Ibn Kathir gathers the parallel verses of respite: 14:42, 86:17, 3:178, 31:24, and *Say: Enjoy! But your destination is the Fire* (14:30).

## 24. Two Arrivals (19:85–87)

*The Day We shall gather those with Taqwa unto the Most Gracious, like a delegation — and drive the criminals to Hell, thirsty.*

**Wafd** is a party that arrives *riding*. Ibn Abbas glosses it in one word: riding. They will come on noble mounts of light, arriving before the Best Receiver of delegations, at the abode of His honour and pleasure. The criminals are driven the other way, *parched and thirsting for drink* ('Ata', Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah).

And Ibn Kathir notes where that leaves 19:73: **the question of who has the better position and the finer meeting-place gets its answer right here.**

Ibn Marzuq's narration is the image the section turns on. When the believer comes out of his grave he meets the most beautiful form he has ever seen, with the finest fragrance.

> *"Who are you?"*
> *"You do not know me?"*
> *"No — but Allah has made you sweet-smelling and handsome."*
> *"I am your righteous deeds. This is how you used to perfume and beautify your deeds in the world. I rode upon you the whole length of your life — will you not ride upon me now?"*

*None shall have the power of intercession, but one who has received a covenant from the Most Gracious* — and the covenant, per Ibn Abbas, is **the testimony that none has the right to be worshipped but Allah**, that all power and strength belong to Him, and that one places hope in Him alone.

## 25. The Heavens Almost Tear (19:88–95)

*And they say: the Most Gracious has begotten a son. Indeed you have brought forth a thing Idda* — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Malik: **terrible**.

*Whereby the heavens are almost torn, the earth split asunder, and the mountains Hadda* — Ibn Abbas: torn down. Sa'id bin Jubayr: broken piece against piece in succession. 'Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd on the earth splitting: **out of its anger on behalf of Allah.**

Ibn Kathir's explanation of *why* creation reacts this way is the heart of the passage: these are Allah's creatures, and they are **established upon His Tawhid**. Ibn Abbas: the heavens, the earth, the mountains and every creature except humans and jinn are terrified by the associating of partners with Allah — creation nearly ceases to exist at it. And, Ibn Abbas adds hopefully: **just as the idolater gains nothing from his good deeds because of his shirk, we hope Allah forgives the sins of those who worshipped Him alone.**

Two hadiths anchor it:

- **"Encourage your dying to say La ilaha illallah"** — whoever says it at death enters Paradise. Asked about one who says it in good health: *that necessitates his entrance even more.* And: if the heavens and the earths and all between and beneath them were placed in one pan of a scale, and this testimony in the other, **the testimony would outweigh them.**
- **"There is no one more patient than Allah about something harmful He hears"** — partners are ascribed to Him and a son attributed to Him, **while He is the One giving them health and sustenance.**

*There is none in the heavens and the earth but comes to the Most Gracious as a slave. He knows each one of them, and has counted them a full counting* — He has known their number since He created them, male and female, small and great. *And every one of them will come to Him alone on the Day of Resurrection* — with no helper and no rescuer, before a Judge who does not wrong anyone by an atom's weight.

## 26. Love Placed in the Hearts (19:96–98)

*Verily, those who believe and work righteous deeds, the Most Gracious will bestow love for them.*

Ibn Kathir treats this as a **guarantee, not a hope**: Allah plants love for such people in the hearts of His righteous servants, and there is no avoiding it. The hadith (Ahmad, Al-Bukhari, Muslim) shows the mechanism:

> When Allah loves a servant, He calls Jibril: *I love so-and-so, so love him.* Jibril loves him. Then Jibril calls out to the dwellers of the heavens: *Allah loves so-and-so, so love him.* They love him — **and then acceptance is placed for him on the earth.**

And the same chain runs in the opposite direction for one whom Allah hates.

*So We have made this easy in your own tongue* — the Qur'an, in pure and eloquent Arabic — *that you may give glad tidings to those with Taqwa, and warn with it a people who are Ludda*: those bent away from truth and inclined toward falsehood, given to quarrel.

The final verse closes the surah the way the argument about mansions and meeting-halls deserved to be closed:

*And how many a generation before them have We destroyed! **Can you find a single one of them, or hear even a whisper of them?*** — Ibn Abbas, Abu Al-'Aliyah, Ikrimah, Al-Hasan, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak and Ibn Zayd: any sound at all. Al-Hasan and Qatadah: do you see one with your eye, or hear one with your ear?

## The Thread

Maryam is the surah of **Ar-Rahman**. The name appears here more than anywhere else in the Qur'an, and it is doing argumentative work every time. Maryam repels a stranger by invoking it. Ibrahim warns his father of a torment from it. The rebellious are given respite by it. And the surah's last movement is the charge that Ar-Rahman took a son — the one claim at which the sky nearly splits.

Underneath that runs a gallery of **fathers and sons**, arranged so the final argument lands. An old man begs for a son and is told the child will inherit prophethood, not property. A woman is given a son with no father at all, and the first word he speaks is *'abd* — servant. A son pleads with an idolatrous father, is threatened with stoning, answers *peace be on you*, and is given sons and grandsons in exchange for the one he left. Musa begs for a brother. Isma'il, who kept every promise, is told to command prayer to his own family.

Then the surah turns and asks what the objectors actually have. Better houses. Fuller meeting rooms. A man who thinks he will be given wealth and children in the Hereafter, and is told he will arrive alone. Gods taken for honour who will turn and testify against them. And the verdict is delivered in the form of two arrivals: one party riding in as a **delegation**, mounted on their own deeds, and one driven in thirsty.

The last verse simply asks where the previous owners of the fine houses went — **can you find one of them, or hear even a whisper?**

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Maryam. All praises and thanks are due to Allah.*
