*Revealed in Makkah · 96 ayat · named for "the Event" — one of the names of the Day of Resurrection, because that Day is real and will surely come*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Waqi'ah is one of the five surahs the Prophet ﷺ named when Abu Bakr remarked that he was turning gray: *"Hud, Al-Waqi'ah, Al-Mursalat, `Amma Yatasa'alun and Idha Ash-Shamsu Kuwwirat have made me gray."* (At-Tirmidhi: Hasan Gharib.) Every one of them is a surah about the end of everything.

The surah does one thing with tremendous force: it **sorts humanity into three groups and refuses to allow a fourth**. Those foremost and nearest to Allah; those on the right; those on the left. It announces this division at 56:7, spends seventy ayat describing what each group receives, and then — this is the structural key Ibn Kathir points out — **returns to the same three groups at the very end (56:88–94), applied to the moment of death**. The sorting that happens at Resurrection has already happened by the time your soul reaches your throat.

In between sits a long argument with the man who says *"When we die and become dust and bones, shall we then indeed be resurrected?"* — answered not with philosophy but with four things he touches every day: **his own semen, his crops, his drinking water, and his cooking fire.** Each one is put as a question: *did you make this?*

And the surah's opening line about the Event tells you what it does to people: **it brings some low and lifts others high** — the mighty of this world to the lowest part of the Fire, the weak of this world to the highest grade of eternal delight.

---

## 1. The Day That Cannot Be Called Back (56:1–3)

> *When the Waqi'ah occurs, there is not, for its occurrence, Kadhibah. Bringing low, exalting.*

**Al-Waqi'ah** is a name of the Day of Resurrection, given because that Day *is real and will surely come.*

**Kadhibah** — Muhammad bin Ka'b: "It will certainly occur." Qatadah: "It shall not be discontinued, taken back or aborted." Once the command is given, nothing stops it, nothing prevents it, nothing averts it:

- *Answer the call of your Lord before there comes from Allah a Day which cannot be averted* (42:47)
- *A questioner asked concerning a torment about to occur — which none can avert* (70:1–2)

**Khafidatun Rafi'ah** — Al-Hasan and Qatadah: it **lowers** people to the lowest depths of the Fire though they were mighty in this life, and **raises** others to the highest grades of eternal delight though they were weak in this life. A second reading from Ibn Abbas and Ikrimah plays on sound rather than rank: *it lowered, so those nearby heard it; it rose, so those far away heard it too.*

---

## 2. The Earth Sifted, the Mountains Made Into Dust (56:4–6)

The earth is shaken violently across its whole surface and through its depths. **Ar-Rabi' bin Anas**: shaken with everything in it, *just as a sifter is shaken with its contents.*

The mountains are relentlessly pulverized. **Ibn Zayd**: they become what Allah described elsewhere — *a heap of sand poured out* (73:14).

Then *floating dust particles* (**haba'an munbaththa**):
- **Ali** (via Al-Harith): like a rising dust storm that fades and leaves no trace.
- **Ibn Abbas**: like the embers rising from a kindled fire, extinguished the moment they land.
- **Ikrimah**: dust the wind scatters everywhere. **Qatadah**: like the dry parts of trees blown about.

The mountains — the most permanent objects a human being ever sees — end as something the wind carries.

---

## 3. Three Groups, No Fourth (56:7–12)

> *And you (all) will be in three groups.*

Ibn Kathir lays them out:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Those on the right** | Brought forth from Adam's right side; given their records in the right hand; taken to the right. **As-Suddi**: they are the majority of the people of Paradise. |
| **Those on the left** | Brought forth from Adam's left side; given their records in the left hand; taken to the left. The residents of the Fire — *may Allah save us from their actions.* |
| **The foremost (As-Sabiqun)** | The nearest to Allah. Higher in grade and status than those on the right; they are the **chiefs** of those on the right, and include the Messengers, the Prophets, the truthful and the martyrs. **Fewer in number.** |

The threefold division is echoed in *Then We gave the Book as inheritance to such of Our servants whom We chose: of them are some who wrong themselves, some who follow a middle course, and some who are, by Allah's leave, foremost in good deeds* (35:32).

**What "foremost" actually means:** they were foremost in *performing acts of righteousness*, as commanded — *march forth in the way to forgiveness* (3:133), *race with one another towards forgiveness* (57:21). Ibn Kathir's rule: **the reward is according to the kind of deed.** Those who rushed to obey here are the ones honoured foremost there.

---

## 4. "A Multitude From the First, a Few From the Later" — A Disagreement Ibn Kathir Settles (56:13–14)

This is one of the sharpest interpretive moments in the surah, and worth following closely.

**Ibn Jarir's view:** "the first ones" means **earlier nations**, and "the later" means **this Ummah**. He supports it with the hadith *"We are the later nation, but the foremost on the Day of Resurrection,"* and with a report from Abu Hurayrah that these ayat **distressed the Companions** when revealed, until the Prophet ﷺ consoled them: *"I hope you will be a quarter of the residents of Paradise — a third — rather, you are half of the residents of Paradise, and will have a share in the other half."*

**Ibn Kathir rejects this**, and does so bluntly: it is *questionable, rather it is a deficient interpretation.* His reason is textual — **this Ummah is the best of all nations by the text of the Qur'an**, so it is not possible that earlier nations produced more foremost believers than this one. The opposite is true.

**The correct view:** both phrases refer to **this Ummah** — a multitude of its *earlier generations*, and few from its *later generations*. This is the reading of **Al-Hasan Al-Basri** and **Muhammad bin Sirin**, who said the Companions "stated, or hoped, that they will all be from this Ummah."

Ibn Kathir then generalises the principle: **the earlier generations of every nation were better than the later ones** — confirmed by *"The best people are my generation, then the next, then the next"* — while noting the parallel guarantee that *"a group of my Ummah will always remain on the truth and dominant... until the Last Hour begins."*

## 5. What the Foremost Receive (56:15–26)

**Thrones (surur mawdunah)** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Zayd bin Aslam, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak: **woven with gold**.

**Reclining, face to face** — meaning **none of them is in the back row**. Nobody in Paradise sits behind anyone.

**Immortal boys** who never grow up, never age, never change in form.

**The wine.** Cups, jugs, and a glass drawn *from a flowing spring, not from containers that might get empty* — the spring flows freely and cannot run out. And then the crucial clause: *neither Yusadda'un nor Yunzifun* — **no headache and no intoxication**, though the taste is strong and tremendously delightful.

**Ad-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas**, listing what worldly wine does — it intoxicates, gives headaches, induces vomiting, causes excessive urination — *"So Allah mentioned the wine of Paradise free of these characteristics."*

**Fruit that they may choose** — Ibn Kathir reads this as a proof that in Paradise **you select what you want**, rather than being served.

**The flesh of fowls that they desire** — with the hadith from Anas: *"Birds of Paradise are like Bukht camels that graze in the trees of Paradise."* Abu Bakr: "Surely, these birds must be wonderful!" The Prophet ﷺ, three times: **"Those who eat them are more wonderful."** Then: *"And I hope that you will be among those who eat from them."*

**Hur with wide lovely eyes, like preserved pearls** — as white and pure as fresh pearls; compare *as if they were eggs preserved* (37:49).

**And the reason for all of it:** *A reward for what they used to do.* Not a windfall. Wages.

**The soundtrack of Paradise:** *No Laghw will they hear therein, nor any sinful speech — but only: "Salaman! Salaman!"* No vain talk, no foul word, nothing needless. Only greetings of peace — *Their greeting therein will be: "Salaman!"* (14:23).

### The woman's dream

A striking narration Ibn Kathir includes here, from Anas: the Prophet ﷺ loved dreams. A woman told him she dreamt she was taken from Al-Madinah into Paradise, heard a noise *because of which Paradise wept*, and saw twelve men — she named them — whom the Prophet ﷺ had sent on an expedition, **brought in with their wounds still bleeding**. They were submerged in a river called Baydakh, their faces turned as radiant as the full moon, and they were brought a golden plate of green dates which replenished each time it was turned. She ate with them.

Later an emissary arrived from the army and named the dead: **the same twelve men.** The Prophet ﷺ called the woman and had her tell the story again. (Ad-Diya': meets the criteria of Muslim.)

## 6. Those on the Right (56:27–40)

**Maymun bin Mihran**: those on the right are **lesser in rank** than the foremost. The surah asks the question — *who are they, what is their condition?* — and then answers it.

**Sidr Makhdud** — the lote tree. Two readings, and Ibn Kathir takes both: **thornless** (Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah and many others) and **laden with fruit**. In this life the lote tree is thorny and carries little; in the Hereafter it is the reverse — no thorns, and fruit heavy enough to strain the trunk.

A Bedouin once asked about the tree in Paradise with the most thorns of anything — meaning the Talh. The Prophet ﷺ answered: **for each place where a thorn was, Allah put fruit instead**, each fruit yielding seventy colours, no two the same.

**Talh Mandud** — a large thorny shrub of the Hijaz; **Mujahid**: its fruits piled one on another. Allah is reminding the Quraysh of the trees whose shade they loved. **Abu Sa'id, Ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayrah, Al-Hasan, Ikrimah, Qatadah, Ibn Zayd**: the **banana tree** — "the people of Yemen call the banana tree Talh."

**Shade extended** — Al-Bukhari and Muslim, from Abu Hurayrah: *"In Paradise there is a tree that a rider can travel a hundred years under its shade and not pass it. Recite if you will: 'And in shade extended.'"*

**Water flowing constantly. Fruit in plenty, whose supply is not cut off nor out of reach** — never seasonal, never exhausted, never blocked by branch, thorn or distance (Qatadah); and whenever a fruit is taken, another grows in its place. The taste is new even when the shape is familiar — *"This is what we were provided with before," and they will be given things in resemblance* (2:25).

The eclipse hadith from Ibn Abbas: the Prophet ﷺ reached out during the prayer and then stepped back. *"I saw Paradise and reached out for a cluster of fruit from it. Had I done so, you would have kept eating from it for the remainder of the life of the world."*

And the long Bedouin dialogue about **Tuba**, the tree of Paradise — like a walnut tree of Ash-Sham, one stem with branches spreading to the fullest extent; a cluster of its fruit taking **a crow a month's unresting flight** to cross; a trunk so vast a four-year-old she-camel would die of old age before circling it; a single grape enough for a man, his family, **and his whole clan**.

### The women of Paradise (56:35–38)

> *Verily, We have created them a special creation. And made them virgins. `Urub, Atrab. For those on the right.*

Ibn Kathir notes a grammatical subtlety: the ayat describe women who were never directly mentioned — **the mention of the couches implies them** (as *"till it was hidden in the veil"* in 38:32 refers to the sun without naming it). Abu Ubaydah traces the reference back to the *Hur* of 56:22.

The meaning: **in the other life, after they had grown old in this one, they are brought back young** — virgin, delightfully passionate with their husbands, beautiful, kind and cheerful.

- **`Urub** — Ibn Abbas (via Sa'id bin Jubayr): infatuated with their husbands. Via Ad-Dahhak: *"they are their husbands' sweethearts, and their husbands are theirs."*
- **Atrab** — Ibn Abbas: **all of one age, thirty-three years old**. Mujahid: equal; Atiyah: comparative.

And the hadith of Abu Hurayrah on the first group to enter Paradise: **shining like the full moon**, the next like the most radiant planet; no urine, no waste, no spit; combs of gold, sweat of musk, censers of aloes; wives from Al-Hur Al-`Ayn; all in the shape of their father Adam, **sixty cubits high**.

### Both multitudes (56:39–40)

Of *those on the right*, unlike the foremost: **a multitude from the earlier generations AND a multitude from the later ones.**

Ibn Kathir attaches the great hadith of Ibn Mas'ud here — the night the Prophet ﷺ was shown the nations. Prophets passed with a few followers; one passed **with nobody at all** (Qatadah recited *Is there not among you a single right-minded man?*, 11:78). Then Musa passed with a great crowd of the Children of Israel. The Prophet ﷺ asked, *"O Lord, where is my Ummah?"* — and was shown faces filling the hill to his right, and faces filling the horizon to his left, and told: *"With them are seventy thousand who will enter Paradise without reckoning."*

Ukkashah bin Mihsan asked to be one of them and the Prophet ﷺ prayed for him; a second man asked and was told, **"Ukkashah beat you to it."**

Then the Prophet ﷺ said: *"May I sacrifice my father and mother for you — try to be among the seventy thousand, or among the crowds on the right, or the crowds covering the horizon."* And: *I hope you will be a quarter... a third... half of the people of Paradise* — each time the Companions said **Allahu Akbar** — before reciting *A multitude from the first, and a multitude from the later.*

Who are the seventy thousand? The Companions guessed: those born into Islam who never associated partners with Allah. The Prophet ﷺ corrected them:

> **"They are the people who do not get themselves cauterized, do not ask for Ruqyah, and do not draw evil omens — but put their trust only in their Lord."**

## 7. Those on the Left (56:41–56)

Against the shade, the water, the wine and the fruit, the surah sets four things:

- **Samum** — a fierce hot wind
- **Hamim** — boiling water
- **Yahmum** — the shade of **black smoke** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatadah, As-Suddi); compare *a shadow in three columns, neither shady nor of any use against the fierce flame* (77:30–31)
- **Neither Barid nor Karim** — no cool breeze, and nothing pleasant to look at. Ad-Dahhak: *"Every drink that is not fresh, is not Karim."*

**Why they are there** — the surah gives three reasons, and they are worth sitting with because they are ordinary:

1. ***Before that, they indulged in luxury*** — they enjoyed life's pleasures and satisfied their lusts **while ignoring what the Messengers brought them**.
2. ***They persisted in great sin*** — persisted with **no intention to repent**. "Great sin" here is **shirk** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, As-Suddi).
3. ***They said: "When we die and become dust and bones, shall we then be resurrected? And also our forefathers?"*** — denial of the Return.

The answer: *Say — those of old and those of later times will all be gathered for the appointed meeting of a **known Day***. Ibn Kathir stresses **known**: precisely designated, arriving neither early nor late, neither increased nor decreased. *That is a Day whereon mankind will be gathered together... And We delay it only for a term fixed* (11:103–105).

Their food: **Zaqqum** until their bellies are full, then **Hamim** on top of it, drunk *like Al-Him*. Al-Him is the **thirsty camel** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ikrimah) — or, per **As-Suddi**, a **disease** that strikes camels and makes them drink until they die. Either way the point stands: **they will never quench their thirst.**

*That will be their **entertainment** on the Day of Recompense* — the same word used of the believers, *Gardens of Al-Firdaws for their entertainment* (18:107), where it means hospitality and honour. Here it is the hospitality of the host they chose.

## 8. The Four Everyday Proofs of Resurrection (56:57–74)

The surah stops arguing and starts pointing at things.

**One — you.** *We created you, then why do you believe not?* You were nothing. Is the One able to start the creation not more able to bring it back? *Do you not see the semen you emit? Is it you who create it, or are We the Creator?* Do *you* keep it in the womb and take it stage by stage?

And: *We have decreed death to you all, and We are not outstripped* — **Ad-Dahhak**: Allah made the residents of the heavens and the earth **equal with regard to death**. Nor is He unable *to transfigure you and create you in forms that you know not.*

*And indeed you have already known the first form of creation, why then do you not remember?* Reinforced with *He originates creation then repeats it, and this is easier for Him* (30:27); *Does not man remember that We created him before, while he was nothing?* (19:67); the man who forgets his own creation and asks who will revive rotten bones (36:77–79); and *Was he not a Nutfah of semen emitted?* (75:36–40).

**Two — your crops.** *Do you not see what you sow? Is it you that make it grow, or are We the Grower?*

The hadith Ibn Jarir records from Abu Hurayrah is the whole point in one line: **"Do not say 'Zara'tu' (I made it grow) — say 'Harathtu' (I tilled)."** Abu Hurayrah then recited this very ayah.

*Were it Our will, We could crumble it to dry pieces, and you would be **Tafakkahun*** — a wonderfully ambiguous word. **Mughramun** = ruined, or (Mujahid, Ikrimah) subject to revenge. **Tafakkahun**: Ikrimah — you would blame each other; Al-Hasan, Qatadah, As-Suddi — you would **feel grief**, for what you spent and for the sins that cost you the harvest. **Al-Kisa'i**: *Tafakkaha is both the synonym and the antonym* — the Arabs use it for enjoying something **and** for grieving.

**Three — your water.** *Is it you who cause it to come down from Al-Muzn* — the clouds — *or are We?* ***If We willed, We verily could make it salty*** — undrinkable, useless for crops — *why then do you not give thanks?* (16:10–11).

**Four — your fire.** *Is it you who made the tree thereof to grow, or are We the Grower?* The Arabs kindled fire by rubbing green branches of **Al-Markh** and **Al-`Afar** together.

And then Allah calls that fire **a Reminder**:

> *"O people, this fire of yours that you kindle is but **one part out of seventy** parts of the fire of Hell."*
> They said, "O Messenger of Allah, this fire alone is sufficiently hot!"
> *"It was submerged in the water twice, so that the children of Adam would be able to benefit from it and draw close to it."*

In another wording: *had it not been struck twice against the sea, Allah would not have made benefit in it for anyone* — and elsewhere, **the fire of Hell was made sixty-nine times hotter**.

**"An article of use for the Muqwin"** — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak and Ibn Jarir: **travellers** (from *aqwat ad-dar*, a house emptied when its people leave). Abdur-Rahman bin Zayd: **the hungry**. Mujahid and Ikrimah, more broadly: **everyone who benefits from cooked food.** Ibn Kathir prefers the wider sense, and adds a lovely observation — Allah placed the capacity to make fire inside **stones**, portable things a traveller can carry in his baggage, so that at a lonely campsite he can strike a flame for warmth, food and comfort. The favour is named for travellers even though everyone shares it.

*Then glorify with praises the Name of your Lord, the Most Great* — the One by whose power **opposites** were made: fresh water that could have been brine, and a fire that burns which is also the servant's livelihood here and a warning of punishment hereafter.

## 9. The Oath by the Setting-Places of the Stars (56:75–82)

> *Fala! I swear by the Mawaqi' of the stars — and verily that is indeed a great oath, if you but know.*

**On "Fala":** Ibn Kathir rejects the idea that the *La* is a meaningless extra particle. It is used **at the start of an oath when the oath is a negation** — as A'ishah said, *"La, by Allah! Allah's Messenger never touched any woman's hand."* So the sense is: **"No! I swear by the setting-places of the stars — the matter is not as you claim. This Qur'an is not magic or sorcery. It is an honourable Qur'an."**

**Mawaqi'** — Mujahid, Al-Hasan, Qatadah, and Ibn Jarir's preference: the **setting and rising positions of the stars** in the sky.

*If you but know* the greatness of the vow, you would know the greatness of what is being sworn to.

**"In a Book Maknun — which none touches but the pure ones."** Ibn Kathir gathers the readings:

- **Ibn Abbas**: the Book **that is in heaven**.
- **Ibn Abbas (via Al-`Awfi), Anas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi and others**: "the pure ones" = **the angels**.
- **Qatadah**: none touch it *with Allah* except the pure — though in this world "the impure Zoroastrian and the filthy hypocrite" handle it. He notes Ibn Mas'ud's recitation: *It is not touched, except by the pure ones.*
- **Abu Al-Aliyah**, pointedly: *"It does not refer to you, because you are sinners!"*
- **Ibn Zayd**: the Quraysh claimed devils brought the Qur'an down; Allah replies that only the pure touch it — *And it is not the Shayatin who have brought it down... verily they have been removed far from hearing it* (26:210–212). Ibn Kathir calls this "a good saying" that **does not contradict** the others.

**"And you make your provision your denial!"** (56:82) — one of the most arresting lines in the surah.

Ali bin Abi Talhah reports that **Ibn Abbas recited it as** *"and you make your **gratitude** your denial."* And Sa'id bin Jubayr reports Ibn Abbas explaining exactly what that means:

> **"It has never rained upon a people except that some of them became disbelievers by saying: 'Such and such a star sent the rain!'"**

Ibn Kathir seals this with the Hudaybiyah hadith from Malik's *Muwatta'* (and the Two Sahihs). After a rainy night the Prophet ﷺ led Fajr, turned to the people and asked, *"Do you know what your Lord has said?"* Allah said:

> *"This morning some of My servants remained believers in Me and some became disbelievers. Whoever said the rain came by the blessing and mercy of Allah — he believes in Me and disbelieves in the stars. And whoever said it rained because of such-and-such a star — he disbelieves in Me and believes in that star."*

**Al-Hasan Al-Basri's summary:** *"How evil is it, that all some people have earned for themselves from the Book of Allah is denying it."*

## 10. The Soul at the Throat (56:83–87)

> *Then why do you not intervene when it reaches Al-Hulqum — and you at that moment are looking — but We are nearer to him than you, though you see not.*

The dying man's family is *right there*, watching the stupor of death, and can do **nothing**. Allah is nearer to him than they are — **by His angels**, whom they cannot see. Compare *He sends guardians over you, until when death approaches one of you, Our messengers take his soul, and they never neglect their duty* (6:61–62), and *Nay, when the soul reaches the collar bone, and it is said: "Who can cure him?"* (75:26–30).

Then the challenge that ends the argument of the whole surah:

> *Then why do you not — if you are not **Madinin** — return the soul, if you are truthful?*

**Sa'id bin Jubayr and Al-Hasan Al-Basri**: *if you do not believe you will be reckoned, recompensed, resurrected and punished — then put this soul back in its body.* **Mujahid**: "if you are not certain."

Nobody has ever done it. That inability, repeated at every deathbed in history, is the surah's final proof.

## 11. The Three Groups, Again — at the Moment of Death (56:88–96)

The surah closes by folding back onto its own opening. The same three categories, now applied to the instant a person dies.

**1. If he is of the Muqarrabun** — those who fulfilled the obligations *and* the recommended acts, avoided the forbidden *and* the disliked, and even some of the permitted — then: ***Rawh, Rayhan, and a Garden of Delights.***

The angels of mercy announce it at the moment of death: *"O good soul in the good body you inhabited — come to Rawh, Rayhan, and a Lord who is not angry."*

The scholars' glosses stack rather than compete: **Ibn Abbas** — Rawh is rest, Rayhan is the place of rest; **Abu Hazrah** — rest *from the world*; **Sa'id bin Jubayr and As-Suddi** — to rejoice; **Qatadah** — mercy; **Mujahid** — Paradise and delights; **Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Sa'id bin Jubayr** — Rayhan means provisions. Ibn Kathir: **all of these are correct and similar in meaning.**

**Abu Al-Aliyah**: *"None of the near believers departs this life until he is brought a branch of the Rayhan of Paradise, and his soul is captured in it."*
**Muhammad bin Ka'b**: *"Every person who dies knows upon his death whether he is of the people of Paradise or the people of the Fire."*

And of those already there — the martyrs: *"Their souls live in the bodies of green birds, flying wherever they wish in the Gardens of Paradise, then returning to nests in chandeliers hung from the Throne."*

**2. If he is of those on the right** — *Then Salam to you, from those on the right.* **Ikrimah**: the angels greet him with Salam and give him the news that he is among them. Be calm; you are being led to safety. This matches *those who say "Our Lord is Allah" and then stand firm — the angels descend on them: fear not, nor grieve, but receive the glad tidings of Paradise which you were promised* (41:30–32).

**3. If he is of the denying, the erring** — *an entertainment with **Hamim*** that dissolves his intestines and skin, *and entry into Hellfire*, which engulfs him from every direction.

### The hadith that ties it together

`Abdur-Rahman bin Abi Layla, riding his donkey behind a funeral, narrated the Prophet's ﷺ words:

> **"He who loves to meet Allah, Allah loves to meet him; and he who hates to meet Allah, Allah hates to meet him."**

The people around him wept and said, *"But all of us hate death."* He answered: **"It does not mean that."** It means the moment of dying — and then he recited these very ayat. When the believer is given the news of *Rawh, Rayhan and a Garden of Delights*, **he loves to meet Allah, and Allah loves to meet him even more**. When the denier is given the news of *Hamim and Hellfire*, he hates to meet Allah, and Allah hates to meet him even more.

*Verily, this! This is an absolute truth with certainty* — **no doubt about it, and no escape from it for anyone.**

### And so: *Glorify with praises the Name of your Lord, the Most Great* (56:96)

The surah ends on the same command it gave at 56:74 — and Ibn Kathir ends the tafsir with the two hadiths that put it into practice:

- **Jabir**: *"Whoever says 'Subhan Allah al-`Azim wa bihamdih' — a date tree is planted for him in Paradise."* (At-Tirmidhi: Hasan Gharib)
- **Abu Hurayrah, in Al-Bukhari**: *"Two statements light on the tongue, heavy on the Balance, most beloved to Ar-Rahman: **Subhan Allah wa bihamdih, Subhan Allah al-`Azim.**"*

Ninety-six ayat of cosmic upheaval, and the response asked of you costs about two seconds.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**There are three kinds of people, and you are already one of them.** The surah opens by sorting humanity into three and closes by sorting the dying into the same three — which means the verdict announced on the Day of Resurrection is not a surprise handed down later. It is read out to you at the throat.

**What separates them is not what they possessed but what they raced toward.** The Event *brings low and exalts* — the mighty go down, the weak go up. The foremost are foremost because they were **foremost in doing**, and the reward is always according to the kind of deed.

**And the argument against denial is not clever — it is domestic.** Your own conception. The seed you did not make grow — *say "I tilled," not "I made it grow."* The rain that could have been brine. The fire that is one-seventieth of a fire you cannot survive. Four things touched daily, each of them asking the same question: *did you do this?*

The failure the surah names is not atheism. It is **crediting the wrong cause** — thanking the star instead of the One who sent the rain, and making your very provision the occasion of your denial.

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Al-Waqi'ah. All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and all the favours come from Him.*
