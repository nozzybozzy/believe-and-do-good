*Revealed in Madinah · 11 ayat · "The Hypocrites" — a surah revealed about one man, and left in the Book for everyone*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-Munafiqun is the shortest sustained **portrait of hypocrisy** in the Qur'an, and it is unusual in that Ibn Kathir can name the occasion almost verse by verse: nearly the entire passage came down concerning **Abdullah bin Ubayy bin Salul**, chief of the Khazraj, the man who would have been crowned king of Yathrib had the Prophet ﷺ not arrived.

The surah works in two halves that seem unrelated until you see the hinge.

The first eight verses **expose** — the beautiful speech, the sworn oaths used as cover, the sealed hearts, the boast about expelling the weak. The last three verses turn away from the hypocrites entirely and address the **believers**: *do not let your wealth and your children divert you from the remembrance of Allah.*

The hinge is this: hypocrisy in the first half is the disease of a man who could not bear losing status and property. The warning in the second half is aimed at the very same appetite, in people who are sincere. The surah exposes the disease, then hands the believers the antidote before they catch it.

---

## 1. The Testimony That Is True and False at Once (63:1)

> *When the hypocrites come to you, they say: "We bear witness that you are indeed the Messenger of Allah."*
> *Allah knows that you are indeed His Messenger, and Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are liars indeed.*

Ibn Kathir draws out the fine point here, and it is the whole surah in one verse: **the statement they made was true; they were still liars.** Allah confirms the content — *Allah knows that you are indeed His Messenger* — and separately convicts the speakers, because *they did not believe inwardly in what they declared outwardly.*

The lie was not in the words. It was in the gap between the tongue and the heart.

---

## 2. Oaths as a Shield (63:2–3)

> *They have made their oaths a screen. Thus they hinder others from the path of Allah.*

**Meaning:** they shelter behind false, sinful oaths — swearing to be what they are not. Ibn Kathir explains why this is not a private sin:

- Some Muslims were **deceived** by them, not knowing the falsehood, and took them for Muslims.
- Some **believed what they said** and even imitated their outward behaviour.
- But inwardly the hypocrites *seek the destruction of Islam and its people* — so trusting them **brings great harm to many people**.

That is the sense of *they hinder others from the path of Allah*: the damage is done through the credibility they are given.

**Why their hearts were sealed (63:3):** *That is because they believed, and then disbelieved.* They reverted from faith to disbelief and exchanged guidance for misguidance — so Allah stamped their hearts, and now no goodness reaches them and no guidance is comprehended. The seal is a consequence, not an arbitrary decree.

---

## 3. Blocks of Wood Propped Up (63:4)

> *And when you look at them, their bodies please you; and when they speak, you listen to their words. They are as blocks of wood propped up.*

Ibn Kathir's description is precise and unsparing: hypocrites have a **graceful appearance and are eloquent** — you would listen to them — *even though they are truly weak and feeble, full of fear, fright and cowardice.* The image of propped-up timber means **shapes without substance**: form, no load-bearing.

> *They think that every cry is against them.*

Every alarm, every incident, they assume is coming for them. This is their cowardice, and Ibn Kathir cross-references the fullest description of it:

> *Then when fear comes, you will see them looking to you, their eyes revolving like one over whom hovers death; but when the fear departs, they will smite you with sharp tongues, miserly towards good.* (33:19)

> *They are the enemies, so beware of them.*

Not rivals — **enemies**. The verse ends with the curse: *How are they denying the right path?* — meaning, how are they being turned away from guidance into misguidance.

**The hadith of the signs (Ahmad, from Abu Hurayrah).** The Prophet ﷺ listed what marks them out:

- their greeting is a curse
- their food is from stealing, their booty from theft
- they shun the Masjid and come to prayer only at its end
- they are arrogant — *it is neither easy for them to blend in, nor for people to blend with them*
- **they are like pieces of wood by night, and noisy by day** — absent from the night prayer, loud in public

---

## 4. The Head That Would Not Bow (63:5–6)

> *And when it is said to them: "Come, so that the Messenger of Allah may ask forgiveness for you," they twist their heads, and you would see them turning away in pride.*

They turn away **in arrogance, belittling what they are being invited to** — and what they are being invited to is the Prophet's ﷺ intercession. So the sentence follows:

> *It is equal to them whether you ask forgiveness or ask not forgiveness for them, Allah will never forgive them.*

Ibn Kathir notes this parallels Surah Bara'ah, and then gives the incident behind it (**Ibn Ishaq**, via Ibn Shihab):

Before Uhud, Abdullah bin Ubayy would stand every Friday, unchallenged, and introduce the Prophet ﷺ to the congregation — *Allah has honoured us by sending him and gave you might through him; support him and obey him* — then sit. After Uhud, having withdrawn a third of the army mid-march, he stood to do it again. The Muslims seized his clothes: **"Sit down, O enemy of Allah! You are not worthy to stand after what you did."**

He left the mosque protesting that he had only meant to support him. Men of the Ansar at the gate told him: *Go back, so that Allah's Messenger asks Allah to forgive you.*

His answer — the verse in a sentence:

> **"By Allah, I do not wish that he ask Allah to forgive me."**

**Qatadah and As-Suddi** report the same refusal in a parallel account: a young relative reported an ugly statement of his; Abdullah swore he had said nothing; revelation came down anyway; and when told to go to the Messenger, *he turned his head away.*

---

## 5. "The More Honourable Will Expel the Weaker" (63:7–8)

Two boasts, made on the campaign against **Bani Al-Mustaliq**, and both answered by Allah in the same breath.

**The occasion (Ibn Ishaq).** At a water source, **Jahjah bin Sa'id Al-Ghifari** — a hired hand of Umar — and **Sinan bin Wabr** fought. Sinan cried *"O Ansar!"*; Jahjah cried *"O Muhajirin!"* Abdullah bin Ubayy, sitting with Zayd bin Arqam and other Ansar, said:

> *"They are bothering us in our land… Feed your dog until it becomes strong, and it will eat you. By Allah, when we go back to Al-Madinah, the most mighty will expel the weak from it."*

And to his own people: *withhold from them, and they will have to move elsewhere* — which is 63:7, *"Spend not on those who are with Allah's Messenger, until they desert him."*

**Allah's two answers, placed immediately after each boast:**

| The boast | The correction |
|---|---|
| *Starve them out* | **To Allah belong the treasures of the heavens and the earth** — but the hypocrites *comprehend not* |
| *The mighty will expel the weak* | **Al-'Izzah belongs to Allah, and to His Messenger, and to the believers** — but the hypocrites *know not* |

He was right that honour expels disgrace. He was simply wrong about which side he was on.

**What happened next.** Zayd bin Arqam, still a boy, reported the words. Umar said: *"Order Abbad bin Bishr to cut off his head."* The Prophet ﷺ replied:

> *"What if people started saying that Muhammad kills his companions? No. Rather, order the people to start the journey."*

Abdullah came and swore he had never said it, and his people suggested the boy had misheard. So the Prophet ﷺ set out at an unusual hour and marched them through the night and the following day until, when they finally made camp, *the minute people felt the ground under their feet, they went to sleep* — **exhausted out of the argument** — and Surah Al-Munafiqun was revealed.

**Usayd bin Al-Hudayr**, meeting him on that march, made the correction personally: *"You are the mighty one, O Messenger of Allah, and he is the disgraced one."* Then, gently: *"Take it easy with him. When Allah brought you to us, we were about to gather the pearls of a crown to make him king. He thinks you have robbed him of his kingship."*

**Jabir's account (Ahmad, Al-Bukhari, Muslim)** preserves the Prophet's ﷺ judgement on the original quarrel — *"What is this call of Jahiliyyah? Abandon it, it is offensive"* — and his repeated refusal to have Abdullah killed: *"Leave him, lest the people say that Muhammad kills his companions."*

**The son at the gate.** The most extraordinary sequel: **Abdullah, the son of Abdullah bin Ubayy**, a sincere believer, stood at the gate of Al-Madinah with drawn sword and stopped his own father: *"By Allah, you will not enter until the Messenger of Allah allows it — for he is the honourable one and you are the disgraced."* He let him pass only on the Prophet's ﷺ permission. And he offered:

> *"I was told you have decided to have my father executed. If you wish, I will bring you his head myself — because I would hate to see the killer of my father and then have to look at him."*

The Prophet ﷺ never gave the order.

## 6. The Antidote: Wealth, Children, and the Remembrance of Allah (63:9)

> *O you who believe! Let not your properties or your children divert you from the remembrance of Allah. And whosoever does that, then they are the losers.*

Ibn Kathir: this is a command to **remember Him frequently** and to refuse to be pulled away from that remembrance by excessive absorption in property and children. Those who let this life and its delights busy them from *the obedience and remembrance of Allah, for which they were created*, will be among the losers —

> **they will lose themselves and their families on the Day of Resurrection.**

Note what he does not say. Wealth and children are not condemned. **Diversion** is.

---

## 7. Spend Now — Because the Request Always Comes Too Late (63:10–11)

> *And spend of that with which We have provided you before death comes to one of you, and he says: "My Lord! If only You would give me respite for a little while, then I should give Sadaqah from my wealth, and be among the righteous."*

Ibn Kathir's comment is one of the plainest lines in the whole tafsir:

> **Every person who fell into shortcomings will regret it at the time of his death and will ask for respite — even a short period — so that he does better and attains what he missed.**

And then: *No. What occurred, has occurred; what is coming, shall come. Each will be held accountable.*

He gathers the parallel pleas so you can hear the same sentence repeating across the Qur'an:

- *"Our Lord! Respite us for a little while, we will answer Your call and follow the Messengers!"* — answered with: *"Had you not sworn aforetime that you would not leave?"* (14:44)
- *"My Lord! Send me back, so that I may do good in that which I have left behind!"* — answered with: *No! It is but a word that he speaks; and behind them is Barzakh, a barrier, until the Day they are resurrected.* (23:99–100)

> *And Allah grants respite to none when his appointed time comes. And Allah is All-Aware of what you do.*

**Meaning:** when the time of death comes, **no one is granted respite.** The surah closes on *All-Aware of what you do* — the same knowledge that convicted the hypocrites in verse 1 now turned toward the believers being addressed.

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**Hypocrisy is not a failure of belief in the mind — it is a gap between the tongue and the heart.** Abdullah bin Ubayy said true things about the Prophet ﷺ from the pulpit for years. What ruined him was that he wanted the status he lost more than he wanted the truth he spoke.

**The eloquence is the warning sign, not the credential.** *Their bodies please you… you listen to their words… they are blocks of wood propped up.* Impressive form, no substance, and *they are the enemies, so beware of them*.

**Honour is not distributed by numbers or land or wealth.** *Al-'Izzah belongs to Allah, and to His Messenger, and to the believers* — this was revealed to a man who was counting heads and thought he had the majority.

**Restraint was the Prophet's ﷺ answer.** Twice Umar asked to strike, twice the answer was no. The reputation of the community mattered more than the satisfaction of the verdict — and revelation exposed the man far more completely than a sword would have.

**And the final three verses are the point of the first eight.** The disease was love of status and wealth. So the surah ends by telling the *believers*: give it away before you are asked for respite you will not receive. Nobody in the Qur'an who asks for more time gets it.
