*Revealed in Makkah · 69 ayat · named for the spider, mentioned once, at 29:41*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-'Ankabut opens with a question that sets the terms for everything after it: *Do people think they will be left alone because they say "We believe," and will not be tested?* The surah is addressed to a community in Makkah under pressure, and its argument runs in a single line — **the claim of faith is not the end of anything; it is the beginning of a test, and the test is the point.**

From there it moves outward in widening circles. First the individual under trial. Then the hypocrite who mistakes persecution by men for punishment by God, and abandons the faith at the first cost. Then a gallery of earlier communities — Nuh, Ibrahim, Lut, Shu'ayb, 'Ad, Thamud, Qarun, Fir'awn — each of which put its messenger under the same pressure and each of which was answered. Then the spider's house: the image that names the surah and explains why the whole apparatus of false gods collapses under weight.

It closes with the two things a tested believer is actually told to do: **migrate if you must** — *My earth is spacious* — and **strive**, with the promise that striving itself is what opens the way: *those who strive for Us, We will guide them to Our paths.*

---

## 1. The Test Is the Membership Fee (29:1–3)

> *Do people think that they will be left alone because they say: "We believe," and will not be tested?*

Ibn Kathir reads this as **a rebuke in the form of a question**. Allah will inevitably test His believing servants, and the level of testing tracks the level of faith. The hadith he cites is the anchor of the whole surah:

> *The people most severely tested are the Prophets, then the righteous, then the next best and the next best. A man will be tested in accordance with the degree of his religious commitment; the stronger his religious commitment, the stronger his test.*

He puts two parallel verses alongside it, both of which ask the same question about Paradise:

- *Do you think you will enter Paradise without Allah knowing those of you who fought and those who are patient?* (3:142)
- *Do you think you will enter Paradise without such trials as came to those who passed away before you?* — poverty, illness, and shaking so severe that even the Messenger and those with him said, *"When will the help of Allah come?"* Then the answer: **Yes! Certainly, the help of Allah is near.** (2:214)

---

## 2. What "So That Allah Will Know" Means (29:3)

A theological knot Ibn Kathir stops to untie. Allah knows the past, the future, **and how that which will never happen would have happened if it were to happen** — all the Imams of Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah are agreed on this. So the verse does not mean He acquires new information.

It means **He will make it known** — the sincere are separated out from the liars in visible fact. On phrases like *only that We know* (2:143), Ibn Abbas and others read it as *only to see*, and Ibn Kathir adds the distinction:

> Seeing has to do with what is there; **knowledge is broader than seeing**, since it includes what is not present as well as what is.

---

## 3. No One Outruns Him (29:4)

> *Or think those who do evil deeds that they can outstrip Us? Evil is that which they judge!*

Those who are not believers should not imagine they have escaped the trials by opting out. **Ahead of them lies something greater and more severe.** "Outstrip" here, Ibn Kathir says flatly, means *escape from Us* — and their calculation is exactly what is being called evil.

---

## 4. The Term Is Coming, and Your Striving Is Your Own (29:5–6)

> *Whoever hopes in meeting with Allah — then Allah's term is surely coming.*

Whoever hopes for the meeting in the Hereafter, does righteous deeds and hopes for a great reward, **will have his hope fulfilled**. This will undoubtedly come to pass, because He hears every supplication and knows the need of every created being.

And *whoever strives, strives only for himself*. The benefit returns to the striver. Allah has no need of the deeds of His servants — **if every one of them were as pious as the most pious man among them, it would not add to His dominion in the slightest.**

---

## 5. The Arithmetic of Mercy (29:7)

Having established that He needs nothing, the surah immediately says what He does anyway. He will **expiate their evil deeds** and **reward them according to the best of what they did**:

- He accepts the fewest good deeds
- For one good deed: **between ten and seven hundred rewards**
- For one bad deed: **one** — or He overlooks and forgives it entirely

> *Surely, Allah wrongs not even the weight of a speck of dust, but if there is any good, He doubles it, and gives from Him a great reward.* (4:40)

---

## 6. Parents: Everything Except Shirk (29:8–9)

The command to honour parents comes **directly after** the call to Tawhid, and Ibn Kathir explains the placement: a person's parents are **the cause of his existence** — the father for spending on him, the mother for her compassion toward him. He quotes the fullest passage on this in the Qur'an:

> *Say not to them a word of disrespect, nor shout at them, but address them in terms of honour. And lower unto them the wing of submission and humility through mercy, and say: "My Lord! Bestow on them Your mercy as they did bring me up when I was young."* (17:23–24)

**And then the single exception.** If they strive to make you associate partners with Allah — *obey them not*. Be aware of them, hold your religion, and know that Allah will reward both your kindness to them and your patience.

The occasion of revelation, from Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas: his mother swore she would not eat or drink until he renounced Islam, and when they tried to feed her they had to force her mouth open. This ayah came down. (Recorded by At-Tirmidhi — "Hasan Sahih" — also Ahmad, Muslim, Abu Dawud and An-Nasa'i.)

The following verse completes the thought: *We shall admit them among the righteous* — **you will be gathered with the righteous, not with your parents**, however close you were to them in the world. A person is gathered on the Day of Resurrection with those he loves, meaning religious love.

---

## 7. Faith That Cannot Survive a Cost (29:10–11)

> *Of mankind are some who say: "We believe in Allah." But if they are made to suffer for Allah, they consider the trial of mankind as Allah's punishment.*

This is the portrait of the liar whose faith sits on his tongue and not in his heart. Ibn Abbas: **their trial is leaving Islam** the moment they are made to suffer. The parallel is the man who *worships Allah as it were upon the edge* — content while things go well, turning on his face at the first affliction (22:11–12).

And then the other face of the same person: *if victory comes from your Lord, they will say, "Verily, we were with you"* — claiming brotherhood once there are spoils to share. Ibn Kathir lines this up with 4:141 (those who wait and watch, ready to claim either side) and 5:52.

The answer given is not an argument but a fact: **Is not Allah Best Aware of what is in the breasts of the creatures?**

So the testing runs both directions — through calamity **and** through ease:

> *Allah will not leave the believers in the state in which you are now, until He distinguishes the wicked from the good.* (3:179, after Uhud)

The point is to see who obeys in hardship as well as comfort, and who obeys only when things go according to his desires.

---

## 8. "Follow Our Way and Let Us Bear Your Sins" (29:12–13)

The Quraysh's offer to the new Muslims: come back to our religion, and **any sin will be on our shoulders**. Ibn Kathir: it is the ordinary human sentence *"Do this, and your sin will be on me."*

Allah calls it a lie in the same breath. **No one bears another's burden:**

> *If one heavily laden calls another to bear his load, nothing of it will be lifted even though he be near of kin.* (35:18)
> *And no friend will ask a friend, though they shall be made to see one another.* (70:10–11)

But the next verse turns the screw. They *will* carry other loads besides their own — **not because the sin was transferred, but because they caused it**, and the original sinner's burden is not reduced by a particle (16:25). The hadith:

> *Whoever calls others to true guidance will have a reward like that of those who follow him until the Day of Resurrection, without it detracting from their reward in the slightest. Whoever calls others to misguidance will have a burden of sin like that of those who follow him until the Day of Resurrection, without it detracting from their burden in the slightest.*

And: **no person is killed unlawfully but a share of the guilt falls on the first son of Adam**, because he was the first to initiate the idea of killing.

---

## 9. The Day the Accounts Are Settled (29:13, continued)

Ibn Kathir hangs one of the longest hadiths in the surah here — from Abu Umamah, on injustice between people:

Allah will swear: *"By My glory and majesty, no injustice will be overlooked today."* A man is called forward, followed by good deeds **like mountains**, and the people gaze at them in wonder. Then the call goes out: whoever is owed anything by this man, or was wronged by him, come forward. They gather, and Allah says, *"Settle the matter for My servant."*

*How can we settle it?*
**"Take from his good deeds and give it to them."**

They keep taking until nothing is left, and there are still claimants.
**"Take from their evil deeds and give them to him."**

The corroborating narration in the Sahih is starker still: a man comes with good deeds like mountains, but he wronged this one, took the wealth of that one, slandered the honour of another — **so each of them takes from his good deeds, and when the good deeds run out, their evil deeds are placed on him.**

---

## 10. Nuh: Nine Hundred and Fifty Years (29:14–15)

> *He stayed among them a thousand years less fifty years; so the Deluge overtook them while they were wrongdoers.*

Ibn Kathir reads this as **consolation to Muhammad ﷺ**. Nuh called them night and day, in secret and openly, for nearly a millennium, and only a few believed. So: do not grieve over those of your people who reject you. *Truly, those against whom the Word of your Lord has been justified will not believe, even if every sign should come to them* (10:96–97).

Ibn Abbas on the timeline: Nuh received his mission at forty; stayed among them 950 years; **lived sixty years after the Flood**, until people had increased and spread again.

*And made it an Ayah for all people* — either the ship itself, which Qatadah said remained on Mount Judi until the beginning of Islam, or the very concept of sailing, left as a permanent reminder of the rescue (36:41–44, 69:11–12). Ibn Kathir notes the grammatical shift here from **one specific ship to ships in general**, and gives parallel shifts elsewhere: the lamps that adorn the heaven and the lamps that are missiles against the devils (67:5) are not the same lamps.

---

## 11. Ibrahim's Case Against the Idols (29:16–18)

Five imperatives in a row: **worship Allah, fear Him, seek provision from Him, worship Him, be grateful to Him.**

The argument against the idols is that they are **invented names attached to created things**: *you only invent falsehood*. Al-'Awfi from Ibn Abbas — you made up names for them and called them gods, but they are created beings just like you. Al-Walibi from Ibn Abbas — *you invent falsehood* means **you carve idols**, which then have no power to provide for you.

*So seek from Allah your provision* — meaning, Ibn Kathir stresses, **seek it from no one else**, since no one else possesses the power. He pairs it with *You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help* (1:5).

And the messenger's job is bounded: *the duty of the Messenger is only to convey plainly*. Qatadah read this line as **consolation to the Prophet ﷺ**, which would mean the narrative about Ibrahim is interrupted here and resumes at 29:24 — Ibn Jarir agreed. Ibn Kathir disagrees: from the context, **Ibrahim is speaking continuously all the way through**, since the passage ends with *"And nothing was the answer of his people…"* And Allah knows best.

---

## 12. Look at What Was Made (29:19–23)

Ibrahim's proof of the Resurrection is drawn from his people's own bodies: **Allah created them when they were nothing at all**, and gave them hearing and sight. *The One Who originated this is able to repeat it* — *and this is easier for Him* (30:27).

Then he sends them outward: *Travel in the land and see how He originated the creation.* Ibn Kathir expands on what there is to see — the heavens with their moving and stationary bodies; plains, mountains, valleys, deserts, trees, rivers, fruits, oceans. All of it announcing that it is **itself created**, and that there must be a Creator who says to a thing *"Be!"* and it is.

Three consequences follow:

- **He punishes whom He wills and shows mercy to whom He wills.** None can question Him; He is the sovereign who cannot be unjust in the slightest. The hadith: *If Allah willed to punish the dwellers of His heavens and His earth, He would do so, while He would not be unjust to them.*
- **You cannot escape in the earth or the heaven** — everything fears Him and needs Him, while He is Independent of all else.
- Those who disbelieve in His signs and the meeting with Him have **no share in His mercy**, and a painful torment in this world and the next.

---

## 13. "Kill Him or Burn Him" (29:24–25)

The people's entire reply to that argument. Ibn Kathir's reading of why: **proof had been clearly established against them, so they resorted to power and strength.** When the argument is lost, the fire is lit.

They gathered firewood for a long time, fenced it, and set it ablaze until the flames reached the sky — *no greater fire had ever been lit*. They put Ibrahim in a catapult and threw him in. **Allah made it cool and safe**, and he emerged unscathed after several days.

And here Ibn Kathir gives the reason Ibrahim became an Imam for mankind, in one of the most quotable lines in the surah:

> He offered **himself** to the Most Merciful, his **body** to the flames, his **son** as a sacrifice, and his **wealth** to care for his guests. For all of these reasons he is beloved by the followers of all religions.

Then his rebuke on the idols: *the love between you is only in the life of this world.* You worship them together and it binds you together — and on the Day of Resurrection **the bond inverts into denial and cursing**, followers cursing leaders and leaders cursing followers (7:37, 43:67). *And your abode will be the Fire, and you shall have no helper.*

---

## 14. Lut Believed, and They Left (29:26–27)

Of all his people, only Lut believed — said to be his brother's son, Lut bin Haran bin Azar — along with **Sarah**, Ibrahim's wife.

Ibn Kathir pauses on an apparent conflict: the Sahih hadith where Ibrahim tells Sarah *"there are no believers on earth except you and I."* His resolution: the meaning is **there is no other Muslim couple on earth**, not that no one else believed at all.

Qatadah: they migrated together **from Kutha, on the outskirts of Kufa, to Syria**. Lut was later sent as a Messenger to Sadum, in Ibrahim's own lifetime.

*I will emigrate for the sake of my Lord* — Ibn Abbas and Ad-Dahhak take the speaker to be Ibrahim, choosing to leave **so he could practise his religion openly**. Note that this is the same solution the surah will prescribe to the reader at 29:56.

Then the gift: **Ishaq and Ya'qub, and prophethood and the Book among his offspring.** Ibn Kathir spells out the scale of it — **after Ibrahim there was no prophet who was not from his descendants**. All the prophets of the Children of Israel down to 'Isa, who stood among his people and announced the coming of the Hashimi Qurashi Arab Prophet, from the line of Isma'il — *and there is no Prophet from the line of Isma'il besides him.*

*And We granted him his reward in this world* — plentiful provision, a fine home, a beautiful and righteous wife, and a name everyone still speaks well of. On *Ibrahim, who fulfilled all* (53:37), Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Qatadah say simply: **"He obeyed Allah in all ways."**

---

## 15. Lut's People (29:28–30)

Three charges, not one:

1. **An immorality none in creation had preceded them in.**
2. **They robbed the wayfarer** — lying in wait on the road, killing and looting.
3. **They practised evil in their gatherings** — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, *you do and say things that are not befitting, and you do not denounce one another for it.*

On what that third one specifically was, he preserves the disagreement rather than resolving it: Mujahid said they had intercourse openly; A'ishah and Al-Qasim said they competed in passing gas and laughing; others said they staged ram fights and cockfights. **They used to do all of these things, and they were even eviler than that.**

Their only reply was *bring Allah's torment upon us if you are truthful* — which Ibn Kathir reads as the signature of disbelief: scorn plus stubbornness. So Lut prayed: **"My Lord! Give me victory over the people who are corrupt."**

---

## 16. The Guests (29:31–35)

The angels answered that prayer, and came first to Ibrahim as guests. He offered hospitality; **when he saw they had no interest in the food, he grew fearful.** They calmed him, gave him news of a son by Sarah — and told him why they had come.

His response was to **argue for the people of Lut**, hoping to buy them time in case they might yet be guided. *But there is Lut in it.* — *We know better who is there. We will save him and his family, except his wife*, who will be among those who remain behind, **because she used to support them in their disbelief.**

Then to Lut, in the form of handsome young men — and his distress is precisely diagnosed: **if he hosted them he feared what his people would do to them; if he did not host them he still feared for them.** At that point he did not know who they were.

Jibril uprooted the town from the depths of the earth, lifted it, and threw it upside down, and Allah rained on them *stones of Sijjil, in a well-arranged manner, marked from your Lord* (11:82–83). Where they lived became **a putrid, stinking lake** — left, the verse says, as *an evident Ayah for a folk who understand*, and Ibn Kathir connects it to the road the Quraysh themselves travelled: *Verily, you pass by them in the morning and at night; will you not then reflect?* (37:137–138)

---

## 17. Shu'ayb (29:36–37)

*Worship Allah and hope for the last Day* — Ibn Jarir records the reading **"fear the Last Day"**, and Ibn Kathir pairs it with *for those who look forward to meeting Allah and the Last Day* (60:6).

*And commit no mischief on the earth* — their specific corruptions were **cheating in weights and measures and ambushing people on the road**, on top of disbelief. Three punishments are named for them: the earthquake that convulsed their land, the **Sayhah** that tore their hearts from their bodies, and the **torment of the Day of Shade**.

*And they lay prostrate in their dwellings.* Qatadah: **"They were dead."** Others: thrown on top of one another.

---

## 18. Four Nations, Four Tailored Ends (29:38–40)

Ibn Kathir first locates them geographically, because the Quraysh knew these places:

| | Who | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **'Ad** | people of Hud | the Ahqaf (curved sand-hills), near Hadramawt in Yemen |
| **Thamud** | people of Salih | Al-Hijr, near Wadi Al-Qura — *the Arabs passed by it often* |
| **Qarun** | owner of the keys to immense treasures | — |
| **Fir'awn & Haman** | king of Egypt and his minister | two Copts |

*Shaytan made their deeds fair seeming to them, and turned them away from the path* — **though they were intelligent.** Intelligence was never the protection.

Then *We punished each for his sins*, and Ibn Kathir's comment is that **the punishments fit the crimes**:

- **'Ad → the Hasib.** They had said *"Who is stronger than us?"* — so a violent, intensely cold wind carrying pebbles lifted a man into the sky and hurled him down headlong, leaving bodies **like uprooted stems of date palms**.
- **Thamud → the Sayhah.** Evidence had been established by the she-camel that came forth exactly as they demanded when the rock split; they persisted anyway, and threatened to stone Salih. The Shout **took away their powers of speech and movement.**
- **Qarun → swallowed by the earth.** He paraded boastfully, filled with self-admiration, thinking himself better than others — **and he will continue sinking into it until the Day of Resurrection.**
- **Fir'awn, Haman and their troops → drowned** in a single morning, **not one of them escaped.**

*It was not Allah Who wronged them, but they wronged themselves.*

---

## 19. The Spider's House (29:41–43)

> *The parable of those who seek protectors other than Allah is that of a spider who builds a house; but indeed, the weakest of houses is the spider's house — if they but knew.*

This is the surah's image and its title. Ibn Kathir's reading: they turn to these gods hoping to be helped and provided for, especially in difficulty — and in doing so they are **like a person holding on to a spider's web, who gains no benefit from it at all.** *If they knew this, they would not take any protectors besides Allah.*

The contrast is drawn immediately: **the believer**, whose heart is devoted to Allah and who still does righteous deeds and follows His Law, **has grasped the most trustworthy handle, which will never break because it is so strong and firm.** Two houses, two grips.

*But none will understand them except those who have knowledge.* Ibn Abi Hatim records 'Amr bin Murrah:

> *"I never came across an Ayah of the Book of Allah that I did not know, but it grieved me"* — because of this very verse.

---

## 20. Recite, and Pray (29:44–45)

The heavens and earth were created **with truth**, not as play: *that every person may be rewarded for that which he strives* (20:15), *that He may requite those who do evil with what they have done, and reward those who do good with what is best* (53:31).

Then the two commands to the Prophet ﷺ and the believers. *Recite what has been revealed to you* means **both reciting it and conveying it to people.**

And *perform the Salah*, which Ibn Kathir says contains two things:

**First, it stops you.** *The Salah prevents from Al-Fahsha' and Al-Munkar.* A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said, *"So-and-so prays at night, but when morning comes, he steals."* The reply: **"What you are saying will stop him from doing that."**

**Second, and greater: the remembrance of Allah.** *And the remembrance of Allah is greater indeed* — more important than the first.

Abu Al-'Aliyah's test, which is worth keeping: prayer has three attributes, and **any prayer containing none of them is not truly prayer** —

- **Ikhlas** (done purely for Allah alone), which makes a person do good deeds
- **Fear of Allah**, which prevents him from doing evil
- **Remembrance of Allah**, which is the Qur'an, containing commands and prohibitions

Ibn Awn Al-Ansari puts it more simply: while you are praying, **you are already doing the good thing, being kept from the bad thing, and remembering Allah — all at once.**

---

## 21. How to Argue with the People of the Book (29:46)

> *And argue not with the People of the Scripture, except with that which is better.*

Anyone seeking to learn about religion from them should argue **in the better manner, because that is more effective** — *invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and fair preaching* (16:125); even to Fir'awn, *speak to him mildly* (20:44).

*Except with such of them as do wrong* — those who turn a blind eye to clear evidence out of stubbornness and arrogance. In that case, Ibn Kathir says, you progress **from debate to deterrence** (57:25; Jabir: *"We were commanded to strike with the sword whoever opposes the Book of Allah."*)

And the formula to use when they tell you something you cannot verify — from the hadith recorded by Al-Bukhari:

> *Do not believe the People of the Book and do not deny them. Say: "We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us and what has been revealed to you. Our God and your God is One, and to Him we have submitted."*

Ibn Kathir explains the logic: **do not rush to call it a lie, because it may be true; do not rush to call it true, because it may be false.** You believe in it *in general* — under the condition that it was revealed and has not been altered.

Two supporting reports:

- **Ibn Abbas**, incredulous: how can you ask them about anything, when your own Book is the most recent, you read it pure and uncontaminated, and it tells you they altered theirs? *"No, by Allah, we have never seen any of them asking you about what was sent down to you."*
- **Mu'awiyah on Ka'b Al-Ahbar**: he was one of the most truthful of those who narrated from the People of the Book, *"even though we found that some of what he said might be lies."* Ibn Kathir's own gloss is generous and precise — **Ka'b did not intend to lie**; he narrated from manuscripts he believed sound, which contained fabricated material, **because those communities had no one so conscientious in memorising scripture by heart as this Ummah.**

---

## 22. The Unlettered Prophet Is the Argument (29:47–49)

> *Neither did you read any book before it, nor did you write any book with your right hand.*

You lived among your people for decades before this Qur'an, and **they all know you cannot read or write.** That is precisely why it functions as proof: *In that case, indeed, the followers of falsehood might have doubted* — meaning, **had you been literate, some would have claimed you copied it from earlier scriptures.** They said it anyway (*Tales of the ancients, dictated to him morning and afternoon*, 25:5) — but they knew better.

He was described this way in advance: *the unlettered Prophet, about whom they find written with them in the Tawrah and the Injil* (7:157). And he remained so until his death, unable to write a line or a letter, keeping scribes for revelation and correspondence.

*Nay, but it is clear Ayat, preserved in the breasts of those who have been given knowledge.* Ibn Kathir's reading of where the miracle actually lives:

- *We have made the Qur'an easy to remember* (54:17)
- **"What I have been given is revelation."** Every Prophet was given something to make people believe; his miracle was the revelation itself — *and I hope that I will have the most followers among them.*
- And the hadith of 'Iyad bin Himar: *"I am revealing to you a Book which cannot be washed away by water, which you recite while you are asleep and while you are awake."*

Meaning, Ibn Kathir explains, **if the manuscript were washed away there would be no need for the manuscript** — it is preserved in hearts, easy on the tongue, miraculous in wording and meaning. **In the previous scriptures this Ummah was described as carrying their holy Books in their hearts.**

---

## 23. "Why No Signs?" (29:50–52)

They wanted a she-camel of their own. The answer: *the signs are only with Allah* — and Ibn Kathir spells out the implication with some bite: **if He knew you would be guided by a sign, He would give you one, because it costs Him nothing. He knows you are being stubborn and testing me.** The precedent is 17:59 — *nothing stops Us from sending the Ayat but that the people of old denied them*, and Thamud got their camel and wronged her.

Then the counter-question that reframes the whole demand:

> *Is it not sufficient for them that We have sent down to you the Book which is recited to them?*

An unlettered man who never mixed with the People of the Book is telling them **what happened before their time, what will happen after they are gone, and judging between them** — settling in clear language the matters the earlier scriptures dispute over. *Is it not a sign to them that the learned scholars of the Children of Israel knew it?* (26:197)

And the witness clause: *Sufficient is Allah for a witness between me and you.* If I were forging this against Him, **He would have seized me by the right hand and cut the aorta, and none of you could have withheld Him** (69:44–47).

---

## 24. Hurrying the Punishment (29:53–55)

They demanded the torment be brought forward — as at 8:32, *rain down stones on us from the sky*. Ibn Kathir's answer: **were it not that Allah has decreed the punishment be delayed to the Day of Resurrection, it would already have come.** As it is, *it will come upon them suddenly while they perceive not.*

*On the Day when the torment shall cover them from above them and from beneath their feet* — a bed of Hell and coverings above (7:41), coverings of Fire above and beneath (39:16), unable to ward it off from their faces or their backs (21:39). **It surrounds them from every side**, which Ibn Kathir notes is more effective as physical punishment.

And then *"Taste what you used to do"* — which he classes separately as **psychological punishment**: threat and rebuke layered on top of the fire, as at 54:48–49 and 52:13–16.

---

## 25. My Earth Is Spacious (29:56–60)

> *O My servants who believe! Certainly, spacious is My earth. Therefore worship Me.*

The command to **migrate from a land where you cannot establish your religion** to one where you can. Ibn Kathir names both migrations: the weak and oppressed believers of Makkah went to **Ethiopia**, where the Negus, Ashamah, gave them refuge, helped them, supported them and honoured them in his land; later the Messenger ﷺ and his remaining Companions went to **Al-Madinah**, formerly Yathrib.

The verse that follows removes the excuse for staying: *Everyone shall taste death.* Wherever you are, **death will catch up with you** — so be where Allah commands you to be, since death is inevitable and the return is to Him.

The reward is lofty dwellings in Paradise with rivers beneath — **water, wine, honey and milk, which they can direct to flow wherever they wish.** And the hadith of Abu Malik Al-Ash'ari on who those rooms are for:

> *In Paradise there are rooms whose outside can be seen from the inside, and their inside from the outside; Allah has prepared them for those who **feed others**, who **speak well**, who **pray and fast continually**, and who **stand in prayer at night while people are asleep**.*

*Those who are patient* — in adhering to their religion, migrating, fighting, leaving behind families and relatives seeking Allah's Face — *and put their trust in their Lord*, in all their affairs, spiritual and worldly alike.

And then the provision argument, aimed straight at the fear of migrating: *so many a moving creature carries not its own provision* — **it cannot gather and store for tomorrow, and Allah feeds it anyway.** The ants in the depths of the earth, the birds in the air, the fish in the sea (11:6). Ibn Kathir adds the historical proof: **when the Muhajirin migrated, their provision became greater and better than before**, and within a short time they were rulers in the land.

---

## 26. They Already Know the Answer (29:61–66)

*Who created the heavens and the earth and subjected the sun and the moon?* — **"Allah."**
*Who sends down water from the sky and gives life to the dead earth?* — **"Allah."**

The idolaters conceded the Lordship and withheld the worship, which Ibn Kathir identifies as the standard move Allah uses against them throughout the Qur'an: **establish His divinity from their own admission of His Lordship.** Even their Talbiyah gave it away — *"At Your service, You have no partner, except the partner that You have, and You possess him and whatever he has."*

If He alone creates, provides, and decides who is rich and who is poor — **why worship anyone else? Why trust anyone else?** Since dominion is His alone, let worship be His alone.

Two illustrations follow:

**The world is amusement and play** — insignificant, transient, soon over — while *the home of the Hereafter, that is the life indeed*, the true everlasting life. *If they but knew*, Ibn Kathir glosses: **they would prefer what lasts over what passes away.**

**And the ship at sea.** On board, when the water rises, they call on Allah *making their faith pure for Him only* — the very Tawhid they deny on land (17:67). *But when He brings them safely to land, behold, they give a share of their worship to others.* Why not do on land what fear made you do at sea?

The story attached is **Ikrimah bin Abi Jahl's**. Fleeing after the conquest of Makkah, on a ship bound for Ethiopia, the boat began to rock and the crew called out: *pray sincerely to your Lord alone, for no one can save us but Him.* Ikrimah's own reasoning:

> *"By Allah, if there is none who can save us on the sea except Him, then there is none who can save us on land except Him either. O Allah, I vow that if I come out of this, I will go and put my hand in the hand of Muhammad, and I will find him kind and merciful."*

**And this is what indeed did happen.**

---

## 27. The Sanctuary They Were Ungrateful For (29:67–68)

> *Have they not seen that We have made a secure sanctuary, while men are being snatched away from all around them?*

A pointed reminder to Quraysh. Inside the Haram, resident and visitor are equal and **whoever enters is safe** — while the desert Arabs immediately around them were raiding, ambushing and killing one another. *So let them worship the Lord of this House, Who has fed them against hunger and made them safe from fear* (106:1–4).

And their thanks for it was to associate others with Him, and to reject the Messenger who was among them — *those who have changed the blessings of Allah into disbelief, and caused their people to dwell in the house of destruction* (14:28). They fought him and expelled him. **So Allah took the blessing away**: killed those He killed at Badr, gave the believers the upper hand, and enabled the conquest of Makkah.

Then the two worst crimes, named side by side, with the verdict that they are not the same crime:

- **Inventing a lie against Allah** — claiming revelation where there was none, or *"I shall reveal something like that which Allah revealed"*
- **Denying the truth when it comes to him**

*The former is a fabricator and the latter is a disbeliever.* **Is there not a dwelling in Hell for the disbelievers?**

---

## 28. The Promise the Surah Ends On (29:69)

> *As for those who strive hard for Us, We will surely guide them to Our paths. And verily, Allah is with the doers of good.*

Addressed, Ibn Kathir says, to **the Messenger, his Companions, and those who follow him until the Day of Resurrection** — meaning *We will help them to follow Our path in this world and the Hereafter.*

The commentary he chooses to close on, from Abbas Al-Hamdani Abu Ahmad, is the whole surah in a sentence:

> **"Those who act upon what they know, Allah will guide them to that which they do not know."**

Ahmad bin Abu Al-Hawari told this to Abu Sulayman Ad-Darani, who liked it and added a note of caution about acting on inspiration alone: *no one who is inspired to do something good should do it until he hears a report concerning it; if he hears a report, let him go ahead, and praise Allah that it accorded with what he himself felt.*

And on *Allah is with the doers of good*, Ash-Sha'bi reports the saying of 'Isa bin Maryam:

> *"Righteousness means doing good to those who ill-treat you; it does not mean doing good to those who do good to you."*

---

## The Thread

Al-'Ankabut is built on one question and one image.

**The question** is the opening line: did you think saying it would be enough? The surah's answer is that the statement of faith is a claim entering evidence, and everything after it — poverty, ridicule, a mother refusing food, exile, a fire in a valley — is the evidence being taken. The most tested are the Prophets, and the degree of testing rises with the degree of faith, which reframes hardship as a rank rather than an abandonment.

**The image** is the spider. Every alternative to Allah in this surah is structurally the same thing: a web that looks like shelter and holds no weight. Carved idols that were given names. Fathers and mothers whose claim ends where shirk begins. Quraysh offering to carry sins they cannot carry. Wealth like Qarun's, a throne like Fir'awn's, intelligence like 'Ad and Thamud's — *and Shaytan made their deeds fair seeming to them, though they were intelligent.* Against all of it stands one thing: the most trustworthy handle, which does not break.

And between the question and the image the surah puts a shape for a life under pressure. **Endure, and if you cannot endure where you are, move** — *My earth is spacious* — because the creature that cannot store a day's food is fed anyway, and the Muhajirin ended up with more than they left. **Pray**, because prayer both stops the hand and remembers Allah, and the remembrance is the greater of the two. **Argue in the better way**, except with those who have made argument pointless. **Do good to those who ill-treat you**, because doing good to those who do good to you was never the test.

Then the last word, which is not a demand for results but a promise about motion: *those who strive for Us, We will guide them to Our paths.* **Act on what you know, and you will be shown what you don't.**

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Al-'Ankabut, and all praise is due to Allah.*
