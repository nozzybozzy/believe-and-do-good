*Revealed in Makkah · 19 ayat · "The Clot" — the first revelation of the Qur'an*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Al-`Alaq is the **first thing ever revealed**, and what it chooses to say first is remarkable. Not a law, not a threat, not a creed — a command to **read**, and a description of God as the One who *teaches*. The first fact revealed to humanity about itself is that it began as a clinging clot; the second is that it was raised out of that lowliness **by knowledge**.

Then, halfway through, the surah turns and shows you what a man does with that gift when he thinks he no longer needs it. He becomes wealthy, considers himself self-sufficient, transgresses — and ends up standing in the sacred precinct threatening a man for praying.

So the surah has two halves and one hinge. **Man honoured by knowledge**, and **man ruined by self-sufficiency**. It closes by pointing at the one posture that resolves the tension: *fall prostrate and draw near*. The creature made from a clot, taught by the pen, has one place where he is closest to his Lord — and it is with his forehead on the ground. Which is precisely the posture Abu Jahl could not tolerate.

---

## 1. Read (96:1–2)

> *Read! In the Name of your Lord Who created. He has created man from a clot.*

Ibn Kathir reads these two ayat as a statement about **the beginning of man's creation from a dangling clot** — and immediately pairs that lowly origin with what follows from Allah's generosity: **He taught man what he did not know.**

**The honour and nobility of man is in his knowledge.** This is the dignity by which Adam, the father of humanity, was distinguished over the angels.

---

## 2. The Three Forms of Knowledge (96:3–5)

> *Read! And your Lord is the Most Generous. Who has taught by the pen. He has taught man that which he knew not.*

A precise piece of analysis Ibn Kathir draws out of the mention of the pen. Knowledge exists in three places:

| Where | Form |
|---|---|
| **In the mind** | intellectual |
| **On the tongue** | spoken |
| **In writing, with the fingers** | written |

And the relationship between them runs one way only: **the written necessitates the first two — but the reverse is not true.** You cannot write what you have not thought and could not say; you can easily think and say what you never write down. Hence the mention of the pen as the sign of Allah's generosity — it is the form of knowledge that preserves the other two.

Two sayings he attaches here:

- *"Record knowledge by writing."*
- *"Whoever acts according to what he knows, Allah will make him inherit knowledge that he did not know."*

The second is the whole ethic of the surah in one line: knowledge is not accumulated by reading more, but by **acting on what you already have**.

---

## 3. The Turn: Self-Sufficiency (96:6–8)

> *Nay! Verily, man does transgress. Because he considers himself self-sufficient.*

Ibn Kathir's gloss is blunt: man becomes **pleased with himself, evil, scornful and transgressive** the moment he considers himself self-sufficient and sees an abundance of wealth around him.

Note what the diagnosis actually is. Not wealth — **the sense of not needing anyone**. The transgression is a *consequence* of the illusion of independence, and the surah answers the illusion in a single line:

> *Surely, unto your Lord is the return.* (96:8)

Meaning: to Allah is the final destination, and **He will hold you accountable for that wealth — where you got it from, and how you spent it.**

---

## 4. The Man Who Stops a Man from Praying (96:9–14)

> *Have you seen him who prevents a servant when he prays?*

This was revealed about **Abu Jahl**, who threatened the Prophet ﷺ for performing Salah at the Ka`bah.

What Ibn Kathir notices about the order of the verses is worth sitting with. Allah does not open with the punishment. He **first admonishes Abu Jahl with what is better** — with a question:

> *Have you seen if he is on the guidance? Or enjoins Taqwa?*

That is: *do you really think this man you are preventing is on the straight path in his actions, and calling to God-consciousness in his words — and you rebuke and threaten him because he prays?*

Only then:

> *Knows he not that Allah sees.* (96:14)

Does the one doing the preventing not know that Allah **sees him, hears his words, and will compensate him in full** for what he has done?

---

## 5. The Forelock and the Council (96:15–18)

> *Nay! If he ceases not, We will scorch his forehead — a lying, sinful forehead!*

*If he ceases not* — meaning: if he does not recant from his **discord and obstinacy**. The scorching Ibn Kathir explains as making that forelock **extremely black on the Day of Judgement**.

And the description of it is a small masterpiece of matching: **lying in its statements, sinful in its actions** — the two faculties by which Abu Jahl opposed the Prophet ﷺ, both named in the thing that will be seized.

> *Then let him call upon his council. We will call out the guards of Hell!*

His *council* is his people and his tribe — let him call them for help. The guards of Hell are **the angels of torment**. The point, in Ibn Kathir's paraphrase, is a challenge: *let him find out whose group wins — Ours or his.*

---

## 6. What Actually Happened

Three narrations, and they build.

**Al-Bukhari, from Ibn `Abbas.** Abu Jahl said: *if I see Muhammad praying at the Ka`bah, I will stomp on his neck.* This reached the Prophet ﷺ, who said:

> *If he does, he will be seized by the angels.*

**Ahmad, At-Tirmidhi, An-Nasa'i and Ibn Jarir, from Ibn `Abbas** — the fuller version. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was praying at the Maqam when Abu Jahl passed and demanded, *haven't I forbidden you from this?* The Prophet ﷺ became angry and reprimanded him. Abu Jahl replied: *what can you threaten me with? By Allah, I have the most kinsmen of anyone in this valley.* Then the ayat about the council were revealed. Ibn `Abbas added: **had he actually called his people, the angels of torment would have seized him on the spot.** At-Tirmidhi graded it *Hasan Sahih*.

**Ibn Jarir, from Abu Hurayrah** — and this is the one that ends the story. Abu Jahl asked the people whether Muhammad still put his face in the dust among them; told yes, he swore by Al-Lat and Al-`Uzza that if he saw it he would stomp on his neck and grind his face into the ground. The Prophet ﷺ came and began to pray. Abu Jahl approached — and then, in full view of everyone, **began backing away on his heels, shielding himself with his hands.** Asked what was wrong, he said:

> *Between me and him is a ditch of fire, and terror, and wings.*

And the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *If he had come near me, the angels would have snatched him limb by limb.*

Recorded also by Ahmad, Muslim, An-Nasa'i and Ibn Abi Hatim. The threat was never carried out — not because Abu Jahl relented, but because **he was physically prevented, and only he could see by what.**

---

## 7. The Closing Command (96:19)

> *Nay! Do not obey him. Fall prostrate and draw near (to Allah)!*

*Do not obey him* — meaning, to the Prophet ﷺ: do not obey him in what he forbids you from, namely **steadfastness in worship and worshipping in abundance**. Ibn Kathir's paraphrase is almost tender: *pray wherever you wish and do not worry about him. Allah will protect you and help you, and He will defend you against the people.*

And then the last word of the first revelation is **prostration**. Confirmed in Sahih Muslim, from Abu Hurayrah:

> *The closest that a servant can be to his Lord is when he is in prostration — so make abundant supplications.*

Fittingly, this is one of the two verses at which the Prophet ﷺ himself would prostrate when reciting — the other being *When the heaven is split asunder* (84:1).

---

## If You Remember Nothing Else

**The first word revealed was a command to learn; the last word of that same revelation was a command to prostrate.** Everything the Qur'an will go on to say sits between those two.

And the surah tells you what breaks the arc: not ignorance, but **the feeling of not needing anything**. Man is honoured by being taught — and ruins himself the moment he concludes he is self-sufficient. Abu Jahl is the case study, and he had the most kinsmen in the valley, and it did not help him move his feet.

The remedy the surah gives is not more argument. It is the ground.
