*Revealed in Makkah · 28 ayat · the only surah named after a prophet and devoted entirely to his mission*

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Surah Nuh is a **field report on da'wah**, filed by the man who did it longer than anyone in history.

Almost the whole surah is Nuh's own speech: first his invitation to his people, then his complaint to his Lord about how it was received. The two halves fit together like a method and its results. The invitation is a complete model — warning, encouragement, evidence from the sky and the earth, appeals made publicly and privately, at every hour, for **nine hundred and fifty years**. The complaint is the record of what that effort actually produced: fingers pushed into ears, garments pulled over heads, and pride.

What makes the surah so useful is that it does not treat this as failure. Nuh's job was clarity — *I am a plain warner to you* — and clarity he delivered exhaustively. The result was never his to control. The surah ends with him handing the outcome back to Allah in a supplication, and Allah answering it.

Along the way it does two other things Muslims still live off: it gives the classical proof-text for **seeking forgiveness as the key to provision** — the reason `Umar climbed the minbar to pray for rain and did nothing but ask for forgiveness — and it gives the Qur'an's only account of **how idolatry began**, which turns out to be a story about good intentions honouring righteous men, one generation at a time, until the knowledge was lost.

---

## 1. The Whole Message, in Three Lines (71:1–4)

> *We sent Nuh to his people: Warn your people before there comes to them a painful torment.*

Nuh is sent to warn them of the punishment **before it arrives**, and to tell them that if they repent and turn to Allah, it will be lifted. Everything that follows is negotiable up until the moment it isn't.

His opening — *I am a plain warner* — means clarity: making the matter apparent and unmistakable. Then the three commands, which Ibn Kathir glosses tightly:

| The command | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Worship Allah** | — |
| **Have Taqwa of Him** | Abandon what He has forbidden; avoid what He has declared sinful |
| **Obey me** | In what I command you to do and what I forbid you from |

And the two things promised in return:

- **He will forgive you your sins** — if you do what I command and believe in what I was sent with.
- **He will respite you to an appointed term** — He will extend your life span and protect you from the torment that would otherwise have befallen you.

### The lifespan question

This ayah is the one cited by those who hold that **obedience, righteousness and maintaining family ties genuinely increase a person's life span**. Ibn Kathir pairs it with the hadith:

> *Maintaining the family ties increases the life span.*

But the very next line closes the door on wishful thinking: **when the term of Allah comes, it cannot be delayed.** So hasten to obedience *before* His vengeance arrives — because once He commands it, nothing repels or prevents it. He is the Great One who compels everything, and the Almighty to whose might all creation succumbs.

## 2. Nine Hundred and Fifty Years (71:5–9)

> *My Lord! I have called to my people night and day, but all my calling added nothing but to their flight.*

This is Nuh reporting to the One from whom nothing escapes — not to inform Him, but to lay out the case. *Night and day* means he never abandoned the work, carrying out the command in obedience. And the result was inverse to the effort: **the more he called them to the truth, the more they fled from it**.

### The gesture that says everything

> *Every time I called unto them that You might forgive them, they thrust their fingers into their ears and covered themselves up with their garments.*

They stopped up their ears so they could not hear what he was calling them to. Ibn Kathir immediately points out that the Quraysh were doing the identical thing to Muhammad ﷺ:

> *Listen not to this Qur'an, and make noise in the midst of it, that you may overcome.* (41:26)

On the garments, two readings are preserved:

- **Ibn `Abbas** (via Ibn Jarir): they disguised themselves so that he would not recognise them.
- **Sa`id bin Jubayr and As-Suddi**: they covered their heads so they could not hear him.

Then: *they persisted* — continuing in their shirk and great disbelief — *and magnified themselves in pride*, meaning they turned away from following the truth and submitting to it.

### Every method available

The three lines that follow are, in effect, Nuh's log of technique:

- **Openly** — among the people at large.
- **In public** — with open speech and a raised voice.
- **In private** — in one-to-one discussion.

He tried varying types of propagation, looking for whatever would be most effective with them. Nothing was left untried.

## 3. Istighfar as the Key to the Sky (71:10–12)

> *Ask forgiveness from your Lord; verily He is Oft-Forgiving. He will send rain to you Midrar.*

Return to Him, turn away from what you are involved in, repent soon — for **He accepts repentance no matter what the sin is, even if it is disbelief and shirk**.

*Midrar* means **continuous, uninterrupted rain**; Ibn `Abbas explained it as one downpour following another.

And then the promised increase, which Ibn Kathir spells out as a chain: repent, seek forgiveness, obey — and He increases your provision, gives you water from the blessings of the sky, causes the blessings of the earth and its crops to grow, multiplies your livestock, and gives you more wealth, more children, gardens of various fruits, and rivers flowing among them.

### `Umar on the minbar

Because of this ayah it is **recommended to recite Surah Nuh in the prayer for rain**. The precedent is `Umar bin Al-Khattab: he ascended the minbar to lead the istisqa' and **did nothing more than seek Allah's forgiveness and recite the ayat about seeking forgiveness** — this one among them. Then he said:

> *I have sought rain with the keys of the sky which cause the rain to descend.*

---

## 4. The Turn to Intimidation: Look Up, Look Around (71:13–20)

Ibn Kathir marks the pivot explicitly. Everything so far was **invitation with encouragement**; now Nuh balances it with **intimidation**.

> *What is the matter with you, that you do not hope for any Waqar from Allah?*

**Waqar** = great majesty (Ibn `Abbas, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak). Ibn `Abbas: *that you do not magnify Allah in the manner He deserves to be magnified* — meaning you do not fear His punishment and His vengeance.

Then the evidence, moving inward-outward-upward-downward:

**Your own body** — *He created you in Atwar (stages)*: a drop of sperm, then a hanging clot, then a lump of flesh (Ibn `Abbas, `Ikrimah, Qatadah, Yahya bin Rafi`, As-Suddi, Ibn Zayd).

**The seven heavens in tiers** — one above another.

**The moon a light (nur), the sun a lamp (siraj)** — Allah distinguished them in their manner of lighting, giving each a set course and distinct quality so that night and day may be known by the sun's rising and setting; and He fixed stations for the moon and made its light vary, waxing to a maximum then waning until it is veiled entirely, so that months and years may be reckoned. As in 10:5 — *that you might know the number of years and the reckoning*.

**The earth, three times over**:
- *Allah brought you forth from the earth* — Ibn Kathir notes the Arabic uses a verbal noun (*nabat*) for emphasis, and calls the usage most excellent.
- *Afterwards He will return you into it* — when you die.
- *And bring you forth* — on the Day of Judgement, repeating your creation just as He originated it.

**And made the earth a wide expanse** — spread out, levelled, settled, stabilised with firm and lofty mountains, *that you may go about therein in broad roads*: created so you may settle in it and travel wherever you wish, through all its sides and regions.

Ibn Kathir's summary of the whole passage: this is Nuh showing them Allah's power and greatness in creating the heavens and the earth, and the favour He did them in providing **both heavenly and earthly benefits** — the heaven as a building, the earth as a bed, provision enlarged for His creatures. Therefore **He is the One it is obligatory to worship and accept as One God**, with no partner, because He has no equal, peer, rival, mate, son, minister or advisor.

## 5. The Complaint: Wealth, Children, and a Mighty Plot (71:21–22)

> *My Lord! They have disobeyed me, and followed one whose wealth and children give him no increase but loss.*

They disobeyed him, opposed him, denied him, and **followed the children of the world** — those heedless of Allah's command who possessed the delights of wealth and children. Ibn Kathir's correction is the sharp point here: those worldly benefits were **istidraj — gradual punishment and temporary respite — not honour and not blessing**.

> *And they have plotted a mighty plot.*

The plot was aimed at their own followers: a deception that tricked them into believing they were following truth and correct guidance. It is the same relationship the followers will throw back at their leaders on the Day of Judgement:

> *Nay, but it was your plotting by night and day, when you ordered us to disbelieve in Allah and set up rivals to Him!* (34:33)

---

## 6. How Idolatry Began (71:23)

> *You shall not leave your gods, nor shall you leave Wadd, nor Suwa`, nor Yaghuth, and Ya`uq and Nasr.*

These are the names of the idols they worshipped besides Allah — and **Al-Bukhari records from Ibn `Abbas that the same idols later resurfaced among the Arabs**:

| Idol | Tribe that took it up |
|---|---|
| **Wadd** | Kalb, at Dawmat Al-Jandal |
| **Suwa`** | Hudhayl |
| **Yaghuth** | Murad, then Bani Ghutayf at Al-Juruf in Saba' |
| **Ya`uq** | Hamdan |
| **Nasr** | Himyar, for the family of Dhu Kala` |

### The mechanism — and it is not what you'd expect

Ibn `Abbas's account is the heart of this section, and every step of it is reasonable except the last:

1. These were the names of **righteous men** among the people of Nuh.
2. When they died, Shaytan inspired the people to **erect statues in their honour** at their usual gathering places, and to name the statues after them.
3. They did so — **and the statues were not worshipped**.
4. Only after that generation died and **the knowledge was lost** did the statues begin to be worshipped.

Reported similarly from `Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah and Ibn Ishaq.

Ibn Jarir preserves a second version from **Muhammad bin Qays**: these were righteous men living between Adam and Nuh, with followers who adhered to their guidance. When they died their companions said, *if we make images of them, it will increase our desire to worship when we remember them.* Generations later, **Iblis came to their descendants and told them: your predecessors used to worship these statues, and it was through them that they were granted rain.** So they worshipped them.

Nobody in the story sets out to commit shirk. It takes three generations, a good intention, a memorial, and a forgotten context.

## 7. The Supplication Against Them (71:23–24)

> *And indeed they have led many astray. Grant no increase to the wrongdoers save error.*

By means of the idols they adopted, they misled an enormous number — and Ibn Kathir notes the consequence reaches into his own time and ours: **the worship of those same idols continued through generations, among Arabs, non-Arabs and all the groups of the Children of Adam.**

Ibrahim made the same complaint about the same problem:

> *Keep me and my sons away from worshipping idols. My Lord! They have indeed led astray many among mankind.* (14:35–36)

And Nuh's supplication against his people — made after rebellion, disbelief and obstinacy — has its exact parallel in Musa's against Fir`awn:

> *Our Lord! Destroy their wealth, and harden their hearts, so that they will not believe until they see the painful torment.* (10:88)

**Allah answered both prophets, and drowned both nations.**

---

## 8. Drowned, Then the Fire (71:25)

> *Because of their sins they were drowned, then were made to enter the Fire. And they found none to help them instead of Allah.*

(An alternate recitation reads *because of their errors*.) They were drowned for their numerous sins, their rebellion, their persistence in disbelief and their opposition to the Messengers.

Ibn Kathir's phrase for the sequence is unforgettable: **they will be carried from the flood of the seas to the heat of the Fire.** No helper, assistant or saviour could rescue them from the punishment of Allah — as in:

> *This day there is no saviour from the decree of Allah except him on whom He has mercy.* (11:43)

---

## 9. "Leave Not One" (71:26–27)

> *My Lord! Leave not one of the disbelievers on the earth Dayyar!*

Not a single one on the face of the earth, not even a lone individual — a construction that gives maximum emphasis to the negation. **Ad-Dahhak**: *Dayyar* means one. **As-Suddi**: it is the one who stays in the home.

Allah answered it, and destroyed every disbeliever on the face of the earth — including **Nuh's own son from his own loins**, who separated himself from his father, said *I will betake myself to some mountain, it will save me from the water*, and was answered with *this day there is no saviour from the decree of Allah except him on whom He has mercy* — and then the waves came in between them (11:43). Only those who believed and boarded the ship were saved.

> *If You leave them, they will mislead Your servants, and they will beget none but wicked disbelievers.*

Wicked in their deeds, disbelieving in their hearts. Ibn Kathir is careful about how Nuh could know this: **he said it on the basis of what he had learned about them over nine hundred and fifty years among them.** It is a judgement earned by nearly a millennium of evidence, not a guess.

---

## 10. The Last Words: A Supplication to Copy (71:28)

> *My Lord! Forgive me, and my parents, and him who enters my home as a believer, and all the believing men and women.*

**Ad-Dahhak** read "my home" as *my masjid*. Ibn Kathir sees no harm in the plain sense: Nuh supplicated for **every believer who entered his house**.

And the final clause is the one Ibn Kathir wants you to take away as practice: he prayed for **all believing men and women — the living among them and the dead among them**. For this reason, *it is recommended to supplicate in this way*, following Nuh's example and the well-known legislated supplications.

The surah then closes as it must:

> *And to the wrongdoers, grant You no increase but destruction.*

**As-Suddi**: destruction. **Mujahid**: loss. Ibn Kathir: **in both this life and the Hereafter.**

## If You Remember Nothing Else

- **Nuh's obligation was clarity, not conversion.** *I am a plain warner.* He delivered it night and day, publicly, privately, aloud, for 950 years — and won almost no one. The surah records this as a complaint to Allah, never as a failure of Nuh.
- **Istighfar is the key to the sky.** Ask forgiveness → continuous rain, wealth, children, gardens, rivers. `Umar prayed for rain by doing nothing but seeking forgiveness. Provision follows repentance more reliably than it follows scheming.
- **Wealth and children are not a scoreboard.** Nuh's people had both and followed the men who had most of them. Ibn Kathir: that was *istidraj* — gradual punishment dressed as favour, not honour and not blessing.
- **Idolatry began with a memorial to good men.** Statues erected in honour, not worship; venerated only after the generation that understood them had died and the knowledge was lost. **Shirk is what happens when reverence outlives explanation.**
- **Look at your own construction.** A drop, a clot, a lump of flesh; heavens in tiers, a moon that measures the months, an earth spread flat with roads through it. The argument Nuh makes is not obscure — it is the ceiling above you and the body you're standing in.
- **Pray for the believers, living and dead.** Nuh's last recorded words include his parents, his guests, and every believing man and woman who ever was. Ibn Kathir recommends you make it your habit too.
