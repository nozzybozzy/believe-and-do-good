*Revealed in Makkah · 60 ayat · named for the Romans, whose defeat and promised return open the surah*

---

## 0. What This Surah Is Doing

Ar-Rum opens with a wager. A small, persecuted community in Makkah is told that a distant empire, freshly beaten and besieged in its own capital, will win within a decade — and the surah stakes its credibility on that happening. It did. From there the surah does something unexpected: having proved that Allah controls the outcome of an empire, it turns to prove He controls everything smaller and closer — sleep, rainfall, marriage, skin colour, the ageing of a body.

The whole surah is a catalogue of **signs** (*wa min ayatihi* — "and among His signs"), and its argument is cumulative: the One who moves armies also stitches affection between two strangers and revives dead soil, so the One who does all that can certainly raise the dead. Running underneath is a diagnosis of the human being — sincere in a crisis, forgetful in comfort, exultant in blessing, despairing in loss — and a prescription for it: the **Fitrah**, the original disposition, and the five prayers that keep returning to it.

---

## 1. The Prophecy: The Romans Will Return (30:1–5)

> *Alif Lam Mim. The Romans have been defeated. In the nearest land, and they, after their defeat, will be victorious. In Bid'i years.*

The setting: **Sabur**, king of Persia, had overrun Ash-Sham, the partisan states of the Arabian Peninsula, and the outlying Roman territories. **Heraclius** was forced to flee to Constantinople and was besieged there for a long period. Then the tide turned.

Ibn Kathir gives two narrations of what happened in Makkah when this was revealed.

**Imam Ahmad, from Ibn Abbas:** the idolaters wanted Persia to prevail because the Persians were idol-worshippers; the Muslims wanted Rome to prevail because the Romans were People of the Book. Abu Bakr took it to the Prophet ﷺ, who said: *"They will certainly prevail."* Abu Bakr relayed this to the idolaters, who proposed a wager with a time limit. He set **five years** — and the Romans did not win. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Why do you not make it less than…"* — the narrator thought he meant **less than ten**. Sa'id bin Jubayr: *Bid'* means less than ten. Then the Romans won.

**At-Tirmidhi, from Niyar bin Mukram Al-Aslami** — the fuller version. On the day the ayat came down, Persia was on top. The Muslims wanted Rome to win because both were peoples of a Book; the Quraysh wanted Persia, because neither Persia nor Quraysh followed a Book or believed in the Resurrection. Abu Bakr went out proclaiming the verses through Makkah. The Quraysh challenged him to bet on it — *this was before betting was forbidden* — and argued that since *Bid'* is between three and nine, they should split the difference at **six years**. Six years passed with no Roman victory, and the idolaters collected. **In the seventh year the Romans won**, and the Muslims rebuked Abu Bakr for conceding six. His answer: *"Because Allah said: In Bid' years."*

> **At that time, many people became Muslim.**

---

## 2. Who the Romans Were (30:2, continued)

Ibn Kathir pauses for a historical excursus, because the audience needs to know who this promise concerns.

- They are descendants of **Al-'Iys bin Ishaq bin Ibrahim** — cousins of the Children of Isra'il, also called **Bani Al-Asfar**.
- They had followed the religion of the Greeks, descendants of **Yafith bin Nuh**. They **worshipped the seven planets and prayed facing the North Pole**. They founded Damascus and built its temple with a prayer niche facing north.
- They stayed on this for roughly three hundred years after the Messiah.

**Constantine**, son of Costas, was the first to enter Christianity — his mother Maryam Al-Hilaniyyah, from the land of Harran, had converted first and invited him; he had been a philosopher. Ibn Kathir notes it was said his belief was **merely an outward show**.

Then came the dispute with **Abdullah bin Ariyus (Arius)**, and a gathering of **three hundred and eighteen bishops** produced a creed for Constantine —

> *"This is what they call the Great Trust, but in fact it is the Worst Betrayal."*

Ibn Kathir lists the changes made to the religion of the Messiah: praying towards the East, moving the Sabbath rites to Sunday, worship of the cross, permitting pork, new observances (the festival of the cross, Mass, baptism, Palm Sunday), and a whole hierarchy — pope, patriarchs, metropolitans, bishops, priests, deacons — plus the invention of monasticism. Constantine built churches and founded the city named after him. Later came the **Jacobites** (after Ya'qub Al-Askaf) and the **Nestorians** (after Nestorius), in line with the hadith: *"They split into seventy-two sects."*

The last Caesar in this line was **Heraclius** — a wise, astute man of deep insight — opposed by **Chosroes**, whose kingdom was larger, and who led the Zoroastrian fire-worshippers.

---

## 3. How Caesar Turned It Around

A remarkable passage of narrative history. Chosroes overwhelmed Caesar until **nothing was left to him but Constantinople**, which held only because it was fortified, with half facing land and half facing sea — supplies came in by water.

So Caesar devised a trick. He asked Chosroes to let him leave the city in return for a peace-payment on any terms. Chosroes demanded an amount of gold, jewels, fabric and servants **no king on earth could pay** — the two of them combined could not have raised a tenth of it. Caesar gave the impression he had it, privately thinking the man mad, and asked leave to travel to Ash-Sham to gather it from his storehouses.

Chosroes let him go. Before leaving, Caesar told his people: if I return within a year I am still your king; if not, the choice is yours — remain loyal or appoint another.

## 4. Reflect on Yourselves; Travel the Earth (30:8–10)

> *Do they not reflect upon themselves! Not but for just ends and for a term appointed did Allah create the heavens and the earth.*

Ibn Kathir: think about how Allah made the upper realms, the lower, and the space between — **not in jest or in vain, but in truth**, and lasting until an appointed time.

Then the argument from history. *Do they not travel in the land* — meaning, do they not understand, think, see and hear about the people of the past. Those nations **were stronger than you**, had more wealth and sons, stayed longer in the world, were more civilised and more prosperous in the land. *You have not been given one-tenth of what they were given.* And when the Messengers came to them mid-luxury, they were punished for their sins, and nothing shielded them.

> *It was not Allah Who wronged them, but they used to wrong themselves.*

They brought it on themselves by rejecting and mocking the signs. Ibn Kathir sets this beside 6:110 (*We shall turn their hearts and eyes away, as they refused to believe the first time*), 61:5 (*when they turned away, Allah turned their hearts away*), and 5:49.

---

## 5. The Day of Separation (30:11–16)

Just as He was able to create in the first place, He is able to repeat it — then to Him you will be returned, each requited by his deeds.

- *The criminals will be plunged into destruction with despair* — **Ibn Abbas**: filled with despair. **Mujahid**: exposed; in another report, they will grieve.
- **No intercessors** from the partners they set up: the false gods will not intercede, and *they will reject and deny their partners* — betraying them at the moment of their most desperate need.
- *That Day shall they be separated.* **Qatadah**: *"By Allah, this refers to the separation after which there will be no reunion."* One taken to the highest heights, another to the lowest depths — the last they will ever see of each other.

Then the two destinations, stated bluntly side by side: those who believed and did righteous deeds, **honoured and made to enjoy a luxurious life in a Garden of Delight** (Mujahid and Qatadah: they will enjoy a life of luxury); and those who disbelieved and denied the meeting of the Hereafter, **brought forth to the torment**.

---

## 6. Glorify Him at the Turning Points of the Day (30:17–19)

> *So glorify Allah when you enter the evening and when you enter the morning… and in 'Ashiyya and when Tuzhirun.*

Ibn Kathir reads these as **the command to pray five times daily** — Allah glorifies Himself and commands His servants to do the same at times that come one after another and testify to His power: the night arriving with darkness, the morning with light. *'Ashiyy* is when darkness is most intense; *Izhar* is the brightest point of the day.

> *Glory be to the One Who created both of them, the Cleaver of the daybreak and the One Who makes night a time of rest.*

He then strings together the oaths of the short surahs on the same theme — 91:3–4, 92:1–2, 93:1–2.

**And He brings the living from the dead, and the dead from the living.** This is His power to create a thing and its opposite:

> the plant from the seed and the seed from the plant; the egg from the chicken and the chicken from the egg; man from sperm and sperm from man; **the believer from the disbelievers and the disbeliever from the believers.**

And He revives the earth after its death — *and thus shall you be brought out.*

---

## 7. Sign: You Were Made From Dust (30:20)

Your father Adam was created from dust — *and then, behold, you are human beings scattered.*

Ibn Kathir traces the arc in one breath: dust, then a despised liquid, then a clot, then a lump of flesh, then bones, then flesh clothing the bones, then the soul breathed in and he can hear and see. Then he comes from his mother's womb **small and weak** — and the longer he lives the stronger he becomes, until he builds cities and strongholds, crosses lands and seas, earns and amasses, and grows smart and crafty with opinions of his own.

> Glory be to the One Who enabled them and made it easy for them to learn every kind of skill for a living, and caused them to vary in knowledge and intellect, in beauty and ugliness, wealth and poverty.

**Imam Ahmad, from Abu Musa** — the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"Allah created Adam from a handful taken from throughout the earth. Hence the sons of Adam vary as the earth varies, so they are white and red and black and (colours) in between, evil and good, easy-going or difficult — or something in between."*

---

## 8. Sign: Spouses, Affection and Mercy (30:21)

> *And among His signs is this, that He created for you wives from among yourselves, that you may find repose in them, and He has put between you affection and mercy.*

Meaning: **females of your own kind**. Ibn Kathir sets it beside 7:189 and refers it to Hawwa', created from Adam — and then makes the argument that gives the verse its force:

> If Allah had made all of Adam's progeny male and created the females from another kind — from Jinn or animals — **there would never have been harmony between them and their spouses**. There would have been revulsion, the spouses being of a different kind.

*And He has put between you affection and mercy* — out of His perfect mercy. And Ibn Kathir is unsentimental about how those two work in practice: a man stays with a woman **because he loves her**, or **because he feels compassion for her** if they have a child together, or **because she needs him to take care of her**.

---

## 9. Sign: Languages and Faces (30:22)

The heavens in their vast height and the beauty of stars and planets; the earth in its density, mountains, valleys, seas, plains, animals and trees. And then the differences among people.

**Languages**: Arabic, and the tongues of the Tatars, Georgians, Romans, Franks, Berbers, Tou Couleurs, Ethiopians, Indians, Persians, Slavs, Khazars, Armenians, Kurds and others. *Only Allah knows the variety of languages spoken among the sons of Adam.*

**Faces**: from the creation of Adam until the Hour, every human has two eyes, two eyebrows, a nose, a forehead, a mouth, two cheeks — and **none of them looks like another**. There is bound to be some difference in posture, appearance or speech, whether obvious or only noticeable on careful observation. Even where a group shares a beautiful or ugly feature, there is still a difference between one person and the next.

*Signs for men of sound knowledge.*

## 10. Sign: Sleep, Lightning, and a Standing Sky (30:23–25)

- **Sleep by night and by day, and seeking His bounty**: the cycle in which people cease moving and rest so exhaustion goes away, and the daytime enabling of earning and travel — *the opposite of sleep*. A sign *for a people who listen* — meaning, Ibn Kathir says, **understand**.
- **Lightning, for fear and for hope**: sometimes you fear the heavy rain and destructive thunderbolts that follow; sometimes the flash gives hope that much-needed rain is coming. And the water sent down revives the dead earth — *a clear sign and proof of the resurrection.*
- **The heaven and the earth stand by His command** — as in 22:65 and 35:41.

> Whenever **Umar bin Al-Khattab** swore an emphatic oath, he would say: *"No, by the One by Whose command the heaven and the earth stand."*

Then, on the Day the earth is exchanged for another earth, one call — and they come out. Ibn Kathir lines up 17:52, 79:13–14 and 36:53: *it will be but a single Sayhah, and behold, they will all be brought up before Us.*

---

## 11. Repeating Creation Is Easier (30:26–27)

Everything in the heavens and earth belongs to Him and is **obedient to Him — humble and submitting, willingly or unwillingly.**

*And He it is Who originates the creation, then repeats it; and this is easier for Him.* Ibn Abbas: it means it is easier for Him. **Mujahid**: repeating it is easier for Him than originating it — *and originating it is easy for Him.*

**Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah** — Allah says:

> *"The son of Adam denied Me, and he had no right to do so. And he reviled Me, and he had no right to do so.*
> *As for his denying Me, it is his saying: 'He will not remake me as He originated me' — while originating the creation is not easier for Me than re-creating him.*
> *As for his reviling Me, it is his saying: 'Allah has taken to Himself a son' — while I am the One, the Self-Sufficient Master; I beget not, nor was I begotten, and there is none comparable to Me."*

*His is the highest description in the heavens and the earth.* Ibn Abbas glosses it with 42:11 — *there is nothing like Him*. **Qatadah**: His description is *La ilaha illallah*.

---

## 12. The Parable That Undoes Shirk (30:28–29)

> *Do you have partners among those whom your right hands possess, to share as equals in the wealth We have bestowed on you, whom you fear as you fear each other?*

The idolaters' own Talbiyah admitted the problem. They used to say:

> *"At Your service, You have no partner **except the partner that You have — You own him and whatever he owns.**"*

So the parable is drawn from their own lives: **none of you would accept his own servant as an equal partner in his wealth**, sharing it and having to fear his claim on it. **Abu Mijlaz**: you do not fear that your servant will share your wealth, because he has no such right — similarly, Allah has no partner.

> The point is, that since any one of you would abhor such a thing, **how can you attribute rivals to Allah from among His creation?**

And when they do it anyway, it is *following their own lusts without knowledge* — worship of false gods on no evidence. *Then who will guide him whom Allah has sent astray?* No one can save them from His power or find them a way out: **what He wills, happens; what He does not will, does not happen.**

---

## 13. The Fitrah (30:30) — the Centre of the Surah

> *So set your face towards the religion as a Hanif. Allah's Fitrah, with which He has created mankind. No change let there be in Allah's Khalq. That is the straight religion, but most men know not.*

Set your face and persevere in the religion Allah prescribed — the worship of Allah alone, the religion of Ibrahim, perfected for you. Do that, and **you are also adhering to the sound Fitrah on which He created His creation.**

Allah created His creation to recognise Him and know His Tawhid — the covenant of 7:172, *"Am I not your Lord?" They said: "Yes."* And in the Hadith Qudsi:

> *"I created My servants Hunafa (monotheists), then the Shayatin misled them from their religion."*

**On "no change let there be in Allah's Khalq"**, Ibn Kathir gives two readings:
1. *Do not change the creation of Allah* — for if you do, you divert people away from the Fitrah on which they were made. (A command in the form of a statement, like *whoever enters it is safe*.) He calls this "a good and correct interpretation."
2. Allah made all His creation **equal in this respect** — all born with the same sound, upright nature, with no disparity among people in it.

And the reading of Ibn Abbas, Ibrahim An-Nakha'i, Sa'id bin Jubayr, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatadah, Ad-Dahhak and Ibn Zayd — followed by Al-Bukhari — is that *Khalq* here means **the religion of Allah**; and the Fitrah is Islam.

**Al-Bukhari, from Abu Hurayrah** — the Prophet ﷺ said:

> *"No child is born except in a state of Fitrah, then his parents make him a Jew or a Christian or a Zoroastrian — just as each animal produces a perfect animal like itself. Do you see any among them that are born mutilated?"*

— and Abu Hurayrah would then recite this very ayah. (Also recorded by Muslim.)

---

## 14. Turn Back, Fear Him, Pray — and Do Not Split (30:31–32)

*Turning in repentance to Him* — Ibn Zayd and Ibn Jurayj: **returning to Him**. *And have Taqwa of Him* — fear Him, and remember He is always watching. *And perform the Salah* — **the greatest act of worship**. *And be not of the idolaters* — be of those who single Him out, devoting worship sincerely to Him alone.

**Ibn Jarir, from Yazid bin Abi Maryam:** Umar passed Mu'adh bin Jabal and asked, *"What is the foundation of this Ummah?"* Mu'adh said, three things, and they are the things that bring salvation:

- **Al-Ikhlas** — doing a deed for Allah alone, which is the Fitrah on which He created mankind;
- **Salah** — which is the thing that tells a believer apart from a disbeliever;
- **Obedience** — which is protection.

Umar said: *"You have spoken the truth."*

**On splitting into sects**: *Farraqu Dinahum* — they changed their religion, believing parts and rejecting parts. Another reading, *Faraqu Dinahum*: **they neglected their religion and left it behind them.** Ibn Kathir applies this to Jews, Christians, Zoroastrians, idol-worshippers and every follower of a false religion, citing 6:159.

The Ummah too has split, all of them misguided apart from one — **Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah**, those adhering to the Book and the Sunnah and what the first generations followed. Al-Hakim's narration, when the Messenger ﷺ was asked which sect was saved:

> *"What I and my Companions are upon."*

---

## 15. The Human Being in Crisis and in Comfort (30:33–37)

> *And when harm touches men, they cry sincerely only to their Lord… but when He gives them a taste of His mercy, behold, a party of them associates partners with their Lord.*

In dire straits, man calls on Allah alone. When ease returns and he has the choice, some go back to worshipping others. Then the warning: *Then enjoy — but you will come to know.* Ibn Kathir quotes one of the Salaf:

> *"By Allah, if a law enforcement officer were to say this to me, I would be afraid — so how about when the One issuing the warning is the One Who merely says to a thing 'Be!' and it is."*

*Or have We revealed to them an authority which speaks of what they associate?* — a rhetorical question of denunciation: **they have no such thing.**

Then the mirror image: given mercy they rejoice; struck by the consequence of their own hands, **they despair**. Man in blessing becomes proud and boastful — *"Ills have departed from me"* (11:10) — and in difficulty despairs of ever having good again. The exception is *those who show patience and do righteous good deeds*: **patient in difficulty, active in good at times of ease.**

> *"How wonderful is the affair of the believer. Allah does not decree anything for him but it is good for him. If good things happen to him he gives thanks, and that is good for him; and if bad things happen to him he bears it with patience, and that is good for him."*

And the expansion and restriction of provision is **His wisdom and justice**, not an accident.

---

## 16. Kinship, Riba, and the Charity That Grows (30:38–40)

- **The kindred** — his due of respect and the upholding of ties.
- **Al-Miskin** — the one with nothing to spend on his needs, or with something insufficient.
- **The wayfarer** — the traveller in need of money and other things on his journey.

*That is best for those who seek Allah's Face* — meaning **to look upon Him on the Day of Resurrection, which is the ultimate aim.**

**The Riba here is not the loan-interest of the fiqh chapters.** Ibn Kathir, following Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ad-Dahhak, Qatadah, Ikrimah, Muhammad bin Ka'b and Ash-Sha'bi, reads it as **a gift given in the hope of getting back more than was given**. There is no reward with Allah for that.

Against it, Zakah sought for Allah's Face — *those shall have manifold increase.* And the hadith from the Sahih:

> *"No person gives in charity the equivalent of a date earned in a lawful manner, but the Most Merciful takes it in His Right Hand and takes care of it for its owner — just as any one of you takes care of his foal or young camel — until the date becomes the size of Mount Uhud."*

Then the four verbs no partner shares: **He created you, provided for you, will cause you to die, then will give you life.** He brings man from the womb naked, knowing nothing, unable to see or hear, without strength — and then supplies him with everything. *Is there any of your partners that can do anything of that?* **None of them.**

## 17. Corruption on Land and Sea (30:41–42)

> *Evil has appeared in Al-Barr and Al-Bahr because of what the hands of men have earned, that He may make them taste a part of what they have done, in order that they may return.*

Ibn Kathir collects the readings:

- **Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, Ad-Dahhak, As-Suddi**: *Al-Barr* is the empty land or wilderness; *Al-Bahr* is towns and cities (in another report from Ibn Abbas and Ikrimah, towns on riverbanks).
- **Others**: the plain meaning — land and sea.
- **Zayd bin Rafi'**: rain is withheld from the land, and famine follows; and withheld from the sea, harming the animals that live in it.
- **Mujahid**: *"Evil on land means the killing of the son of Adam, and evil on the sea means piracy."*

On the first reading, the shortfall in crops and fruits **is because of sins**. **Abu Al-Aliyah**: *"Whoever disobeys Allah in the earth has corrupted it, because the good condition of the earth and the heavens depends on obedience to Allah."*

Hence the hadith recorded by Abu Dawud: *any prescribed punishment carried out in the land is better for its people than if it were to rain for forty days* — because enforcement deters people from violating Allah's prohibitions, and giving up sin becomes a cause of blessing from the sky and the earth.

Ibn Kathir extends the principle to the end of time: when 'Isa bin Maryam descends and judges by this Shari'ah, and Allah destroys the Dajjal and Ya'juj and Ma'juj, **the earth will be told to bring forth its blessing** — a group will eat from one pomegranate and shade beneath its skin, and one camel's milk will suffice a whole group. And the rule he draws from it:

> **The more justice is established, the more the blessings and good things will increase.**

Two closing images. From the Sahih: *"When the evildoer dies, it is a relief for the people, the land, the trees and the animals."* And from Imam Ahmad, via Abu Qahdham — in the time of Ziyad or Ibn Ziyad, a man found a cloth wrapping grains of wheat **as big as date stones**, and written on it:

> *"This grew at a time when justice prevailed."*

## 18. Sign: The Winds and the Rain (30:46–51)

The winds come as **harbingers of His mercy** — meaning rain will follow — giving you a taste of that mercy, driving the ships, and enabling you to seek His bounty by trading, earning and travelling from one land to another. *In order that you may be thankful* — for favours innumerable, **both visible and hidden**.

And the mechanics of the rain, described with real attention: He raises the clouds, spreads them across the sky and causes them to increase and grow — *from a little He makes a lot* — until they fill the horizon; then He breaks them into fragments (**Mujahid, Abu Amr bin Al-Ala', Matar Al-Warraq, Qatadah**: pieces; **Ad-Dahhak**: piled up; others: black, because so full of water) — until you see the drops come out from their midst.

The psychological detail is the point:

> *And verily, before that — just before it was sent down upon them — they were in despair.*

They had been waiting for the rain at its due time and it did not come. It was late. A long time passed. Then it came **suddenly, after they had begun to despair**, and after their land had gone dry and barren — so it was a tremendous event for them.

*Look then at the effects of Allah's mercy, how He revives the earth after its death.* This is the direct analogy for **the revival of bodies after they have died and disintegrated into nothing**. The One Who does that is able to raise the dead.

And the ingratitude test: *if We send a wind and they see it turn yellow* — the crops they tended and cultivated to maturity now drying and rotting — **they would become unthankful**, denying every earlier blessing.

Between these, one interjection worth keeping (30:47): *it was incumbent upon Us to help the believers* — **a duty Allah took upon Himself** as a favour to them, like *your Lord has prescribed mercy for Himself* (6:54). And from Abu Ad-Darda':

> *"No Muslim man defends the honour of his brother except that there would be a right upon Allah to defend him from the fire of Hell on the Day of Resurrection."*

---

## 19. You Cannot Make the Dead Hear (30:52–53)

> *So verily, you cannot make the dead to hear, nor can you make the deaf to hear the call when they show their backs and turn away.*

Just as you cannot make the dead in their graves hear you, and cannot get your words to a deaf man who is walking away, **so you cannot guide the blind out of their misguidance**. That rests with Allah alone — by His power He could make the dead hear the voices of the living if He willed.

*You can make to hear only those who believe in Our Ayat and have submitted* — those who are humble, who respond and obey. That is the state of the believers; deafness and blindness is the state of the disbelievers (cf. 6:36).

**The dispute at the well of Badr.** A'ishah used this ayah against Ibn Umar, who had reported that the Prophet ﷺ addressed the slain disbelievers thrown into a dry well three days after Badr, rebuking them, until Umar asked, *"O Messenger of Allah, are you addressing people who are dead bodies?"* He ﷺ said:

> *"By the One in Whose Hand is my soul, you do not hear what I say any better than they do, but they cannot respond."*

A'ishah interpreted the event as the Prophet ﷺ making the point that **they would now know that what he had told them was true**. **Qatadah** took the other view: Allah brought them back to life for him, so they could hear the rebuke.

---

## 20. Weakness, Strength, Weakness (30:54)

> *Allah is He Who created you in weakness, then gave you strength after weakness, then after strength gave you weakness and grey hair.*

Man passes through stage after stage. Dust, then a Nutfah, then a clot, then a lump of flesh, then bones, then flesh clothing them, then the soul. Then out of the womb — **weak, thin and powerless**. Then growth little by little: child, puberty, young man — *strength after weakness*. Then middle age, old age, senility — *weakness after strength* — losing resolve, movement and the ability to fight, hair turning grey, and his characteristics **both inward and outward** beginning to change.

*He creates what He wills* — doing whatever He wills and directing His servants however He wants.

---

## 21. The Hour, and the Lie Told at the End (30:55–57)

The criminals will swear they stayed **not but an hour**. Ibn Kathir reads this as ignorance carried over: in this world they worship idols, and in the Hereafter they display the same ignorance. Their meaning is that they were never given time enough for proof to be established against them — **an excuse, offered under oath.**

And the answer comes from *those bestowed with knowledge and Faith*, who established Allah's proof against them in this world and now do it again: *you stayed according to the decree of Allah* — **the Book of deeds** — *until the Day of Resurrection*, i.e. from the day you were created until the day you were raised. *So this is the Day of Resurrection — but you knew not.*

> *So, on that Day, no excuse of theirs will avail those who did wrong, nor will they be allowed to return to seek Allah's pleasure.*

Meaning: **they will not be allowed to return to this world** (cf. 41:24).

---

## 22. The Closing Command: Be Patient (30:58–60)

Every kind of parable has been set forth — the truth explained and made clear so they may understand it and follow it. And still: bring them any sign at all and they call it *falsehood and magic*, as they did when the moon was split. Ibn Kathir cites 10:96–97: *even if every sign should come to them, until they see the painful torment.*

> *So be patient. Verily, the promise of Allah is true; and let not those who have no certainty of faith discourage you.*

Bear their stubborn opposition patiently — **Allah will fulfil His promise** to grant you victory and to make you and those who follow you prevail in this world and the Hereafter.

> *Remain steadfast in the mission with which Allah has sent you, for it is truth in which there is no doubt. Do not turn away from it, for nowhere else is there truth to be followed; the truth rests exclusively in the Message with which you have been sent.*

## 23. Its Virtue: Recited at Fajr

**Imam Ahmad**, from a man among the Companions: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ led them in **Fajr** and recited **Ar-Rum**, but became confused in his recitation. He said:

> *"We have become confused in our recitation of Qur'an, because some people among you are praying with us but they have not performed Wudu' properly. Whoever attends the prayer with us, let him perform Wudu' properly."*

Ibn Kathir calls the chain **Hasan** and the text Hasan, and draws out what he finds amazing in it: **the Prophet ﷺ was affected by the faulty Wudu' of those he was leading** — which shows that the prayer of one praying in congregation is connected to the prayer of the Imam.

## The Thread

Ar-Rum begins by asking you to believe something you cannot yet see — that a beaten empire will rise within nine years — and it ends by asking you to be patient with people who will not believe anything they *can* see. Between those two demands it lays out its evidence, and the evidence is almost entirely ordinary: dust, sleep, marriage, faces, languages, lightning, clouds, grey hair.

The logic is one move repeated. **The One who revives the dead earth revives the dead body.** The rain that arrives after despair, at the moment the land has gone barren and the waiting has failed, is not a decoration on the argument — it *is* the argument, and it is also a portrait of the believer's situation in Makkah when the surah came down.

And its diagnosis of us is unflattering and exact: sincere when drowning, forgetful when saved; boastful in blessing, despairing in loss. The cure it offers is not new information but **a return** — to the Fitrah every child is born on, kept alive by the five prayers that mark the day's turning points, and expressed in the three things Mu'adh named: sincerity, prayer, and obedience.

Meanwhile the surah quietly insists that public life obeys the same law as private life. Corruption on land and sea comes from what human hands have earned; the more justice is established, the more the blessing. Somewhere a man once found grains of wheat the size of date stones, wrapped in cloth, with a note explaining them:

> *This grew at a time when justice prevailed.*

*This is the end of the Tafsir of Surah Ar-Rum. Allah's is the praise and thanks.*
