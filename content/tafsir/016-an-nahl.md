*Revealed in Makkah · 128 ayat · "The Bee"*

---

## 1. It Opens with the Hour — in the Past Tense

> **"The Event ordained by Allah has come, so do not seek to hasten it."** (16:1)

Ibn Kathir's first observation is grammatical, and it sets the tone for everything after. Allah announces the Hour using a *past-tense* verb — not because it has happened, but to confirm that it undoubtedly will. What was far is now near. The same construction appears in 21:1 and 54:1.

He then records the hadith of `Uqbah bin `Amir: as the Hour approaches, a black cloud like a shield rises from the west, and a voice calls out three times, *"O mankind!"* — people asking each other whether they heard it, some believing, some doubting — until the third call delivers this very verse. And then:

> *"Two men will spread out a cloth and never fold it again; a man will prepare his water trough and never water his animals from it; a man will milk his camel and never drink the milk."*

The surah that follows is, in effect, an answer to the question that verse raises: *if this is coming, what have you been living inside of all along?*

---

## 2. The Spine of the Surah: A Catalogue of Blessings

More than any other surah, An-Nahl is an inventory. Ibn Kathir walks through it item by item, and the cumulative effect is the argument:

| The blessing | What Ibn Kathir draws from it |
|---|---|
| **Cattle** (16:5–7) | Warmth from wool and hair, milk, meat — and *beauty*: "when you bring them home in the evening and lead them out in the morning." Flanks fattened, udders full, humps grown. They carry loads to lands you could not otherwise reach — Hajj, `Umrah, campaigns, trade. |
| **Horses, mules, donkeys** (16:8) | For riding *and adornment* — adornment named as a purpose, not an afterthought. And then: *"He creates things you have no knowledge of"* — the door left open. |
| **Rain** (16:10–11) | Fresh, not salty. Drink for you, pasture for your animals, then olives, dates, grapes, "every kind of fruit" — one water producing endless variety of taste, colour, scent and shape. |
| **Night, day, sun, moon, stars** (16:12) | Each travelling in its own ordained orbit, deviating in nothing. |
| **The sea** (16:14) | Fresh meat lawful whether caught alive or dead, even in ihram; pearls and ornaments; ships. Ibn Kathir notes shipbuilding is the inheritance of Nuh — the first to sail, taught how, and the knowledge passed down ever since. |
| **Mountains, rivers, roads, landmarks, stars** (16:15–16) | Mountains stabilise the earth. Rivers arise in one place to feed people in another, meandering north and south, great and small, some flowing always and some seasonally. He even opened gaps *between* mountains so there would be roads. |
| **Spouses, children, grandchildren** (16:72) | Mates "of your own kind" — had they been of another kind there would be no harmony, love or mercy between them. |
| **Hearing, sight, and hearts** (16:78) | Brought out of your mothers' wombs knowing nothing, then given the faculties by which you distinguish harm from benefit — and they develop gradually, growing as you grow. |
| **Birds** (16:79) | Held up in the middle of the sky by nothing but Him. |
| **Homes, tents, furnishings, shade, refuges, clothing, armour** (16:80–81) | Houses of stone to settle in, houses of hide light enough to carry; wool, fur and hair for furnishing and trade; trees for shade, mountains for refuge, garments against the heat, mail against each other's violence. |

And then the verse that closes the ledger:

> **"If you tried to count the favours of Allah, you could never enumerate them. Truly Allah is Forgiving, Most Merciful."** (16:18)

Ibn Kathir catches what is strange here: why *Forgiving* and *Merciful* at the end of a list of gifts? Because — as Ibn Jarir puts it — He forgives you for failing to thank Him properly. If He required you to give thanks fully for all of it, you could not. He forgives much and rewards for little.

## 3. The Pivot at 16:9 — From Roads to *the* Road

Having listed the animals that carry you across physical distance, Allah turns without warning to the routes people take to reach *Him*:

> **"And upon Allah is the direct way; but there are ways that stray. Had He willed, He would have guided you all."**

Ibn Kathir notes this as a recurring Qur'anic move — from the tangible to the spiritual: *"Take provision, but the best provision is taqwa"* (2:197); *"We have granted clothing to cover you and for adornment — but the garment of righteousness is better"* (7:26). Ibn `Abbas glossed "the ways that stray" as the competing religions and whims of the age.

## 4. The Bee — and Why the Surah Carries Her Name

> **"And your Lord inspired the bee: take homes in the mountains, in the trees, and in what they build."** (16:68–69)

The *wahy* here, Ibn Kathir says, means guidance. What follows is one of the most affectionate passages in the whole tafsir:

- Her home is a *solid structure* — hexagonal, interlocking, with no looseness in the comb.
- She is permitted every fruit and given routes made easy, roaming vast wildernesses, valleys and high mountains.
- Then every bee returns **straight** to her hive, swerving neither right nor left, to where her offspring and her honey are.
- She makes wax from her wings, brings honey out of her mouth, lays eggs from her rear — and goes out again the next morning.

> *"There comes from their bellies a drink of varying colours, in which there is healing for people."*

A precise reading follows: the verse says **"a healing,"** not *the* healing — so honey is not a cure-all, but the right treatment for "cold" ailments, since it is "hot," and a disease is treated by its opposite. He cites the man whose brother had a stomach complaint, sent back with honey twice more as the man reported no improvement, until the Prophet ﷺ said, *"Allah has spoken the truth and your brother's stomach has lied"* — and he recovered. And the broader hadith: healing is in three — the cupper's incision, a drink of honey, cauterising with fire — *"but I forbid my Ummah cauterisation."*

The lesson Ibn Kathir extracts is not medical: that Allah inspires *this* small, weak creature to cross vast fields, gather from everything, and produce wax and honey, is a sign for anyone who will think about the Power behind it.

## 5. Milk, and the Discipline of the Body

> **"From between excretions and blood, pure milk, palatable to those who drink."** (16:66)

Ibn Kathir lingers on the anatomy as an argument: after digestion, each thing goes its own way and none contaminates another — blood to the veins, milk to the udder, urine to the bladder, waste to the bowel. Separated, and unaffected by one another. That is the sign.

Immediately after comes the verse on dates and grapes yielding *sakar* (intoxicant) **and** good provision (16:67) — which, he notes, indicates this was **before** intoxicants were forbidden. Ibn `Abbas: the intoxicant is what became unlawful, the good provision is what stayed lawful. And the verse closes by addressing *"people of reason"* — which is the point: Allah later forbade this Ummah intoxicants precisely to protect the noblest faculty in a human being.

## 6. Two Answers to One Question

Twice the same question is asked, and the surah divides humanity by the answer:

| Question | Answer | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| *"What has your Lord sent down?"* (16:24) | **"Tales of the ancients."** | They carry their own burdens in full **plus** a share of the burdens of those they misled — *"whoever calls to misguidance bears a burden like that of those who follow him, without lessening theirs in the least."* |
| *"What has your Lord sent down?"* (16:30) | **"That which is good."** | Good in this world, and the home of the Hereafter is better — Gardens of `Adn, rivers beneath, whatever they wish. |

And the two deaths that follow match the two answers. The wrongdoers, seized by the angels, offer a false submission — *"we used to do no evil"* — and are answered, *"Yes, indeed. Allah knows well what you used to do."* The pious are met with **"Salamun `alaykum — enter Paradise for what you used to do."**

---

## 7. "If Allah had willed, we would not have worshipped others"

The idolaters' central alibi (16:35), and Ibn Kathir's answer is one of the most important theological passages in the surah. The claim was: *if Allah hated what we do, He would have stopped us.*

The refutation turns on a distinction:

- **The legislative will** (what Allah commands and is pleased with) — clear, and delivered to every single nation: *"We sent to every Ummah a Messenger: worship Allah and shun the Taghut"* (16:36). No community was left without warning, from Nuh, the first messenger to the people of the earth, to Muhammad ﷺ, the last.
- **The universal will** (what Allah permits to occur, though it does not please Him) — under which He allowed them to do what was decreed.

He created Hell and its inhabitants, yet He does not *like* disbelief for His servants. So the decree is no argument in their favour: they were forbidden on the tongue of every Messenger sent to them.

## 8. The Daughters — The Surah's Sharpest Rebuke

They assigned daughters to Allah (16:57–59) — attributing offspring to Him who has none, and then attributing the kind of offspring *they themselves did not want*. Ibn Kathir sets the verse against the scene it describes: when one of them is given news of a daughter, his face darkens, he is choked with silent grief, he hides from people — *shall he keep her in humiliation, or bury her in the dust?* Keeping her meant not letting her inherit, not caring for her, preferring the sons.

> *"Evil indeed is their decision."* — evil in what they say, in how they divide, and in what they attribute to Him.

And the closing line of the passage restores the balance: *"For those who do not believe in the Hereafter is an evil description, and for Allah is the highest description."*

The same logic returns at 16:71 in the parable of servants: you would not accept your own servant as an equal shareholder in your wealth — so how do you assign Allah's servants to Him as partners? Ibn `Abbas: *"How can they accept for Me what they will not accept for themselves?"*

## 9. His Forbearance

A theme threaded all the way through:

- Those who plotted before them had their building struck at the foundation, the roof falling on them from above, punishment arriving from a direction they never watched (16:26).
- He could make the earth swallow them, or seize them mid-journey while they are busy going to and fro, or take them by the very thing they most fear (16:45–47) — *"Indeed your Lord is full of kindness, Most Merciful,"* meaning: **He does not hasten it.** *"No one is more patient in the face of offensive speech than Allah — they attribute a son to Him while He gives them provision and health."*
- But the reprieve is not a pardon: *"Allah gives the wrongdoer respite until, when He seizes him, He never lets him go."*
- And 16:61 — if Allah punished people for their wrongdoing immediately, He would not leave a single creature on the earth. Abu Hurayrah, hearing a man say *"the wrongdoer harms only himself,"* corrected him: *"By Allah, no — even the buzzard dies in its nest because of the sins of the wrongdoer."*

Meanwhile everything else in creation is already in submission: shadows leaning right and left, morning and evening, prostrating. Mujahid: *"the prostration of everything is its shadow."* Abu Ghalib: *"the waves of the sea are its prayers."*

## 10. The Most Comprehensive Verse in the Qur'an

> **"Allah commands justice, excellence (ihsan), and giving to relatives; and He forbids indecency, wrongdoing and oppression. He admonishes you, that you may take heed."** (16:90)

Ibn Mas`ud called this **the most comprehensive ayah in the Qur'an**. Ibn Kathir defines the terms: *`adl* is fairness and moderation; *ihsan* is generous good treatment beyond what is owed; *fahsha'* covers all forbidden things and *munkar* those done openly; *baghy* is aggression against people — and *"no sin more deserves that its punishment be hastened in this world, on top of what is stored up in the Hereafter, than tyrannical aggression and severing the ties of kinship."*

Then the eyewitness account of `Uthman bin Maz`un, who was sitting with the Prophet ﷺ when he suddenly stared at the sky, lowered his gaze to the right, turned away, and tilted his head as if listening to something. When it ended, `Uthman asked what had happened. *"A messenger from Allah came to me just now, while you were sitting here."* — And what did he say? — He recited this verse.

> `Uthman: *"That was when faith settled in my heart, and I began to love Muhammad."*

## 11. Oaths, Covenants, and the Woman Who Unravelled Her Thread

Fulfil covenants made in Allah's name; do not break oaths after confirming them, *"having made Allah your guarantor"* (16:91).

> **"Do not be like her who unravelled her yarn after it was strong."** (16:92)

Some said this was a literal woman of Makkah who spun thread and undid it again. Mujahid, Qatadah and Ibn Zayd read it as a parable of covenant-breaking — which Ibn Kathir considers the clearer view, *whether or not such a woman existed*.

The specific practice condemned: making a pact with one group, then finding a stronger and more numerous ally and cancelling the first. Ibn Kathir's note is precise — Allah forbids treachery **in exactly the case where it would be most tempting and most excusable**; how much more so when you are already in a position of strength.

He resolves the apparent tension with the hadith *"if I swear an oath and see something better, I do the better and expiate"*: the oaths under discussion here are the oaths of **covenant and treaty**, not the personal oaths a man makes to spur or restrain himself.

And then the two verses people carry with them:

> *"Whatever you have will be exhausted; what is with Allah remains."* (16:96)
> *"Whoever does righteousness — male or female — while a believer, We will give them a good life."* (16:97)

On that **good life** (*hayat tayyibah*) the Salaf differed productively: lawful provision (Ibn `Abbas), contentment (`Ali), happiness, obedience and the joy found in it (Ad-Dahhak), or nothing less than Paradise (Al-Hasan, Mujahid, Qatadah). Ibn Kathir's verdict: **it includes all of them** — as in the hadith, *"He has succeeded who submits, is granted sufficient provision, and is made content by Allah with what he is given."*

## 12. Under Compulsion: The Concession, and Those Who Refused It

> **"Whoever disbelieves in Allah after his belief — except one who is compelled while his heart is at peace with faith..."** (16:106)

The exemption covers the person who utters words of disbelief under beating and torture while his heart refuses what his mouth says. Ibn Kathir reports scholarly consensus: such a person **may** go along with them to preserve his life, **or** may refuse. Then he tells the stories of those who refused:

- **Bilal**, a boulder on his chest in the desert heat, told to name partners with Allah, answering only *"Ahad, Ahad"* — One, One — and adding, *"By Allah, if I knew a word more annoying to you than this, I would say it."*
- **Habib bin Zayd al-Ansari**, asked by Musaylimah whether he bore witness that Muhammad was the Messenger of Allah — *"Yes."* And that Musaylimah was? — *"I do not hear you."* Cut to pieces, unmoved.
- **`Abdullah bin Hudhafah**, offered a share of the Roman kingdom and the king's daughter, answering that not everything the king and all the Arabs possessed would make him leave the religion of Muhammad ﷺ for an instant. Shown a Muslim prisoner boiled to bare bones, then brought to the same vat, he wept — and the king, thinking he had broken him, asked why. *"Because I have only one soul to give in this vat for Allah's sake. I wish I had as many souls as I have hairs on my body."* Released along with all the Muslim prisoners in exchange for kissing the king's head, he returned to `Umar, who said: *"Every Muslim should kiss the head of `Abdullah bin Hudhafah — and I will be first."*

Ibn Kathir's own judgement: the concession is real, but **steadfastness is better and more beloved**, even to death. And for those who did give way under torture and then emigrated, fought and endured — 16:110 promises that *"after that, your Lord is Forgiving, Most Merciful."*

## 13. Makkah as a Parable

> **"Allah presents the example of a township that was secure and content, its provision coming to it in abundance from every side — then it denied Allah's favours, so He made it taste the garment of hunger and fear."** (16:112)

A sanctuary where a man was safe while people were being snatched away everywhere outside it. The greatest of its blessings was the Messenger sent from among its own people — and that was what it denied. So security became fear of the Prophet's armies, and abundance became the famine years, when they were reduced to eating *`ilhiz*, camel hair mixed with blood.

Ibn Kathir sets the reversal out in full: **the disbelievers went from security to fear and from plenty to hunger; the believers went from fear to security and from poverty to provision, and became rulers and leaders of mankind.** The blessing did not disappear — it changed hands.

This is the surah's diagnosis in a single earlier line:

> **"They recognise the favour of Allah, then they deny it — and most of them are disbelievers."** (16:83)

Not ignorance. Recognition, then denial.

## 14. How to Call People to This

The surah's closing instruction, and one of the most quoted verses in the Qur'an on method:

> **"Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good preaching, and argue with them in the way that is best."** (16:125)

- **Hikmah** — Ibn Jarir: what was revealed to him, the Book and the Sunnah.
- **Good preaching** — exhortation, and the accounts of earlier peoples that warn.
- **Arguing in the best way** — if anyone wants to dispute, meet it with kindness, gentleness and good speech. Ibn Kathir recalls that Musa and Harun were sent to *Pharaoh* with the command *"speak to him gently, that perhaps he may take heed"* (20:44).

And the relief that follows it: *"Your Lord knows best who has strayed from His path."* That is already settled with Him. So call — but do not exhaust yourself with grief over those who turn away. **Guidance is not your assignment; conveying is.**

## 15. The Final Three Verses

> **"If you punish, punish with the like of what you were afflicted with. But if you are patient — it is better for the patient."**
> **"And be patient; your patience is only by Allah. Do not grieve over them, and do not be distressed by their plotting."**
> **"Truly Allah is with those who have taqwa and those who do good."** (16:126–128)

Justice is capped at equivalence — Ibn Sirin: *"if a man takes something from you, take the like of it from him."* Then patience is named as the better of two lawful options. Then patience itself is denied to human strength: *your patience is only by Allah*, by His will, help and power.

Ibn Kathir closes on the last phrase by distinguishing two senses of Allah being *with* someone. There is the **general** sense — knowledge, hearing and sight: *"He is with you wherever you are"* (57:4); *"never three in secret counsel but He is their fourth"* (58:7). And there is the **special** sense, which is what this verse promises: support, help, and victory. It is the sense in which He said to Musa and Harun, *"Fear not; I am with you both, hearing and seeing"* (20:46) — and the sense in which the Prophet ﷺ said to Abu Bakr in the cave:

> *"Do not grieve. Allah is with us."*

---

## The Surah in One Line

An-Nahl spends a hundred verses proving that everything you have was given — the milk, the shade, the road, the mate, the hearing, the honey — so that the only real question left is the one it asks in the middle of the list: **"Is He who creates like one who does not create?"** (16:17)

And its answer to those who see all of it and still turn away is not force, but the bee's method: go out, gather from everything, come straight home, and produce something that heals.
