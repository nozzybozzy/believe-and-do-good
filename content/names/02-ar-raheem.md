## Meaning

The same root as Ar-Raḥmān, but a different form and a different work. Where Ar-Raḥmān is mercy as an attribute of the One, Ar-Raḥīm is mercy as an act that arrives — continuous, particular, directed.

The classical reading is that Ar-Raḥmān is the mercy of this world, extended to all, and Ar-Raḥīm is the mercy reserved for the believers in the next.

## Lessons for the believer

The two names are almost always said together, and the pairing is doing something. General mercy is not enough to rest on; it reaches the tyrant too. Particular mercy has to be sought.

So the Book opens by naming both: the mercy you are already inside, and the mercy you must turn toward.
