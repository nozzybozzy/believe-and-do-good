## Meaning

The root carries two senses that sit strangely together in English but not in Arabic. *Jabr* is compelling — imposing a will nothing can resist. And *jabr* is also setting a broken bone: the splint an Arab doctor bound on a fracture was a *jabīrah*.

So Al-Jabbār is both the One whose will overrides everything, and the One who mends what has been broken.

## Lessons for the believer

Those two meanings are not in tension; they are the same fact seen twice. Only a power that nothing resists can repair what nothing else can.

Between the two prostrations the Muslim says *wa-jburnī* — from this root. *Mend me.* It is asked in the lowest physical position a person can take, which is the point.
